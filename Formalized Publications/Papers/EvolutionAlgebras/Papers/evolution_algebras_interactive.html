Abstract. We study finite-dimensional solvable evolution algebras, that is, evolution algebras for which their derived series terminates at zero. We disprove a conjecture by García-Martínez and Pérez-Rodríguez. Then, in terms of idempotent subspaces, we propose a novel characterization of solvable evolution algebras as well as of evolution algebras without nonzero idempotent elements. We remark on the use of AI in this research, see Statement of AI Use.

1. Introduction

Introduced by Tian in their doctoral thesis [Tia04], and subsequently in their monograph [Tia08], evolution algebras provide an algebraic framework for studying non-Mendelian genetics. The study of these algebras is an active area of research providing tools for various disciplines, including biology, physics, economics and logistics. Mathematically, evolution algebras are linked to graph theory, group theory, stochastic processes and dynamical systems. For a thorough historical survey, we refer to [CFNT22].

Formally, an evolution algebra over a field \(K\) is a (non-associative) \(K\)-algebra, along with a distinguished basis \(B\), called a natural basis, such that \(xy = 0\) for distinct \(x,y\in B\). We focus on solvable algebras, that is, algebras for which their derived series terminates at zero. These algebras have already been studied for their amenable properties in [FGN22] and [LPP25]. Recently, García-Martínez and Pérez-Rodríguez ([GP26]) proposed the following conjecture.

In this paper, we show that this conjecture is false, and in turn, provide an appropriate characterization of solvable evolution algebras in terms of idempotent subspaces. We also study evolution algebras which admit no nonzero idempotents.

2. Preliminaries

Throughout, we will consider an algebraically closed field \(K\), and by algebra we always mean (non-associative) \(K\)-algebra. Whenever we refer to a finite-dimensional evolution algebra \(\mathcal{E}\), we assume that it comes equipped with a natural basis \(B = \{e_1,\ldots, e_n\}\) and a structure matrix \(M_B(\mathcal{E})\).

Let \(V\) be a subspace of the evolution algebra \(\mathcal{E}\). We denote \(V^2 = \operatorname{Span}\{xy\mid x,y\in V\}\). Recursively, we define \(\mathcal{E}^{(1)} = \mathcal{E}\) and \(\mathcal{E}^{(i+1)}=(\mathcal{E}^{(i)})^2\) for \(i\geq 1\). Then, \(\mathcal{E}\) is called solvable if there exists \(m\in \mathbb{N}^+\) such that \(\mathcal{E}^{(m)} = 0\). It is easy to see that for \(i\geq 1\), \(\mathcal{E}^{(i+1)}\subseteq \mathcal{E}^{(i)}\). Hence, if \(\mathcal{E}\) is finite-dimensional, then the chain \(\mathcal{E}^{(1)}\supseteq \mathcal{E}^{(2)}\supseteq\ldots\) stabilizes at some \(l\in \mathbb{N}^+\). We define \(\mathcal{E}^{(\infty)} = \mathcal{E}^{(l)}\).

A counterexample to the original conjecture.

As originally stated in [GP26], Conjecture 1.1 holds in dimension two. However, starting at dimension three, the conjecture fails. The following example shows a three-dimensional complex evolution algebra which is not solvable and does not admit nonzero idempotent elements. This example is certainly not unique and can easily be extended to any dimension.

Example 2.1. Let \(\mathcal{E}_0\) be the complex evolution algebra whose structure matrix with respect to the natural basis \(\{e_1,e_2,e_3\}\) is \[ A = \begin{pmatrix} - 2 & 0 & 2 \\ 0 & 1 & -1 \\ 0 & -1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}. \] This defines the relations \(r_1 = e_1 \cdot e_1 = -2 e_1 + 2 e_3\), \(r_2 = e_2\cdot e_2 =e_2 - e_3\), and \(r_3 = e_3\cdot e_3 = -e_2 + e_3\). Directly from the definition, \(\mathcal{E}_0^{(2)} = \operatorname{Span}\{r_1, r_2, r_3\} = \operatorname{Span}\{r_1, r_2\}\). Moreover, note that \[ r_1 \cdot r_1 = -8e_1 -4 e_2 +12e_3 = 4 ( r_1 - r_2), \] \[ r_1 \cdot r_2 = 2e_2 - 2 e_3 = 2r_2, \quad \text{and} \quad r_2\cdot r_2 = 0. \] It follows that \(\mathcal{E}_0^{(k)} = \mathcal{E}_0^{(2)}\neq 0\) for \(k\geq 2\), showing that \(\mathcal{E}_0\) is not solvable. On the other hand, any idempotent element \(x e_1 + y e_2 + ze_3 \in \mathcal{E}_0\) must satisfy the system of equations \[ A^T \begin{pmatrix} x^2 \\ y^2 \\ z^2 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \\ z \end{pmatrix}. \] The only solution to this system of equations is \((x,y,z) = (0,0,0)\), so \(\mathcal{E}_0\) admits no nonzero idempotent elements.

3. Solvable Evolution Algebras and Idempotent Elements

It turns out that solvable evolution algebras are characterized by the lack, not of nonzero idempotent elements, but of nontrivial idempotent subspaces. We say that a subspace \(V\) of an evolution algebra \(\mathcal{E}\) is idempotent if \(V^2=V\). Our characterization follows from the following simple lemma.

Definition IsIdempotentSubmodule 
variable (K E : Type*) [CommSemiring K] [NonUnitalNonAssocSemiring E] [Module K E]
def IsIdempotentSubmodule (V : Submodule K E) : Prop :=
  square K E V = V
source
Lemma 3.1. Let \(\mathcal{E}\) be a finite-dimensional evolution algebra. Then, \(\mathcal{E}^{(\infty)}\) is the greatest idempotent subspace of \(\mathcal{E}\). That is, \(\left(\mathcal{E}^{(\infty)}\right)^2 = \mathcal{E}^{(\infty)}\), and if a subspace \(V\subseteq \mathcal{E}\) satisfies \(V^2 = V\), then \(V\subseteq \mathcal{E}^{(\infty)}\).
Lemma einfty_is_idempotent 
variable (K E : Type*) [Field K] [NonUnitalNonAssocRing E]
theorem einfty_is_idempotent [NonUnitalNonAssocAlgebra K E]
    [EvolutionAlgebra K E] [FiniteDimensional K E] :
    square K E (derivedSeriesInf K E) = derivedSeriesInf K E ∧
      ∀ V : Submodule K E, square K E V = V → V ≤ derivedSeriesInf K E
source
Proof.

The identity \(\left(\mathcal{E}^{(\infty)}\right)^2 = \mathcal{E}^{(\infty)}\) follows directly from the definition of \(\mathcal{E}^{(\infty)}\). Suppose that the subspace \(V\subseteq \mathcal{E}\) is idempotent. Since \(V\subseteq \mathcal{E}\), \(V = V^2 \subseteq \mathcal{E}^{(2)}\). Inductively, \(V\subseteq \mathcal{E}^{(k)}\) for any positive integer \(k\); in particular, \(V\subseteq \mathcal{E}^{(\infty)}\).

□

  1. \(\mathcal{E}\) is solvable,
  2. \(\mathcal{E}^{(\infty)} = 0\),

In turn, the existence (or lack thereof) of nonzero idempotent elements in an evolution algebra also admits a simple characterization. For the next result, we will need some elementary facts from algebraic geometry, for which we refer to [Har92].

Theorem 3.3. Let \(\mathcal{E}\) be a finite-dimensional evolution algebra. Then, \(\mathcal{E}\) admits a nonzero idempotent element if and only if \(\mathcal{E}\) admits a nontrivial idempotent subspace \(V\) such that \(N(V) = 0\).
Theorem IdempotentIff.idempotent_iff 
variable (K E : Type*) [Field K] [NonUnitalNonAssocRing E]
  [NonUnitalNonAssocAlgebra K E]
theorem idempotent_iff [IsAlgClosed K] [EvolutionAlgebra K E]
    [FiniteDimensional K E] :
    (∃ x : E, IsIdempotentElem x ∧ x ≠ 0) ↔
      ∃ V : Submodule K E, V ≠ ⊥ ∧ IsIdempotentSubmodule K E V ∧
        absNilpotent K E V = {0}
source
Proof.

Suppose \(x\in \mathcal{E}\) satisfies \(x^2 = x\). Then, \(V=\operatorname{Span}\{x\}\) is an idempotent subspace and \(N(V) = 0\).

Conversely, suppose that \(V\subseteq \mathcal{E}\) is an idempotent subspace such that \(N(V)=0\). Since \(V^2 = V\), the map \(\varphi:V\to V\) given by \(\varphi(x) = x^2\) is a well-defined homogeneous map of degree two. We denote the projectivization of \(V\) by \(\mathbb{P}(V)\). Thus, \(N(V) = 0\) implies that \(\varphi\) induces the map \(\overline{\varphi}:\mathbb P(V)\to \mathbb P(V)\) given by \(\overline{\varphi}([x]) = [x^2]\).

Since the base field is algebraically closed, \(\overline{\varphi}\) has a fixed point, say \([x_0]\). Hence, \(x_0^2 =\lambda x_0\) for some \(\lambda \neq 0\). Therefore, \(\frac{1}{\lambda} x_0\) is an idempotent in \(\mathcal{E}\).

Proof.

Trivially, it is always true that if \(\mathcal{E}\) is solvable, then \(\mathcal{E}\) does not admit nonzero idempotent elements. Conversely, suppose that \(\mathcal{E}\) is not solvable. By Theorem 3.2, \(\mathcal{E}\) contains a nontrivial idempotent subspace \(V\). Then, \(V\subseteq \mathcal{E}^{(\infty)}\) implying \(N(V) \subseteq N(\mathcal E^{(\infty)}) = 0\). Therefore, by Theorem 3.3, \(\mathcal{E}\) admits a nonzero idempotent element.

Remark 3.5. Given a finite-dimensional evolution algebra \(\mathcal{E}\), we are yet to find idempotent subspaces of dimension greater than two which do not properly contain a nontrivial idempotent subspace.

As we indicated earlier, Corollary 3.4 implies Proposition 2.8 from [HW26]. This follows directly from the following remark.

Remark 3.6. Let \(\mathcal{E}\) be a finite-dimensional evolution algebra such that \(\mathcal{E}^{(\infty)}\) is also an evolution algebra with natural basis \(\{e_1,\ldots,e_n\}\). Then \(N(\mathcal{E}^{(\infty)}) = 0\).
Remark EvolutionAlgebras.einfty_absNilpotent_eq_zero 
variable (K E : Type*) [Field K] [NonUnitalNonAssocRing E]
  [NonUnitalNonAssocAlgebra K E] [EvolutionAlgebra K E] [FiniteDimensional K E]
theorem einfty_absNilpotent_eq_zero
    [EvolutionAlgebra K ↥(derivedSeriesInf K E)] :
    absNilpotent K E (derivedSeriesInf K E) = {0}
source
Proof.

Indeed, by Lemma 3.1, \((\mathcal{E}^{(\infty)})^2 = \mathcal{E}^{(\infty)}\). This implies that \(\{e_1^2,\ldots,e_n^2\}\) is a basis for \(\mathcal{E}^{(\infty)}\). Then, for \(x=(x_1e_1+\cdots+x_ne_n)\in \mathcal{E}^{(\infty)}\), \(0=x^2=x_1^2e_1^2+\cdots+x_n^2e_n^2\) implies \(x_i^2=0\) for \(i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\). In particular, \(x=0\).
