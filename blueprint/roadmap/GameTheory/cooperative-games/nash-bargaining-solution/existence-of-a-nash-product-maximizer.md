# Existence and uniqueness of the Nash bargaining solution

Let $S \subseteq \mathbb{R}^2$ be compact and convex and $d \in S$, and suppose some $s \in S$ has $s_1 > d_1$ and $s_2 > d_2$. Then $N(s) = (s_1 - d_1)(s_2 - d_2)$ attains its maximum over $\{s \in S : s \ge d\}$, the maximum is positive, and the maximizing utility vector is unique: if $s \ne s'$ both attain the maximum, their midpoint lies in the set and, since $\log N$ is strictly concave where both coordinates exceed $d$, has a larger product. Distinct physical allocations may give the same utility vector. Nash's axiomatic characterization of this point is a separate statement.
