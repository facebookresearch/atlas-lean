# Cobb–Douglas and CES demands

For Cobb–Douglas utility $u(x) = \prod_g x_g^{a_g}$ with $a_g \ge 0$, $\sum_g a_g = 1$, the Marshallian demand at prices $p > 0$ and income $w$ is $x_g = a_g w / p_g$; Cobb–Douglas exchange economies satisfy WGS, and their equilibrium prices are characterized by a system of linear inequalities. For CES utility $u(x) = (\sum_g a_g x_g^{\rho})^{1/\rho}$ with $\rho < 1$, $\rho \ne 0$, demand is $x_g = w\, a_g^{\sigma} p_g^{-\sigma} / \sum_h a_h^{\sigma} p_h^{1-\sigma}$ with $\sigma = 1/(1-\rho)$, and CES economies with $0 < \rho < 1$ satisfy GS.

## Proof sketch

Solve the budget-constrained maximization by Lagrange multipliers. For Cobb–Douglas, a trader's spending on good $g$ is a fixed share of its income, which increases when other prices rise; for CES with $\sigma > 1$ the cross-price derivatives are positive.
