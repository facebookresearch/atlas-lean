# First welfare theorem for exchange and Fisher markets

In an exchange market (or a Fisher market) with locally nonsatiated utilities, the allocation of every competitive equilibrium is Pareto optimal among feasible allocations: no feasible allocation makes every trader weakly better off and some trader strictly better off. No convexity or continuity assumption is needed.

## Proof sketch

A Pareto improvement $y$ must cost each trader at least its budget, and strictly more for the strictly better-off trader, so $p \cdot \sum_i y_i > p \cdot \sum_i e_i$, contradicting feasibility of $y$ with nonnegative prices.
