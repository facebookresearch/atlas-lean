# Arrow–Debreu exchange market and competitive equilibrium

An *exchange market* has finitely many traders $i$ with nonnegative endowments $e_i \in \mathbb{R}^G_{\ge 0}$ of finitely many divisible goods and utility functions $u_i$ on nonnegative bundles. At prices $p \ge 0$, trader $i$'s *demand set* consists of the bundles $x \ge 0$ with $p \cdot x \le p \cdot e_i$ maximizing $u_i$. A *competitive equilibrium* is a pair $(x, p)$ with $p \ge 0$, each $x_i$ in $i$'s demand set, and markets clearing with free disposal: the excess demand $z = \sum_i (x_i - e_i)$ satisfies $z_g \le 0$ and $p_g z_g = 0$ for every good. Utility $u_i$ is *locally nonsatiated* if every bundle has arbitrarily close strictly preferred bundles.

## Sources

- Kenneth J. Arrow and Gerard Debreu, "Existence of an Equilibrium for a Competitive Economy", Econometrica, 1954.
- Bruno Codenotti and Kasturi Varadarajan, "Computation of Market Equilibria by Convex Programming", Algorithmic Game Theory, Cambridge University Press, 2007.
