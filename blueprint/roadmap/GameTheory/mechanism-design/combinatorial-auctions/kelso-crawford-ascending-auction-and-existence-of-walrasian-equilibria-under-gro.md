# Kelso–Crawford ascending auction and existence of Walrasian equilibria under gross substitutes

If all bidders have gross-substitutes valuations, the ascending auction in which prices of over-demanded items rise by $\varepsilon$ while bidders hold their current items terminates after finitely many steps at an $\varepsilon$-Walrasian equilibrium, whose welfare is at least the optimum minus $\varepsilon m$, where $m$ is the number of items. Consequently, by compactness or by integrality of the LP relaxation, an exact Walrasian equilibrium exists for gross-substitutes valuations.

## Proof sketch

Gross substitutes guarantees that bidders keep demanding the items they hold when other prices rise, so allocations remain feasible; prices are bounded by the maximum value, so the process stops. At termination every bidder demands its bundle up to $\varepsilon$ per item.
