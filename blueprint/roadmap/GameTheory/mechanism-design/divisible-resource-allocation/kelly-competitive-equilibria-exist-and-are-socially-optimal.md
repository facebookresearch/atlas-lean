# Kelly competitive equilibria exist and are socially optimal

Every price-taking competitive equilibrium of Kelly's mechanism induces a socially optimal allocation (first welfare theorem, without concavity or differentiability assumptions). If utilities are admissible, a competitive equilibrium exists, and it is unique when the utilities are strictly concave.

## Proof sketch

Price-taking optimality means $U_i(d_i) - \rho d_i$ is maximized, so the equilibrium solves the Lagrangian of the social problem with multiplier $\rho$. Existence comes from the KKT conditions of the concave social optimization problem.
