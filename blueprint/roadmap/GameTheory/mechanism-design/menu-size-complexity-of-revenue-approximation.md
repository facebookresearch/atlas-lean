# Menu-size complexity of revenue approximation

For one additive buyer with independent item values, can a polynomial-size menu approximate optimal revenue to any fixed multiplicative accuracy?

## What would settle it

For every fixed ε > 0, prove or refute the existence of d(ε) with $C(n,\varepsilon) = O(n^{d(\varepsilon)})$, where $C(n, \varepsilon)$ is the least menu size such that, for all product distributions $F_1 \times \dots \times F_n$ of item values on the nonnegative reals, some mechanism with at most that many menu entries extracts at least $(1-\varepsilon)$ of the optimal revenue from one additive buyer. Incentive compatibility, individual rationality and lotteries are part of the mechanism model.

## Status

The cited sources pose this as an open question in the versions cited; this page does not certify its present status. The supporting open-problem statement is older or its status evidence is incomplete; check later literature before relying on it.

Babaioff, Gonczarowski and Nisan prove that the menu size C(n, ε) is finite for every n and ε > 0 and pose the polynomial question as Open Problem 1.6.

This page records the cited research question and asserts no theorem or present-status certification.
