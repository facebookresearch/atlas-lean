# Combinatorial agency and the price of unaccountability

In *combinatorial agency*, $n$ agents each choose to exert effort (cost $c_i > 0$) or not; a technology maps the effort profile $a$ to a success probability $t(a)$, strictly increasing in each agent's effort. A principal valuing success at $v$ offers contracts paying agent $i$ an amount $p_i$ upon success. The contract inducing a target set of exerting agents as a Nash equilibrium with minimal expected payment pays each exerting agent $c_i / \Delta_i(a_{-i})$, where $\Delta_i$ is $i$'s marginal contribution to the success probability. The *price of unaccountability* is the worst-case ratio between the first-best welfare (effort observable) and the welfare of the principal's optimal contract under hidden action.
