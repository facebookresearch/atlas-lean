# Rural hospitals theorem, marriage case: every stable matching matches the same women

Let $M$ (the men) and $W$ (the women) be finite sets; each man has a strict total order of preference on $W \cup \{\varnothing\}$ and each woman one on $M \cup \{\varnothing\}$, where $\varnothing$ stands for remaining single. A matching with singles is a partial bijection $\mu$ between $M$ and $W$: $\mu(m) \in W \cup \{\varnothing\}$ is the partner of man $m$ and $\mu^{-1}(w) \in M \cup \{\varnothing\}$ that of woman $w$, with $\varnothing$ meaning single.
For stable matchings with singles $\mu$ and $\nu$ and every woman $w$, $w$ has a partner in $\mu$ if and
only if she has a partner in $\nu$. With the women in the role of Roth's hospitals and all quotas equal to
one, this is Roth's Theorem: a hospital that fills fewer than its one position at a stable outcome, that
is, a woman single at a stable matching, has the same (empty) assignment at every stable outcome.

The proof applies the men's statement to the market with the two sides exchanged.
