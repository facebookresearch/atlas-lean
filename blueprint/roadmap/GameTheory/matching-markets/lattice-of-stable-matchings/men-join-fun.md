# Men's pointwise best partner

Let $M$ (the men) and $W$ (the women) be sets, every man with a strict total order of preference on $W$ and every woman with one on $M$. A perfect matching is a bijection $\mu : M \to W$; a pair $(m, w)$ blocks $\mu$ if $m$ prefers $w$ to $\mu(m)$ and $w$ prefers $m$ to $\mu^{-1}(w)$, and $\mu$ is stable if no pair blocks it.
For perfect matchings $\mu$ and $\nu$, the men's pointwise best partner gives every man $m$ the partner he
prefers among $\mu(m)$ and $\nu(m)$: $(\mu \vee \nu)(m) = \nu(m)$ if $m$ prefers $\nu(m)$ to $\mu(m)$, and
$(\mu \vee \nu)(m) = \mu(m)$ otherwise (Roth, *Econometrica* 54 (1986), Lemma 2, the choice set
$\mathrm{Ch}(x(h) \cup y(h))$ with quota one).
