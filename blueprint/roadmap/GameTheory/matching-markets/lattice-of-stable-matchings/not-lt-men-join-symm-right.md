# Opposition of interests: no woman prefers her men's-join partner to her second partner

Let $M$ (the men) and $W$ (the women) be finite sets, every man with a strict total order of preference on $W$ and every woman with one on $M$. A perfect matching is a bijection $\mu : M \to W$; a pair $(m, w)$ blocks $\mu$ if $m$ prefers $w$ to $\mu(m)$ and $w$ prefers $m$ to $\mu^{-1}(w)$, and $\mu$ is stable if no pair blocks it.
Let $\mu$ and $\nu$ be stable perfect matchings. No woman $w$ prefers her partner in the men's join
$\mu \vee \nu$ to her partner $\nu^{-1}(w)$. With the companion statement for $\mu$, every woman receives the
partner she likes less among her two partners.

Proof: if $w$ prefers her join partner $m$ to her $\nu$-partner, then $\nu(m) \neq w$, so the join gave $m$
his $\mu$-partner $w$, which he prefers to $\nu(m)$; then $(m, w)$ blocks $\nu$.
