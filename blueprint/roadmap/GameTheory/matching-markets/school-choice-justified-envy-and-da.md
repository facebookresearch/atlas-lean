# School choice: justified envy and DA

Consider a school-choice problem with finitely many students and schools, school capacities, strict student preferences over acceptable
schools and the outside option, and strict school priorities over students, inducing a many-to-one matching market. (1) A matching is
stable if and only if it is individually rational, non-wasteful and has no justified envy; a matching without justified envy may be wasteful,
and then it is not stable. (2) The student-optimal stable matching Pareto-dominates, for students (every student weakly prefers it), every other matching that
eliminates justified envy (Abdulkadiroğlu and Sönmez, Proposition 1, attributed there to Gale and Shapley), with matchings and
justified envy as defined in the source. Statement (1) is an elementary translation of the many-to-one blocking-pair condition: a blocking
pair is either an unfilled seat at a school the student prefers (waste) or a seat held by a student of lower priority (justified envy).
