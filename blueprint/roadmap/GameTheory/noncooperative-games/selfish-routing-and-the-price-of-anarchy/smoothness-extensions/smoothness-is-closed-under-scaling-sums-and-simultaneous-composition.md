# Smoothness is closed under scaling, sums and simultaneous composition

Let $\lambda \ge 0$ and $\mu$ be reals. If a cost game $G$ is $(\lambda, \mu)$-smooth, then so is the game with all costs scaled by $\alpha \ge 0$; if $G$ and $G'$ on the same players and strategies are $(\lambda, \mu)$-smooth, so is the game with costs $c_i + c'_i$; and the simultaneous composition of finitely many $(\lambda, \mu)$-smooth games (players play one strategy in each game and pay the sum of costs) is $(\lambda, \mu)$-smooth. Smoothness is monotone: $(\lambda, \mu)$-smooth implies $(\lambda', \mu')$-smooth for $\lambda' \ge \lambda$, $\mu' \ge \mu$ when social costs are nonnegative.

## Proof sketch

The smoothness inequality is linear in the cost functions and additive over independent components.
