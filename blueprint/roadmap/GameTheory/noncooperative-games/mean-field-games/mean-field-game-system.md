# The mean field game system

On $\mathbb{R}^d \times [0, T]$, a classical solution of the mean field game system is a pair
$(u, m)$, continuous on $\mathbb{R}^d \times [0, T]$ and of class $C^2$ in space and $C^1$ in time on
$\mathbb{R}^d \times (0, T)$, with

$$\begin{aligned}
-\partial_t u - \Delta u + \tfrac12 |Du|^2 &= F(x, m(t)), \\
\partial_t m - \Delta m - \operatorname{div}(m\, Du) &= 0, \\
m(0) = m_0, \qquad u(x, T) &= G(x, m(T)),
\end{aligned}$$

where $m(t)$ is a probability density with finite first moment, identified with the probability
measure it defines, so that the costs $F, G : \mathbb{R}^d \times P_1 \to \mathbb{R}$ can be evaluated.
The two equations hold on $\mathbb{R}^d \times (0, T)$. Cardaliaguet's definition asks for the
$C^2$/$C^1$ regularity up to $t = 0$ and $t = T$; requiring it only in the open interval lets the
Hölder initial densities and Lipschitz terminal costs of his existence theorem be data.

The first equation is the Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman equation of a typical player who, facing the
crowd $m$, controls $dX_t = \alpha_t\, dt + \sqrt2\, dB_t$ to minimise
$\mathbb{E}[\int_0^T (\frac12|\alpha_t|^2 + F(X_t, m(t)))\, dt + G(X_T, m(T))]$; it runs backward from the
terminal cost. Its optimal feedback is $\alpha = -Du$ (Cardaliaguet, Lemma 3.7), and the second
equation, a Fokker–Planck equation run forward from $m_0$, says that $m(t)$ is the law of the
players who use it. An equilibrium is a fixed point: the crowd generated by the best responses is
the crowd they respond to. The second equation is the Fokker–Planck equation
$\partial_t m = \Delta m + \operatorname{div}(m\, Du)$ with unit diffusion and drift $-Du$, and
$\Delta u = \operatorname{div} \nabla u$.
