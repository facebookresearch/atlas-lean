# Press–Dyson zero-determinant strategies

In an iterated $2 \times 2$ game with stage payoffs $S_X = (R, S, T, P)$ and $S_Y = (R, T, S, P)$ indexed by the outcomes $cc, cd, dc, dd$ from $X$'s point of view, let $X$ play the memory-one strategy $p = (p_1, p_2, p_3, p_4)$ (her cooperation probabilities after each outcome) and $Y$ the memory-one strategy $q$ in his own order, and let $v$ be the stationary vector of the resulting Markov chain. Press and Dyson (2012) show that $v\cdot f$ is, up to normalization, a $4 \times 4$ determinant $D(p, q, f)$ whose second column $\tilde p = (-1 + p_1, -1 + p_2, p_3, p_4)$ depends only on $p$, whose third column $\tilde q = (-1 + q_1, q_3, -1 + q_2, q_4)$ depends only on $q$ (their eqs. (3)–(4)), and whose fourth column is $f$; hence $s_X = D(p, q, S_X)/D(p, q, \mathbf 1)$, $s_Y = D(p, q, S_Y)/D(p, q, \mathbf 1)$ (eq. (5)) and $\alpha s_X + \beta s_Y + \gamma = D(p, q, \alpha S_X + \beta S_Y + \gamma\mathbf 1)/D(p, q, \mathbf 1)$ (eq. (6)). If $X$ chooses $p$ with $\tilde p = \alpha S_X + \beta S_Y + \gamma\mathbf 1$, the numerator has two equal columns and the linear relation $\alpha s_X + \beta s_Y + \gamma = 0$ (eq. (7)) holds whatever $Y$ does: a zero-determinant strategy, feasible when $p \in [0, 1]^4$. With $\alpha = 0$, $X$ sets $Y$'s score, with $p_2, p_3$ given by eq. (8) in terms of $p_1, p_4$; in the prisoner's dilemma ($T > R > P > S$) exactly the scores $P \le s_Y \le R$ can be forced, while $X$ cannot unilaterally set her own score.

## Expansion

1. The model: memory-one strategies, the Markov chain on outcomes and its stationary distribution, with the hypothesis under which long-run scores are well defined.
2. The determinant lemma: column operations on $M - I$ and the representation $v\cdot f \propto D(p, q, f)$.
3. Zero-determinant strategies and the enforced linear relation.
4. Equalizer strategies, eq. (8), the range $[P, R]$ in the prisoner's dilemma, and the impossibility of setting one's own score; extortionate strategies (eqs. (12)–(16)), as separate statements.
