# Uniqueness of the subgame-perfect equilibrium without continuation ties

In a finite perfect-information game, backward induction produces a pure subgame-perfect equilibrium, which is in particular a pure Nash equilibrium. If at every decision node distinct choices yield distinct continuation payoffs to the moving player under backward-induction play (no continuation ties), then the backward-induction profile is the unique pure subgame-perfect equilibrium; this holds in particular when all leaf payoffs of each player are distinct and there are no chance nodes.

## Proof sketch

By the one-shot deviation principle every subgame-perfect equilibrium picks, at each node, an optimal choice given optimal play below. Without ties this choice is unique, and induction on height shows it coincides with backward induction.
