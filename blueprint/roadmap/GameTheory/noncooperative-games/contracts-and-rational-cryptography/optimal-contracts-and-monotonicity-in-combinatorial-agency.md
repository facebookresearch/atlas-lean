# Optimal contracts and monotonicity in combinatorial agency

In combinatorial agency, the minimal-payment contract inducing a given effort profile makes it a pure Nash equilibrium, in fact a strong equilibrium, and every contract inducing that profile pays each exerting agent at least as much in expectation. Both the first-best and the hidden-action optimal effort sets change monotonically with the principal's value $v$: the number of exerting agents (and the success probability) at the optimum is nondecreasing in $v$. For anonymous technologies (depending only on the number of exerting agents), the optimal contract under hidden action can skip intermediate numbers of agents, and the price of unaccountability of AND-technologies is unbounded while that of OR-technologies is bounded in specific regimes.

## Proof sketch

Agent $i$ exerts effort iff $p_i \Delta_i \ge c_i$, so the minimal contract makes each constraint tight. Monotonicity in $v$ follows from a revealed-preference exchange argument comparing optimal profiles at two values.
