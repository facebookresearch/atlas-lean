# Fishburn's infinite-electorate escape from Arrow's theorem

For an infinite set of voters and at least three alternatives, every ultrafilter $\mathcal U$ on the voters defines a social welfare function (society prefers $a$ to $b$ iff the set of voters preferring $a$ to $b$ belongs to $\mathcal U$) that satisfies weak Pareto and independence of irrelevant alternatives. It is dictatorial if and only if $\mathcal U$ is principal; hence a free ultrafilter yields a Pareto, IIA, non-dictatorial social welfare function.

## Proof sketch

Ultrafilter properties make the induced relation complete and transitive (intersections of decisive sets are decisive) and Pareto (the whole electorate is in $\mathcal U$); IIA holds by construction. A dictator $i$ means $\{i\} \in \mathcal U$, i.e. principality.
