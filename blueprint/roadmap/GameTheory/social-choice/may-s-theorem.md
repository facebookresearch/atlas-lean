# May's theorem

Let $N$ be a finite set of voters and consider social decision functions $D : \{-1, 0, 1\}^N \to \{-1, 0, 1\}$ on two alternatives, where an
individual entry $1$, $-1$ or $0$ means preferring the first alternative, preferring the second, or indifference, and the output means the
same for society. $D$ is anonymous if it is invariant under permutations of voters; neutral if $D(-d) = -D(d)$; and positively responsive if,
whenever $D(d) \in \{0, 1\}$ and $d'$ is obtained from $d$ by raising at least one entry and lowering none, $D(d') = 1$. Then $D$ is anonymous,
neutral and positively responsive if and only if $D(d) = \mathrm{sign}(\sum_{i \in N} d_i)$ (simple majority rule). May states positive responsiveness for a change of a single entry; the two forms are equivalent, by
changing entries one at a time. That $D$ is defined on all of $\{-1, 0, 1\}^N$ is May's condition that the rule be always decisive.
