# Binary encoding of natural numbers

The bitstring encoding of $\mathbb{N}$ that writes $0$ as the empty string and a positive integer $n$ as its
binary digits, least significant bit first, together with the decoding map, which recovers $n$ from its
code. It is the concrete encoding chosen to state the binary-input form of Theorem 3, with lists of piles
written as self-delimiting binary blocks; the theorem itself only says that integers are presented in
binary.

## Sources

- Vladimir Gurvich, Denis Martynov, Vladislav Maximchuk, and Michael Vyalyi, *On Remoteness Functions of Exact Slow k-NIM with k+1 Piles*, INTEGERS 24 (2024), Article G1, Theorem 3 ("integers are presented in binary").
