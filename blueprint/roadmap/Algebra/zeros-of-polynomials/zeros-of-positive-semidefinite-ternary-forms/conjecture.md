# Psd ternary forms with many zeros that are not h-squared multiples

This is the proposition that for every integer $k \ge 3$ there is a real ternary form $p \in \mathbb{R}[x_0, x_1, x_2]$, homogeneous of degree $2k$ and nonnegative at every real point, which has $k^2 + 1$ pairwise non-proportional nonzero real zeros and which cannot be written as $p = h^2 q$ with $h, q \in \mathbb{R}[x_0, x_1, x_2]$ and $h$ taking both a positive and a negative value (an unfolded reading of the question whether the zero threshold for positive semidefinite ternary forms of degree $2k$ is at least $k^2 + 1$).

Source: Real Algebraic Geometry With a View Toward Systems Control and Free Positivity, Oberwolfach Reports (2014), DOI 10.4171/owr/2014/17.
