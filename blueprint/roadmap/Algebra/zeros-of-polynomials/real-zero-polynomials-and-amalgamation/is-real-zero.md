# Real zero polynomial

For a real polynomial $p \in \mathbb{R}[x_i : i \in \sigma]$, the proposition that $p$ is real zero: for every real point $a \in \mathbb{R}^\sigma$ and every complex number $t$, if $p(ta) = 0$ (evaluating $p$ with complex arguments) then $t$ is real. Taking $a = 0$ shows $p(0) \ne 0$.

Source: M. Schweighofer, *Spectrahedral relaxations of hyperbolicity cones*, in Real Algebraic Geometry with a View Toward Hyperbolic Programming and Free Probability, Oberwolfach Reports 17 (2020), issue 1 (Report 12/2020), 652–654, real zero amalgamation conjecture, p. 653.
