# Unweighted Newton-type coefficient differences

For a real polynomial $f = \sum_k a_k x^k$ of degree $n$ and $k \in \mathbb{N}$, the number $c_k = a_k^2 - a_{k-1}a_{k+1}$ (with $a_{-1} = a_{n+1} = 0$); the coefficients vanish above the degree, so $c_0 = a_0^2$ and $c_n = a_n^2$.

Source: B. Shapiro, *Problems around polynomials: the good, the bad and the ugly...*, Arnold Math. J. 1 (2015), arXiv:1503.05295, Conjecture 10.
