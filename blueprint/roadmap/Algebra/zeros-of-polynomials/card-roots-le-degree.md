# A polynomial over an integral domain has at most as many roots as its degree

Let $R$ be an integral domain and $p \in R[X]$ a polynomial. Then the number of roots of $p$ in $R$, counted with
multiplicity, is at most the degree of $p$. (The zero polynomial is given no roots and degree $0$.)

## Sources

- S. Lang, *Algebra*, revised third edition, Graduate Texts in Mathematics 211, Springer (2002), Chapter IV, §1
  (a polynomial of degree $n \ge 0$ over an integral domain has at most $n$ roots).
