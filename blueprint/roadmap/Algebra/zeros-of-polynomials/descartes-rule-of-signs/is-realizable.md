# Pairs realizable for a sign pattern

A sign pattern of degree $d$ is a tuple $\sigma = (\sigma_0, \dots, \sigma_d) \in \{+, -\}^{d+1}$, $\sigma_k$ prescribing the sign of the coefficient of $x^k$. For natural numbers $\mathrm{pos}, \mathrm{neg}$, the proposition that $(\mathrm{pos}, \mathrm{neg})$ is realizable for $\sigma$ when some real polynomial of degree $d$ whose coefficient of $x^k$ is nonzero with sign $\sigma_k$ (for every $k \le d$) has all its real roots simple, exactly $\mathrm{pos}$ of them positive and exactly $\mathrm{neg}$ negative (in the sense of Cheriha, Gati and Kostov).

Source: B. Shapiro, *Problems around polynomials: the good, the bad and the ugly...*, Arnold Math. J. 1 (2015), arXiv:1503.05295, Conjecture 7; H. Cheriha, Y. Gati and V. P. Kostov, arXiv:1911.12255.
