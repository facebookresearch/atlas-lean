# Pairs admissible by Descartes' rule of signs

A sign pattern of degree $d$ is a tuple $\sigma = (\sigma_0, \dots, \sigma_d) \in \{+, -\}^{d+1}$, $\sigma_k$ prescribing the sign of the coefficient of $x^k$. For natural numbers $\mathrm{pos}, \mathrm{neg}$, the proposition that $(\mathrm{pos}, \mathrm{neg})$ is admissible for $\sigma$: with $c$ the number of sign changes of $\sigma$ and $p = d - c$, $\mathrm{pos} \le c$, $\mathrm{pos} \equiv c \pmod 2$, $\mathrm{neg} \le p$ and $\mathrm{neg} \equiv p \pmod 2$ (natural-number subtraction; $c \le d$).

Source: B. Shapiro, *Problems around polynomials: the good, the bad and the ugly...*, Arnold Math. J. 1 (2015), arXiv:1503.05295, Conjecture 7.
