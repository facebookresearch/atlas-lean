# An even-degree polynomial such that neither it nor G1 has a real zero

It is not the case that for every real polynomial $p$ of even degree $k \ge 4$, the polynomials $G_1 = (k-1)(p')^2 - k\,p\,p''$ (where $k = \deg p$) and $p$ together have a real zero: the number of real zeros of $G_1$ plus the number of real zeros of $p$, both counted with multiplicity, is positive. For $n \ge 2$ the polynomial $p(x) = x^{2n}/(2n) + x^2/2 + 1$ of degree $2n$ has no real zeros and neither has $G_1 = (2n-1)(p')^2 - 2n\,p\,p''$, so for this $p$ the number of real zeros of $G_1$ plus the number of real zeros of $p$ is $0$. For $n = 2$, $p = x^4/4 + x^2/2 + 1 > 0$ and $G_1 = -x^4 - 11x^2 - 4 < 0$ on the real line.
