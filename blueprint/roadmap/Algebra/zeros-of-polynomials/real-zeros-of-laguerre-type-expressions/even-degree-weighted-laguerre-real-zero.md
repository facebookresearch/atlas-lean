# Shapiro's Conjecture 12 in even degree at least four

This is the proposition that for every real polynomial $p$ of even degree $k \ge 4$, the polynomials $G_1 = (k-1)(p')^2 - k\,p\,p''$ (where $k = \deg p$) and $p$ together have a real zero: the number of real zeros of $G_1$ plus the number of real zeros of $p$, both counted with multiplicity, is positive. A count is the size of the multiset of real roots, which is empty for the zero polynomial: for $p = x^4$, $G_1 = 3(4x^3)^2 - 4x^4 \cdot 12x^2 = 0$ contributes $0$ although every real number is a zero of it, and the sum is $4$, the count for $p$. (In degree $2$ the statement fails trivially, e.g. $p = x^2 + 1$, $G_1 = -4$.)

Source: B. Shapiro, *Problems around polynomials: the good, the bad and the ugly...*, Arnold Math. J. 1 (2015), arXiv:1503.05295, Conjecture 12.
