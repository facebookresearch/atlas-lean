# Invariance of trace forms and of the Killing form

For a finite-dimensional Lie algebra $L$ over a field, the Killing form $\kappa(x, y) = \operatorname{tr}(\operatorname{ad} x \circ \operatorname{ad} y)$ is invariant: $\kappa([x, y], z) = \kappa(x, [y, z])$ for all $x, y, z \in L$. Mathlib proves this more generally, for the trace form of any Lie module. Let $R$ be a commutative ring, $L$ a Lie algebra over $R$ and $M$ a Lie module over $L$: an $R$-module on which each $x \in L$ acts by an $R$-linear map $\rho(x) \colon M \to M$, depending $R$-linearly on $x$, with $\rho([x, y]) = \rho(x) \circ \rho(y) - \rho(y) \circ \rho(x)$. The *trace form* of $M$ is the bilinear form $B_M(x, y) = \operatorname{tr}(\rho(x) \circ \rho(y))$ on $L$, where $\operatorname{tr}$ is Mathlib's trace of an $R$-linear map $M \to M$: the trace of its matrix in a finite basis of $M$ when $M$ has one, and $0$ otherwise. Then $B_M([x, y], z) = B_M(x, [y, z])$ for all $x, y, z \in L$. This is the invariance of a bilinear form, not an associativity property of the bracket, and it needs no hypothesis such as a principal ideal domain, characteristic zero or nondegeneracy. Taking $M = L$ with $\rho(x) = \operatorname{ad} x$, the trace form is the Killing form, which Mathlib defines in exactly this way; for a finite-dimensional Lie algebra over a field the trace is the usual one, which gives the familiar statement.
