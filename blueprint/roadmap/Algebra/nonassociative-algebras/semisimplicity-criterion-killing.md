# Cartan's criterion for a zero solvable radical in characteristic zero

Over a field of characteristic $0$, a finite-dimensional Lie algebra has zero solvable radical, that is, its largest solvable ideal is $0$, if and only if its Killing form $\kappa(x, y) = \operatorname{tr}(\operatorname{ad} x \circ \operatorname{ad} y)$ is nondegenerate. Mathlib proves this over principal ideal domains of characteristic $0$. Let $R$ be a principal ideal domain of characteristic $0$ and let $L$ be a Lie algebra over $R$ that is free and Noetherian as an $R$-module. Then the radical of $L$, its largest solvable ideal, is $0$ if and only if the Killing form of $L$ has zero kernel: the only $x \in L$ with $\kappa(x, y) = 0$ for all $y \in L$ is $x = 0$. Over a principal ideal domain the second condition says that $x \mapsto \kappa(x, \cdot)$ maps $L$ injectively into its dual, which in general is weaker than a perfect pairing. A finite-dimensional Lie algebra over a field of characteristic $0$ is free and Noetherian, which gives the familiar statement; the field need not be algebraically closed. Mathlib uses the name semisimple for a different notion, a direct sum of simple Lie algebras; the zero radical here is semisimplicity in the sense of the familiar statement.
