# Solvable Lie algebra

A Lie algebra over a field is solvable when its derived series $L^{(0)} = L$, $L^{(i+1)} = [L^{(i)}, L^{(i)}]$, where $[L^{(i)}, L^{(i)}]$ is the span of the brackets $[x, y]$ with $x, y \in L^{(i)}$, reaches $0$ after finitely many steps. Mathlib defines solvability for Lie rings, without reference to scalars. Let $L$ be a Lie ring. Its derived series is $L^{(0)} = L$ and $L^{(i+1)} = [L^{(i)}, L^{(i)}]$, the additive subgroup generated by the brackets $[x, y]$ with $x, y \in L^{(i)}$; every term of the series is an ideal of $L$. The Lie ring $L$ is *solvable* if $L^{(k)} = 0$ for some $k \ge 0$; the zero Lie ring is solvable with $k = 0$. If $L$ is a Lie algebra over a commutative ring $R$, these terms are the same sets as the terms of the derived series formed with $R$-spans, since $r[x, y] = [rx, y]$, so solvability does not depend on the scalars. For a Lie algebra over a field this gives the familiar definition.
