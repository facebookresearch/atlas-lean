# Zhao's vanishing conjecture for quartic forms

This is the proposition that for every $n$ and every homogeneous quartic $P \in \mathbb{C}[X_1, \dots, X_n]$, if $\Delta^m(P^m) = 0$ for all $m \ge 1$, where $\Delta = \sum_i \partial^2/\partial X_i^2$, then $\Delta^m(P^{m+1}) = 0$ for all sufficiently large $m$. By Zhao's theorem it is equivalent, over all $n$, to the Jacobian conjecture.

Source: Affine Algebraic Geometry, Oberwolfach Reports 4 (2007), no. 1, abstract on the Jacobian conjecture, Conjecture 5.1 (W. Zhao); W. Zhao, *Hessian nilpotent polynomials and the Jacobian conjecture*, Trans. Amer. Math. Soc. 359 (2007), 249–274, arXiv:math/0409534.
