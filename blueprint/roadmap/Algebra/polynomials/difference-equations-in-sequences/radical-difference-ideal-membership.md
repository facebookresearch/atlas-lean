# Radical difference ideal membership is undecidable

Over a field of characteristic zero, no algorithm decides whether a difference polynomial vanishes on every sequence solution of a finite system of difference equations, nor whether it lies in the radical of the difference ideal the system generates.

**Theorem (Pogudin–Scanlon–Wibmer).** Let $M$ be $\mathbb{N}$ or $\mathbb{Z}$, let $k$ be a field of characteristic zero, and let $k_0 \subseteq k$ be a computable subfield. Write $k_0[\sigma^m(\boldsymbol{X}) \mid m \in M]$ for the ring of $M$-polynomials in $\boldsymbol{X} = (X_1, \dots, X_n)$ over $k_0$. Each of the following problems, whose input is $n$ and $M$-polynomials $f_1, \dots, f_\ell, g \in k_0[\sigma^m(\boldsymbol{X}) \mid m \in M]$, is undecidable:

1. whether $g(\mathbf{x}) = 0$ for every solution $\mathbf{x}$ of $f_1 = \dots = f_\ell = 0$ in $k^M$;
2. whether the system $f_1 = \dots = f_\ell = 0$, $g \neq 0$ has a solution in $k^M$;
3. whether $g \in \sqrt{\langle \sigma^m(f_1), \dots, \sigma^m(f_\ell) \mid m \in M \rangle}$, the radical of the ideal of $k_0[\sigma^m(\boldsymbol{X}) \mid m \in M]$ generated by all shifts of $f_1, \dots, f_\ell$ (the radical difference ideal generated by them).

Conventions: rings of sequences, $M$-polynomials, solutions and computable subfields are as in the linked definitions; $\ell$, $n$ and the orders of the shifts are part of the input, and $g \neq 0$ in clause 2 means that $g(\mathbf{x})$ is not the zero sequence. For $g = 1$, clause 3 asks whether the difference ideal is the whole ring, and for an algebraically closed difference field that question is decidable by an effective bound of Ovchinnikov, Pogudin and Scanlon, which the source recalls; the undecidability comes from general $g$.

## Expansion

The key step is a translation between iterations of piecewise polynomial maps and the strong Nullstellensatz: for a piecewise polynomial map $\mathbf{p} \colon \mathbb{A}^n_k \to \mathbb{A}^n_k$ (a partition of affine space into finitely many locally closed pieces with a polynomial map on each) and a closed set $V$, one can compute difference polynomials $f_1, \dots, f_\ell, g$ such that, over every field extension $K$ of $k$, some orbit $\mathbf{x}_0 \in V$, $\mathbf{x}_{i+1} = \mathbf{p}(\mathbf{x}_i)$ avoids $x_{n,i} = 0$ for all $i \ge 1$ exactly when $f_1 = \dots = f_\ell = 0$ has a solution in $(K^M)^r$ on which $g$ does not vanish. Given $P \in \mathbb{Z}[t_1, \dots, t_n]$ with $P(0) \ne 0$, a piecewise polynomial map over $\mathbb{Q}$ runs $n!$ copies of an enumeration of all nondecreasing $n$-tuples of natural numbers, each conjugated by a permutation, so that together they reach all of $\mathbb{N}^n$, and at each iterate sets one more coordinate to the product of the values of $P$ at the $n!$ current tuples; this coordinate is nonzero at every iterate $i \ge 1$ exactly when $P$ has no zero in $\mathbb{N}^n$. Hilbert's tenth problem over $\mathbb{N}$ is undecidable by the Davis–Putnam–Robinson–Matiyasevich theorem, which gives clause 1, and clause 2 is its negation. For clause 3, the strong difference Nullstellensatz over an uncountable algebraically closed field $K \supseteq k$ (proved in the source for $|K| > |M|$) says that $g$ lies in the radical exactly when it vanishes on every solution in $K^M$, and the reduction is the same.

A good expansion adds and links pages for the correspondence between orbits of piecewise polynomial maps and the strong difference Nullstellensatz, the piecewise polynomial enumeration of $\mathbb{N}^n$, and the strong Nullstellensatz for difference equations over an uncountable algebraically closed field.
