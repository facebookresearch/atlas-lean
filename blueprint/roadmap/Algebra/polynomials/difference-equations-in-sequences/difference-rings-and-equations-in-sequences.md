# Difference rings, rings of sequences and difference equations

A difference ring is a commutative ring with an endomorphism; more generally a monoid can act by endomorphisms. Sequences over a ring, with the shift, form such a ring, and systems of difference polynomials can be asked to have solutions in it.

Let $M$ be a monoid. All rings are commutative with identity.

- A *difference ring* is a pair $(A, \sigma)$ of a commutative ring $A$ and a ring endomorphism $\sigma \colon A \to A$. More generally, an *$M$-ring* is a commutative ring $A$ with an action $m \mapsto \sigma^m$ of $M$ on $A$ by ring endomorphisms; a difference ring is an $\mathbb{N}$-ring for the additive monoid $(\mathbb{N}, +)$, where $\mathbb{N} = \{0, 1, 2, \dots\}$. A morphism of $M$-rings is a ring homomorphism that commutes with the actions.
- For a commutative ring $R$, the ring $R^M$ of *$M$-sequences* is the ring of all maps $M \to R$, with pointwise addition and multiplication, and with the action

  $$
  \sigma^m\bigl((x_\ell)_{\ell \in M}\bigr) = (x_{\ell m})_{\ell \in M} \qquad (m \in M).
  $$

  For $M = \mathbb{N}$ or $M = \mathbb{Z}$ this is the ring of one-sided or two-sided sequences with the shift $\sigma((x_i)_i) = (x_{i+1})_i$. For $M = \mathbb{N}^2$ or $\mathbb{Z}^2$ it is the ring of sequences indexed by the grid, with the two shifts, and for the free monoid $\Sigma^\ast$ on a finite alphabet $\Sigma$ it is the ring of functions on words, with $\sigma^w((x_u)_u) = (x_{uw})_u$.
- For an $M$-ring $A$ and unknowns $\boldsymbol{X} = (X_1, \dots, X_n)$, the ring of *$M$-polynomials* $A[\sigma^m(\boldsymbol{X}) \mid m \in M]$ is the ordinary polynomial ring over $A$ in the indeterminates $\sigma^m(X_i)$, $m \in M$, $1 \le i \le n$, with the action $\sigma^{m_1}(\sigma^{m_2}(X_i)) = \sigma^{m_1 m_2}(X_i)$. It is the free $M$-algebra over $A$ on $X_1, \dots, X_n$. For $M = \mathbb{N}$ its elements are the *difference polynomials*, and an equation $f = 0$ with $f$ a difference polynomial is a *difference equation*.
- Let $A \to B$ be a morphism of $M$-rings and $F \subseteq A[\sigma^m(\boldsymbol{X}) \mid m \in M]$. A tuple $\mathbf{x} = (x_1, \dots, x_n) \in B^n$ is a *solution* of the system $F = 0$ in $B$ when the unique $M$-ring morphism $A[\sigma^m(\boldsymbol{X}) \mid m \in M] \to B$ that extends $A \to B$ and sends $X_i$ to $x_i$ maps every element of $F$ to $0$; the image of $f$ is written $f(\mathbf{x})$.

The decision problems on the linked result pages take their inputs from a *computable subfield* $k_0$ of a field $k$: a subfield whose elements are encoded by finite strings so that equality and the field operations are computable. The field $k$ itself may be uncountable; $k_0$ and $k$ are $M$-rings with the trivial action, so the inclusion $k_0 \to k$ and the map $k \to k^M$ sending a constant to the constant sequence are morphisms of $M$-rings, and an instance is a finite set of $M$-polynomials with coefficients in $k_0$, given by the codes of their coefficients. Solutions are sought in $k^M$.

Example: the Fibonacci sequence $(1, 1, 2, 3, 5, \dots) \in \mathbb{C}^{\mathbb{N}}$ is a solution of the difference equation $\sigma^2(X) - \sigma(X) - X = 0$.

## Expansion

The ring of $M$-polynomials is the coordinate ring of the infinite-dimensional affine space with coordinates $\sigma^m(X_i)$, and a solution of $F = 0$ in $k^M$ is the same as a point, with coordinates in $k$, of the affine variety defined by all the shifted equations $\sigma^m(f) = 0$, $f \in F$, $m \in M$. This translation lets the source compare consistency in $k^M$ with the strong Nullstellensatz for the ideal generated by the shifts of $F$.
