# Polynomials in one variable over a semiring

A polynomial over a commutative ring is specified by its coefficients, only finitely many of which are nonzero. Mathlib defines polynomials more generally over any semiring $R$ with identity, not necessarily commutative: $R[X]$ is the additive monoid algebra of the natural numbers over $R$, so a polynomial is given by a coefficient function $\mathbb{N} \to R$, $i \mapsto a_i$, with $a_i = 0$ for all but finitely many $i$, and is written as the formal sum $\sum_i a_i X^i$. Two polynomials are equal exactly when their coefficients of $X^i$ agree for every $i$; addition is coefficientwise, $\sum_i a_i X^i + \sum_i b_i X^i = \sum_i (a_i + b_i) X^i$; and multiplication is $\left(\sum_i a_i X^i\right)\left(\sum_j b_j X^j\right) = \sum_k \left(\sum_{i+j=k} a_i b_j\right) X^k$, with each $a_i$ multiplied by $b_j$ in that order. The zero polynomial, all of whose coefficients are $0$, is included, and $X$ commutes with every polynomial even when $R$ is not commutative. When $R$ is a commutative ring with identity, this is the polynomial ring $R[x]$ of formal sums $a_n x^n + \cdots + a_1 x + a_0$ with $n \ge 0$.
