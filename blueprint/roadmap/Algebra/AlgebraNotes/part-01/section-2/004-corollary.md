# Corollary (Section 2, Subsection 2.3 (Subgroups of the Integers))

Given $a, b \in \mathbb{Z},$ consider $S = \{ai + bj \mid i, j \in \mathbb{Z}\}.$ The subset $S$ satisfies all the subgroup conditions, so by the theorem on subgroups of $\mathbb{Z}$, there is some $d$ such that $S = d\mathbb{Z}.$ In fact, $d = \gcd(a, b).$
