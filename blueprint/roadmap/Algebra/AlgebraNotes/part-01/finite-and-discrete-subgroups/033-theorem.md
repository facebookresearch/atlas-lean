# Theorem (Section: Finite and Discrete Subgroups, Continued, Subsection: Discrete Subgroups of R2)

If $G \subseteq \mathbb{R}^2$ is discrete, then (1) $G = \{0\}$; or (2) there exists some $\vec{\alpha} \in \mathbb{R}^2$ such that $G = \mathbb{Z} \vec{\alpha}$; or (3) there exist linearly independent vectors $\vec{a}, \vec{b} \in \mathbb{R}^2$ such that $G = \mathbb{Z} \vec{a} + \mathbb{Z} \vec{b}.$ This is called a *lattice* inside $\mathbb{R}^2.$
