# Baer's criterion for injective modules

A left module $Q$ over a ring with identity is injective if and only if every homomorphism from a left ideal of the ring to $Q$ extends to the whole ring. Mathlib proves this with an explicit universe condition. Let $R$ be a ring with identity, not necessarily commutative, and $Q$ a left $R$-module. Call $Q$ *injective* if for every injective homomorphism $\mu \colon A \to B$ of left $R$-modules and every homomorphism $\alpha \colon A \to Q$ there is a homomorphism $\beta \colon B \to Q$ with $\beta \circ \mu = \alpha$, where $A$ and $B$ range over the left $R$-modules in the universe of $Q$. Say that $Q$ satisfies *Baer's criterion* if for every left ideal $J$ of $R$, every homomorphism of left $R$-modules $J \to Q$ is the restriction to $J$ of a homomorphism $R \to Q$. If $R$ is in bijection with a type in the universe of $Q$, then $Q$ is injective if and only if it satisfies Baer's criterion. Baer's criterion implies injectivity without this condition; the converse uses it to replace $R$ and its left ideals by copies in the universe of $Q$. For modules in one universe that contains $R$, as in the usual category of left $R$-modules, this gives the familiar statement.
