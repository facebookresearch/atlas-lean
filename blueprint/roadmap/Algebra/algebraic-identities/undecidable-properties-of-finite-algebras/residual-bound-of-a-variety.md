# Residual bound of a variety

The residual bound of a variety is the least cardinal larger than the size of each of its subdirectly irreducible members, or infinity when there is none.

Let $\mathcal{V}$ be a [variety](varieties-of-algebras-and-finitely-generated-varieties.md) of a finite type. Its *residual bound* $\kappa(\mathcal{V})$ is the least cardinal strictly larger than the cardinality of every [subdirectly irreducible](subdirectly-irreducible-algebras.md) member of $\mathcal{V}$, and $\kappa(\mathcal{V}) = \infty$ when no such cardinal exists. For an algebra $A$ one writes $\kappa(A) = \kappa(\mathcal{V}(A))$, with $\mathcal{V}(A)$ the [variety generated](variety-generated-by-an-algebra.md) by $A$.

## Expansion

McKenzie associated to each Turing machine $T$ a finite algebra $A(T)$ with $\kappa(A(T)) < \omega$ if and only if $T$ halts, so having a finite residual bound is an undecidable property of a finite algebra.
