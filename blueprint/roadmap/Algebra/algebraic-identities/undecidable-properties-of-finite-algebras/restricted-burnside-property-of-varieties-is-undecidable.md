# The restricted Burnside property of varieties is undecidable

No algorithm decides, given finitely many identities, whether for every n the variety they define has only finitely many finite n-generated algebras up to isomorphism.

A variety $\mathcal{V}$ of algebras of finite type has the *restricted Burnside property* if for every $n \ge 1$ there are, up to isomorphism, only finitely many finite algebras in $\mathcal{V}$ generated by $n$ elements. For groups of exponent $m$ this is the restricted Burnside problem, solved positively by Zelmanov.

**Theorem (Sapir).** There is no algorithm that decides, given a finite type and a finite set $\Sigma$ of identities of that type, whether for every $n$ there are only finitely many finite $n$-generated algebras satisfying $\Sigma$, that is, whether the variety defined by $\Sigma$ has the restricted Burnside property.

Conventions: the type is part of the input, and isomorphic algebras are counted once.

## Expansion

The survey derives the result from a theorem of Bridson and Wilton, itself proved from Slobodskoi's undecidability of the universal theory of finite groups: there is no algorithm that decides, given a finite presentation of a group, whether the group has only finitely many finite quotients. The survey states that Sapir's monograph uses this theorem to prove the result, by a reduction from finitely presented groups to finite sets of identities; the survey does not reproduce the reduction.

A good expansion adds and links pages for the Bridson–Wilton theorem on finitely presented groups with finitely many finite quotients and for the reduction from it.
