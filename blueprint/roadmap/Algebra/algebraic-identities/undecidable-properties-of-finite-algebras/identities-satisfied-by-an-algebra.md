# Identities satisfied by an algebra

An identity is a pair of terms, read as the statement that the two terms agree for all values of their variables; an algebra satisfies it when its two term functions are equal.

Let $\tau$ be a finite type and $s, t$ [terms](terms-of-a-finite-type.md) of type $\tau$ in the variables $x_1, \dots, x_m$. The *identity* $s \approx t$ is the sentence $\forall x_1 \cdots \forall x_m\, (s = t)$. An algebra $A$ of type $\tau$ *satisfies* $s \approx t$, written $A \models s \approx t$, when $s^A(\bar a) = t^A(\bar a)$ for all $\bar a \in A^m$; it satisfies a set $\Sigma$ of identities when it satisfies each member of $\Sigma$.
