# Congruences and subdirectly irreducible algebras

A congruence of an algebra is an equivalence relation compatible with its operations; an algebra is subdirectly irreducible when its nontrivial congruences have a least member, and the residual bound of a variety measures how large its subdirectly irreducible members can be.

Let $A$ be an algebra of a finite type, as in [varieties of algebras and finitely generated varieties](varieties-of-algebras-and-finitely-generated-varieties.md).

- A *congruence* of $A$ is an equivalence relation $\theta$ on $A$ compatible with the operations: if $\omega$ has arity $n$ and $(a_i, b_i) \in \theta$ for $i \le n$, then $(\omega^A(a_1, \dots, a_n), \omega^A(b_1, \dots, b_n)) \in \theta$. For $a, b \in A$, $\operatorname{Cg}^A(a, b)$ is the least congruence containing the pair $(a, b)$, the *principal congruence* generated by it.
- $A$ is *subdirectly irreducible* when it has at least two elements and its congruences other than the identity relation have a least member.
- The *residual bound* $\kappa(\mathcal{V})$ of a variety $\mathcal{V}$ is the least cardinal strictly larger than the size of every subdirectly irreducible member of $\mathcal{V}$, and $\kappa(\mathcal{V}) = \infty$ when no such cardinal exists; for an algebra $A$ one writes $\kappa(A) = \kappa(\mathcal{V}(A))$.

## Expansion

McKenzie associated to each Turing machine $T$ a finite algebra $A(T)$ with $\kappa(A(T)) < \omega$ if and only if $T$ halts, so having a finite residual bound is an undecidable property of a finite algebra; the linked result pages build on this construction.
