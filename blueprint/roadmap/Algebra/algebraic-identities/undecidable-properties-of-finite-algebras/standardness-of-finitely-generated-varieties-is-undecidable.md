# Standardness of finitely generated varieties is undecidable

No algorithm decides, for a finite algebra of finite type, whether every Boolean topological algebra in the variety it generates is profinite; having finitely determined syntactic congruences, and first-order definability of the profinite members, are undecidable too.

Let $\mathcal{V}$ be a variety of finite type. A *topological algebra* is an algebra with a topology in which every basic operation is continuous; it is *Boolean* when the topology is compact, Hausdorff and totally disconnected. A Boolean topological algebra is *profinite* when it is isomorphic, as a topological algebra, to an inverse limit of finite discrete algebras. Write $\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{Bt}}$ for the class of Boolean topological algebras whose algebraic reduct lies in $\mathcal{V}$ and $\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{Bc}}$ for its profinite members, the *Boolean core*. $\mathcal{V}$ is *standard* if $\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{Bt}} = \mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{Bc}}$.

For an equivalence relation $\theta$ on an algebra $A$ and a set $F$ of terms $t(x, \bar p)$, let $\theta_F$ be the set of pairs $(a, b)$ with $(t(a, \bar e), t(b, \bar e)) \in \theta$ for all $t \in F$ and all parameters $\bar e$ from $A$; the *syntactic congruence* of $\theta$ is $\theta_F$ for $F$ the set of all terms, the largest congruence contained in $\theta$. A class $\mathcal{C}$ has *finitely determined syntactic congruences* (FDSC) if one finite set $F$ of terms gives the syntactic congruence of every equivalence relation on every member of $\mathcal{C}$.

**Theorem (Nurakunov–Stronkowski).** There is no algorithm that decides, given a finite algebra $A$ of finite type, whether

1. the variety $\mathcal{V}(A)$ is standard;
2. $\mathcal{V}(A)$ has finitely determined syntactic congruences;
3. the Boolean core of $\mathcal{V}(A)$ is definable, relative to the class of all Boolean topological algebras of the same type, by a set of first-order sentences.

Clause 2 answers Problem 9.3 of Clark, Davey, Freese and Jackson. Their Problem 9.1, whether a given finite algebra generates a standard universal Horn class, is not settled by this result.

## Expansion

The proof uses Moore's algebras $A'(T)$. The authors introduce *term finite principal subcongruences*, a common weakening of DPSC and FDSC, and show that for classes closed under homomorphic images it is equivalent to FDSC; hence a variety with DPSC has FDSC. FDSC implies standardness for a variety generated by a finite algebra (the FDSC-HSP theorem of Clark, Davey, Freese and Jackson), so if $T$ halts, $\mathcal{V}(A'(T))$ has DPSC, FDSC, a first-order definable Boolean core and is standard. If $T$ does not halt, an infinite subdirectly irreducible algebra $Q_\omega$ lies in $\mathcal{V}(A'(T))$ up to term equivalence and carries a compatible Boolean topology that is not profinite, so the variety is not standard and has no FDSC; a further argument shows its Boolean core is not first-order definable. Since $T \mapsto A'(T)$ is computable and halting is undecidable, all three properties are undecidable.

A good expansion adds and links pages for the equivalence of term finite principal subcongruences with FDSC, the theorem that FDSC implies standardness, and the non-profinite Boolean topology on $Q_\omega$.
