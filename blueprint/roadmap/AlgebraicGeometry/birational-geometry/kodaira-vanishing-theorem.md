# Kodaira vanishing theorem

Let $X$ be a smooth projective variety of dimension $n$ over a field of characteristic zero, with
canonical sheaf $\omega_X = \Omega^n_X$, and let $A$ be an ample invertible sheaf on $X$. Then

$$H^b(X, \omega_X \otimes A) = 0 \quad \text{for } b > 0, \qquad \text{equivalently} \qquad H^b(X, A^{-1}) = 0 \quad \text{for } b < n,$$

the two forms being equivalent by Serre duality.

Esnault and Viehweg state the theorem for complex projective manifolds (Theorem 1.2) and note that,
since every algebraic variety in characteristic zero is defined over a subfield of $\mathbb{C}$, flat
base change extends it to projective manifolds over any algebraically closed field of characteristic
zero; their introduction recalls that GAGA and base change for field extensions give the algebraic
statement over any field of characteristic zero. It is the case $D = 0$, $L$ ample, of the
[Kawamata–Viehweg vanishing theorem](kawamata-viehweg-vanishing-theorem.md) (Lazarsfeld, after
Theorem 2.2).

Esnault and Viehweg give an algebraic proof (Corollary 5.2). Choose $N \ge 2$ such that $A^N$ is
generated by its global sections and $H^b(X, A^{-N-1}) = 0$ for $b < n$, and let $D$ be the zero
divisor of a general section of $A^N$. Theorem 5.1 a), applied to $L = A$, to the divisor $D$ (with
coefficient $1 < N$) and to $D' = D$, shows that $H^b(X, A^{-1}(-D)) \to H^b(X, A^{-1})$ is surjective
for every $b$; as $A^{-1}(-D) \cong A^{-N-1}$, the target vanishes for $b < n$. Theorem 5.1 is their
vanishing theorem for restriction maps, which they derive from the $E_1$-degeneration of the
logarithmic de Rham complexes of cyclic covers.
