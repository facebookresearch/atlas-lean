# Algebraic Geometry

Algebraic geometry studies spaces cut out by polynomial equations and translates between their geometric and algebraic invariants.

The largest field, [varieties and projective geometry](varieties-and-projective-geometry/README.md), includes
[Bézout's theorem](varieties-and-projective-geometry/b-zout-s-theorem/README.md) for $n$ hypersurfaces in
$\mathbb{P}^n$ over an algebraically closed field that meet in finitely many points and Seshadri constants of ten
points in the plane, whose generic value Nagata's conjecture predicts to be $1/\sqrt{10}$. For
[elliptic curves](elliptic-curves/README.md) over a field with $q$ elements, the
[Hasse bound](elliptic-curves/hasse-bound/README.md) $(q + 1 - N)^2 \le 4q$ limits the number $N$ of points and
[Waterhouse's theorem](elliptic-curves/point-counts-and-frobenius-traces/README.md) says which integers occur as
$q + 1 - N$. [Algebraic curves](algebraic-curves/README.md) come with the Abel–Jacobi map into the Jacobian and the
uniform bound of Dimitrov, Gao and Habegger on the rational points of curves of genus at least $2$, and
[arithmetic geometry](arithmetic-geometry/README.md) has
[Faltings's theorem](arithmetic-geometry/faltings-s-theorem/README.md) and the Weil conjectures over finite fields.
