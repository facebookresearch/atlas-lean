# Positivity of hermitian line bundles

Let $X$ be an arithmetic variety of dimension $d + 1$ in the sense of Zhang, Moriwaki and Yuan (an
integral scheme, projective and flat over $\mathbb{Z}$) and $\bar L = (L, \lVert \cdot \rVert)$ a
hermitian line bundle on $X$.

*Semipositivity and Zhang's ampleness.* The metric of $\bar L$, or its curvature, is semipositive if
for every analytic map $f$ from the unit polydisc of $\mathbb{C}^d$ to $X(\mathbb{C})$ the curvature
of $f^* \bar L$ is semipositive. The hermitian line bundle $\bar L$ is relatively semipositive if
its curvature is semipositive and $\deg(L|_C) \ge 0$ for every closed curve $C$ contained in a fibre
of $X$ over a closed point of $\operatorname{Spec} \mathbb{Z}$; the second condition says that $L$
is nef on every such fibre. If $\bar L$ is relatively semipositive, then $L_\mathbb{Q}$ is nef.
Following Zhang, $\bar L$ is ample if

1. $L_\mathbb{Q}$ is ample;
2. $\bar L$ is relatively semipositive;
3. $\bar L$ is horizontally positive: $(\hat c_1(\bar L|_Y)^{\dim Y}) > 0$ for every horizontal
   irreducible closed subvariety $Y$ of $X$, that is, every one that dominates $\operatorname{Spec}
   \mathbb{Z}$.

If $L_\mathbb{Q}$ is ample and $\bar L$ is relatively semipositive, then $(L, e^{-c} \lVert \cdot
\rVert)$ is ample for every sufficiently large real number $c$. A nonzero section $s \in H^0(X, L)$
is effective if $\lVert s \rVert_{\sup} \le 1$ and strictly effective if $\lVert s \rVert_{\sup} <
1$; $\bar L$ is effective, or strictly effective, if it has such a section. Yuan calls $\bar L$ big
if there are $c > 0$ and $N_0$ such that $\log \#\{ s \in H^0(X, L^{\otimes N}) : \lVert s
\rVert_{\sup} < 1 \} > c N^{d+1}$ for all $N > N_0$, and strongly big if $\chi_{\sup}(\bar
L^{\otimes N}) > c N^{d+1}$ for all $N > N_0$.

*Moriwaki's ample, nef and big hermitian line bundles.* Moriwaki calls $\bar L$

- ample if $L$ is ample on $X$, the first Chern form $c_1(\bar L)$ is positive on $X(\mathbb{C})$,
  and for every sufficiently large $n$ the sheaf $L^{\otimes n}$ is generated by sections $s \in
  H^0(X, L^{\otimes n})$ with $\lVert s \rVert_{\sup} < 1$; on a singular $X(\mathbb{C})$,
  positivity and semipositivity of $c_1(\bar L)$ are tested on pull-backs by analytic maps from
  smooth spaces, positivity meaning that semipositivity survives small compactly supported smooth
  perturbations of the metric;
- nef if $c_1(\bar L)$ is semipositive and $\widehat{\deg}(\bar L|_\Gamma) \ge 0$ for every
  one-dimensional closed subscheme $\Gamma$ of $X$;
- big if $L_\mathbb{Q}$ is big on $X_\mathbb{Q}$ and some $H^0(X, L^{\otimes n})$, $n \ge 1$, has a
  nonzero section $s$ with $\lVert s \rVert_{\sup} < 1$.

For $L$ relatively ample and $n$ large, so that $L^{\otimes n}$ is generated by its global sections,
generation of the sheaf $L^{\otimes n}$ by small sections is weaker than the existence of a basis of
the lattice $H^0(X, L^{\otimes n})$ made of small sections (in general a basis of a space of global
sections need not generate the sheaf, for instance when the line bundle has base points); that such
bases exist for all large $n$ when $\bar L$ is ample in the arithmetic sense above is a theorem of
Zhang (see [Zhang's arithmetic ampleness theorem](arithmetic-ampleness-theorem.md)). Relative
ampleness alone does not suffice: on $X = \operatorname{Spec} \mathbb{Z}$, $L = \mathcal{O}$ with
$\lVert 1 \rVert = 2$ is relatively ample and generated by its global sections, but $\lVert m \rVert
= 2^n |m|$ on $H^0(X, L^{\otimes n}) = \mathbb{Z}$, so there is no nonzero small section for $n >
0$. An element of $\widehat{\mathrm{Pic}}(X) \otimes \mathbb{Q}$, a hermitian $\mathbb{Q}$-line
bundle, is ample, nef or big if some positive multiple of it is represented by an ample, nef or big
hermitian line bundle. That Moriwaki's bigness is equivalent to Yuan's is a theorem (Moriwaki,
Theorem 4.5 and Remark 4.6), stated with the
[arithmetic volume](arithmetic-volumes/arithmetic-volume.md).
