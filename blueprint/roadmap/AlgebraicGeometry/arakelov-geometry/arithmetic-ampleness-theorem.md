# Zhang's arithmetic ampleness theorem

Let $X$ be an arithmetic variety in the sense of Zhang (an integral scheme, projective and flat over
$\mathbb{Z}$) and let $\bar L$ be a hermitian line bundle on $X$ that is ample in Zhang's sense:
$L_\mathbb{Q}$ is ample, $\bar L$ is relatively semipositive, and $(\hat c_1(\bar L|_Y)^{\dim Y}) >
0$ for every horizontal irreducible closed subvariety $Y$ of $X$. Assume that there are a closed
embedding $i \colon X(\mathbb{C}) \to Y$ into a projective complex manifold $Y$ and an ample line
bundle $L'$ on $Y$ with $i^* L' = L_\mathbb{C}$ such that the metric of $L_\mathbb{C}$ extends to a
hermitian metric on $L'$ with semipositive curvature; this holds whenever $X$ is generically smooth.
Then for every hermitian line bundle $\bar E$ on $X$ and every sufficiently large integer $N$, the
$\mathbb{Z}$-module $H^0(X, E \otimes L^{\otimes N})$ has a basis consisting of sections of sup norm
less than $1$.

In particular, if $X_\mathbb{Q}$ is smooth, $L_\mathbb{Q}$ is ample, $\bar L$ is relatively
semipositive and $(\hat c_1(\bar L|_Y)^{\dim Y}) > 0$ for every horizontal irreducible closed
subvariety $Y$, then for every hermitian line bundle $\bar E$ and all large $N$ the lattice $H^0(X,
E \otimes L^{\otimes N})$ has a basis of sections of sup norm less than $1$. Yuan quotes the theorem
as a combination of Zhang's Theorems 4.2 and 3.5, see also Zhang's Corollary 4.8. Moriwaki uses it,
citing Zhang's Theorem (4.2) and Corollary (4.8), in the form: for $\bar L$ ample in his sense,
$H^0(X, mL + N)$ is generated by sections of sup norm less than $1$ for all large $m$. In the same
way, if $\bar L$ is ample in Moriwaki's sense, then for every sufficiently large $n$ the
$\mathbb{Z}$-module $H^0(X, L^{\otimes n})$ has a basis of sections of sup norm less than $1$
(Zhang, Corollary (5.7), for relatively semiample metrized line bundles, as cited in Moriwaki's
Conventions and terminology 7).

Zhang's proof of Corollary (4.8) applies his Theorem (3.5), p. 200, which proves that the metric is
semiample, the small sections given by the arithmetic Hilbert–Samuel theorem on each horizontal
subvariety (his Corollary (1.9)), and his Theorem (4.2), p. 202, with its proof pp. 203–207, which
builds bases of small sections from strictly small sections on the horizontal subvarieties through
lattice filtrations and successive minima and the extension of prescribed products of small sections
with control of their norms (Theorem (3.3)), whose archimedean part rests on the $L^2$ estimates for
$\bar\partial$ of (2.2)–(2.4).
