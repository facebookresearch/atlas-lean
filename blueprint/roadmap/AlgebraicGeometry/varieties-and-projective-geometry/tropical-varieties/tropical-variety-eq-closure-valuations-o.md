# Fundamental Theorem of Tropical Algebraic Geometry

Let $K$ be an algebraically closed field with a nontrivial valuation
$\mathrm{val} : K \to \mathbb{R} \cup \{\infty\}$, let $I$ be an ideal of Laurent polynomials in
$n$ variables over $K$, and let $X = V(I)$ be its variety in the torus $(K^*)^n$. The tropical
variety $\operatorname{trop}(X) = \operatorname{trop}(I)$, the intersection of the tropical
hypersurfaces of the nonzero polynomials in $I$, equals the closure in $\mathbb{R}^n$ of the set
$\{(\mathrm{val}(y_1), \dots, \mathrm{val}(y_n)) : y \in X\}$ of coordinatewise valuations of the
points of $X$.

Maclagan and Sturmfels state the theorem as the coincidence of three sets, the second being the
closure of the set of weight vectors $w$ with $\mathrm{in}_w(I) \neq \langle 1 \rangle$; the
statement above is the equality of the first and the third. The intersection cannot be
restricted to a generating set of $I$ in general, which is why tropical bases are needed for
computation. The proof projects to the hypersurface case, Kapranov's theorem.
