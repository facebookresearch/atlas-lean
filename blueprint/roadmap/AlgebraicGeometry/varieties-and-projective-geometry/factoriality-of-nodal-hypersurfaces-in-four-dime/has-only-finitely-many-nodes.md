# Hypersurface with finitely many nodes

A polynomial $f \in \mathbb{C}[x_0, \dots, x_4]$ has only finitely many nodes when its singular locus $\operatorname{Sing}(f) \subseteq \mathbb{P}^4(\mathbb{C})$ (the points at which all partial derivatives of $f$ vanish at a chosen representative) is finite and, for every $P \in \operatorname{Sing}(f)$ with chosen representative $a \in \mathbb{C}^5$, the Hessian matrix $\left(\partial^2 f / \partial x_j \partial x_i (a)\right)_{i,j}$ has rank exactly $4$.

For homogeneous $f$, rank $4$ is the maximum possible at a singular point, since by Euler's formula $a$ lies in the kernel of the Hessian.
