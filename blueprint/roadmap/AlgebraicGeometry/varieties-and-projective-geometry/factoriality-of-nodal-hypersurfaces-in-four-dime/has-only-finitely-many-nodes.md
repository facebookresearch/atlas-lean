# Hypersurface with finitely many nodes

A polynomial $f \in \mathbb{C}[x_0, \dots, x_4]$ has only finitely many nodes when its singular locus $\operatorname{Sing}(f) \subseteq \mathbb{P}^4(\mathbb{C})$ (the points $P$ at which all partial derivatives of $f$ vanish at the representative $a_P$ fixed for $P$) is finite and, for every $P \in \operatorname{Sing}(f)$, the Hessian matrix $\left(\partial^2 f / \partial x_j \partial x_i (a_P)\right)_{i,j}$ has rank exactly $4$.

For an arbitrary polynomial both conditions are tests at the fixed representatives. For homogeneous $f$ they do not depend on the representatives, and for homogeneous $f$ of degree $d \ge 3$, as in the factoriality statement, they say that the hypersurface $\{f = 0\}$ has finitely many singular points, all of them ordinary double points. Rank $4$ is then the maximum possible at a singular point, since by Euler's formula $a_P$ lies in the kernel of the Hessian.
