# Mumford's construction of degenerating abelian varieties

This node is still to be broken down: the statement is precise, but the stages of its proof are not.

Let $A$ be an excellent integrally closed noetherian ring with fraction field $K$, and $I \subset A$
an ideal with $\sqrt{I} = I$ such that $A$ is complete for the $I$-adic topology; let $S =
\operatorname{Spec} A$, $\eta$ its generic point and $S_0 = \operatorname{Spec}(A/I)$. A smooth
commutative group scheme of finite type over a scheme $Z$ is **semi-abelian** if every fibre is
connected and is an extension of an abelian variety by a torus.

Let $\tilde G$ be a split torus of rank $r$ over $S$, with character group $X$ and characters
$\mathcal{X}^\alpha \in \Gamma(\tilde G, \mathcal{O}_{\tilde G})$ for $\alpha \in X$, so that
$\tilde G(K) \cong (K^*)^r$. A **set of periods** is a subgroup $Y \subset \tilde G(K)$ isomorphic
to $\mathbb{Z}^r$, and a **polarization** of $Y$ is a homomorphism $\phi \colon Y \to X$ such that
$\mathcal{X}^{\phi(y)}(z) = \mathcal{X}^{\phi(z)}(y)$ for all $y, z \in Y$ and
$\mathcal{X}^{\phi(y)}(y) \in I$ for all $y \ne 0$.

**Theorem** (Mumford). Let $Y \subset \tilde G(K)$ be a set of periods that admits a polarization.
There is a scheme $G$ over $S$, depending only on $\tilde G$ and $Y$ and not on the polarization or
the auxiliary model, such that:

1. $G$ is a semi-abelian group scheme over $S$;
2. its generic fibre $G_\eta$ is an abelian variety over $K$;
3. the $I$-adic completions of $G$ and of $\tilde G$ are canonically isomorphic; in particular $G
   \times_S S_0 \cong \tilde G \times_S S_0$ is a split torus over $S_0$.

This is the split, totally toric case: the special fibre of $G$ is a torus. The general
degenerations of Faltings and Chai, extensions of an abelian scheme by a torus over $S_0$, are not
covered. The theorem does not determine $Y$ as a subgroup of $\tilde G(K)$ from the scheme $G$
alone: for $A = k[[t]]$, $I = (t)$ and the split torus of rank $2$, the subgroups $Y_1$ generated by
$(t, 1)$ and $(1, t^2)$ and $Y_2$ generated by $(t^2, 1)$ and $(1, t)$ both carry the diagonal
polarization, the automorphism of $\tilde G$ exchanging the two coordinates carries the data of
$Y_1$ to those of $Y_2$ and so induces an isomorphism of the resulting schemes, but $Y_1 \ne Y_2$.
Mumford's Corollary 4.7 is the independence of $G$ from the polarization and the model for fixed
$\tilde G$ and $Y$.

Mumford builds a relatively complete model $\tilde P$ of $\tilde G$ with respect to $Y$ and a
multiple $n\phi$ of the polarization (Theorem 2.5), divides it by $Y$ over each $A/I^n$ to obtain
projective schemes $P_n$, with the descent of $\mathcal{O}(1)$ (Theorem 3.10), algebraizes the
resulting formal scheme, and the closed subscheme of its boundary, with
[Grothendieck's existence theorem](../deformations-and-p-divisible-groups/grothendieck-existence-theorem.md) to a scheme $P$ projective
over $S$ and a closed subscheme of it, and takes for $G$ the open complement, whose completion is
that of $\tilde G$. He then shows that $G$ is smooth over $S$ (Corollary 4.2), extends morphisms
(Theorem 4.6, which starts from an $S$-homomorphism of the split tori $\tilde G_1 \to \tilde G_2$
carrying the period lattice $Y_1$ into $Y_2$) to show that $G$ does not depend on the polarization
or the model (Corollary 4.7) and is a group scheme (Corollary 4.8) with abelian generic fibre
(Corollary 4.9), and shows that its fibres are connected, so that $G$ is semi-abelian (Corollary
4.12).

A good expansion has pages for: relatively complete models and their existence after scaling the
polarization (Definition 2.1, Theorem 2.5); the quotients $P_n$ and the descent of their ample
sheaves (Theorem 3.10 and the paragraph after it); the extension of morphisms from such
homomorphisms of tori (Theorem 4.6) and Corollaries 4.7–4.9 and 4.12; with the algebraization of the
coherent ideal of the boundary and of the projective formal scheme by Grothendieck's existence
theorem.
