# Abelian scheme

Let $S$ be a scheme. An **abelian scheme** over $S$ is a group scheme $\pi \colon A \to S$, that is, a
group object in the category of $S$-schemes, with multiplication $m \colon A \times_S A \to A$,
inverse $A \to A$ and zero section $e \colon S \to A$, such that $\pi$ is smooth and proper and every
geometric fibre of $\pi$ is connected. A homomorphism of abelian schemes over $S$ is a morphism of
group schemes over $S$. An **abelian variety** over a field $k$ is an abelian scheme over
$\operatorname{Spec} k$. An **isogeny** $\varphi \colon A \to B$ of abelian schemes over $S$ is a
homomorphism that is finite and surjective; that its kernel is then finite and flat over $S$ is a
theorem, recorded with the [isogenies of abelian schemes](isogenies-of-abelian-schemes.md).

As an explanatory comparison, not part of the definition: over a field this is Milne's definition of
an abelian variety as a complete connected group variety, with his convention that varieties are
geometrically reduced and separated: with it, Milne shows that a group variety is nonsingular and
that a connected group variety is geometrically connected. Over a variety $S$ over a field, an
abelian scheme is what Milne calls a family of abelian varieties over $S$: a proper smooth map with
a law of composition and a section such that each fibre is an abelian variety.

## Sources

- D. Mumford, *Geometric Invariant Theory*, Ergebnisse der Mathematik 34, Springer, 1965:
  Definition 6.1 (abelian schemes), as cited in Chai–Oort below.
- D. Robert, *Theory of abelian varieties and their moduli spaces*, draft book (version of 7 March
  2023), <https://www.math.u-bordeaux.fr/~damienrobert/pro/publications/books/avtheory.pdf>:
  Definition 2.3.1, p. 12.
- C.-L. Chai and F. Oort, "Moduli of abelian varieties and p-divisible groups: density of Hecke
  orbits, and a conjecture of Grothendieck", lecture notes, arXiv:math/0701479 (version 1): (10.12)
  (abelian varieties and abelian schemes, with references to Mumford's *Abelian Varieties*, §II.4,
  and to *Geometric Invariant Theory*, 6.1; an isogeny is a finite surjective homomorphism, whose
  kernel is finite and flat over the base by *Geometric Invariant Theory*, Lemma 6.12).
- J. S. Milne, *Abelian Varieties*, course notes, version 2.00 (2008),
  <https://www.jmilne.org/math/CourseNotes/AV.pdf>: the conventions of the preface (varieties are
  geometrically reduced and separated); Chapter I, §1, pp. 7–8 (group varieties are
  nonsingular; connected group varieties are geometrically connected; an abelian variety is a complete
  connected group variety) and §16, p. 67 (families of abelian varieties).
