# Theta structures of level r and theta embeddings

This node is still to be broken down. It covers the projective embeddings by theta functions of
level $r$ that define the [theta height](theta-height.md), following Pazuki, §2.3.1–§2.3.3,
pp. 27–29.

Let $k$ be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero, $A$ an
[abelian variety](../abelian-scheme.md) of dimension $g$ over $k$, $L$ an ample line bundle on $A$,
rigidified at the origin, that is
[symmetric](../../not-yet-classified/canonical-polarization-data/is-symmetric.md),
$[-1]^* L \cong L$, and defines a principal [polarization](../polarization.md), so that
$\varphi_L \colon A \to A^\vee$ is an isomorphism, and let $r > 0$ be an even integer. Write
$A_n(k)$ for the points of $A$ of order dividing $n$ and $t_x$ for translation by $x$. The targets
are:

1. Since $L$ is symmetric there is a unique isomorphism $j \colon [r]^* L \to L^{\otimes r^2}$
   compatible with the rigidification; it gives, for $x \in A_r(k)$, canonical isomorphisms
   $i_x \colon t_x^* L^{\otimes r^2} \to L^{\otimes r^2}$, compatible in the sense that
   $i_x \circ t_x^* i_y = i_{x+y}$ under $t_{x+y}^* = t_x^* t_y^*$.
2. For every $x \in A_{r^2}(k)$ there is an isomorphism $i_x \colon t_x^* L^{\otimes r^2} \to
   L^{\otimes r^2}$, and the $i_x$ can be chosen so that $i_x \circ t_x^* i_y$ equals $i_{x+y}$ up to
   an $r^2$-th root of unity for all $x, y \in A_{r^2}(k)$, a *good choice*. Two good choices differ
   by a family of $r^4$-th roots of unity. A theta structure on $L^{\otimes r^2}$,
   in Mumford's sense, induces a good choice.
3. For a good choice, let $\varphi_x \colon \Gamma(A, L) \to \Gamma(A, L^{\otimes r^2})$,
   $s \mapsto (i_x \circ t_x^* \circ j \circ [r]^*) s$. If $x - x' \in A_r(k)$ then $\varphi_{x'} =
   \mu \varphi_x$ for an $r^4$-th root of unity $\mu$ (Lemma 2.4). For a set $\Gamma$ of
   representatives in $A_{r^2}(k)$ of $A_{r^2}(k) / A_r(k)$, the map
   $\Gamma(A, L)^{\Gamma} \to \Gamma(A, L^{\otimes r^2})$, $(s_x) \mapsto \sum_{x \in \Gamma}
   \varphi_x(s_x)$, is an isomorphism of $k$-vector spaces of dimension $r^{2g}$ (Proposition 2.5).
4. A bijection $\Gamma \cong \{1, \dots, r^{2g}\}$ then gives an isomorphism
   $\mathbb{P}(\Gamma(A, L^{\otimes r^2})) \cong \mathbb{P}_k^{r^{2g} - 1}$, and, as
   $L^{\otimes r^2}$ is very ample by Lefschetz's theorem ($r^2 \ge 4 > 3$), a closed embedding
   $\Theta \colon A \to \mathbb{P}_k^{r^{2g} - 1}$, the *theta embedding*. Its formation commutes
   with extension of algebraically closed fields, such as $\overline{\mathbb{Q}} \subset
   \mathbb{C}$. The isomorphisms obtained for the various choices (of the rigidification,
   the good choice, $\Gamma$ and the bijection) differ by an element of the finite group
   $G \subset \operatorname{GL}_{r^{2g}}(k)$ generated by the permutation matrices and the
   diagonal matrices with entries in $\mu_{r^4}(k)$, which Pazuki writes
   $\mathfrak{S}_{r^{2g}} \vartriangleleft \mu_{r^4}(K)$; and a second symmetric ample line bundle
   defining the same polarization gives the same embedding up to $G$.
5. (Analytic description.) For $k = \mathbb{C}$, if $A(\mathbb{C}) \cong \mathbb{C}^g / (\mathbb{Z}^g +
   \tau \mathbb{Z}^g)$ as principally polarized complex abelian varieties, with $\tau$ in the
   Siegel upper half space, then for a suitable choice of theta coordinates, that is up to the
   action of $G$, $\Theta(0)$ has projective coordinates the theta constants
   $\theta_{(m_1, m_2)}(\tau, 0)$, $m_1, m_2 \in \{0, \frac{1}{r}, \dots, \frac{r - 1}{r}\}^g$,
   where $\theta_{(m_1, m_2)}(\tau, z) = \sum_{n \in \mathbb{Z}^g} \exp\bigl(i\pi \,
   {}^t(n + m_1) \tau (n + m_1) + 2 i \pi \, {}^t(n + m_1)(z + m_2)\bigr)$; they do not all vanish.
   In Pazuki's words, the Theta Nullwerte of the embedding "can be chosen as the $r^{2g}$ complex
   numbers" $\theta_{(m_1, m_2)}(\tau, 0)$ (§2.3.4, p. 30).

Pazuki states the construction over a field $K$ of characteristic zero over which the points of
order dividing $r^2$ are rational, enlarging $K$ if needed (§2.3.1, p. 27). Over such a field there
need not be a theta embedding defined over it. Take $r = 2$, $E \colon y^2 = x^3 - x$, $L =
\mathcal{O}([0])$ and $K = \mathbb{Q}(i, \sqrt{2})$: all of $E[4]$ is rational over $K$, since
$(i, 1 - i)$ and $(1 + \sqrt{2}, 2 + \sqrt{2})$ are points of $E(K)$ whose doubles are $(0, 0)$ and
$(1, 0)$. As $E(\mathbb{C}) \cong \mathbb{C} / (\mathbb{Z} + i \mathbb{Z})$, item 5 gives, for one
choice of theta coordinates, two nonzero coordinates of $\Theta(0)$ with ratio
$\theta_{(1/2, 0)}(i, 0) / \theta_{(0, 0)}(i, 0) = \theta_2(0 \mid i) / \theta_3(0 \mid i) =
2^{-1/4}$, because $\theta_2(0 \mid i) = \theta_4(0 \mid i)$ and $\theta_3^4 = \theta_2^4 +
\theta_4^4$ (DLMF 20.7.31 and 20.7.5). Any other choice changes the coordinates by an element of
$G$, a permutation followed by multiplication of the coordinates by roots of unity, so it still has
two nonzero coordinates whose ratio $u$ satisfies $u \bar u = |u|^2 = 1/\sqrt{2}$. A theta
embedding defined over $K$ would make $\Theta(0)$ rational over $K$, so $u$ would lie in $K$. Write
$u = a + ib$ with $a, b \in \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2}) \subset \mathbb{R}$; then $a^2 + b^2 =
1/\sqrt{2} = \sqrt{2}/2$, and the automorphism of $K$ that sends $\sqrt{2}$ to $-\sqrt{2}$ and
fixes $i$ turns this into a sum of two squares of real numbers equal to $-\sqrt{2}/2 < 0$, which is
impossible. Pazuki asserts only that the quotients of the theta constants are algebraic (§2.3.4,
p. 30), which is why the construction is stated here over an algebraically closed field.

A good expansion states and links: the isomorphism $j$ and the $i_x$ for $x \in A_r(k)$, from
the theorem of the cube (Birkenhake–Lange, Corollary 3.6, p. 34, as cited by Pazuki, p. 27);
Mumford's theta group of $L^{\otimes r^2}$, theta structures and good choices (Birkenhake–Lange,
Lemma 4.7 (c), p. 38, and Mumford, *On the equations defining abelian varieties* I and II, Invent.
Math. 1 (1966) and 3 (1967), as cited by Pazuki, p. 28); the basis of Proposition 2.5; Lefschetz's theorem that $M^{\otimes n}$ is very ample for
every ample line bundle $M$ on an abelian variety and every $n \ge 3$, which item 4 uses with
$n = r^2$ and which Pazuki uses without a reference (§2.3.3, p. 29) (Robert, Proposition 2.3.14,
p. 16, the item "Lefschetz", for a relatively ample line bundle on an abelian scheme, citing
Mumford–Fogarty–Kirwan, *Geometric Invariant Theory*, 3rd ed., Proposition 6.13; Milne states the
case $n = 3$ without proof, Chapter I, §6, p. 30); the theta
embedding with its ambiguity group $G$ (Birkenhake–Lange, p. 168, and David–Philippon, Comment.
Math. Helv. 77 (2002), p. 654, as cited by Pazuki, p. 29); and the analytic description
through the Riemann theta function with characteristics (Birkenhake–Lange, p. 213, and Igusa,
*Theta functions*, Grundlehren 194, 1972, pp. 168–170, as cited by Pazuki, p. 30), with pages for
the Siegel upper half space.
