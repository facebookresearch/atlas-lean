# Abelian varieties are projective

Every abelian variety $A$ over a field $k$ is projective over $k$: there is a closed immersion of
$A$ into a projective space $\mathbb{P}^n_k$.

Milne first assumes $k$ algebraically closed. He chooses finitely many prime divisors $Z_i$ whose
intersection is $\{0\}$ and whose tangent spaces at $0$ intersect in $0$, sets $D = \sum_i Z_i$, and
shows with the theorem of the square that the complete linear system of $3D$ separates points and
tangent vectors, so that $3D$ is very ample. Proposition I.6.6 (d), that a variety has an ample
divisor if its extension of scalars to an algebraic closure of $k$ has one, removes the assumption
that $k$ is algebraically closed. The theorem is due to Barsotti, Matsusaka and Weil; Milne presents
Weil's proof.
