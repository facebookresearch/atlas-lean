# Abelian varieties are projective

Every abelian variety $A$ over a field $k$ is projective over $k$: there is a closed immersion of
$A$ into a projective space $\mathbb{P}^n_k$.

Milne first assumes $k$ algebraically closed. He chooses finitely many prime divisors $Z_i$ whose
intersection is $\{0\}$ and whose tangent spaces at $0$ intersect in $0$, sets $D = \sum_i Z_i$, and
shows with the theorem of the square that the complete linear system of $3D$ separates points and
tangent vectors, so that $3D$ is very ample by the embedding criterion of Proposition I.6.3. Proposition
I.6.6 (d), that a variety has an ample divisor if its extension of scalars to an algebraic closure of
$k$ has one, removes the assumption that $k$ is algebraically closed. Van der Geer and Moonen prove the
theorem through the finiteness of the stabilizer $H(D)$ of a divisor whose complement is
quasi-affine, again first over an algebraically closed field. The theorem is due to Barsotti,
Matsusaka and Weil; Milne presents Weil's proof. It concerns abelian varieties over a field: an
abelian scheme need not be projective over its base.
