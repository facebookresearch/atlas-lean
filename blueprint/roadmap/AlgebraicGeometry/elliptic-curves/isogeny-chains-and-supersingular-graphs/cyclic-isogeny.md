# Cyclic isogeny

A cyclic isogeny is a separable isogeny whose kernel, a subgroup of the geometric points, is a cyclic group. A cyclic $N$-isogeny has kernel isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}$; every separable isogeny of prime degree is cyclic.

Let $E$, $E'$ be elliptic curves over a field $k$. A nonzero separable [isogeny](../isogenies-of-elliptic-curves/isogeny-of-elliptic-curves.md) $\varphi\colon E\to E'$ is *cyclic* if its kernel $\ker\varphi\subseteq E(\bar k)$ is a cyclic group, and a *cyclic $N$-isogeny* if moreover $\deg\varphi=N$.

Convention. Following Costache, Feigon, Lauter, Massierer and Puskás, cyclic isogenies are separable, so a purely inseparable isogeny of degree greater than $1$, whose geometric kernel is trivial, is not called cyclic; an isomorphism is both separable and purely inseparable and is a cyclic $1$-isogeny. Sutherland calls an isogeny cyclic when its kernel is cyclic and uses the notion for degrees prime to the characteristic, where every isogeny is separable.

Facts used with this definition, proved on the pages listed under Proof depends on: the kernel of a cyclic $N$-isogeny has exactly $N$ points, so it is isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}$ and generated by any of its points of order $N$; over $\bar k$, the cyclic $N$-isogenies from $E$ are, up to an isomorphism of the target, the quotient maps $E\to E/\langle P\rangle$ by the cyclic subgroups $\langle P\rangle\subseteq E(\bar k)$ of order $N$ (over $k$ itself only when the subgroup descends); every separable isogeny of prime degree is cyclic; and $[N]$ is not cyclic for $N>1$ prime to the characteristic, its kernel $E[N]\cong(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^2$ not being cyclic.

## Expansion

The definition itself needs only the notion of an isogeny and its kernel. Each recorded fact is one line from the pages it cites: the kernel order is the degree for separable isogenies; separable isogenies are determined by their kernels up to an isomorphism of the target, over $\bar k$, with descent to $k$ for Galois-stable kernels over perfect $k$; and $E[N]$ has rank two for $N$ prime to the characteristic.
