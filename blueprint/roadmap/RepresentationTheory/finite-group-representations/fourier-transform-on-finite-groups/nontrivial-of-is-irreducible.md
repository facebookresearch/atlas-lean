# An irreducible representation lives on a nonzero space

If a representation $\rho$ of a group $G$ on a complex vector space $V$ is irreducible, then $V \ne 0$, so $d_\rho = \dim V \ge 1$ when $V$ is finite-dimensional. This is part of the definition of irreducibility: the subrepresentations of $\rho$ are exactly $0$ and $V$, and these two are distinct (compare Diaconis and Shahshahani 1981, Section 2: $V$ admits no invariant subspaces other than $\{0\}$ or $V$).
