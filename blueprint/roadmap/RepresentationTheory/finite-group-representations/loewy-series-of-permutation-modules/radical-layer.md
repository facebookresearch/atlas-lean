# Powers of the augmentation ideal on a permutation module

For a group $G$ acting on a set $\Omega$ and a field $F$, let $M = F^\Omega$ be the module of all functions $\Omega \to F$, with $(g \cdot m)(\omega) = m(g^{-1}\omega)$. The sequence of subspaces $J^k M$ ($k \in \mathbb{N}$) is defined by $J^0 M = M$ and $J^{k+1} M = \operatorname{span}_F\{\, g \cdot m - m : g \in G,\ m \in J^k M \,\}$. Here $J$ stands for the augmentation ideal of $FG$, spanned by the elements $g - 1$, so $J^k M$ is the image of its $k$-th power: these are layers of the function module.

For finite $\Omega$, $M$ is the permutation module $F\Omega$; for infinite $\Omega$ it is larger than the module of finitely supported functions (for the trivial group acting on $\mathbb{N}$ over the field with two elements, the constant function $1$ lies in $J^0 M$ and is not finitely supported). When moreover $G$ is a finite $p$-group and $F$ has characteristic $p$, $J$ is the radical of $FG$ and this is the radical (Loewy) series of the permutation module. In general it is not: for the group of order two swapping two points over $\mathbb{Q}$, the module is the direct sum of the trivial and the sign line, so its radical is $0$, while $J M$ is the sign line.
