# Annihilators of powers of the augmentation ideal on a permutation module

For a group $G$ acting on a set $\Omega$ and a field $F$, let $M = F^\Omega$ be the module of all functions $\Omega \to F$, with $(g \cdot m)(\omega) = m(g^{-1}\omega)$. The sequence of subspaces $\operatorname{soc}^k M$ ($k \in \mathbb{N}$) is defined by $\operatorname{soc}^0 M = 0$ and $\operatorname{soc}^{k+1} M = \{\, m \in M : g \cdot m - m \in \operatorname{soc}^k M \text{ for every } g \in G \,\}$: the elements of the function module annihilated by the $k$-th power of the augmentation ideal.

For finite $\Omega$, $M$ is the permutation module $F\Omega$; for infinite $\Omega$ it is larger than the module of finitely supported functions (for the trivial group acting on $\mathbb{N}$ over the field with two elements, the constant function $1$ lies in $\operatorname{soc}^1 M$ and is not finitely supported). When moreover $G$ is a finite $p$-group and $F$ has characteristic $p$, this is the socle series of the permutation module. In general it is not: for the group of order two swapping two points over $\mathbb{Q}$, the socle is the whole module, while $\operatorname{soc}^1 M$ is the line of constant functions.
