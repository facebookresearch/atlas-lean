# Definition 5.7

Let $(\mathfrak{g}, K)$ be a Harish-Chandra pair. (i) A $(\mathfrak{g}, K)$-module is a vector space $M$ with actions of $K$ and $\mathfrak{g}$ such that $M$ is a direct sum of finite-dimensional continuous $K$-modules, and the two actions of $\mathfrak{k}$ on $M$ (coming from the actions of $\mathfrak{g}$ and $K$) coincide. (ii) Such a module is said to be admissible if for every $\rho \in \operatorname{Irr} K$ we have $\operatorname{dim} \operatorname{Hom}_{K}(\rho, M)\lt \infty$. (iii) An admissible $(\mathfrak{g}, K)$-module which is finitely generated over $U(\mathfrak{g})$ is called a Harish-Chandra module.
