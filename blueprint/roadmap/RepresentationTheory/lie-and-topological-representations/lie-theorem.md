# Lie's theorem: a common eigenvector for a solvable Lie algebra

Over an algebraically closed field of characteristic $0$, a solvable Lie algebra of endomorphisms of a nonzero finite-dimensional vector space has a common eigenvector. Mathlib proves this for representations of solvable Lie algebras over any field of characteristic $0$, assuming instead that each operator is triangularizable. Let $k$ be a field of characteristic $0$, $L$ a solvable Lie algebra over $k$, and $V$ a nonzero finite-dimensional representation of $L$ over $k$ (a Lie module), with action $(x, v) \mapsto [x, v]$. Suppose that for each $x \in L$ the operator $v \mapsto [x, v]$ of $V$ is triangularizable: $V$ is spanned by its generalized eigenspaces for that operator. Then there are a linear functional $\chi \colon L \to k$ and a nonzero $v \in V$ with $[x, v] = \chi(x)\,v$ for every $x \in L$. Triangularizability is required of each operator separately, not as a common triangular basis, and $L$ need not be finite-dimensional. Over an algebraically closed field every operator on a finite-dimensional space is triangularizable; applied to a solvable Lie subalgebra $L$ of $\mathfrak{gl}(V)$ acting on $V$, this gives the familiar statement. The hypothesis cannot be dropped: over $\mathbb{R}$, the infinitesimal rotations of $\mathbb{R}^2$ form a one-dimensional, hence solvable, Lie algebra without a common eigenvector.
