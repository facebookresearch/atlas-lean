# Classical invariant theory and harmonics

This node is still to be broken down. It covers the classical invariant theory of Howe on which the
[duality for compact dual pairs](howe-duality-for-compact-dual-pairs.md) rests. Let $G$ be a classical
complex group with standard module $V$, let $U$ and $W$ be direct sums of copies of $V$ and of its dual,
and let $\mathcal{A}(U, W)$ be the resulting polynomial (Fock-type) module with the commuting actions of $G$
and of the Lie algebra $\Gamma'$ centralizing the image of the Lie algebra of $G$ in the oscillator Lie
algebra, decomposed as $\Gamma'^{(2,0)} \oplus \Gamma'^{(1,1)} \oplus \Gamma'^{(0,2)}$.

- **First fundamental theorem.** The algebra of $G$-invariants in the Weyl algebra is generated by
  $\Gamma'$, from the fact that the invariants of $G$ acting on direct sums of copies of $V$ and $V^*$ are
  generated by the quadratic invariants (Howe, Theorems 2, 6 and 7).
- **Joint isotypic components.** The joint action of $G$ and $\Gamma'$ on each $G$-isotypic component of
  $\mathcal{A}(U, W)$ is irreducible, and the correspondence between the occurring representations of $G$
  and of $\Gamma'$ is a bijection (Howe, Theorem 8).
- **Harmonics.** With $\mathcal{H}$ the polynomials killed by $\Gamma'^{(0,2)}$ and $\mathcal{I}$ the
  $G$-invariants: multiplication $\mathcal{I} \otimes \mathcal{H} \to \mathcal{A}(U, W)$ is surjective; the harmonics
  of a type are the elements of lowest degree of its isotypic component; each isotypic part of $\mathcal{H}$ is
  an irreducible module for the joint action of $G$ and $\Gamma'^{(1,1)}$, with a bijective correspondence,
  and is cyclic for $\Gamma'^{(2,0)}$ in its isotypic component (Howe, Theorem 9).

A good expansion has pages for the first fundamental theorem for the classical groups, for the
decomposition of the isotypic components, and for the theory of harmonics, each with a precise statement
and Howe's locators, together with the passage to compact real forms and to the twofold covers acting on
the Fock model.
