# Classical invariant theory and harmonics

This node is still to be broken down. It covers the parts of Howe's classical invariant theory on which
the [duality for compact dual pairs](howe-duality-for-compact-dual-pairs.md) and
[Howe duality over the reals](howe-duality-over-the-reals.md) rest, in the purely polynomial case. Let $G$
be one of the complex classical groups $\mathrm{GL}(V)$, the full orthogonal group $\mathrm{O}(V)$ or $\mathrm{Sp}(V)$ (not a
subgroup such as $\mathrm{SO}(V)$), with standard module $V$; let $U$ be a direct sum of copies of $V$ and,
for $\mathrm{GL}(V)$, of its dual; let $\mathcal{P}(U)$ be the algebra of polynomial functions on $U$ (Howe's
$\mathcal{A}(U^\vee, 0) = S(U^\vee)$: his $S(U)$ is the algebra of polynomial functions on $U^\vee$; replacing $U$ by $U^\vee$
exchanges the copies of $V$ and $V^\vee$, and $V \cong V^\vee$ for the orthogonal and symplectic groups), with the action of $G$ and of the Weyl algebra of polynomial-coefficient
differential operators; and let $\Gamma'$ be the Lie algebra spanned by the quadratic elements of the Weyl
algebra that are fixed by the group $G$ (for the standard orthogonal example, up to scaling, $r^2$, the Laplacian
$\Delta$ and the shifted Euler operator $E + N/2$ with $N = \dim U$, since $[\Delta, r^2] = 4E + 2N$), decomposed as $\Gamma'^{(2,0)} \oplus \Gamma'^{(1,1)} \oplus \Gamma'^{(0,2)}$ into
multiplication, degree-preserving and differentiation operators.

- **First fundamental theorem.** The invariants of $G$ in $\mathcal{P}(U)$ are generated by the quadratic
  invariants, and the algebra of $G$-invariants in the Weyl algebra is generated by $\Gamma'$ (Howe, Theorems 2,
  6 and 7; invariance under the group, not only under its Lie algebra, matters for disconnected $G$: for
  $\mathrm{O}_2(\mathbb{C})$ on $\mathbb{C}[z_1, z_2]$ the operator $z_1 \partial_2 - z_2 \partial_1$ commutes with $\mathfrak{so}_2$ but is not
  fixed by a reflection).
- **Joint isotypic components.** The joint action of $G$ and $\Gamma'$ on each $G$-isotypic component of
  $\mathcal{P}(U)$ is irreducible, and the correspondence between the occurring representations of $G$ and of
  $\Gamma'$ is a bijection (Howe, Theorem 8).
- **Harmonics.** With $\mathcal{H}$ the polynomials killed by $\Gamma'^{(0,2)}$ and $\mathcal{I}$ the $G$-invariants:
  multiplication $\mathcal{I} \otimes \mathcal{H} \to \mathcal{P}(U)$ is surjective; the harmonics of a type $\sigma$ are the
  elements of lowest degree of its isotypic component; each isotypic part $\mathcal{H}_\sigma$ is an irreducible
  module for the joint action of $G$ and $\Gamma'^{(1,1)}$, with a bijective correspondence; and
  $\mathcal{U}(\Gamma'^{(2,0)}) \cdot \mathcal{H}_\sigma$ is the whole isotypic component $\mathcal{P}(U)_\sigma$ (Howe, Theorem 9).
- **Correspondence of infinitesimal characters.** For a reductive dual pair $(E, E')$, the subalgebras
  $\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{e}_{\mathbb{C}})^{\mathrm{Ad}\, E}$ and $\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{e}'_{\mathbb{C}})^{\mathrm{Ad}\, E'}$ of the centres of the enveloping algebras fixed by the
  adjoint actions of the groups have the same image in the algebra of operators on the Fock model, so that a
  representation of one member on which this invariant centre acts by scalars (quasisimple in Howe's sense)
  corresponds to one of the same kind of the other, with central characters determined by each other (Howe,
  Remarks, §5 (a), p. 568; Transcending, pp. 536 and 547). For a disconnected member the full centre need not
  transfer (for $\mathrm{O}(3) = \mathrm{SO}(3) \times \{\pm I\}$ it does, since $-I$ is central and acts trivially on the enveloping algebra, so the
  invariant centre is the whole centre): for $\mathrm{O}(2)$ on
  $\mathbb{C}[z_1, z_2]$ the generator $z_1 \partial_2 - z_2 \partial_1$ of $\mathcal{Z}(U(\mathfrak{so}_{2, \mathbb{C}}))$ acts
  non-trivially and is sent to its negative by a reflection, whereas every operator coming from the commuting
  symplectic partner commutes with the reflection. Together with Harish-Chandra's theorem that a finitely
  generated $(\mathfrak{g}, K)$-module annihilated by an ideal of finite codimension of the centre is admissible (the
  centre is integral over its $\mathrm{Ad}$-invariant subalgebra, since the component group acts on it through a finite
  group, so a finitely generated quasisimple module in the above sense is annihilated by such an ideal), this
  gives the admissibility of the algebraic theta lifts in [Howe duality over the reals](howe-duality-over-the-reals.md)
  (Howe, Transcending, p. 547).

## Expansion

A good expansion has pages for the first fundamental theorem for the classical groups listed, for the
decomposition of the isotypic components, for the theory of harmonics, for the correspondence of
infinitesimal characters and for Harish-Chandra's admissibility theorem, each with a precise statement and
locators, together with the passage to compact real forms and to the twofold covers acting on the Fock
model. Howe's general theorems for $\mathcal{A}(U, W) = S(U) \otimes \Lambda(W)$, with an exterior factor and a
$\mathbb{Z}/2$-graded oscillator algebra (for $U = 0$ and $W = \mathbb{C}$ the module has dimension $2$), are not needed
and should get their own pages if wanted.
