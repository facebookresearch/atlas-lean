# Weighted cuspidal spectrum with a fixed central character

This node is still to be broken down. It covers the decomposition of the weighted cuspidal Hilbert space
with a fixed central character on a Levi factor, which the page on
[constituents of induced representations](automorphic-representations-as-constituents-of-induced-representations.md)
uses after Langlands's Lemma 6, and which comes after the
[discreteness of the cuspidal spectrum](discreteness-of-the-cuspidal-spectrum.md). Let $k$ be a number field, $G$
a connected reductive group over $k$, $P = MN$ a parabolic subgroup of $G$ over $k$, $A_M$ the split component
of the centre of $M$, $\chi$ a character of $A_M(\mathbb{A})$ and $\nu$ the positive character of $M(\mathbb{A})$ with
$\nu = \lvert \chi \rvert$ on $A_M(\mathbb{A})$.

- **Decomposition** (Langlands). Let $L^2_0(M(k) \backslash M(\mathbb{A}), \chi)$ be the Hilbert space of measurable
  cuspidal functions $\psi$ on $M(k) \backslash M(\mathbb{A})$ with $\psi(am) = \chi(a) \psi(m)$ for $a \in A_M(\mathbb{A})$ and
  $\int_{A_M(\mathbb{A}) M(k) \backslash M(\mathbb{A})} \nu^{-2} \lvert \psi \rvert^2 < \infty$. Under right translation by $M(\mathbb{A})$ it is
  a Hilbert direct sum of irreducible invariant subspaces.
- **Reduction to the unitary case.** Multiplication by $\nu^{-1}$ is an isometry onto the space of
  square-integrable cuspidal functions with the unitary central character $\chi \nu^{-1}$; it carries invariant
  subspaces to invariant subspaces, twisting the action by the character $\nu$, so the decomposition follows
  from the discreteness of the cuspidal spectrum of $M$.
- **Use.** For $\psi$ in the space $V$ generated by a function with central character $\chi$ (Langlands's
  Lemma 6), the functions $m \mapsto \psi(mg)$ lie in this space; projecting to an irreducible component realizes
  the subquotient in an induced representation (Langlands, pp. 3–4).

A good expansion has a statement page for the decomposition, proved from the discreteness of the cuspidal
spectrum by the twist above, with the locators below.
