# Harish-Chandra's convolution identity and uniform moderate growth

This node is still to be broken down. It covers Harish-Chandra's theorem that $K$-finite and
$\mathcal{Z}$-finite functions are reproduced by convolution, and its consequence for moderate growth,
which the definition of the action of the Hecke algebra on automorphic forms and the page on
[constant terms](constant-terms-and-convolution-identity.md) use. Let $k$ be a number field, $G$ a
connected reductive group over $k$, $G_\infty = \prod_{v \mid \infty} G(k_v)$, $K = K_\infty K_f$ a maximal
compact subgroup of $G(\mathbb{A})$, $\mathcal{Z}$ the centre of the enveloping algebra of the complexified
Lie algebra of $G_\infty$, and $\lVert \cdot \rVert$ a two-sided height on $G(\mathbb{A})$.

- **Convolution identity** (Harish-Chandra). For a smooth function $\varphi$ on $G(k) \backslash G(\mathbb{A})$
  that is $K$-finite and $\mathcal{Z}$-finite, and every neighbourhood $U$ of $1$ in $G_\infty$, there is a
  function $\alpha \in C^\infty_c(U)$, invariant under conjugation by $K_\infty$, with
  $\varphi = \varphi \star \alpha$.
- **Uniform moderate growth.** If such a $\varphi$ moreover has moderate growth,
  $\lvert \varphi(g) \rvert \le C \lVert g \rVert^N$, then there is one $N$ such that every derivative $D\varphi$,
  $D$ in the enveloping algebra, satisfies $\lvert D\varphi(g) \rvert \le C_D \lVert g \rVert^N$: indeed
  $D(\varphi \star \alpha) = \varphi \star D\alpha$, and convolution with a compactly supported function
  preserves moderate growth with the same exponent, because $\lVert gh \rVert$ and $\lVert g \rVert$ are
  comparable for $h$ in a compact set.

## Expansion

A good expansion has a page for Harish-Chandra's theorem on a connected real reductive group, with its
hypothesis (the translates under the algebra generated by $\mathcal{Z}$ and $\mathfrak{k}_{\mathbb{C}}$ span a
finite-dimensional space; an elliptic element annihilating the function appears in the proof, not as a
hypothesis), one for the passage to $G(k) \backslash G(\mathbb{A})$ with one $\alpha$ for all the finite-adelic slices,
and one for uniform moderate growth, citing the sources below with locators.

It also writes out these steps of its own proof:

- Harish-Chandra's theorem on a connected real reductive Lie group (Acta Math. 116 (1966), §8, Theorem 1, pp. 18–19, in the setting of §7, p. 15): a smooth function $f$ on $G$ with values in a finite-dimensional space, whose translates under the algebra generated by $\mathcal{Z}$ and $\mathfrak{k}_{\mathbb{C}}$ span a finite-dimensional space, satisfies $f \star \alpha = f$ for some $\alpha \in C^\infty_c(U)$ invariant under conjugation by $K$; there is no growth hypothesis, and an elliptic annihilator enters only the proof, for the convolution identity on $G_\infty$.
- the passage to $G(k) \backslash G(\mathbb{A})$ with one $\alpha$: a $K_f$-finite smooth $\varphi$ is invariant under an open compact subgroup $L$ of $G(\mathbb{A}_f)$; $G(k) \backslash G(\mathbb{A}) / L$ is a finite union of quotients $\Gamma_i \backslash G_\infty$; the finitely many slices $g_\infty \mapsto \varphi(g_\infty g_i)$ form one vector-valued function on $G_\infty$ that satisfies the hypothesis, applied on the identity component and, for the finitely many components, averaged over the compact action on them, so that one $\alpha$ serves all slices (Borel, §4.2, applies Harish-Chandra's theorem to an arithmetic quotient), for the convolution identity.

## Proof depends on

- [Siegel sets and reduction theory for adelic groups](../../../NumberTheory/algebraic-number-theory/adeles-class-field-theory/reduction-theory-for-adelic-groups.md), for $\lVert xy \rVert \le c \lVert x \rVert \lVert y \rVert$, which makes convolution with a compactly supported function preserve moderate growth, and for the finiteness of $G(k) \backslash G(\mathbb{A}_f) / L$, $L$ open compact in $G(\mathbb{A}_f)$, by which $G(k) \backslash G(\mathbb{A}) / L$ is a finite union of arithmetic quotients $\Gamma_i \backslash G_\infty$ (not a finite set: for $\mathbb{G}_m$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ and $L = \prod_p \mathbb{Z}_p^\times$ the idele norm maps it onto $\mathbb{R}_{>0}$), in the passage to the adelic quotient
- [Analytic elliptic regularity](../../../Analysis/partial-differential-equations/analytic-elliptic-regularity.md), for the analyticity, in Harish-Chandra's proof, of the auxiliary coefficient $F(x) = \beta(r(x) f)$ attached after a Hahn–Banach separation to a continuous linear form $\beta$: $F$ is killed by the finite-codimensional annihilator, which contains elliptic operators, so it is analytic, and vanishing with all its derivatives at the identity it vanishes, which proves the density of the infinitesimal cyclic space
