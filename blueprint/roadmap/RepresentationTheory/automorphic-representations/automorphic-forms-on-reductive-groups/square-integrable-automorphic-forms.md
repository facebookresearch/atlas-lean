# Square-integrable K-finite and Z-finite functions are automorphic forms

This node is still to be broken down. It covers the regularity of square-integrable functions on arithmetic quotients that
are finite under $K$ and under the centre of the enveloping algebra, which the decomposition of the cuspidal spectrum uses.
Let $\mathbf{G}$ be a reductive group over a number field $k$, $G = G_\infty^1$ the subgroup of $\mathbf{G}(k \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathbb{R})$ on which
$\prod_{v \mid \infty} \lvert \chi(g_v) \rvert_v = 1$ for every $k$-rational character $\chi$, and $\Gamma = \mathbf{G}(k) \cap g L g^{-1}$ for a compact open subgroup
$L$ of $\mathbf{G}(\mathbb{A}_f)$ and $g \in \mathbf{G}(\mathbb{A}_f)$, a congruence subgroup, so that $\Gamma \backslash G$ has finite volume; let $K$ be a maximal
compact subgroup of $G$ and $\mathcal{Z}$ the centre of the enveloping algebra. For a fixed $L$,
$\mathbf{G}(k) \backslash \mathbf{G}(\mathbb{A})^1 / L$ is the finite union of the quotients $\Gamma_i \backslash G$ for representatives $g_i$, so the
statements below also hold for functions on $\mathbf{G}(k) \backslash \mathbf{G}(\mathbb{A})^1$ invariant under $L$. Borel proves them for every
arithmetic subgroup; the restriction to congruence subgroups is that of the adelic convolution identity linked below.

- **Regularity.** Let $f$ be a class in $L^p(\Gamma \backslash G)$ for some $p \ge 1$ that is $K$-finite and is annihilated, in the
  sense of distributions, by an ideal of finite codimension of $\mathcal{Z}$. Then $f$ has a unique representative that is
  smooth and of moderate growth, and this representative is an
  [automorphic form](automorphic-forms-and-automorphic-representations.md). Other representatives need not be smooth, since a
  class does not see the values of a function on a null set.
- **Discrete spectrum.** In particular every $K$-finite, $\mathcal{Z}$-finite class in $L^2(\Gamma \backslash G)$ has a unique
  smooth representative of moderate growth, which is an automorphic form.

## Expansion

A good expansion has statement pages for the regularity and for its consequence for $L^2$, each citing the source below with
locators. Borel (§4.1, for semisimple $\mathfrak{g}$) first shows that $f$ is annihilated by a nonconstant polynomial in the elliptic
operator $\Omega = 2 C_{\mathfrak{k}} + C_{\mathfrak{g}}$, which elliptic regularity turns into the real analyticity of a representative. This
page extends that step to reductive $G$: the operator built from the semisimple part $[\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g}]$ misses the central directions, and is not
elliptic when the real centre $\mathfrak{z}$ is nonzero: the norm-one torus of a real quadratic field has a positive-dimensional
real Lie algebra, all central, and an arithmetic quotient of finite volume. One uses
$\Omega + \sum_j Z_j^2$ for a basis $(Z_j)$ of $\mathfrak{z}$, which is elliptic and lies, like $\Omega$, in the subalgebra generated by
$Z(\mathfrak{g})$ and $U(\mathfrak{k})$; $f$ is annihilated by an ideal of finite codimension of that subalgebra, hence by a nonconstant
polynomial in this operator.
Only then does Borel apply Harish-Chandra's convolution identity $f = f \star \alpha$ to that smooth representative, and he
obtains its moderate growth from the $L^p$ bound, the convolution and the counting estimate
$\#(\Gamma \cap G_t) \ll t^m$ for the elements of norm at most $t$.
