# Square-integrable K-finite and Z-finite functions are automorphic forms

This node is still to be broken down. It covers the regularity of square-integrable functions on arithmetic quotients that
are finite under $K$ and under the centre of the enveloping algebra, which the decomposition of the cuspidal spectrum uses.
Let $G$ be the real points of a reductive group, $\Gamma$ an arithmetic subgroup with $\Gamma \backslash G$ of finite volume,
$K$ a maximal compact subgroup and $\mathcal{Z}$ the centre of the enveloping algebra; the same holds on
$G(k) \backslash G(\mathbb{A})^1$, which is a finite union of such quotients.

- **Regularity.** A measurable function on $\Gamma \backslash G$ that lies in some $L^p$, $p \ge 1$, is $K$-finite and is annihilated
  by an ideal of finite codimension of $\mathcal{Z}$ (in the sense of distributions) is smooth and of moderate growth, hence an
  [automorphic form](automorphic-forms-and-automorphic-representations.md).
- **Discrete spectrum.** In particular the $K$-finite, $\mathcal{Z}$-finite elements of $L^2(\Gamma \backslash G)$ are automorphic forms.

## Expansion

A good expansion has statement pages for the regularity and for its consequence for $L^2$, each citing the source below with
locators. Borel deduces the smoothness from the convolution identity $f = f \star \alpha$ of Harish-Chandra, which holds in the
sense of distributions, and the moderate growth from the $L^p$ bound and the same convolution.
