# Base change and automorphic induction for GLₙ

This node is still to be broken down. It covers the base change and automorphic induction of Arthur and
Clozel for $\mathrm{GL}_n$ along cyclic, and then solvable, extensions of number fields. Let $E/F$ be a
cyclic extension of prime degree $\ell$ with Galois group generated by $\sigma$, and $\eta$ a character of
$\mathbb{A}_F^\times / F^\times$ attached to $E/F$ by class field theory.

- **Cyclic base change of prime degree** (Arthur–Clozel). There is a map $\pi' \mapsto \pi$ from the
  representations of $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{A}_F)$ induced from unitary cuspidal automorphic representations of Levi
  subgroups ($\pi' = \pi'_1 \boxplus \dots \boxplus \pi'_p$) to those of $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{A}_E)$, characterized by the Satake
  parameters at the places $v$ where $\pi'$ and $E$ are unramified ($c_w(\pi) = c_v(\pi')$ if $v$ splits in $E$,
  $c_v(\pi')^\ell$ otherwise, for $w \mid v$), whose image is the set of $\sigma$-invariant $\pi$, whose fibre through
  a cuspidal $\pi'$ is $\{\pi', \pi' \otimes \eta, \dots, \pi' \otimes \eta^{\ell-1}\}$, and which sends a cuspidal $\pi'$ to a
  cuspidal $\pi$ exactly when $\pi' \not\simeq \pi' \otimes \eta$.
- **Automorphic induction in prime degree.** If $\pi'$ is cuspidal and $\pi' \simeq \pi' \otimes \eta$, its base change
  is $\pi_1 \boxplus \pi_1^\sigma \boxplus \dots \boxplus \pi_1^{\sigma^{\ell - 1}}$ for a cuspidal representation $\pi_1$ of
  $\mathrm{GL}_{n/\ell}(\mathbb{A}_E)$ with $\pi_1^\sigma \not\simeq \pi_1$. Conversely, for every cuspidal $\pi_1$ of
  $\mathrm{GL}_{n/\ell}(\mathbb{A}_E)$ with $\pi_1^\sigma \not\simeq \pi_1$, the preimage of
  $\pi_1 \boxplus \pi_1^\sigma \boxplus \dots \boxplus \pi_1^{\sigma^{\ell-1}}$ consists of a cuspidal $\pi'$ with $\pi' \simeq \pi' \otimes \eta$, the
  automorphic induction of $\pi_1$; this is an $\ell$-to-$1$ map, whose fibres are the Galois orbits
  $\{\pi_1, \pi_1^\sigma, \dots\}$.
- **Extension to isobaric sums.** Both maps are extended to the class of representations induced from unitary
  cuspidal data (isobaric sums), on which they are characterized by the Satake parameters at the unramified
  places (Clozel's formulas (1.4)–(1.6)). Base change is already defined on that class and takes values in it,
  being additive: $\mathrm{BC}(\tau_1 \boxplus \tau_2) = \mathrm{BC}(\tau_1) \boxplus \mathrm{BC}(\tau_2)$. Automorphic induction is extended as follows: for a
  cuspidal $\pi_1$ of $\mathrm{GL}_{n/\ell}(\mathbb{A}_E)$ with $\pi_1^\sigma \not\simeq \pi_1$ it is the cuspidal representation above; for a cuspidal $\pi_1$
  with $\pi_1^\sigma \simeq \pi_1$, which by the first item is the base change of a cuspidal $\tau$ of $\mathrm{GL}_{n/\ell}(\mathbb{A}_F)$ determined
  up to twists by powers of $\eta$, it is the isobaric sum $\tau \boxplus \tau\eta \boxplus \dots \boxplus \tau\eta^{\ell - 1}$, which is not cuspidal and does not
  depend on the choice of $\tau$; and it is extended additively to isobaric sums. At an unramified place its Satake
  parameter is that of the representation of the Weil group induced from the parameter of $\pi_1$ (at an inert
  place, the $\ell$-th roots of the eigenvalues).
- **Solvable extensions.** Since each step produces an isobaric sum of unitary cuspidal representations, which
  is again an admissible input of the extended maps, the extended maps can be composed along a tower
  $F = F_0 \subset F_1 \subset \dots \subset F_k = E$ of cyclic extensions of prime degree, also when an intermediate result is not
  cuspidal. This gives the existence of base change and automorphic induction on the whole class for a
  solvable extension (Clozel's Theorem 1.2), with uniqueness from the characterization by Satake parameters
  and strong multiplicity one for isobaric sums. It does not give a description of the image and fibres in
  one step for a general cyclic or solvable extension (the argument of Arthur and Clozel works only for cyclic
  extensions of prime degree).
- **Method.** The comparison of the invariant trace formula of $\mathrm{GL}_n$ over $F$ with the twisted trace
  formula of $\mathrm{R}_{E/F}\mathrm{GL}_n$ for the automorphism $\sigma$, the local transfer, and Rankin–Selberg
  $L$-functions in the last step. The classification of the discrete spectrum of $\mathrm{GL}_n$ by Mœglin and
  Waldspurger came after Arthur and Clozel's work; combined with it, base change extends to the
  representations occurring in the discrete spectrum.

## Expansion

A good expansion has pages for the statements of cyclic base change and automorphic induction, for the
solvable case, and for the trace formula comparison with its inputs (the twisted trace formula, the
fundamental lemma for base change, the local base change of representations), citing J. Arthur and
L. Clozel, *Simple Algebras, Base Change, and the Advanced Theory of the Trace Formula*, Annals of
Mathematics Studies 120, Princeton University Press, 1989, openly available as no. 30 of Arthur's collected
works: Chapter 3, (2.1)–(2.4), p. 200, Theorem 3.1, p. 201, Definition 4.1 and Theorem 4.2, p. 202, and Theorem 6.2
with its construction, pp. 215–217; Chapter 1, §§4, 6 and 7; Chapter 2, Theorems A and B, with the gap at p. 196
that Arthur points out.

It also writes out these steps of its own proof:

- the comparison of the invariant trace formula of $\mathrm{GL}_n$ over $F$ with the $\sigma$-twisted trace formula of $\mathrm{R}_{E/F}\mathrm{GL}_n$, through the matching of twisted orbital integrals by the norm map, the extension of the fundamental lemma from unit elements to spherical Hecke functions with the simple forms of the two trace formulas (Arthur–Clozel, Chapter 1, §4), the local base change lifting (Chapter 1, §§6–7) and the averaging over the twists by powers of $\eta$ (Clozel's ICM report, §2, Theorems 2.1–2.3, Theorem 2.3 being the identity of traces; Arthur, §26, pp. 189–191).
