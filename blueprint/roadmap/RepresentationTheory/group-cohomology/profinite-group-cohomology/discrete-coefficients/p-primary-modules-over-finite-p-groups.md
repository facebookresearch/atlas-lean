# Modules over a finite p-group with no invariants

Let $p$ be a prime number, $G$ a finite $p$-group and $A$ a $p$-primary $G$-module, that is, every element of $A$ has
$p$-power order. If $A^G = 0$, or if $A_G = A / I_G A = 0$, where $I_G A$ is the subgroup generated by the elements
$\sigma a - a$, then $A = 0$.

For $A^G$ the proof reduces to finite $A$, since every element of $A$ generates a finite $G$-submodule, where
$\#A \equiv \#A^G \pmod p$ because the orbits outside $A^G$ have order divisible by $p$; the case of $A_G$ follows by
Pontryagin duality, as $(A^*)^G \cong (A_G)^*$ for $A^* = \operatorname{Hom}(A, \mathbb{Q}_p/\mathbb{Z}_p)$. The
congruence for the number of fixed points of a $p$-group acting on a finite set is Mathlib's
`IsPGroup.card_modEq_card_fixedPoints`.
