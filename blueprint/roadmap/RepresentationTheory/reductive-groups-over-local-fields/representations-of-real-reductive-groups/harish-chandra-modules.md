# Harish-Chandra modules

Let $G$ be the group of real points of a Zariski-connected reductive algebraic group over
$\mathbb{R}$, $K$ a maximal compact subgroup with Lie algebra $\mathfrak{k}_0$, $\mathfrak{g}$ the
complexified Lie algebra of $G$ and $Z(\mathfrak{g})$ the centre of its enveloping algebra
$U(\mathfrak{g})$. A **$(\mathfrak{g}, K)$-module** is a $\mathfrak{g}$-module $V$ with an action of
$K$ such that every vector lies in a finite-dimensional $K$-stable subspace on which $K$ acts
continuously, the two resulting actions of $\mathfrak{k}_0$ agree, and
$\pi(\mathrm{Ad}(k)X) = \pi(k)\pi(X)\pi(k)^{-1}$ for $k \in K$, $X \in \mathfrak{g}$. A
**Harish-Chandra module** is a $(\mathfrak{g}, K)$-module with finite $K$-multiplicities that is
annihilated by an ideal of finite codimension in $Z(\mathfrak{g})$.

1. For a $(\mathfrak{g}, K)$-module with finite $K$-multiplicities, every vector is annihilated by
   an ideal of finite codimension in $Z(\mathfrak{g})$; being a Harish-Chandra module, being
   finitely generated over $U(\mathfrak{g})$ and having finite length are equivalent. In particular
   an irreducible $(\mathfrak{g}, K)$-module with finite $K$-multiplicities, being generated by any
   nonzero vector, is a Harish-Chandra module.
2. **Closed invariant subspaces.** Let $(\pi, V)$ be a continuous
   [admissible representation](admissible-representations-of-real-reductive-groups.md) of $G$.
   Its $K$-finite vectors $V_K$ form a $(\mathfrak{g}, K)$-module, and $W \mapsto \overline{W}$ is a
   bijection from the $(\mathfrak{g}, K)$-submodules of $V_K$ onto the closed $G$-stable subspaces
   of $V$, with inverse $U \mapsto U \cap V_K$. In particular $V$ is topologically irreducible if
   and only if $V_K$ is irreducible.
3. **Realizability and matrix coefficients.** Every finitely generated Harish-Chandra module is
   the $(\mathfrak{g}, K)$-module of $K$-finite vectors of a continuous representation of $G$. If
   the $K$-finite vectors of a continuous representation $(\pi, V)$ form a Harish-Chandra module,
   then for $v \in V_K$ and every $K$-finite continuous linear form $\lambda$ on $V$ the scalar
   matrix coefficient $g \mapsto \lambda(\pi(g)v)$ is real-analytic on $G$. This is a statement
   about scalar functions; that the orbit map $g \mapsto \pi(g)v$ itself is analytic, that is,
   that $K$-finite vectors are analytic vectors, is a further theorem of Harish-Chandra that this
   page does not state.
4. **Infinitesimal and unitary equivalence** (Harish-Chandra). For $G$ connected semisimple with
   finite centre, two irreducible unitary representations of $G$ whose $(\mathfrak{g}, K)$-modules
   of $K$-finite vectors are isomorphic are unitarily equivalent. For $\mathrm{U}(p, q)$, whose
   connected centre $Z$ acts on an irreducible unitary representation by a character determined
   by the action of its Lie algebra, this follows from the case of $\mathrm{SU}(p, q)$, since
   $\mathrm{U}(p, q) = Z \cdot \mathrm{SU}(p, q)$.
