# Casselman–Wallach globalization theorem

Let $G$ be the group of real points of a Zariski-connected reductive algebraic group over
$\mathbb{R}$, embedded as a Zariski-closed subset of a real affine space, with the algebraic norm
$\lVert g \rVert$ this gives, and $K$ a maximal compact subgroup. A continuous representation
$(\pi, V)$ of $G$ has **moderate growth** if $V$ is a Fréchet space and for every continuous
seminorm $p$ on $V$ there are an integer $N$ and a continuous seminorm $\nu$ with
$p(\pi(g)v) \le \lVert g \rVert^N \nu(v)$ for all $g \in G$, $v \in V$; it is **smooth** if every
vector is smooth and $V$ carries the topology of its smooth vectors.

**Theorem (Casselman, Wallach).** Every [Harish-Chandra module](harish-chandra-modules.md) $V$ is,
up to canonical topological isomorphism, the $(\mathfrak{g}, K)$-module of $K$-finite vectors of
exactly one smooth representation $V^\infty$ of $G$ of moderate growth on a Fréchet space, and
the assignment $V \mapsto V^\infty$ is functorial and exact. Equivalently, taking $K$-finite
vectors is an equivalence from the category of smooth admissible Fréchet representations of $G$ of
moderate growth whose $K$-finite vectors form a Harish-Chandra module, with continuous $G$-maps,
onto the category of Harish-Chandra modules. Moreover:

1. if $U$ and $V$ are smooth Fréchet representations of moderate growth whose spaces of
   $K$-finite vectors $U_K$ and $V_K$ are both Harish-Chandra modules, every
   $(\mathfrak{g}, K)$-map $U_K \to V_K$ extends uniquely to a continuous $G$-map $U \to V$, and
   this extension has closed range;
2. if the $K$-finite vectors of a smooth Fréchet representation $V$ of moderate growth form a
   Harish-Chandra module generated by a finite-dimensional subspace $U$, every element of $V$ is a
   finite sum $\sum_i \pi(f_i)u_i$ with $u_i \in U$ and $f_i$ in the Schwartz space
   $\mathcal{S}(G)$ of smooth functions rapidly decreasing with all their derivatives.

Casselman's Corollary 10.5 states item 1 assuming only that $V_K$ is a Harish-Chandra module, and
his argument uses that the quotient of $U$ by the closure of the kernel is a Harish-Chandra
representation. The hypothesis on $U_K$ cannot be dropped: for $G = K = \mathrm{U}(1)$ acting
trivially on $U = \ell^2$ and on $V = \mathbb{C}$, both smooth Fréchet representations of moderate
growth with $U_K = U$, every linear form on $\ell^2$ is a $(\mathfrak{g}, K)$-map $U_K \to V_K$,
and a discontinuous one has no continuous extension.
