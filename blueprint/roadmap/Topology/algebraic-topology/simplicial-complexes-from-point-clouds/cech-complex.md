# Čech complex

For a pseudometric space $M$ and a scale $\varepsilon \in \mathbb{R}$, the Čech complex
$\check{C}(M, \varepsilon)$ is the nerve of the family of closed balls $\bar{B}(x, \varepsilon)$,
$x \in M$: a nonempty finite set of points spans a simplex exactly when the closed $\varepsilon$-balls
around them have a common point of $M$ (Carlsson, *Topology and data*, Bull. AMS 2009, §2.2, the
construction $\check{C}(V, \varepsilon)$ with $V = M$, closed balls). The source does not say whether its
balls are open or closed; closed balls are what make the second inclusion of Prop. 2.6 hold: with
open balls, if $\varepsilon > 0$ and $M$ consists of two points at distance exactly $2\varepsilon$, these points span
an edge of $VR(M, 2\varepsilon)$ but not of $\check{C}(M, 2\varepsilon)$.
