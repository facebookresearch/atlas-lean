# Kan complex

A simplicial set $K$ is a Kan complex when, for every $n \ge 1$ and every $0 \le i \le n$, every map of simplicial sets $\Lambda^n_i \to K$ from the horn extends along the inclusion $\Lambda^n_i \subseteq \Delta^n$ to a map $\Delta^n \to K$; the outer horns, $i = 0$ and $i = n$, are included, and the extension need not be unique. Mathlib defines a simplicial set $K$ to be a Kan complex when it is fibrant, that is, when the map from $K$ to the terminal simplicial set is a fibration, where a map of simplicial sets is by definition a fibration when it has the right lifting property with respect to every horn inclusion $\Lambda^n_i \to \Delta^n$ with $n \ge 1$; being a Kan complex is a property, the existence of lifts, not a choice of lifts. Mathlib proves that every Kan complex has the horn-extension property for all $n \ge 1$ and $0 \le i \le n$. Conversely, a family of maps $\Delta^{n-1} \to K$ on the faces of $\Delta^n$ other than the $i$-th face that agree on common faces glues, by a result of Mathlib, to a map $\Lambda^n_i \to K$ restricting to the family on these faces, so the horn-extension property gives a map $\Delta^n \to K$ with the given faces, and Mathlib proves that a simplicial set in which every such compatible family is the family of faces of some map $\Delta^n \to K$ is a Kan complex.
