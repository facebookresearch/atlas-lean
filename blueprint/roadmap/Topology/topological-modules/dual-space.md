# Continuous dual of a topological module

The dual space of a normed space $X$ over $\mathbb{F} = \mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$ is the space $X^*$ of bounded linear functionals $X \to \mathbb{F}$, with pointwise addition and scalar multiplication and the operator norm $\|f\| = \sup\{|f(x)| : \|x\| \le 1\}$. Mathlib defines, for a semiring $R$ with a topology and an $R$-module $M$ with a topology, the continuous dual of $M$ as the type of continuous $R$-linear maps $M \to R$. For a normed space $M$ over $\mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$ these are the continuous linear functionals, not all linear functionals, which in infinite dimension is a genuine restriction, and they form a vector space under pointwise addition and scalar multiplication, which Mathlib equips with the operator norm, the infimum of the constants $c \ge 0$ with $\|f(x)\| \le c\|x\|$ for all $x \in M$. For seminormed spaces over a nontrivially normed field, such as normed spaces over $\mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$, Mathlib proves that a map is linear and continuous exactly when it is linear and bounded, $\|f(x)\| \le C\|x\|$ for some constant $C > 0$ and every $x$, so the continuous linear functionals are the bounded linear functionals. For a normed space over a densely normed field, such as $\mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$, Mathlib proves that the operator norm of $f$ is the supremum of $\|f(x)\|$ over the closed unit ball $\|x\| \le 1$; no completeness of the space is assumed.
