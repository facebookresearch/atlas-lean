# Weak-star topology on the continuous dual

For a normed space $X$ over $\mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$, the weak* topology on the dual space $X^*$ is the locally convex topology defined by the seminorms $p_x(f) = |f(x)|$, one for each $x \in X$. Mathlib defines, for a module $E$ with a topology over a commutative semiring $\mathbb{K}$ with a topology in which addition and multiplication by a fixed scalar are continuous, the weak dual of $E$ as a copy of the type of continuous linear functionals $E \to \mathbb{K}$, with the same elements as the continuous dual but with its own topology: the topology induced by the map sending $f$ to the family of values $(f(x))_{x \in E}$ in the product of copies of $\mathbb{K}$ indexed by $E$, that is, the coarsest topology for which every evaluation $f \mapsto f(x)$ is continuous; it is defined through evaluations rather than through a norm, and on the dual of a normed space it need not coincide with the topology of the operator norm. For a seminormed space $E$ over a nontrivially normed field $\mathbb{K}$, in particular a normed space over $\mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$, Mathlib proves that this topology is the topology defined by the family of seminorms indexed by the vectors $x \in E$ whose value at a functional $f$ is $\|f(x)\|$, which is $|f(x)|$ for real or complex scalars; no completeness or reflexivity is assumed.
