# Kourovka Problem 10.7 (Arnautov's question on countable groups)

The statement that in every countable group $G$, every non-discrete Hausdorff group topology $\tau$ satisfying the first axiom of countability has a finer group topology $\sigma$ that is still non-discrete and in which $G$ is Raikov complete. A group $G$ with a topology is Raikov complete when every proper filter $\mathcal{F}$ on $G$ that is Cauchy for the two-sided uniformity (for every neighbourhood $U$ of $1$ some $A \in \mathcal{F}$ has $x^{-1}y \in U$ and $yx^{-1} \in U$ for all $x, y \in A$) converges to some point of $G$.
