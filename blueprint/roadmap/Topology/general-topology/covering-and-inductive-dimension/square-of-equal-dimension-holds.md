# A separable metric space whose square has the same dimension

For every integer $n \ge 2$ there is a separable metric space $S$ with $\dim S = \dim(S \times S) = n$; Erdős Problem 909 has a positive answer. Dimension means small inductive dimension (for separable metric spaces it agrees with the large inductive and the covering dimension). For $n = 1$ an example is the space of rational points of Hilbert space; Anderson and Keisler constructed examples for every $n$.

## Sources

- T. F. Bloom, Erdős Problems, Problem #909, https://www.erdosproblems.com/909; resolution: R. D. Anderson and J. E. Keisler, An example in dimension theory, Proc. Amer. Math. Soc. 18 (1967), 709-713.
