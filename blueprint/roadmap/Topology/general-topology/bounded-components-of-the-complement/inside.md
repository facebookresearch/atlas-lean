# The inside of a set

Let $X$ be a topological space with a bornology and $K \subseteq X$. The inside of $K$ is the set of
points $x \notin K$ whose connected component in the complement $X \setminus K$ is bounded. For a
simple closed curve in $\mathbb{C}$ it is the interior of the curve. This is the general form, in any
topological space with a bornology, of
[the region enclosed by a planar set](../../../Geometry/discrete-geometry/part-01/pick-s-theorem/inside.md).
