# Countable fan tightness of C_p(X) does not imply the weak sequence selection property

There is a set of reals $X$ such that $C_p(X)$ has countable fan tightness but not the weak sequence selection property; Tsaban's Milestone Problem 15.3 has a negative answer. For a topological space $X$, $C_p(X)$ is the space of continuous functions $X \to \mathbb{R}$ with the topology of pointwise convergence (the subspace topology of the product $\mathbb{R}^X$), and $\mathbf{0}$ is the constant zero function. A topological space $Y$ has countable fan tightness when for every $y \in Y$ and all sets $A_0, A_1, \dots \subseteq Y$ with $y \in \overline{A_n}$ for every $n$ there are finite $F_n \subseteq A_n$ with $y \in \overline{\bigcup_n F_n}$. $C_p(X)$ has the weak sequence selection property when for every family $(f^n_k)_{n,k \in \mathbb{N}}$ in $C_p(X)$ such that each sequence $(f^n_k)_{k}$ converges to $\mathbf{0}$ there are indices $k_n$ with $\mathbf{0}$ in the closure of $\{f^n_{k_n} : n \in \mathbb{N}\}$.

Sakai proved that the weak sequence selection property of $C_p(X)$ forces $X$ to be zero-dimensional, so $C_p([0,1])$ lacks it, while $C_p([0,1])$ has countable fan tightness because all finite powers of $[0,1]$ are compact.
