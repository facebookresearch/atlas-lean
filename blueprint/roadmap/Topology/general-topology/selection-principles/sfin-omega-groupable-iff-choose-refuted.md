# S_fin(Ω, Ω^gp) is not equivalent to (Ω Ω^gp)

It is not the case that every set of reals satisfies $S_{fin}(\Omega,\Omega^{gp})$ exactly when it satisfies $(\Omega\ \Omega^{gp})$; Tsaban's Milestone Problem 10.6 has a negative answer. For a set of reals $X$ (a subset of $\mathbb{R}$ with the subspace topology), covers of $X$ are countable families of open subsets of $X$ with union $X$ not containing $X$; $\Omega$ is the collection of open $\omega$-covers of $X$ (every finite subset of $X$ lies in a member) and $\Omega^{gp}$ that of its $\Omega$-groupable members (unions of pairwise disjoint nonempty finite pieces $\mathcal{F}_n$ such that $\{\bigcup \mathcal{F}_n : n \in A\} \in \Omega$ for every infinite $A \subseteq \mathbb{N}$). $S_{fin}(\Omega,\Omega^{gp})$ asks that every sequence of members of $\Omega$ admit finite subfamilies $\mathcal{F}'_n$ with $\bigcup_n \mathcal{F}'_n \in \Omega^{gp}$, and $(\Omega\ \Omega^{gp})$ that every member of $\Omega$ contain a member of $\Omega^{gp}$.

Sakai showed that the Baire space $\mathbb{N}^\mathbb{N}$, a set of reals homeomorphic to the irrationals, satisfies $(\Omega\ \Omega^{gp})$, while it is not even Menger and so fails $S_{fin}(\Omega,\Omega^{gp})$.
