# Ω-groupable ω-covers

Let $T$ be a topological space. An open cover of $T$ here is a countable family $\mathcal{U}$ of open subsets of $T$ with $\bigcup \mathcal{U} = T$ and $T \notin \mathcal{U}$ (the source's nontrivial covers). An $\omega$-cover is such a cover in which every finite subset of $T$ lies in some member; $\Omega$ denotes the collection of open $\omega$-covers of $T$. A member $\mathcal{U}$ of $\Omega$ is $\Omega$-groupable when it is the union of pairwise disjoint nonempty finite sets $\mathcal{F}_0, \mathcal{F}_1, \dots$ such that for every infinite $A \subseteq \mathbb{N}$ the family $\{\bigcup \mathcal{F}_n : n \in A\}$ is again in $\Omega$; $\Omega^{gp}$ denotes the collection of $\Omega$-groupable members of $\Omega$.
