# Simon's conjecture on epimorphisms of knot groups

The statement that every knot group surjects onto only finitely many knot groups up to isomorphism: for every knot $K$ there are finitely many knots $K_1, \dots, K_m$ such that every knot group onto which $\pi_1(\mathbb{R}^3 \setminus K)$ admits a surjective homomorphism is isomorphic to some $\pi_1(\mathbb{R}^3 \setminus K_i)$. A knot here is a smooth $2\pi$-periodic map $\gamma : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}^3$ with nowhere-vanishing derivative that is injective on $[0, 2\pi)$; its knot group is the fundamental group $\pi_1(\mathbb{R}^3 \setminus K, x_0)$ of the complement of its image $K$, which is isomorphic to $\pi_1(S^3 \setminus K)$, at any base point $x_0$.
