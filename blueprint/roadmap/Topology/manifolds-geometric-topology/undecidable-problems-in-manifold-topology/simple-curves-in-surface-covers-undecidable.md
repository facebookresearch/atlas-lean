# Deciding whether a normal subgroup of a surface group contains an essential simple closed curve is undecidable

There is no algorithm that decides, for a regular covering space of a surface of genus at least two given by its normal subgroup of the fundamental group, whether that subgroup contains an element represented by a homotopically nontrivial simple closed curve on the surface.

Let $S$ be a pointed surface, not necessarily closed, of genus at least $2$, and let $\tilde{S} \to S$ be a regular covering space, so that $\pi_1(\tilde{S})$ is identified with a normal subgroup of $\pi_1(S)$. A loop on $S$ is *simple* if it is freely homotopic to an embedded closed curve.

**Theorem (Irmer).** There does not exist an algorithm that determines whether a normal subgroup $\pi_1(\tilde{S}) \subset \pi_1(S)$ contains a word representing a homotopically nontrivial simple closed curve on $S$.

Conventions: regular covers enter as finite data through their deck transformation groups $\pi_1(S) / \pi_1(\tilde{S})$, which the proof builds from finite group presentations. Simplicity refers to curves on $S$, not on $\tilde{S}$. For a single given word in $\pi_1(S)$ there are classical algorithms deciding whether it represents a simple curve; the source notes that deciding whether a given simple curve lies in $\pi_1(\tilde{S})$ is undecidable as well (Corollary 5). The answer depends on how $\pi_1(\tilde{S})$ sits inside $\pi_1(S)$, not only on its isomorphism type.

## Expansion

The proof modifies Rabin's construction. Let $S_2 \to S$ be the mod $2$ homology cover; $\pi_1(S_2)$ is normally generated by the squares $c_i^2$ of simple nonseparating curves $c_i$, one for each nontrivial deck transformation (Lemma 6 of the source), and these generate a free subgroup. For a finitely presented group $H$ with unsolvable word problem and a word $w$, a chain of free products and HNN-type relations on the $c_i^2$ gives a group $D_w$, trivial iff $w = 1$ in $H$, which is the deck group of a cover $S_2' \to S_2$. Intersecting the translates of $\pi_1(S_2')$ under the deck group of $S_2 \to S$ gives a normal subgroup $\pi_1(\tilde S_2)$ of $\pi_1(S)$. If $w = 1$, then $\tilde S_2 = S_2$, whose fundamental group contains words representing simple curves on $S$ (Section 4 of the source); if $w \ne 1$, every word of $\pi_1(\tilde S_2)$ lies in $[\pi_1(S), [\pi_1(S), \pi_1(S)]]$ or represents a nonprimitive homology class, so none represents a simple curve. An algorithm for the problem would therefore solve the word problem of $H$. The easier undecidability of whether a given simple curve lies in $\pi_1(\tilde S)$ (Corollary 5) follows directly from embedding finitely presented groups into deck groups (Corollary 4). A good expansion links Lemma 6 on the mod $2$ homology cover, the construction of the groups $D_w$ and the analysis of simple curves in the resulting covers.
