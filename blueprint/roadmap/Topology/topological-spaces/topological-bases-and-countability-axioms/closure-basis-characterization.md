# A point is in the closure of a set iff every basis element containing it meets the set

Let $X$ be a topological space and let $\mathcal{B}$ be a basis of its topology. Here a *basis* of the topology of $X$ is a collection $\mathcal{B}$ of subsets of $X$ that covers $X$, such that for all $B_1, B_2 \in \mathcal{B}$ and $x \in B_1 \cap B_2$ some $B_3 \in \mathcal{B}$ satisfies $x \in B_3 \subseteq B_1 \cap B_2$, and such that the topology of $X$ is the topology generated by $\mathcal{B}$. Let $A \subseteq X$ and $x \in X$. Then $x$ lies in the closure $\overline{A}$ of $A$ if and only if every $B \in \mathcal{B}$ with $x \in B$ satisfies $B \cap A \neq \varnothing$.
