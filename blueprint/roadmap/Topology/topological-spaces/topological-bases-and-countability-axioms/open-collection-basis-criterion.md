# A collection of open sets refining every open neighbourhood is a basis for the topology

Let $X$ be a topological space and let $\mathcal{C}$ be a collection of open subsets of $X$. Suppose that for every open set $U \subseteq X$ and every point $x \in U$ there is $C \in \mathcal{C}$ with $x \in C \subseteq U$. Then $\mathcal{C}$ is a basis of the topology of $X$: the sets in $\mathcal{C}$ cover $X$; for all $C_1, C_2 \in \mathcal{C}$ and $x \in C_1 \cap C_2$ there is $C_3 \in \mathcal{C}$ with $x \in C_3 \subseteq C_1 \cap C_2$; and the topology generated by $\mathcal{C}$ is the topology of $X$.
