# Uniform continuity of a function on a subset

A function $f\colon A \to \mathbb{R}$ on a set $A \subseteq \mathbb{R}$ is uniformly continuous on $A$ when for every $\varepsilon > 0$ there is a $\delta > 0$, the same for every pair of points of $A$, such that $|f(x) - f(y)| < \varepsilon$ for all $x, y \in A$ with $|x - y| < \delta$. Mathlib defines uniform continuity on a subset for maps between uniform spaces: for uniform spaces $\alpha$ and $\beta$, a function $f\colon \alpha \to \beta$ defined on all of $\alpha$ and a subset $s \subseteq \alpha$, the function $f$ is uniformly continuous on $s$ when the map $(x, y) \mapsto (f(x), f(y))$ sends the uniformity of $\alpha$, restricted to pairs of points of $s$, into the uniformity of $\beta$, so that pairs of points of $s$ that are close are sent to pairs that are close; this is a condition on the values of $f$ on $s$ only, not uniform continuity on all of $\alpha$, and no interval, compactness or continuity assumption is made. For metric, or more generally pseudometric, spaces Mathlib proves that $f$ is uniformly continuous on $s$ exactly when for every $\varepsilon > 0$ some $\delta > 0$ gives $d(f(x), f(y)) < \varepsilon$ for all $x, y \in s$ with $d(x, y) < \delta$, and on $\mathbb{R}$ the distance is $d(x, y) = |x - y|$. A function $f$ given only on $A$ is extended to all of $\mathbb{R}$ in any way, for instance by $0$ outside $A$, and since only its values on $A$ enter, this gives the condition above.
