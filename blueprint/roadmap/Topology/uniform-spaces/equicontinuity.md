# Equicontinuous set of functions into a uniform space

A set $\mathcal{F}$ of continuous functions from a topological space $X$ to a metric space $(Y, d)$ is equicontinuous when, for every point $x_0 \in X$ and every $\varepsilon > 0$, one neighborhood $U$ of $x_0$ serves all members at once: $d(f(x), f(x_0)) < \varepsilon$ for every $x \in U$ and every $f \in \mathcal{F}$. Mathlib defines equicontinuity for functions from a topological space $X$ to a uniform space $\alpha$, with no continuity requirement on the functions: a family $(F_i)_{i \in \iota}$ of functions $X \to \alpha$ is equicontinuous at $x_0$ when for every entourage $V$ of $\alpha$ the condition $(F_i(x_0), F_i(x)) \in V$ for every $i$ holds at all points $x$ near $x_0$, that is, on a set belonging to the neighborhood filter of $x_0$; the family is equicontinuous when it is equicontinuous at every point of $X$, and a set of functions $X \to \alpha$ is equicontinuous when the family of its members, indexed by the set itself, is equicontinuous. When $\alpha$ is a metric, or more generally pseudometric, space, Mathlib proves that equicontinuity at $x_0$ holds exactly when for every $\varepsilon > 0$ the condition $d(F_i(x_0), F_i(x)) < \varepsilon$ for every $i$ holds at all points $x$ near $x_0$, and such a condition holds near $x_0$ exactly when it holds on some open set containing $x_0$. Since $d$ is symmetric, at every point this is the condition above, with a single neighborhood working for every member of the set; it is neither uniform equicontinuity nor the continuity of each member separately.
