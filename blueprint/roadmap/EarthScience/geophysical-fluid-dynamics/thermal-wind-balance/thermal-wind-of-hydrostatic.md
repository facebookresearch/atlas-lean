# Thermal wind from hydrostatic and geostrophic balance

In three-dimensional space with coordinates pointing east, north and up (f-plane, Boussinesq),
let the pressure `p` be twice continuously differentiable and the density `ρ` satisfy
hydrostatic balance `∂p/∂z = -ρ g` everywhere, and let the velocity `(u, v)` satisfy the
geostrophic balance `f u = -(1/ρ₀) ∂p/∂y`, `f v = (1/ρ₀) ∂p/∂x` everywhere, with `f ≠ 0` and
`ρ₀ ≠ 0`. Then at every point `f ∂u/∂z = (g/ρ₀) ∂ρ/∂y` and `f ∂v/∂z = -(g/ρ₀) ∂ρ/∂x`: the
horizontal density gradient determines the vertical shear of the geostrophic wind. That the
mixed partial derivatives of `p` commute follows from its regularity; it is not assumed.
