# Nearest-neighbor adjacency on the discrete torus

For $L \in \mathbb{N}$ and $u = (u_1, u_2)$, $v = (v_1, v_2)$ in $(\mathbb{Z}/L\mathbb{Z})^2$ (with $\mathbb{Z}/0\mathbb{Z} = \mathbb{Z}$), $u$ and $v$ are adjacent when

$$\big(u_1 = v_1 \text{ and } u_2 = v_2 \pm 1\big) \quad\text{or}\quad \big(u_2 = v_2 \text{ and } u_1 = v_1 \pm 1\big).$$

The relation is defined for every $L$. For $L = 0$ it is the nearest-neighbour adjacency of $\mathbb{Z}^2$; for $L = 1$ every site is adjacent to itself (since $1 = 0$); for $L = 2$ one has $+1 = -1$, so each site has only two distinct neighbours. It gives the ordinary simple $L \times L$ torus graph, with four distinct neighbours per site, exactly when $L > 2$.
