# Crystalline order at positive temperature

This node records a research direction: for a three-dimensional continuum system of classical particles with a realistic pair interaction, prove that some low-temperature equilibrium state is crystalline.

## What would settle it

Fix an explicit pair potential, the ensemble (canonical in a box of volume proportional to the particle number, or grand canonical) and the thermodynamic limit. Define a crystalline-order property precisely (for example a symmetry-broken periodic one-particle density or nonzero Bragg peaks) and prove that it holds for some infinite-volume Gibbs state at small positive temperature. A singularity of the free energy alone does not prove crystallization.

## Status

No cited source poses this question in the form above; it is a research direction motivated by the cited results. This page does not certify the status or novelty of any particular formulation. The status evidence for this direction is incomplete; check later literature before relying on it.

Blanc and Lewin review the crystallization conjecture at zero temperature and, in their Section 3.1, give a possible formulation at positive temperature through the Gibbs measure of the canonical ensemble in a bounded domain, noting that it seems intuitive that crystallization occurs only at small temperature.

This page records a research direction and asserts no theorem or present-status certification.
