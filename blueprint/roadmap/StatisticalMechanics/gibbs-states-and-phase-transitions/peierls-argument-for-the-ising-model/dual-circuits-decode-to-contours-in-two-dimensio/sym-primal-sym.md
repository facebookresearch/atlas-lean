# Primal edge crossed by an unordered pair of faces

The faces of $\mathbb{Z}^2$ are the unit squares, each labelled by its lower-left corner $f \in \mathbb{Z}^2$. For faces $f, g$, the associated primal edge is: if $f_0 = g_0$, the edge from
$(f_0, M)$ to $(f_0 + 1, M)$ with $M = \max(f_1, g_1)$; otherwise the edge from $(A, m)$ to $(A, m + 1)$
with $A = \max(f_0, g_0)$ and $m = \min(f_1, g_1)$. It is symmetric in $f$ and $g$, and for adjacent
faces it is the primal edge they share, the edge crossed by the dual edge $\{f, g\}$.
