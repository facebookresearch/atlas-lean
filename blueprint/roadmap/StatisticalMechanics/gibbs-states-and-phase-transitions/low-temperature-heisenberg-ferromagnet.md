# Spontaneous magnetization of the quantum Heisenberg ferromagnet

Fix integers $d\ge3$ and $\ell\ge1$, and set $S=\ell/2$. At each site $x\in\mathbb Z^d$, use magnetic levels $k=0,\ldots,\ell$ with $m_k=k-S$. The Hilbert space is complex $\ell^2$ of configurations equal to level $0$ off a finite set. The local matrix unit $E^x_{ab}$ replaces level $b$ at $x$ by $a$ and kills vectors with any other level there. The quasi-local algebra $\mathcal A$ is the operator-norm closure of the complex unital $*$-algebra generated by these units. Let $S_x^z$ be diagonal with entries $m_k$, and $S_x^+$ raise level $k<\ell$ by one with coefficient $\sqrt{S(S+1)-m_k(m_k+1)}$; put $S_x^-=(S_x^+)^*$, $S_x^x=(S_x^++S_x^-)/2$ and $S_x^y=(S_x^+-S_x^-)/(2i)$.

On a finite set $\Lambda$, set $H_\Lambda=-\sum_{\{x,y\}}(S_x^x S_y^x+S_x^y S_y^y+S_x^z S_y^z)$, counting each unordered nearest-neighbor edge contained in $\Lambda$ once and using no external field. For each $A\in\mathcal A$ and $t\in\mathbb R$, the operators $e^{itH_{[-n,n]^d}}Ae^{-itH_{[-n,n]^d}}$ converge in norm to an element $\alpha_t(A)$ of $\mathcal A$ as $n\to\infty$.

There exists a real $\beta_0>0$ such that for every real $\beta\ge\beta_0$ there is a continuous complex-linear functional $\omega$ on $\mathcal A$ with $\omega(1)=1$ and $\omega(A^*A)\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}$. It is invariant under every lattice translation. For every $A,B\in\mathcal A$ there is a complex function $F$ continuous and bounded on the closed strip $0\le\operatorname{Im}z\le\beta$, holomorphic in the open strip, with $F(t)=\omega(A\alpha_t(B))$ and $F(t+i\beta)=\omega(\alpha_t(B)A)$ for all real $t$. Moreover $\omega(S^z_0)$ is real and at least $\ell/8=S/4$.

## Expansion

Verify the matrix-unit relations and embed each finite tensor algebra, with translations intertwining the embeddings. Separate the box-limit norm convergence from compactness of finite-volume states. Identify the strip boundary data for the limiting zero-field dynamics and retain the source magnetization lower bound in the thermodynamic and small-field limits.
