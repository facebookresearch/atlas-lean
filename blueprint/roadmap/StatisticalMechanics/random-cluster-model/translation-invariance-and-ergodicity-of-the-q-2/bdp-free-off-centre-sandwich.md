# Free off-centre sandwich hypothesis

For $d, N \in \mathbb{N}$, the free off-centre sandwich holds when for every edge $e'$ of $\Lambda_N = \{-N, \dots, N\}^d$ and every $t \in \mathbb{R}$, there are sets $I_n$ of edges of $\Lambda_n = \{-n, \dots, n\}^d$ and numbers $\delta_n \ge 0$ with $\delta_n \to 0$ such that every $e \in I_n$ satisfies $\theta^0_e(\sigma(t)) = \theta^0_{e'}(\sigma(t))$ and $\theta^0_{e'}(\sigma(t)) - \delta_n \le \phi_n(e \text{ open})$, and $(E(n) - |I_n|)/E(n) \to 0$ (read as $0$ when $E(n) = 0$). Here $\sigma(t) = e^t/(1 + e^t)$, $E(n)$ is the number of edges of $\Lambda_n$, $\theta^0$ and $\theta^1$ are the [free](../per-box-agreement-of-free-and-wired-random-clust/free-edge-density.md) and [wired edge densities](../per-box-agreement-of-free-and-wired-random-clust/wired-edge-density.md) at $q = 2$, and $\phi_n(e \text{ open})$, $\phi^w_n(e \text{ open})$ are the edge marginals of the free and wired random-cluster probabilities on $\Lambda_n$ at $(\sigma(t), 2)$.
