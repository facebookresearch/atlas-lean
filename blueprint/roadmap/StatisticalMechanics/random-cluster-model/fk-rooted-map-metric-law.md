# Rooted-map FK weight and vertex metric law

A rooted map on $m>0$ edges is a connected rotation system on $2m$ darts: edge reversal is an involution without fixed points, vertices are rotation orbits, and faces are orbits of rotation composed with reversal, with Euler vertex-plus-face count $m+2$. Root at dart zero and quotient by simultaneous relabelling fixing the root. Loops and parallel edges are allowed. For an arbitrary subset $A$ of its edges, give the pair $(M,A)$ weight $q^{k_M(A)+(|A|-|V(M)|)/2}$, counting isolated vertices in $k_M(A)$; normalize over all decorated rooted maps of that size. The measured metric space forgets the root and decoration, uses full-map vertex distance, and pushes the uniform dart law to vertices. It is not the distance in the open subgraph or the uniform law on vertices.
