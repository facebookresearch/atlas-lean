# Periodic Ising lattices with a prescribed edge

A periodic Ising lattice on the half-plane tessellates copies of one finite Ising block, with the spins of its edge row prescribed by an external field; its ground states are the configurations whose energy no finite change lowers, and its magnetization is the density of $1$-spins.

**Definition (after Gu, Weedbrook, Perales and Nielsen).** Spins $s_{\mathbf x} \in \{0, 1\}$ sit at the sites $\mathbf x$ of the half-plane $\mathbb Z \times \mathbb N$, whose row $\mathbb Z \times \{0\}$ is the *edge*. For $K \geq 2$, a finite Ising block is a $K \times K$ square of sites with a Hamiltonian

$$
H_K = \sum c_{\mathbf x, \mathbf y}\, s_{\mathbf x} s_{\mathbf y} + \sum M_{\mathbf x}\, s_{\mathbf x},
$$

in which only adjacent sites interact ($c_{\mathbf x, \mathbf y} = 0$ unless $|\mathbf x - \mathbf y|_1 = 1$); here the couplings $c_{\mathbf x, \mathbf y}$ and fields $M_{\mathbf x}$ are integers. The *periodic Ising lattice* with block Hamiltonian $H_K$ places a copy of the block, carrying $H_K$, on each square $\{(K-1)i, \dots, (K-1)i+K-1\} \times \{(K-1)j, \dots, (K-1)j+K-1\}$ with $i \in \mathbb Z$ and $j \in \mathbb N$, so that adjacent copies share a row or column. The external field on the edge prescribes the edge spins: the edge row is fixed to an eventually periodic configuration $e \in \{0,1\}^{\mathbb Z}$, given by a finite word together with the periodic words it repeats to the left and to the right. The energy of the infinite lattice, a sum of infinitely many block energies, need not converge, so the source's ground states, the configurations minimizing the energy (section "Reductionism and the Periodic Ising Lattice", after eq. (1)), are read through finite changes: a configuration $s$ is a *ground state* if it agrees with $e$ on the edge and, for every configuration $s'$ that agrees with $e$ and differs from $s$ at finitely many sites, the sum of $H_K(s'|_C) - H_K(s|_C)$ over the finitely many copies $C$ containing a changed site is at least $0$. A configuration that agrees with $e$ and whose restriction to every copy of the block minimizes $H_K$ is a *simultaneous block minimizer*; every simultaneous block minimizer is a ground state, but a lattice may have ground states and no simultaneous block minimizer: for $K = 2$, the all-one edge and $H_2 = a - 2b$ with $a$ and $b$ the lower-left and lower-right spins of the block, two horizontally adjacent copies cannot both have $(a, b) = (0, 1)$, while the all-one configuration is a ground state, since each site above the edge carries the net field $1 - 2 = -1$. The *magnetization* of $s$ is the density of $1$-spins,

$$
m(s) = \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{\#\{\mathbf x \in [-n, n] \times [0, n] : s_{\mathbf x} = 1\}}{(2n+1)(n+1)},
$$

when this limit exists.

Gu, Weedbrook, Perales and Nielsen encode the run of a one-dimensional cellular automaton in the ground states of such lattices, the edge holding the initial condition, and conclude that macroscopic ground-state observables are undecidable, the magnetization being their first example (section "Undecidable Macroscopic Observables", item 1); the review of Perales-Eceiza et al. (Section 5.1.1) presents this as established. The source asks whether "$P(s) = p$ at ground state $s$" without fixing which ground states are meant, how the infinite-volume density is taken, or how the edge is given. These choices matter: for $H_K = 0$ and the all-zero edge, both the all-zero configuration and the one equal to $1$ everywhere above the edge are ground states (indeed simultaneous block minimizers), with densities $0$ and $1$. The source thus states no precise decision problem about the magnetization. The source treats finite-range correlations $\langle s_{l,m} s_{l,m+r} \rangle$ and $\langle s_{l,m} s_{l+r,m} \rangle$, with $\langle \cdot \rangle$ the average over all sites, and the ground-state degeneracy in the same way, and the correlation length by a separate reduction, from whether the encoded automaton eventually has no live cells. Its general "Rice's theorem for physical systems" for *averaging* properties needs a condition tying the excluded interval to the range of the block average, since as printed constant properties satisfy it. Its formal argument also infers, from the decidability of "$P(s) = p$ at ground state $s$" for each $p$, that "$A(s)$ lies outside $[a, b]$" is decidable, while the averaging condition gives only that $P(s) = p$ implies $A(s) \notin [a, b]$; the step from equality to the side of an interval is not supplied.

Toward a theorem: in the source's construction (section "The Cellular Automata Encoding" and Appendix A), "designer" Ising blocks implement the automaton's update rule, a three-way fan-out and a swap, and every simultaneous block minimizer encodes the run of the automaton from the initial condition on the edge. The source argues that the *prosperity* of the run, the proportion of live cells averaged over all time steps, depends on the result of an encoded Turing machine for the Game of Life and Life without Death; these are two-dimensional automata, which its encoding of a $d$-dimensional automaton in a $(d+1)$-dimensional lattice places in three dimensions. Rule 110 appears in the source only as an example of a universal automaton (section "The Approach"), and no prosperity argument is given for a one-dimensional automaton, which the half-plane needs. Replacing the fan-out blocks by large "magnifier" blocks whose ground states are all $0$ or all $1$ according to their input makes the magnetization reveal on which side of $1/2$ the prosperity lies; this supports a decision problem of the form "is the magnetization of the ground state less than $1/2$?", given a prosperity bound with a margin (below $1/3$ or above $2/3$, say), and not the equality form "$P(s) = p$". Stating the result as a theorem requires a source that fixes the decision problem: the encoding of the edge, the ground states quantified over (including inputs with no simultaneous block minimizer), the limit defining the density, and the predicate.

For finite Ising lattices, Gu and Perales relate these emergent properties to the NP-completeness of the ground-state decision problem: given an explicitly encoded finite square-lattice Ising Hamiltonian with integer coefficients and an integer threshold $E$, does some configuration have energy at most $E$? A threshold, or equivalently an additive constant in the Hamiltonian, is part of the input; with the threshold fixed at $0$ and no constant the all-zero configuration always has energy $0$ (Gu and Perales, Sections I and V, after Barahona).
