# Ring Theory

Ring theory studies addition and multiplication through ideals, modules, localization, dimension, and homological structure.

[Ramification theory](ramification/README.md), the largest field with about a quarter of the subject's articles,
follows primes through finite extensions of Dedekind domains and discrete valuation rings: a prime is unramified
exactly when it does not divide the [discriminant](ramification/different-and-discriminant-ideals/README.md), the norm
of the different, and at an unramified prime with finite residue field the
[Frobenius element](ramification/frobenius-elements/README.md) generates the decomposition group and is trivial
exactly when the prime splits completely. In [Dedekind domains](dedekind-domains/README.md) the
[ideal class group](dedekind-domains/ideal-class-group/README.md) is trivial exactly when the domain is a principal
ideal domain, equivalently a unique factorization domain. [Formal power series](formal-power-series/README.md) include
Hahn series, and the subject also has Hilbert's syzygy theorem, the Quillen–Suslin theorem and the weak
[Nullstellensatz](ideals-and-constructions/hilbert-s-nullstellensatz/README.md) over $\mathbb{C}$.
