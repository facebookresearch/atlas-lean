# Formal integration raises agreement on an axis

If `a` agrees with `b` through degree `k` on axis `i`, and `c` agrees with
`d` through degree `0` on axis `i` (they have the same coefficients at all
exponents with `n i = 0`), then `pIntegral i a c` agrees with `pIntegral i b
d` through degree `k + 1` on axis `i`.

Here `K` is a field, `σ` is an index set of variables, `i : σ` is the
selected axis, and two series agree through degree `k` on axis `i` when
their coefficients coincide at every exponent `n` with `n i ≤ k`. `pIntegral
i f b` is the formal integral of `f` along `i` with the coefficients of `b`
at exponents with `n i = 0`; its other coefficients are those of `f` with the `i`-degree lowered by one, divided by the `i`-degree in `K` (with `x / 0 = 0`), so it is an antiderivative of `f` only in characteristic zero.
