# Finitely generated algebra

An algebra $S$ over a commutative ring $R$ is finitely generated when there are finitely many elements $s_1, \dots, s_n \in S$ such that the only subring of $S$ containing the image of $R$ and $s_1, \dots, s_n$ is $S$ itself; one writes $S = R[s_1, \dots, s_n]$. Mathlib defines this for algebras over commutative semirings. Let $R$ be a commutative semiring and $A$ a semiring, not necessarily commutative, with an $R$-algebra structure: a ring homomorphism $R \to A$ whose image commutes with every element of $A$, with $r \cdot a$ the product of the image of $r$ and $a$. Then $A$ is of *finite type* over $R$ when there is a finite set $t \subseteq A$ whose generated $R$-subalgebra is all of $A$; the subalgebra generated by $t$ is the smallest subsemiring of $A$ containing $t$ and the image of $R$. The map $R \to A$ need not be injective, $A$ need not be a finitely generated $R$-module, and $t$ may be empty. When $R$ and $A$ are rings, a subring of $A$ containing the image of $R$ is the same thing as an $R$-subalgebra, since multiplication by elements of the image gives the scalar action. So the smallest such subring containing $t$ is the subalgebra generated by $t$, which gives the familiar definition.
