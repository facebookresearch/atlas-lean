# Geometric points detect elements when the generic fiber is reduced

Let $R$ be an integral domain with fraction field $K$, and let $B$ be a commutative $R$-algebra
that is torsion-free as an $R$-module and such that $K \otimes_R B$ is reduced and finite-dimensional
over $K$. Let $\Omega$ be an algebraically closed field extending $K$, viewed as an $R$-algebra
through $R \to K \to \Omega$. If $b \in B$ satisfies $\varphi(b) = 0$ for every homomorphism of
$R$-algebras $\varphi \colon B \to \Omega$, then $b = 0$. The field $\Omega$ must extend $K$: for
$R = B = \mathbb{Z}$ and $\Omega = \overline{\mathbb{F}}_p$, the nonzero element $p$ vanishes under
the only ring homomorphism $\mathbb{Z} \to \Omega$.

This is the statement that geometric points detect functions: it is used to show that maps of
schemes are determined by their values on geometric points, to pass from vanishing on fibers to
vanishing globally, and to prove reducedness from pointwise vanishing.
