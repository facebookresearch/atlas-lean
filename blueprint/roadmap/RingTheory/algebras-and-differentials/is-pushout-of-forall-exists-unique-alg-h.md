# Universal-property criterion for a pushout square of algebras

Consider a commutative square of commutative rings formed by $R \to S \to S'$ and
$R \to R' \to S'$, with all maps compatible. It is a pushout square, that is,
$S' \cong S \otimes_R R'$ through the given maps, when it satisfies the universal property for
algebra homomorphisms: for every commutative $R'$-algebra $T$ and every ring homomorphism
$g \colon S \to T$ compatible with the maps from $R$, there is a unique $R'$-algebra homomorphism
$S' \to T$ whose composite with $S \to S'$ is $g$.

This is the hands-on criterion for recognizing pushouts (tensor products) without computing the
tensor product: it is used to identify fiber products of schemes, base changes, and tensor-product
decompositions from their mapping properties.
