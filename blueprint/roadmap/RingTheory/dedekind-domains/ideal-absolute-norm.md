# Absolute norm of an ideal

Let $S$ be a Dedekind domain. The absolute norm of ideals of $S$ is the map $I \mapsto N(I)$ from the ideals of $S$ to
the natural numbers with $N(0) = 0$ and, for $I \neq 0$, $N(I)$ the number of elements of $S/I$, taken to be $0$
if $S/I$ is infinite. It is a homomorphism of monoids with zero: $N(IJ) = N(I) N(J)$ for all ideals $I$ and $J$ of
$S$, and $N(S) = 1$.

## Sources

- F. Keune, *Number Fields*, Radboud University Press, Nijmegen (2023, version 2023-01), open access,
  <https://doi.org/10.54195/IPVU4488>: Definition 3.18 ($N(\mathfrak{a}) = \#(\mathcal{O}_K / \mathfrak{a})$ for a nonzero ideal
  $\mathfrak{a}$ of the ring of integers of a number field) and Proposition 3.19 ($N(\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b}) = N(\mathfrak{a}) N(\mathfrak{b})$).
