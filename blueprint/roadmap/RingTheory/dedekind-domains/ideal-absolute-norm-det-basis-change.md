# The absolute norm of an ideal as a determinant

Let $S$ be a Dedekind domain that is a free $\mathbb{Z}$-module of finite rank, and for an ideal $I$ of $S$ let
$N(I)$ be its absolute norm, the number of elements of $S/I$ for $I \neq 0$ and $0$ for $I = 0$. Let
$(b_i)_{i \in \iota}$ be a $\mathbb{Z}$-basis of $S$ indexed by a finite set $\iota$, and let $I$ be an ideal of $S$
with a $\mathbb{Z}$-basis $(c_j)_{j \in \iota}$ indexed by the same set. Then $N(I) = |\det T|$, where $T$ is the
matrix whose column $j$ holds the coordinates of $c_j$ in the basis $(b_i)$.

## Sources

- F. Keune, *Number Fields*, Radboud University Press, Nijmegen (2023, version 2023-01), open access,
  <https://doi.org/10.54195/IPVU4488>: Lemma 1.40 ($(A : B) = |\det(T)|$ for a subgroup $B$ of the same rank of a free
  abelian group $A$ of finite rank and the transition matrix $T$ between bases) and Definition 3.18 (the norm of a
  nonzero ideal is its index).
