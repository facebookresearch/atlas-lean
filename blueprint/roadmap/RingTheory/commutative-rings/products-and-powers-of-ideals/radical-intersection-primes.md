# The radical of an ideal is the intersection of the prime ideals containing it

In a commutative ring, the radical of an ideal is the intersection of all prime ideals containing it. Mathlib proves this more generally for commutative semirings: let $R$ be a commutative semiring with identity and $I$ an ideal of $R$, and let $\sqrt{I}$ be the radical of $I$, the ideal of elements of $R$ some power of which lies in $I$. Then $\sqrt{I}$ is the intersection of all prime ideals $P$ of $R$ with $I \subseteq P$, where an ideal $P$ is *prime* if $P \neq R$ and $xy \in P$ implies $x \in P$ or $y \in P$. When $I = R$ no prime ideal contains $I$, and the intersection of the empty family of ideals is read as $R$ itself.
