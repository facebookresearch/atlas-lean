# Integral algebra over a commutative ring: every element is a root of a monic polynomial

An extension of commutative rings is integral when each element of the larger ring satisfies a monic polynomial over the smaller ring. Mathlib defines integrality more generally for algebras: let $R$ be a commutative ring with identity and let $A$ be a ring with identity, not necessarily commutative, that is an $R$-algebra, meaning that it comes with a ring homomorphism $\varphi \colon R \to A$ whose image commutes with every element of $A$, and $r \cdot a = \varphi(r) a$; the map $\varphi$ need not be injective. Then $A$ is *integral over* $R$ if every $a \in A$ is a root of a monic polynomial with coefficients in $R$, evaluated through $\varphi$: there are $n \ge 0$ and $c_0, \dots, c_{n-1} \in R$ with $a^n + \varphi(c_{n-1}) a^{n-1} + \cdots + \varphi(c_1) a + \varphi(c_0) = 0$. A monic polynomial of degree $0$ is the constant $1$, which vanishes only in the zero ring, so one may always take $n \ge 1$. For a subring $R$ of a commutative ring $S$ containing the identity of $S$, with $\varphi$ the inclusion, this says exactly that every element of $S$ is integral over $R$, that is, that $S$ is integral over $R$.
