# Transitivity of integrality

If $R \subseteq S \subseteq T$ are commutative rings with a common identity, $S$ is integral over $R$ and $T$ is integral over $S$, then $T$ is integral over $R$: every element of $T$ is a root of a monic polynomial with coefficients in $R$. Mathlib proves this for towers of algebras, in which the structure maps need not be injective. Let $R$ and $A$ be commutative rings and $B$ a ring, not necessarily commutative. Suppose that $A$ is an $R$-algebra and that $B$ is both an $A$-algebra and an $R$-algebra, through ring homomorphisms $R \to A$, $A \to B$ and $R \to B$ whose images in $B$ commute with every element of $B$. Suppose also that the tower is compatible: $(r \cdot a) \cdot b = r \cdot (a \cdot b)$ for all $r \in R$, $a \in A$ and $b \in B$, which says that $R \to B$ is the composite $R \to A \to B$. An element $x$ of an $R$-algebra is *integral* over $R$ if $p(x) = 0$ for some monic polynomial $p$ with coefficients in $R$, the coefficients acting through the structure map. If every element of $A$ is integral over $R$ and every element of $B$ is integral over $A$, then every element of $B$ is integral over $R$. Since the structure maps need not be injective, $R$ and $A$ need not be subrings of $B$. For unital inclusions $R \subseteq S \subseteq T$ of commutative rings, take $A = S$, $B = T$ and the inclusion maps, which form a compatible tower; this gives the familiar statement.
