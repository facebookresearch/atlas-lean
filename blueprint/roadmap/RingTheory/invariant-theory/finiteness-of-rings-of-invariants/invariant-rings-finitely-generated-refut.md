# Rings of invariants of linear algebraic groups need not be finitely generated

It is not the case that for every $n$ and every Zariski closed subgroup $G \le \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{C})$ (a linear algebraic group acting linearly), the ring of invariants $\mathbb{C}[x_1, \dots, x_n]^G = \{f : f(gx) = f(x) \text{ for all } g \in G\}$ is a finitely generated $\mathbb{C}$-algebra. Hilbert's fourteenth problem has a negative answer: Nagata gave the first counterexample, and Totaro gives explicit linear representations of $G_a^3$ on $\mathbb{C}^{18}$, of $G_a^4$ on $\mathbb{C}^{16}$ and of $G_a^6$ on $\mathbb{C}^{18}$ whose rings of invariants are not finitely generated (the image of such a representation is a Zariski closed subgroup with the same invariants).
