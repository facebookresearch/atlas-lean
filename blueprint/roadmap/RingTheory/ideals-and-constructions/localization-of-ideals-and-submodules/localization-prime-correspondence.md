# Prime ideals of a localization correspond to the prime ideals disjoint from the inverted set

Let $R$ be a commutative semiring, for instance a commutative ring with identity, let $S \subseteq R$ be a multiplicatively closed subset, so that $1 \in S$ and $st \in S$ whenever $s, t \in S$, and let $A$ be a localization of $R$ at $S$, such as $S^{-1}R$, with canonical map $\iota \colon R \to A$. Then contraction $Q \mapsto \iota^{-1}(Q)$ and extension $P \mapsto PA$, the ideal of $A$ generated by $\iota(P)$, are mutually inverse bijections between the set of prime ideals $Q$ of $A$ and the set of prime ideals $P$ of $R$ with $P \cap S = \varnothing$. Both maps preserve inclusion, so the bijection is an isomorphism of partially ordered sets under inclusion.
