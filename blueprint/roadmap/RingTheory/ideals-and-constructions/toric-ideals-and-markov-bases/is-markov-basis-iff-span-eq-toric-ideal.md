# Fundamental theorem of Markov bases

Let $X$ be a set of cells, possibly infinite, and let $T$ assign to each cell $x$ a finitely
supported exponent vector $T(x) \in \mathbb{N}^{(J)}$, for a set $J$ of parameters. Tables of counts
are finitely supported maps $X \to \mathbb{N}$, moves are finitely supported maps
$X \to \mathbb{Z}$, and the sufficient statistic of a table $a$ is $T(a) = \sum_x a(x)\, T(x)$. For
a field $k$, $k[X]$ is the polynomial ring in the variables $x \in X$, $x^a = \prod_x x^{a(x)}$ for
a table $a$, and the toric ideal $I_T$ is the kernel of the monomial map
$k[X] \to k[t_j : j \in J]$, $x \mapsto t^{T(x)}$. For every field $k$, a family of moves
$(f_i)_{i \in I}$ is a Markov basis for $T$ (each $f_i$ lies in the lattice
$\sum_x f_i(x)\, T(x) = 0$, and any two tables with the same sufficient statistic are joined by
steps $\pm f_i$ that keep all counts nonnegative) if and only if the binomials
$x^{f_i^+} - x^{f_i^-}$ generate the toric ideal $I_T$.

This is Theorem 3.1 of Diaconis and Sturmfels. It turns the search for moves of a Metropolis chain
on the fibre of an exact conditional test into the computation of a generating set, in practice a
Gröbner basis, of a toric ideal.

The direction from Markov bases to generation telescopes $x^a - x^b$ along a path of moves, using
that $I_T$ is generated by binomials. For the converse, the linear map $x^a \mapsto [a]$ into the
monoid algebra of the connectivity classes of tables kills the ideal generated by the move
binomials. The paper assumes $X$ finite, $T(x) \ne 0$ and finitely many moves; the statement holds
without these assumptions, for finitely supported tables and moves.
