# Over a Noetherian ring, the annihilator of a nonzero element lies in an associated prime

Let $R$ be a Noetherian commutative semiring, for instance a Noetherian commutative ring with identity, and let $M$ be an $R$-module. A prime ideal $P$ of $R$ is an *associated prime* of $M$ if $P$ is the radical of the annihilator $\operatorname{ann}(y) = \{r \in R : ry = 0\}$ of some $y \in M$; since $R$ is Noetherian, the associated primes of $M$ are exactly the prime ideals of the form $\operatorname{ann}(y)$ with $y \in M$. Then for every $x \in M$ with $x \neq 0$, the annihilator $\operatorname{ann}(x)$ is contained in an associated prime of $M$. Consequently, if $M \neq 0$, then $M$ has at least one associated prime. Moreover, if $x \neq 0$ and $\operatorname{ann}(x)$ is maximal among the annihilators of nonzero elements of $M$, then $\operatorname{ann}(x)$ is an associated prime: the associated prime $P \supseteq \operatorname{ann}(x)$ equals $\operatorname{ann}(y)$ for some $y \in M$, with $y \neq 0$ because $P \neq R$, and maximality gives $\operatorname{ann}(x) = P$. In general the containing prime need not equal $\operatorname{ann}(x)$: for $M = \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$ over $\mathbb{Z}$ and $x = 1$, $\operatorname{ann}(x) = 6\mathbb{Z}$ is not prime.
