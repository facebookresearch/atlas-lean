# A module is Noetherian iff every increasing chain of submodules stabilizes

A commutative ring is Noetherian, in the sense that every ideal is finitely generated, exactly when every ascending sequence of ideals eventually stops growing. Mathlib proves this more generally for modules over a semiring: let $R$ be a semiring with identity, not necessarily commutative, and let $M$ be an $R$-module, with $M$ a commutative monoid under addition. Then every increasing sequence $N_0 \subseteq N_1 \subseteq N_2 \subseteq \cdots$ of submodules of $M$ indexed by the natural numbers is eventually constant, that is, there is an $n$ with $N_m = N_n$ for all $m \ge n$, if and only if $M$ is *Noetherian*, meaning that every submodule of $M$ is finitely generated, that is, spanned by a finite subset of $M$. This condition concerns all submodules of $M$ and is stronger than $M$ itself being finitely generated. Taking $R$ commutative and $M = R$, whose submodules are exactly the ideals of $R$, gives the statement for rings.
