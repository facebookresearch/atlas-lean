# Artinian ring

A commutative ring $R$ with identity is Artinian when it satisfies the descending chain condition on ideals: every sequence $I_1 \supseteq I_2 \supseteq I_3 \supseteq \cdots$ of ideals of $R$ is eventually constant. Mathlib defines a semiring $R$ with identity, not necessarily commutative, to be Artinian when it is Artinian as a left module over itself, and an $R$-module $M$ to be Artinian when strict inclusion of submodules of $M$ is a well-founded relation; the submodules of $R$ over itself are its left ideals, which for a commutative ring are its ideals, and no statement about right ideals is made. Mathlib proves, for every module $M$ over a semiring, that $M$ is Artinian exactly when every sequence $N_1 \supseteq N_2 \supseteq \cdots$ of submodules of $M$ indexed by the natural numbers is eventually constant, the sequences being decreasing for inclusion but allowed to repeat terms; taking for $M$ a commutative ring $R$ over itself gives the descending chain condition on ideals.
