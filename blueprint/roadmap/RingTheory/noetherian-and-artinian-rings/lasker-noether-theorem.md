# Lasker–Noether theorem: primary decomposition in a Noetherian module

In a Noetherian commutative ring, every proper ideal is a finite intersection of primary ideals. Mathlib proves this more generally for submodules of a Noetherian module: let $R$ be a commutative ring with identity and $M$ an $R$-module that is Noetherian, meaning that every submodule of $M$ is finitely generated; then for every submodule $N$ of $M$ there is a finite set $\{Q_1, \dots, Q_n\}$ of primary submodules of $M$ with $N = Q_1 \cap \cdots \cap Q_n$. Here a submodule $Q$ of $M$ is *primary* if $Q \neq M$ and, whenever $r \in R$ and $x \in M$ satisfy $rx \in Q$, either $x \in Q$ or $r^k M \subseteq Q$ for some natural number $k$. The intersection of the empty set of submodules is $M$, so $N = M$ is the case $n = 0$, while $n \ge 1$ whenever $N \neq M$. No uniqueness or minimality of the decomposition is asserted. For $M = R$ the submodules are the ideals, the module $R$ is Noetherian exactly when $R$ is a Noetherian ring, and a primary submodule is a primary ideal: an ideal $Q \neq R$ such that $xy \in Q$ implies $x \in Q$ or $y^k \in Q$ for some natural number $k$.
