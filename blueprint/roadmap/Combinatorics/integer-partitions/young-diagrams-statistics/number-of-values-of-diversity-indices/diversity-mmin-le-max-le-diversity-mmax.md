# Bounds on the maximal frequency in terms of homozygosity

Let $I \ge 2$ be a natural number and let $p = (p_1, \dots, p_I)$ be a probability vector: real numbers $p_i \ge 0$ with $\sum_i p_i = 1$, at least two of them non-zero. Let $J = \sum_i p_i^2$ and let $M = \max_i p_i$. Then

$$M_{\min}(J) \le M \le M_{\max}(I, J),$$

where $M_{\min}(J) = \frac{1}{c}\left(1 + \sqrt{(cJ - 1)/(c - 1)}\right)$ with $c = \lceil 1/J \rceil$, and $M_{\max}(I, J) = \frac{1}{I}\left(1 + \sqrt{(IJ - 1)(I - 1)}\right)$.

## Sources

- Bradley K. Moon and Noah A. Rosenberg, "Integer Sequences for Diversity Statistics", Journal of Integer Sequences 29 (2026), Article 26.1.5, Proposition 1 (quoting Rosenberg, Theorem 2.7).
