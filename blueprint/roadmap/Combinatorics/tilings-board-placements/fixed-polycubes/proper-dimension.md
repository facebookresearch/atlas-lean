# Proper dimension

The proper dimension of a finite set $S$ of lattice cells in
$\mathbb{Z}^d$ is the number of axes $k$ on which $S$ varies, that is, for which there are
cells $x, y \in S$ with $x_k \ne y_k$.

## Sources

- Sebastian Luther and Stephan Mertens, *The Perimeter of Proper Polycubes*, Journal of Integer Sequences 20 (2017), Article 17.9.5, Section 1: a polycube that spans $i$ dimensions is proper in $i$ dimensions, <https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/journals/JIS/VOL20/Mertens/mert4.pdf>.
- Andrei Asinowski, Gill Barequet, Ronnie Barequet, and Günter Rote, *Proper n-Cell Polycubes in n − 3 Dimensions*, Journal of Integer Sequences 15 (2012), Article 12.8.4, Section 1: a polycube is proper in $d$ dimensions when the convex hull of the centers of its cells is $d$-dimensional, <https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/journals/JIS/VOL15/Barequet/barequet2.pdf>. For a polycube, a nonempty face-connected set of cells of $\mathbb{Z}^d$, this dimension is the number of coordinates that vary.
