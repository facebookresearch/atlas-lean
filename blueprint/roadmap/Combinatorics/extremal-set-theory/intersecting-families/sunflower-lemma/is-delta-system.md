# Δ-system (sunflower)

A finite family $S$ of finite sets is a Δ-system, or sunflower, when all pairwise intersections of distinct members are equal to one set $Y$, the kernel: $A \cap B = Y$ for all distinct $A, B \in S$. The members are the petals. Erdős and Rado called such families Δ-systems; the name sunflower is due to Deza and Frankl.

This is the kernel form, for finite families of finite sets with no prescribed number of petals; the [sunflower](is-sunflower.md) of the Alweiss–Lovett–Wu–Zhang statement is the form for families of arbitrary sets with exactly $r$ petals. For a family with at least two members the two notions agree, since the common pairwise intersection is then the intersection of all members.
