# Dilworth's theorem

Let $\alpha$ be a finite partially ordered set. The maximum antichain
size of $\alpha$ equals its minimum chain cover size: the largest number of elements of an
antichain in $\alpha$ equals the smallest number of chains whose union is $\alpha$.

## Background

- [Mirsky's theorem](../mirsky-s-theorem/mirsky-dual.md): the dual statement, with chains and
  antichains exchanged, which has a much shorter proof.

## Sources

- R. P. Dilworth, A decomposition theorem for partially ordered sets, Annals of Mathematics 51 (1950), 161-166, DOI 10.2307/1969503.
- R. P. Stanley, *Enumerative Combinatorics*, vol. 1, 2nd ed., author's draft of 15 July 2011, https://math.mit.edu/~rstan/ec/ec1.pdf, Chapter 3, Exercise 77(d), which derives it from the Greene–Kleitman theorem that the
  chain and antichain partitions of a finite poset are conjugate (Exercise 77(a)–(c)).
