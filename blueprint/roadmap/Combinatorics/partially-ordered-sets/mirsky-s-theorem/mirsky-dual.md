# Mirsky's theorem

For every finite partially ordered set $\alpha$, the maximum chain size of
$\alpha$ equals its minimum antichain cover size: the largest cardinality of a chain in $\alpha$
equals the smallest number of antichains whose union is $\alpha$.

## Background

- [Dilworth's theorem](../dilworth-s-theorem/dilworth-decomposition.md): the same statement with
  chains and antichains exchanged. Stanley remarks that this dual is much easier to prove: the
  successive sets of minimal elements give a cover by as many antichains as the longest chain has
  elements.
