# Alternating sum of $\binom{n}{k} 2^{2k}/(k+1)$

For every natural number $n$, the
following identity holds in $\mathbb{Q}$:

$$\sum_{k=0}^{n} (-1)^{n-k} \binom{n}{k} \frac{2^{2k}}{k+1}
= \sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/2 \rfloor} \binom{n}{2k} \frac{2^{2k}}{2k+1}.$$
