# Erdős–Fuchs theorem

It is not the case that there are a set $A$ of natural numbers and reals $c > 0$ and $C$ with $\bigl|\sum_{n=0}^{N} r_A(n) - cN\bigr| \le C$ for every natural number $N$, where $r_A(n)$ is the number of ordered pairs $(a, b) \in A \times A$ with $a + b = n$. Equivalently, for every set $A$ of natural numbers and every $c > 0$, $\sum_{n \le N} r_A(n) - cN$ is unbounded.

This refutes [Erdős Problem 763 (Erdős–Turán question on representation counts)](pair-representation-sum-linear.md), the statement recorded as a proposition; the refutation is due to Erdős and Fuchs (1956), cited under Sources.
