# Restricted sums from an additive basis can have unbounded gaps

It is not the case that for some $k \ge 3$, every set $A$ of natural numbers such that every sufficiently large integer is a sum of at most $k$ elements of $A$ (repetitions allowed) has the property that the integers which are sums of at most $k$ distinct elements of $A$ have bounded gaps: there is $C$ such that every interval $[x, x + C]$ contains one.

This refutes [Erdős Problem 880 (Burr–Erdős question on restricted sums from a basis)](restricted-sums-bounded-gaps-high-order.md), the statement recorded as a proposition; the refutation is due to Hegyvári, Hennecart and Plagne (2007), cited under Sources.

## Sources
