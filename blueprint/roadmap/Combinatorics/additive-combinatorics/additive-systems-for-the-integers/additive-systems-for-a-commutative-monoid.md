# Additive systems for a commutative monoid

An additive system for a commutative monoid is a collection of subsets, each containing $0$ and another element, giving every element of the monoid a unique representation as a sum with one term from each set, almost all terms $0$.

Let $M$ be a commutative monoid, written additively, and let $\mathcal A=\{A_0,A_1,\dots\}$ be a finite or countable collection of subsets of $M$, each containing $0$ and at least one other element. The *sumset* of $\mathcal A$ is the set of sums $a_0+a_1+\cdots$ with $a_i\in A_i$ and all but finitely many $a_i$ equal to $0$. The sumset is *direct* if each of its elements has only one such representation, and $\mathcal A$ is an *additive system for $M$* if its sumset is direct and equal to $M$.

## Expansion

The decimal digit sets $\{0,1,\dots,9\}, 10\{0,\dots,9\}, 100\{0,\dots,9\},\dots$ form an additive system for $\mathbb N_0$, and de Bruijn's theorem describes all additive systems for $\mathbb N_0$ through mixed-radix number systems and their contractions.
