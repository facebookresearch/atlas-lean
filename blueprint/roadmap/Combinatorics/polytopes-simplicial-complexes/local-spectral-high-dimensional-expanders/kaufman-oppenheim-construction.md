# Kaufman–Oppenheim elementary bounded-degree local spectral expanders

For every $d \ge 2$ and every $\lambda > 0$ there is $q_0(d, \lambda)$ such that for every prime power $q \ge q_0$ there is an infinite family of groups $G_s = \mathrm{EL}_{d+1}\bigl(\mathbb{F}_q[t]/(t^s)\bigr)$, $s \to \infty$, generated by elementary matrices, and subgroups $K_0, \dots, K_d \le G_s$ such that the coset complexes $\mathcal{CC}(G_s; K_0, \dots, K_d)$ form a family of $d$-dimensional, $(d+1)$-partite one-sided $\lambda$-local spectral expanders whose vertex degrees are bounded by a function of $q$ and $d$ only. The $1$-skeletons give explicit bounded-degree expander graph families whose second eigenvalue is $O(1/\sqrt{q})$.

## Proof sketch

All links of faces of codimension at least $2$ are again coset complexes of subgroups generated by unipotent elementary matrices, so by symmetry only links of $(d-2)$-faces need be analysed directly. These are bipartite graphs isomorphic to incidence graphs of points versus lines in a polynomial-ring analogue of projective space, whose second eigenvalue is $O(1/\sqrt{q})$ by an explicit computation with characters. Oppenheim's trickling-down theorem then propagates the bound to all lower links, and connectivity of all links is checked combinatorially.
