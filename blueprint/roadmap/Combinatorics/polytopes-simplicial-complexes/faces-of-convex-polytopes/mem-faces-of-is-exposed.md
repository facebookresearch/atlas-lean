# A face of a face of a polytope is a face of the polytope

Let $s$ be finite, $F$ a face of $P = \operatorname{conv} s$, and $G$ a face of $F$ (an exposed
subset of $F$). Then $G$ is a face of $P$. Together with the converse (a face of $P$ contained in $F$ is a face of $F$), the faces of $F$ are exactly the faces of $P$ contained in $F$, so the face lattice of $F$ is the interval $[\emptyset, F]$ of the face lattice of $P$.

For general convex sets the corresponding statement about exposed subsets is false. For polytopes it holds:
if $\ell_1$ exposes $F$ in $P$ and $\ell_2$ exposes $G$ in $F$, then for $\varepsilon > 0$ small
enough the functional $\ell_1 + \varepsilon \ell_2$ is maximal on the points of $s$ exactly at
those in $G$, because $\ell_1$ has a positive gap below its maximum on the finitely many points of
$s$ outside $F$. Since $G = \operatorname{conv}(G \cap s)$, this functional exposes $G$ in $P$.
