# Balinski's theorem

Let $s$ be a nonempty finite set of points in $\mathbb{R}^n$ and let $P = \operatorname{conv} s$
have dimension $d$. Then the graph of $P$ is $d$-connected: it has more than $d$ vertices, and it
stays connected after deleting any set of fewer than $d$ vertices.

The polytope need not be full-dimensional; $d$ is the dimension of its affine hull. Nonemptiness
is needed, since the graph of the empty polytope has no vertex. For $d = 3$ this is the
3-connectivity half of the easy direction of Steinitz's theorem.
