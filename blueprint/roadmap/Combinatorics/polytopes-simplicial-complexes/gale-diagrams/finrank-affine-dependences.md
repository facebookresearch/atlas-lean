# Dimension of the space of affine dependences

If $n$ points $v_1, \dots, v_n$ affinely span a real vector space $E$ of finite dimension $d$, then
their affine dependences form a subspace of $\mathbb{R}^n$ of dimension $n - d - 1$. This is the
dimension of the space in which a Gale diagram of the configuration lives; for the vertex set of a
$d$-polytope with $n$ vertices, Gale diagrams are low-dimensional exactly when $n$ is close to
$d$. The proof is rank–nullity for the surjective affine dependence map
$\mathbb{R}^n \to E \times \mathbb{R}$.

Example: four points spanning the plane ($n = 4$, $d = 2$) have a one-dimensional space of
affine dependences. Since $E$ is affinely spanned, $n \ge d + 1$, so the natural-number
subtraction does not truncate.
