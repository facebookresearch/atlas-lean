# Mean, variance, central limit and large deviations for overlapping pattern counts in Markov texts

Let $H$ be a pattern of length $m$ with stationary probability $P(H) > 0$, and let $O_n(H)$ be the number of possibly overlapping occurrences of $H$ in a text of length $n$ generated by a stationary first-order Markov chain over a finite alphabet whose transition matrix is primitive, that is, irreducible and aperiodic, as the source assumes for its Markov model (the Bernoulli model, with all letter probabilities positive, being a special case). Régnier and Szpankowski (1998, Theorem 2.1) give the generating functions of $O_n(H)$, for exactly $r$ occurrences and bivariate, in terms of $D_H(z) = (1 - z)A_H(z) + z^mP(H)(1 + (1 - z)F(z))$, where $A_H$ is the autocorrelation polynomial of $H$ and $F$ is an explicit function of the transition matrix ($F = 0$ in the Bernoulli model). Assuming $nP(H) \to \infty$, their Theorem 2.2 gives: $E[O_n(H)] = P(H)(n - m + 1)$ for $n \ge m$; $\mathrm{Var}[O_n(H)] = c_1n + c_2 + O(r^{-n})$ for some $r > 1$, with explicit constants in terms of $A_H$, $P(H)$ and the fundamental matrix of the chain, where in the Bernoulli model $c_1 = P(H)(2A_H(1) - 1 - (2m - 1)P(H))$ and the formula is exact for $n \ge 2m - 1$; for fixed $r$, an expansion of $P(O_n = r)$ in powers of the reciprocal of the smallest root $\rho_H$ of $D_H$, which is real, positive and of modulus greater than $1$; when $c_1 > 0$, the local central limit theorem $P(O_n = r) = (2\pi c_1 n)^{-1/2}e^{-x^2/2}(1 + O(n^{-1/2}))$ for integers $r = EO_n + x\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}\,O_n}$ with $x$ bounded; and a large deviation estimate for $r = (1 + \delta)EO_n$, $\delta \ne 0$, with exponential rate given by a Legendre-type transform of the root of $1 - e^tM_H(e^\rho) = 0$.
