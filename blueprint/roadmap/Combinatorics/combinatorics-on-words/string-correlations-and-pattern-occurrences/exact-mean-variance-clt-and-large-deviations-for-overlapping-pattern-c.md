# Mean, variance, central limit and large deviations for overlapping pattern counts in Markov texts

Let $H$ be a nonempty pattern of length $m \ge 1$ with stationary probability $P(H) > 0$, and let $O_n(H)$ be the number of possibly overlapping occurrences of $H$ in a text of length $n$ generated by a stationary first-order Markov chain over a finite alphabet with at least two letters whose transition matrix is primitive, that is, irreducible and aperiodic, as the source assumes for its Markov model (the Bernoulli model, with all letter probabilities positive, being a special case). Régnier and Szpankowski (1998, Theorem 2.1) give the generating functions of $O_n(H)$, for exactly $r$ occurrences and bivariate, in terms of $D_H(z) = (1 - z)A_H(z) + z^mP(H)(1 + (1 - z)F(z))$, where $A_H(z) = \sum_k P(H_{k+1}\cdots H_m \mid H_k)\,z^{m-k}$, the sum over the lengths $k \in \{1, \dots, m\}$ with $H_1\cdots H_k = H_{m-k+1}\cdots H_m$, is the autocorrelation polynomial of $H$ weighted by the conditional probabilities of the overlap completions (Definition 2 and Eq. (4); the term $k = m$ is $1$, and in the Bernoulli model the weight is $P(H_{k+1}\cdots H_m)$), and $F$ is an explicit function of the transition matrix ($F = 0$ in the Bernoulli model). With at least two letters every letter has positive stationary probability, so $0 < P(H) < 1$. Assuming $nP(H) \to \infty$, their Theorem 2.2 gives, among other estimates: $E[O_n(H)] = P(H)(n - m + 1)$ for $n \ge m$; $\mathrm{Var}[O_n(H)] = c_1n + c_2 + O(r^{-n})$ for some $r > 1$, with explicit constants in terms of $A_H$, $P(H)$ and the fundamental matrix of the chain, where in the Bernoulli model $c_1 = P(H)(2A_H(1) - 1 - (2m - 1)P(H))$ and the formula is exact for $n \ge 2m - 1$ (for fair binary letters and $H = 00$, $A_H(1) = 3/2$ and $c_1 = 5/16$); and, in the Bernoulli model with all letter probabilities positive and $c_1 > 0$, the local central limit theorem $P(O_n = r) = (2\pi c_1 n)^{-1/2}e^{-x^2/2}(1 + O(n^{-1/2}))$ for integers $r = EO_n + x\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}\,O_n}$ with $x$ bounded. For a general primitive Markov chain the local limit needs an additional lattice hypothesis: for the chain on $\{0, 1\}$ with transition rows $(1/2, 1/2)$ and $(1, 0)$ and $H = 00$, every transition other than $00$ flips the state, so for odd $n$ the count is even exactly when the endpoints agree, with limiting probability $5/9$ rather than $1/2$. The expansion of $P(O_n = r)$ for fixed $r$ and the large deviation estimate, which rest on the dominant root of $D_H$ and on the root of $1 - e^tM_H(e^\rho) = 0$, are separate targets in the Expansion.

## Expansion

1. The model: the Markov text, the pattern, overlapping occurrences, and the weighted autocorrelation polynomial $A_H$ of Definition 2 and Eq. (4) (compare the [correlation-polynomial generating function for pattern avoidance](../../../Combinatorics/combinatorics-on-words/string-correlations-and-pattern-occurrences/correlation-polynomial-generating-function-for-pattern-avoidance-and-c.md), which provides the set of overlap lengths but not the probability weights).
2. Theorem 2.1: the language decomposition (right, minimal and left languages) and the generating functions (6)–(12).
3. The exact mean (13) and the variance (14)–(19), with the constants $E_1, E_2$ from the fundamental matrix, in the Markov and Bernoulli models.
4. The local central limit theorem (23) in the Bernoulli model with positive letter probabilities when $c_1 > 0$.
5. Separate target: the local central limit theorem for Markov texts under a lattice (span-one) hypothesis excluding parity obstructions such as the one above.
6. Separate target: the regime $r = O(1)$, equations (20)–(22), under the conditions of Theorem 2.2(ii) that $D_H$ has a unique root $\rho_H$ of smallest modulus, real, greater than $1$, and a simple zero of $D_H$. The generating function of $P(O_n(H) = r)$ for exactly $r$ occurrences then has a pole of order $r + 1$ at $\rho_H$, simple for $r = 0$ and of higher order for $r \ge 1$, so $P(O_n(H) = r)$ is $\rho_H^{-n}$ times a polynomial in $n$ of degree $r$ up to an exponentially smaller error: for the one-letter pattern $a$ in a fair binary text, $D_H(z) = 1 - z/2$ and exactly one occurrence has probability $n/2^n$, with generating function $(z/2)/(1 - z/2)^2$.
7. Separate target: the large deviation regime, equations (24)–(27), with its conditions on the root of $1 - e^tM_H(e^\rho) = 0$.
