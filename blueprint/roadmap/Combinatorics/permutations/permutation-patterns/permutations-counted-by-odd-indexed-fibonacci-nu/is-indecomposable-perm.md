# Indecomposable permutation

A permutation $\sigma$ of $\{0, 1, \dots, n-1\}$ is indecomposable when for every $k$ with $1 \le k < n$, $\sigma$ does not map $\{0, \dots, k-1\}$ into itself; equivalently, $\sigma$ is not a direct sum of two nonempty permutations.
