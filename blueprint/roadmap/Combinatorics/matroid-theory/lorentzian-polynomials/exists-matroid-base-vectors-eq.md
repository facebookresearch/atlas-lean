# Zero-one M-convex sets are matroid base sets

Let $\iota$ be finite and let $J \subseteq \mathbb{N}^\iota$ be a nonempty M-convex set of
zero-one vectors. Then there is a matroid on $\iota$, with ground set all of $\iota$, whose
bases have exactly the vectors of $J$ as indicator vectors.

For zero-one vectors the M-convex exchange is the base exchange axiom, so the supports of the
vectors of $J$ satisfy the axioms of a matroid given by its bases on a finite ground set.

This is the implication (7) ⇒ (8) of Brändén and Huh's Theorem 3.10.

- P. Brändén and J. Huh, *Lorentzian polynomials*, Ann. of Math. 192 (2020), 821–891,
