# Matroids are the zero-one M-convex sets

On a finite index set $\iota$, a nonempty set $J \subseteq \mathbb{N}^\iota$ of zero-one
vectors is M-convex if and only if it is the set of indicator vectors of the bases of a matroid
on $\iota$ whose ground set is all of $\iota$.

This is the equivalence (7) ⇔ (8) of Brändén and Huh's Theorem 3.10. The other conditions of that theorem concern Lorentzian and $c$-Rayleigh polynomials with support $J$; for matroids the Lorentzian condition is that [basis generating polynomials of matroids are Lorentzian](basis-generating-polynomial-mem-l.md).
