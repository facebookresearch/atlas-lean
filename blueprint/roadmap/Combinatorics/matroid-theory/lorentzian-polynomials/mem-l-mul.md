# Products of Lorentzian polynomials

If $f$ lies in $L^d_n$ and $g$ lies in $L^e_n$, then $fg$ lies in $L^{d+e}_n$.

This is the content of the proof of Brändén and Huh's Corollary 2.32, which, through the
equivalence of Lorentzian and strongly log-concave homogeneous polynomials (their
Theorem 2.30), answers a question of Gurvits. In two variables it recovers Liggett's theorem
that the convolution of two ultra log-concave sequences with no internal zeros is again ultra
log-concave with no internal zeros.

## Informally uses

- [Nonnegative linear changes of variables preserve Lorentzian polynomials](mem-l-comp-nonneg.md):
  $f(w)\,g(u)$ in disjoint variables is Lorentzian, and the substitution $u = w$ is a
  nonnegative linear change of variables.
