# Nonnegative linear changes of variables preserve Lorentzian polynomials

If $f(w_1, \dots, w_n)$ lies in $L^d_n$ and $A$ is an $n \times m$ matrix with nonnegative
entries, then $f(Av)$ lies in $L^d_m$, where $(Av)_i = \sum_{j} A_{ij} v_j$.

This is Brändén and Huh's Theorem 2.10. Its three special cases (splitting a variable,
rescaling a variable by $\theta \ge 0$, identifying two variables) generate all nonnegative
substitutions, and each preserves M-convexity of supports and the signature condition. It is
the basic tool of the theory: it yields closure under nonnegative directional derivatives and
products, and the specialisation $w_1 = \cdots = w_n$ that turns Lorentzian polynomials into
ultra log-concave sequences.
