# Monochromatic-solution property for disjunctive Rado problems

Let $m$ and $R$ be natural numbers, $a$ an integer and $C$ a finite set of integers. The property $\mathrm{good}(m, a, C, R)$ holds when for every $2$-coloring $\chi \colon \mathbb{N} \to \{\text{true}, \text{false}\}$ there are $c \in C$ and natural numbers $x_1, \dots, x_{m-2}, y, z \in \{1, \dots, R\}$, all of the same color as $y$ under $\chi$, with

$$x_1 + \dots + x_{m-2} + a y - z = c$$

in the integers. Only the values of $\chi$ on $\{1, \dots, R\}$ matter, and the variables need not be distinct. The number $m - 2$ of summands is truncated subtraction, so for $m \le 2$ there are no variables $x_i$. For $R = 0$ the property fails, as there are no variables to choose.

## Sources

- Srashti Dwivedi and Amitabha Tripathi, *On the Two-Color Disjunctive Rado Number for the Equations $\sum_{i=1}^{m-2} x_i + a x_{m-1} - x_m = c_j$, $j = 1, 2$*, INTEGERS 25 (2025), Article A108, Section 1 (definition of the 2-color disjunctive Rado number).
