# Monotonicity of the monochromatic-solution property in the bound

Let $m$ be a natural number, $a$ an integer, $C$ a finite set of integers and $R \le R'$ natural numbers. If every $2$-coloring of $\{1, \dots, R\}$ admits, for some $c \in C$, a monochromatic solution $x_1, \dots, x_{m-2}, y, z \in \{1, \dots, R\}$ of $x_1 + \dots + x_{m-2} + a y - z = c$, then the same holds with $R'$ in place of $R$. Hence the set of admissible bounds is closed upward, and the disjunctive Rado number is the threshold beyond which every bound is admissible.

## Sources

- Srashti Dwivedi and Amitabha Tripathi, *On the Two-Color Disjunctive Rado Number for the Equations $\sum_{i=1}^{m-2} x_i + a x_{m-1} - x_m = c_j$, $j = 1, 2$*, INTEGERS 25 (2025), Article A108, Section 1.
