# The product $s(n)\,S(n)$ is the identity

For a natural number $n$, let $s(n)$ be the $n \times n$ rational matrix with entries $s(i,j)$, the signed Stirling numbers of the first kind, and let $S(n)$ be the $n \times n$ rational matrix with entries $S(i,j)$, the Stirling numbers of the second kind, all indices running over $1, \dots, n$. Then $s(n)\, S(n) = I_n$, the $n \times n$ identity matrix.
