# $\tilde V(n)$ is the inverse of $V(n)$

For a natural number $n$, let $V(n)$ be the $n \times n$ rational matrix, with rows and columns indexed by $1, \dots, n$, whose entries are $V(n)_{i,i} = i$, $V(n)_{i,i+1} = 1$ and $0$ elsewhere, and let $\tilde V(n)$ be the upper triangular matrix with $\tilde V(n)_{i,j} = (-1)^{j-i} (i-1)!/j!$ for $j \ge i$ and $\tilde V(n)_{i,j} = 0$ for $j < i$. Then $V(n)\, \tilde V(n) = I_n$ for every natural number $n$.
