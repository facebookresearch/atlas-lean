# Stirling matrix of the first kind times a shifted Stirling matrix

For natural numbers $m$ and $n$, let $s(n)$ be the $n \times n$ rational matrix with entries $s(i,j)$, the signed Stirling numbers of the first kind, let $S^{[m]}(n)$ be the $n \times n$ rational matrix with entries $S(i+m, j)$, where $S(i,j)$ denotes the Stirling numbers of the second kind, and let $V(n)$ be the upper bidiagonal matrix with $V(n)_{i,i} = i$, $V(n)_{i,i+1} = 1$ and all other entries $0$, all indices running over $1, \dots, n$. Then

$$s(n)\, S^{[m]}(n) = V(n)^m.$$
