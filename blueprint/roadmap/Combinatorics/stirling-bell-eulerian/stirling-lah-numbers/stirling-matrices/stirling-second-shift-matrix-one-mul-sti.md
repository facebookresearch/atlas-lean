# The shifted Stirling matrix $S^{[1]}(n)$ times $\tilde V(n)$

For a natural number $n$, let $S(n)$ be the $n \times n$ rational matrix with entries $S(i,j)$, the Stirling numbers of the second kind, let $S^{[1]}(n)$ be the $n \times n$ rational matrix with entries $S(i+1, j)$, and let $\tilde V(n)$ be the upper triangular matrix with $\tilde V(n)_{i,j} = (-1)^{j-i} (i-1)!/j!$ for $j \ge i$ and $\tilde V(n)_{i,j} = 0$ for $j < i$, all indices running over $1, \dots, n$. Then

$$S^{[1]}(n)\, \tilde V(n) = S(n).$$
