# Pansiot Factor Complexity Wanted

Pansiot's classification of the factor complexity of a pure morphic sequence generated by a
non-erasing morphism.

The quotation omits two hypotheses that the `Θ` lower bounds need; both are added here:
`w` is not eventually periodic, and every letter of `A` occurs in `w`. Without the first,
`σ a = [a, b]`, `σ b = [b]` fails the `Θ(n^2)` case, since `w = a b b b ⋯` has only two
factors of each positive length. Without the second, letters absent from `w` can change the
growth class of `σ` without changing `w`.

The five cases are: everywhere-growing and quasi-uniform gives `O(n)`; polynomially
diverging gives `Θ(n log log n)`; exponentially diverging gives `Θ(n log n)`;
non-everywhere-growing with infinitely many bounded-letter factors gives `Θ(n^2)`;
otherwise the sequence is a morphic image of an everywhere-growing pure fixed point
with the same complexity up to `Θ`.
