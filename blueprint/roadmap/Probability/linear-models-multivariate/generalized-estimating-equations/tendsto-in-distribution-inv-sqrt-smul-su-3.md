# Cluster central limit theorem with sandwich covariance

Let $E$ be a finite-dimensional real inner product space. Let $U_k(\beta)$ be cluster scores on a probability space. At the true parameter $\beta_0$ suppose the $U_k(\beta_0)$ are measurable, mutually independent, mean zero and square integrable, $n^{-1}\sum_{k<n}\mathbb{E}\langle U_k(\beta_0), t\rangle^2 \to \langle Bt, t\rangle$ for every $t$, and each projected array $n^{-1/2}\langle U_k(\beta_0), t\rangle$, $k < n$, satisfies the Lindeberg condition. Write $\Sigma(A, B) = A^{-1}\frac{B + B^*}2A^{-*}$ for the sandwich covariance of a bread $A$ and a meat $B$ (with $A^{-1} = 0$ for singular $A$). Then $n^{-1/2}\sum_{k<n}(-A^{-1}U_k(\beta_0))$ converges in distribution to every $W$ with $\langle W, t\rangle \sim \mathcal N(0, \langle\Sigma(A, B)t, t\rangle)$; invertibility of $A$ is not needed.
