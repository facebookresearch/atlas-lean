# Triangular-array central limit theorem for a nonlinear cluster estimating equation

Let rows of $n$ clusters contribute $\psi_{n,i}(\beta)$ on a probability space, where at the truth the scores $\psi_{n,i}(\beta_0)$ are independent within rows, measurable, mean zero and bounded by $K m_{n,i}$ for some $K \ge 0$ ($m_{n,i}$ the cluster sizes), the row variance $B_n^2 = \sum_i\mathbb{E}\psi_{n,i}(\beta_0)^2$ is eventually positive and $KM_n/B_n \to 0$ with $M_n$ the largest cluster size. Assume $\beta \mapsto \Psi_n(\beta) = \sum_i\psi_{n,i}(\beta)$ is nonincreasing and differentiable with derivative $\Psi_n'$, $\hat\beta_n$ is almost everywhere measurable and a root with probability tending to one, $\Psi_n(\beta_0 - \varepsilon) > 0 > \Psi_n(\beta_0 + \varepsilon)$ with probability tending to one for every $\varepsilon > 0$, and for a deterministic $D_n > 0$ the slope $\Psi_n'$ is locally uniformly within $\varepsilon D_n$ of $-D_n$ with probability tending to one. Then $D_n(\hat\beta_n - \beta_0)/B_n$ converges in distribution to a standard normal variable: $\hat\beta_n$ has sandwich variance $B_n^2/D_n^2$.
