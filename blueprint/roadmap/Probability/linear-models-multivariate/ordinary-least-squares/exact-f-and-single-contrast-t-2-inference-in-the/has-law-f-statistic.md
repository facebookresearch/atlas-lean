# Exact F law for a linear hypothesis

Let $X$ be a real $m \times k$ design matrix with $X^\top X$ invertible and $k < m$, and consider the linear model $y = X\beta + \varepsilon$ with $\beta \in \mathbb{R}^k$ and a random error vector $\varepsilon$ in $\mathbb{R}^m$ on a probability space. Write $\hat\beta(y) = (X^\top X)^{-1} X^\top y$ for the ordinary least-squares estimator. Suppose $\sigma > 0$ and $\sigma^{-1}\varepsilon$ is a standard Gaussian vector (independent standard normal coordinates). Let $C$ be a real $r \times k$ matrix with $r > 0$ and $C(X^\top X)^{-1}C^\top$ invertible, and $c = C\beta$ (the null hypothesis holds). Then the $F$ statistic $F(y) = \dfrac{(C\hat\beta - c)^\top\big(C(X^\top X)^{-1}C^\top\big)^{-1}(C\hat\beta - c)/r}{\|y - X\hat\beta\|^2/(m - k)}$, $\hat\beta = \hat\beta(y)$, at $y = X\beta + \varepsilon$ has Snedecor's $F$ law with $(r, m - k)$ degrees of freedom.
