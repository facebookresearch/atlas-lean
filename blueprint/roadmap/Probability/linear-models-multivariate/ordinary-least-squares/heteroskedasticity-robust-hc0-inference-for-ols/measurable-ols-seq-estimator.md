# The least-squares estimator is measurable

Let $(x_i)_{i \ge 0}$ be measurable random vectors in $\mathbb{R}^p$ and $(y_i)_{i \ge 0}$ measurable real random variables. For every sample size $n$, the least-squares estimator $\hat\beta_n = (X_n^\top X_n)^{-1} X_n^\top Y_n$ from the first $n$ observations (where the inverse of a singular matrix is taken to be the zero matrix) is a measurable function of the outcome.
