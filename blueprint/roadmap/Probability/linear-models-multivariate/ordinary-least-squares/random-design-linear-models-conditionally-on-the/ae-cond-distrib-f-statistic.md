# Conditional F law of a linear hypothesis under a random design

Let $X$ be a random real $m \times k$ design matrix and $\varepsilon$ a random vector in $\mathbb{R}^m$ on a probability space, both almost everywhere measurable, let $\sigma > 0$, and suppose the conditional law of $\sigma^{-1}\varepsilon$ given $X$ is almost surely the standard Gaussian law $\mathcal N(0, 1)^{\otimes m}$. Let $\beta \in \mathbb{R}^k$ and $y = X\beta + \varepsilon$, and call a design $x$ full rank when $x^\top x$ is invertible. Let $k < m$, let $C$ be a real $r \times k$ matrix of rank $r > 0$, and $c = C\beta$. Then for almost every full-rank $x$, the conditional law given $X = x$ of the $F$ statistic $F = \dfrac{(C\hat\beta - c)^\top(C(X^\top X)^{-1}C^\top)^{-1}(C\hat\beta - c)/r}{\|y - X\hat\beta\|^2/(m - k)}$, is Snedecor's $F$ law with $(r, m - k)$ degrees of freedom.
