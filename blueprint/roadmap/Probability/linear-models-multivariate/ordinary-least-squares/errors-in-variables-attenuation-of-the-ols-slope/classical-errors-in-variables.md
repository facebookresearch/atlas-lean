# Classical errors-in-variables hypotheses

Let $(\Omega, \mu)$ be a measure space and $\xi_i, u_i, \varepsilon_i$ ($i \ge 0$) real random variables: a latent regressor $\xi$, a measurement error $u$ and an equation error $\varepsilon$, observed through $x_i = \xi_i + u_i$ and $y_i = \beta_0 + \beta\,\xi_i + \varepsilon_i$. They satisfy the classical errors-in-variables hypotheses when

- the triples $(\xi_i, u_i, \varepsilon_i)$ are pairwise independent across $i$;
- the triples are identically distributed;
- $\xi_0$, $u_0$ and $\varepsilon_0$ are square integrable;
- $\xi_0$, $u_0$ and $\varepsilon_0$ are pairwise uncorrelated.

No mean-zero condition is imposed.
