# Restricted log-likelihood as a log density

Let $A$ be a real $r \times n$ matrix, $V$ a real $n \times n$ matrix and $y \in \mathbb{R}^n$. The restricted log-likelihood $\ell_A(V; y)$ equals $\log \varphi_{A V A^\top}(A y)$, the logarithm of the centered Gaussian density formula with covariance $S = A V A^\top$ at the contrast vector $A y$, with the conventions $\sqrt{t} = 0$ for $t < 0$, $1/0 = 0$, $S^{-1} = 0$ for singular $S$ and $\log 0 = 0$. When $S = A V A^\top$ is positive definite (for example when $V$ is positive definite and $A A^\top$ is invertible), $\varphi_S$ is the Lebesgue density of $N(0, S)$, which is the law of the contrast $A y$ when $y \sim N(X\beta, V)$ and $A$ is an error contrast for $X$ ([density of the error contrast](has-law-error-contrast-with-density.md)). For other $A$ and $V$ the expression is only defined by the conventions above and is not a log density: with $A = 0$ and $V = I$, the contrast $A y = 0$ has a point-mass law, which has no Lebesgue density.
