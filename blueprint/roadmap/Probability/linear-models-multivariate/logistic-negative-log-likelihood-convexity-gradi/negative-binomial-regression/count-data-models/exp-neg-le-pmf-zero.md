# Zero counts are at least as likely as under the Poisson law

A count law is either the Poisson law or the negative binomial law with shape $\theta$ (valid when $\theta > 0$); at mean $\mu \ge 0$ it has mass function $p_\mu(k)$, Poisson $e^{-\mu}\mu^k/k!$ or negative binomial $\frac{\Gamma(k + \theta)}{k!\,\Gamma(\theta)}\big(\frac{\theta}{\theta + \mu}\big)^\theta\big(\frac{\mu}{\theta + \mu}\big)^k$, and dispersion $\alpha$ ($0$ for Poisson, $1/\theta$ for the negative binomial). For a valid law and $\mu \ge 0$, $p_\mu(0) \ge e^{-\mu}$, with equality for the Poisson law.
