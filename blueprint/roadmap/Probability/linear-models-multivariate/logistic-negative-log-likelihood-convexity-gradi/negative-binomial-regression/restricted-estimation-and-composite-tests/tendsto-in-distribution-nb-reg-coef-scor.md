# Central limit theorem for the coefficient score under the null

Consider negative binomial regression with joint parameter $p = (\beta, \theta)$, coefficients $\beta$ and shape $\theta$: an observation $z = (x, y)$ (covariate row $x$, count $y$) has density $f_p(z) = \mathrm{NB}(y; \theta, e^{x^\top\beta})$, with closed-form score $s_p(z)$ and observed information $J_p(z)$. Let $z_i$ be mutually independent random-design negative binomial regression observations with true parameter $(\beta_0, \theta_0)$, $\theta_0 > 0$, and square-integrable score with mean zero. Then the scaled coefficient score at the truth converges in distribution to the coefficient block of any $Y$ with $\langle Y, t\rangle \sim \mathcal N(0, \langle I(p_0)t, t\rangle)$, that is, to $\mathcal N(0, B)$ with $B$ the coefficient block of $I(p_0)$.
