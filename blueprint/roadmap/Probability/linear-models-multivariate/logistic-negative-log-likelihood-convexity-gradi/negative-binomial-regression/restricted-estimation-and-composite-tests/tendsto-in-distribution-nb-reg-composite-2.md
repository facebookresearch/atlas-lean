# Composite likelihood-ratio test for negative binomial coefficients

Consider negative binomial regression with joint parameter $p = (\beta, \theta)$, coefficients $\beta$ and shape $\theta$: an observation $z = (x, y)$ (covariate row $x$, count $y$) has density $f_p(z) = \mathrm{NB}(y; \theta, e^{x^\top\beta})$, with closed-form score $s_p(z)$ and observed information $J_p(z)$. Let $z_i$ be mutually independent random-design observations (covariates of an s-finite law) with true parameter $p_0 = (\beta_0, \theta_0)$, $\theta_0 > 0$, a square-integrable centred score, integrable observed information with mean $I = I(p_0)$ positive definite (at least one coefficient). With $S_n = n^{-1/2}\sum_{i<n}s_{p_0}(z_i)$, $D = \langle I(0,1), (0,1)\rangle$ and $\ell_n$ the log-likelihood, assume $2(\ell_n(\hat p_n) - \ell_n(p_0)) - \langle I^{-1}S_n, S_n\rangle \to 0$ and $2(\ell_n(\beta_0, \tilde\theta_n) - \ell_n(p_0)) - (S_n)_\theta^2/D \to 0$ in probability, and that the statistic is almost everywhere measurable. Then $2(\ell_n(\hat p_n) - \ell_n(\beta_0, \tilde\theta_n))$ converges in distribution to the chi-square law with $|\kappa|$ degrees of freedom.
