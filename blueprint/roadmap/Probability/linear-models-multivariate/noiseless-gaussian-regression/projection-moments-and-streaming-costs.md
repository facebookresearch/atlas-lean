# Gaussian and Haar projection moments, cap domination, and streaming costs

Let $A x=(\langle A_i,x\rangle)_i$ in Euclidean coordinates. For a finite ambient measure $\mu$, use the joint lower density $f_\mu(A,y)=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mu\{x:\|Ax-y\|_2\le1/(n+1)\}/\operatorname{Vol}(B_{1/(n+1)})$ in the label dimension, with closed balls. Mass control $K,\beta$ means $\mu(\overline B(z,r))\le Kr^\beta$ for all ambient centers and positive radii. For a fixed orthonormal $k$-frame, Haar frame law is its image under uniform orthogonal rotation. For Gaussian $X$ and each supplied orthonormal frame $u$, form the positive semidefinite square root $R_X$ of the real Gram matrix by continuous functional calculus. The polar rows are $R_X^{-1}X$ if the rows of $X$ are linearly independent, and exactly $u$ otherwise. In the normalized moment, the version is the extended volume of the closed unit ball times the lower density at these polar rows. Its pointwise vanishing claim is for every $X$, including the fallback, and the density identity for almost every $X$ uses scaled unrestricted Lebesgue measure; the moment integrates only its restriction to the unit ball. Sphere probability in dimension $d$ is denoted $\sigma$; weight it by measurable $0\le h\le1$, optionally restricting to a measurable set. All densities and the moments of them are extended nonnegative.

* **Integrated frame moment.** One $C\ge1$ works for $k\ge1,q\ge2,k+q\le d$, $0<\beta\le d$, $1\le\beta-k-(q-2)$, $K\ge0$, $R>0$, and any finite measure of mass control $K,\beta$ supported in $\overline B(z,R)$. If its pushforward is absolutely continuous by Lebesgue measure for Haar-almost every frame, then $f_\mu$ is jointly measurable, is its density for almost every frame, and is zero at every orthonormal frame and label with $\|y-Az\|>R$. The $q$th moment integrated over frames and Lebesgue labels, raised to $1/q$, is at most $C^d(C\sqrt d)^{k(1-1/q)}KR^{\beta-k(1-1/q)}$.
* **Polar normalized moment.** For each $D>0$ there is $C_D>0$ working for $d\ge32$, $k=\lfloor d/16\rfloor$, $\beta=(d-1)/2$, finite mass at most one supported in the closed unit ball and mass control $D^d,\beta$. Assume absolute continuity at almost every Gaussian polar frame. The version $\operatorname{Vol}(B_1)f_\mu(A,y)$ is jointly measurable, is the density against Lebesgue divided by $\operatorname{Vol}(B_1)$ at almost every $X$, and vanishes for $\|y\|>1$ at every $X$. Its integrated $k$th moment under Gaussian $X$ and uniform unit-ball label is at most $e^{C_Ddk}$.
* **Sphere at all radii.** One $C\ge1$ works for $d\ge16$, $k=\lfloor d/4\rfloor$, $\beta=d-1-k$, weight $0\le h\le1$ of mass control $K,\beta$, and every ambient center $z$ and radius $R>0$. For $\mu=(h\sigma)|_{\overline B(z,R)}$, $f_\mu$ is jointly measurable, represents its projected density at every orthonormal frame and is zero for $\|y-Az\|>R$. Its integrated moment bound is the first bound with $q=k$, without the extra absolute-continuity input.
* **Positive cap domination.** Fix $d\ge32$, $k=\lfloor d/16\rfloor$, $\alpha=(d-1)/2$, a unit center $c$, and $0<r\le2$. For measurable nonnegative $K_j(X,y)$ ($j<N$) with sum at most one, let $p_j=\mathbb E_{X,U}K_j(X,Xc+r(XX^{\mathsf T})^{1/2}U)$ for Gaussian rows and a uniform unit-ball $U$ of label dimension. Then $\sum_jp_j\le1$. For each $j$, the cap-normalized sphere measure on $\overline B(c,r)$ weighted by $s\mapsto\mathbb E_X K_j(X,Xs)$ is dominated by a finite positive countable mixture of normalized caps with unit centers $c_i$, radii $0<r_i\le r$, and weights $a_i\ge0$ such that $\sum_i a_i(r/r_i)^\alpha\le e^{Cd}p_j^{1-1/k}$ for one $C>0$. The dictionary is countable and contains the parent cap with its exact center and radius. The scale costs $a_i(r/r_i)^\alpha$ are summable and their sum has the stated bound; the dominating measure itself has finite total mass. The dictionary has finitely many indices with $(r/r_i)^\alpha$ at most any given real bound, and for every unit $s$ and $0<t\le1/4$ contains some $i$ with $4rt\le r_i<8rt$ and $\|c_i-s\|\le r_i/2$.
* **Riesz fixed-offset bound.** One $C\ge1$ works for $d\ge16$, $k=\lfloor d/8\rfloor$, $\alpha=d/2$, $\mu=(h\sigma)|_F$, $F\subseteq\overline B(z,R)$ measurable, $R>0$. The potential $P_\alpha(h)=\sup_a\int h(s)\|s-a\|^{-\alpha}\,d\sigma$ is finite. The lower density is measurable and gives the correct label pushforward for each full-rank array. For every fixed label offset $b$, $[\mathbb E_X f_\mu(X,Xz+b)^k]^{1/k}\le C^dP_\alpha(h)R^{\alpha-k}$. It is zero whenever $\|y-Az\|>\|A\|_{2\to2}R$, for every array.
* **Block mass control.** For $d\ge16$, $k=\lfloor d/4\rfloor$, $\beta=d-1-k$, measurable $0\le f_j\le1$ with each $f_j\sigma$ of mass control $K,\beta$, and measurable nonnegative $Q_j$ with sum at most one, the weight $s\mapsto\mathbb E_X\sum_j Q_j(X,Xs)f_j(s)$ gives mass control $C^dN^{1/k}K,\beta$ for one $C\ge1$.

For the streaming clauses the learner has $2^M$ states, a probability law of an initial stop bit and state, at each time and state a probability law of the next stop bit and state from a row and its exact label, and a unit-sphere output law depending on the terminal time and state. A stop, including one at initialization, tags the state with its time. At the horizon, assign terminal time $T$ to every still-active run. A seed $\xi$ of probability law $\rho$ selects the rules $K_\xi$. For almost every seed, the rules are completed: for every time, active state, and destination action, the transition atom probability is almost-everywhere measurable under Gaussian row times Lebesgue label.

At fixed $(\xi,s,A)$ let $Q_\xi(s,A)$ be the law of the terminal time and state and the output, formed by iterating $K_\xi$ on the exact row/label array for $s$. Let $q_\xi(s,A)$ be its mass of outputs at angle at most $\epsilon$ to $s$, and let $q_{\xi,t}(s,A)$ be its mass of the intersection of that event with terminal time $t$. Thus $q_{\xi,T}$ includes runs still active at the horizon. With $\gamma_T$ the law of the $T$ Gaussian rows, set

$$
S=\int_\Xi\!\left[\int_{(s,A)} q_\xi(s,A)\,d(\sigma\otimes\gamma_T)(s,A)\right]d\rho(\xi),\qquad
S_t=\int_\Xi\!\left[\int_{(s,A)} q_{\xi,t}(s,A)\,d(\sigma\otimes\gamma_T)(s,A)\right]d\rho(\xi).
$$

All these integrals are nonnegative lower integrals. The order is part of the definition: integrate over sphere and rows at fixed seed, then over the seed. The per-seed completion assumption does not assert joint experiment measurability or replace these expressions by a single product lower integral. The cost and precision bounds below use $0<\epsilon\le1/10$ and are valid for all indicated $M,T$ and seed laws; constants in different bullets need not agree.

* For $d\ge16$, $k=\lfloor d/4\rfloor$, $S\le C^d\epsilon^k[C^d((k+2)2^M)^{1/k}]^{\lceil T/k\rceil}$ for a universal $C\ge1$.
* For $d\ge16$, $q=\lfloor d/8\rfloor$, $S\le C_0^d\epsilon^{d/2-1}[1+C_0^d(1+\log(1/\epsilon))((T+2)2^M)^{1/q}]^{\lceil T/q\rceil}$ for a universal $C_0\ge1$.
* For $d\ge32$, $k=\lfloor d/16\rfloor$ and $\alpha=(d-1)/2$, $S_t\le e^{Cd}(\epsilon/2)^\alpha[e^{Cd}2^{M/k}]^{\lceil t/k\rceil}$ for every $t\le T$. Also $S\le(T+1)e^{Cd}(\epsilon/2)^\alpha[e^{Cd}2^{M/k}]^{\lceil T/k\rceil}$, with some universal $C>0$ for each assertion.
* For $M(d)=o(d^2)$ and sufficiently large $d$ (depending on the memory sequence), $S_t\le e^{Cd(1+\lceil t/k\rceil)}(\epsilon/2)^\alpha$ at each $t\le T$, and $S\le(T+1)e^{C_1d+C_2T-d\log(1/\epsilon)/3}$, with universal positive constants.
* At dimension at least three, for each individual probability-measure learner with completed rules, there are a probability law on the tape space and a deterministic learner on that tape. The tape is an element of $(\{0,1\}\times\mathrm{State})\times([0,1]^T\times[0,1])$: initial action, observation-indexed uniform coordinates and a terminal uniform coordinate. The tape law is independent of the signal and row experiment through the product law; the existence clause does not prescribe the internal joint distribution of the tape. The deterministic learner has measurable initial choice, jointly measurable next action on tape and observation at each time and state, and measurable output at each time and state. It is admissible for this tape law: initial choice, transitions and outputs satisfy the corresponding almost-everywhere measurability conditions, and the final estimate is almost-everywhere measurable both for each fixed signal and jointly under the sphere prior. It preserves all angular-success probabilities at every radius and all joint success-and-terminal-time probabilities. The terminal-time event includes an unstopped run evaluated at $T$. This representation is not a claim of equality of full joint output measures or of one common tape law for all learners.
* With $M(d)=o(d^2)$, an absolute $c>0$ works for all sufficiently large $d$: if $S\ge2/3$, or if both the following pointwise condition and the separate joint measurability condition hold, then $T\ge cd\log(1/\epsilon)$. The pointwise condition is that for every fixed $s$, the lower integral of $q_\xi(s,A)$ under $\rho\otimes\gamma_T$ is at least $2/3$. The joint measurability condition is almost-everywhere measurability of $(s,\xi,A)\mapsto q_\xi(s,A)$ under $\sigma\otimes(\rho\otimes\gamma_T)$. For each fixed $A_0>0$, another $c>0$ works for all sufficiently large $d$ with $M\le A_0d^2$ given $S\ge2/3$ alone.

**Conventions.** Use the native Gaussian/Haar frame, ball differentiation, polar projection and cap/Riesz models and the finite-state completed-rule learner, including its stopping tags, independent seed and noiseless labels. The source uses natural quotients for the block counts $(T+k-1)/k$ and their fixed-time analogues.

## Proof depends on

Integrated geometric moment bounds, positive-cap domination with scale cost, fixed-offset Riesz control, and separate block-recurrence and stopping-to-horizon arguments. The randomized realization requires a measure-preserving finite-action sampler and a measurable terminal output realization, not a replacement of the kernel by a deterministic choice.

## Expansion

Split the six geometric estimates, then separate the six finite-size streaming estimates, the tape bridge and the two memory lower bounds. Match real versus extended reciprocals and distinguish a joint event at one stopping time from conditional success given that time. The result is a selected set of bounds, not a proof at dimensions below their stated ranges.
