# Chi-square law of the between-group sum of squares

Let $\iota$ be a finite index set of $n$ observations, $g : \iota \to G$ an assignment to a finite set of groups with every group nonempty, and $X = (X_i)_{i \in \iota}$ i.i.d. $\mathcal N(\mu_0, v)$ on a probability space with $v > 0$ (all group means equal). With group means $\bar X_h$ and grand mean $\bar X$, write $\mathrm{SSB} = \sum_i(\bar X_{g(i)} - \bar X)^2$ and $\mathrm{SSW} = \sum_i(X_i - \bar X_{g(i)})^2$. If $|G| \ge 2$, then $\mathrm{SSB}/v$ has the chi-square law with $|G| - 1$ degrees of freedom.
