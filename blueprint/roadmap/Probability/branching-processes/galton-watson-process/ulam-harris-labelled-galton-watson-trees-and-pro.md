# Labelled Galton–Watson trees and projection to generation counts

Let $\nu$ be a probability law on $\mathbb N$. Vertices are finite words
in positive integers, including the empty root. A locally finite ordered
tree contains the root, is closed under prefixes, and has, for each of its
vertices $u$, children $u1,\ldots,u\xi_u$ for some finite $\xi_u\ge0$.
Equip the space of these trees with the sigma-field generated by membership
of each word. Equivalently it is generated by finite-height truncations:
each level is finite, and there are countably many finite ordered trees.

An independent array $(\xi_u)$ with common law $\nu$, indexed by all finite
words, determines a measurable random tree by retaining the root and then
exactly those children of every retained vertex. Write $Z_n$ for the
number of vertices at level $n$, and $\mathcal F_n$ for the sigma-field of
the first $n$ levels. Then $Z_0=1$ and, conditionally on $\mathcal F_n$,
$Z_{n+1}$ is a sum of $Z_n$ independent copies of $\nu$. Thus its count
process has the usual Galton–Watson transition law, and its generation
marginals are the canonical [generation-size laws](law.md).

A marked tree is a pair $(T,v)$, where $v=(v_0,v_1,\ldots)$ is an infinite
ray, with $v_0$ the root and $v_{n+1}$ a child of $v_n$. Its sigma-field is
the trace of the product sigma-field, generated by finite tree truncations
and finite ray prefixes. The projection forgetting the ray is measurable.
An ordinary finite tree has no such mark; no uniform choice among its
extinct levels is defined. For $\nu=\delta_2$, the unmarked tree is the
full ordered binary tree and $Z_n=2^n$.

## Expansion

The finite-dimensional product laws of the countable offspring array are
projective, giving its law by Kolmogorov extension on countable discrete
spaces. Membership of a word $i_1\cdots i_n$ is the intersection of the
finitely many conditions $i_j\le\xi_{i_1\cdots i_{j-1}}$, so the tree map
is measurable. Induction gives finite levels. The first $n$ levels reveal
no offspring variable at depth $n$, so conditional independence gives the
stated transition kernel. Induction on $n$ identifies the generation laws.
Coordinate projections give measurability of forgetting the mark.
This constructs the concrete labelled/marked spaces needed by the
size-biased-tree theorem; it does not assume a tree structure can be
recovered from generation counts alone.
