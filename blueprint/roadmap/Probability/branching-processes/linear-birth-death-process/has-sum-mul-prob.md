# Mean population size of the linear birth–death process

Let $b, d \ge 0$ and $t \ge 0$, and let $p_n(t)$ denote [Kendall's law](prob.md) at time $t$.
Then the series $\sum_{n \ge 0} n\, p_n(t)$ converges to $e^{(b - d) t}$: started from one
individual, the mean population size at time $t$ is $E[X_t] = e^{(b - d) t}$.

## Sources

- D. G. Kendall, *On the generalized "birth-and-death" process*, Annals of Mathematical
  Statistics 19 (1948), 1–15.
- N. T. J. Bailey, *The Elements of Stochastic Processes with Applications to the Natural
  Sciences*, Wiley, 1964, Chapter 8.
