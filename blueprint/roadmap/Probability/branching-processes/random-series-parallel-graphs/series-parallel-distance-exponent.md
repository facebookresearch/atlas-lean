# Distance exponent of random series-parallel graphs

In the Hambly–Jordan random series-parallel graph, at each step every edge is replaced independently by two edges in series with probability $p$ and by two parallel edges otherwise; let $D_n(p)$ be the graph distance between the two extremal vertices after $n$ steps. The claim (Benjamini's Question 9.6, precise form) is that $\alpha(p) = \lim_n\frac1n\log\mathbb ED_n(p)$ exists for every $p \in [0, 1]$, $\alpha(1/2) = 0$, and $\alpha(1/2 + \varepsilon)/\sqrt\varepsilon \to \pi/\sqrt6$ as $\varepsilon \to 0^+$.
