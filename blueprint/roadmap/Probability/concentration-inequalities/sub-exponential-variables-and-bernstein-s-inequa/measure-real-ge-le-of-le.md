# Sub-exponential tail bound, Gaussian regime

Let $\mu$ be a probability measure and $X$ sub-exponential with parameters $(\nu, \alpha)$. For
every $t$ with $0 \le t$ and $\alpha t \le \nu^2$ (that is $0 \le t \le \nu^2/\alpha$ when
$\alpha > 0$),

$$\mu(X \ge t) \le \exp\Big(-\frac{t^2}{2\nu^2}\Big).$$

When $\nu = 0$ the right-hand side is read as $1$.
