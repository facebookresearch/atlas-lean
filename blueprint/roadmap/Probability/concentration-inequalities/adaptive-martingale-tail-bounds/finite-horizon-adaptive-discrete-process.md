# Finite-horizon adaptive discrete process and its supermartingales

Let $\alpha$ be a set (possibly infinite) and $n \in \mathbb{N}$. A *step kernel* assigns to every $t < n$ and every history $h = (x_0, \dots, x_{t-1}) \in \alpha^t$ a discrete probability distribution $K_t(h)$ on $\alpha$. The *adaptive process* generated by $(K_t)$ is the discrete distribution $P_n$ on $\alpha^n$ with

$$
P_n(x_0, \dots, x_{n-1}) = \prod_{t=0}^{n-1} K_t(x_0, \dots, x_{t-1})(x_t).
$$

A *history-indexed process* is a family of real functions $X_k : \alpha^k \to \mathbb{R}$, $0 \le k \le n$. It is a *supermartingale* along $(K_t)$ if for every $k < n$ and every history $h$ of positive $P_k$-probability, $\sum_{x} K_k(h)(x)\, X_{k+1}(h, x) \le X_k(h)$; it is a *submartingale* if the reverse inequality holds and a *martingale* if both hold. Here $(h, x)$ denotes the history $h$ extended by $x$.
