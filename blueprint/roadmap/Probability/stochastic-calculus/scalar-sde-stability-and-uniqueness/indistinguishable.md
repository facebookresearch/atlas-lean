# Pathwise uniqueness for scalar Lipschitz SDEs

Let $B$ have real Brownian finite-dimensional laws, be adapted to $(\mathcal F_t)$, and have increments independent of the past filtration. Suppose $\sigma,b$ are globally Lipschitz with a common constant $K\ge0$. If $X,Y$ are scalar solutions driven by the same process and filtration, with the same deterministic initial value, then

$$P\{\omega: X_t(\omega)=Y_t(\omega)\text{ for every }t\ge0\}=1.$$

Thus the solutions are indistinguishable: one common full-measure event works at every time. Mean-square stability first gives equality almost surely at each fixed time. Continuous solution paths and a countable dense time set make the exceptional set independent of time.

Neither completeness nor right continuity of the filtration is needed, and the driving process need not have continuous paths. This result concerns uniqueness of given solutions, not their existence. The argument is the classical Lipschitz uniqueness proof in Le Gall, *Brownian Motion, Martingales, and Stochastic Calculus* (2016), Theorem 8.3.
