# Continuous modifications are indistinguishable

Let $I$ be a separable topological space, $E$ a Hausdorff topological space, and $(\Omega,\mathcal A,P)$ any measure space. Let $X,Y:I\times\Omega\to E$ have continuous paths almost surely. Suppose that, for every fixed $t\in I$, $X_t=Y_t$ almost surely. Then

$$P\text{-almost surely},\qquad \forall t\in I,\quad X_t=Y_t.$$

Thus the two processes are indistinguishable: the exceptional null set can be chosen independently of time. No filtration, probability normalization, or measurable structure on $I$ or $E$ is required. The proof takes a countable dense subset of $I$, intersects its full-measure equality events with the two continuity events, and extends equality by continuity into the Hausdorff space $E$.
