# Integrated-energy closure of ordinary primitives

For a real process $H$ indexed by nonnegative time, let

$$D(H)_t(\omega)=\int_0^t H_s(\omega)\,ds.$$

Write $e_T(H)=\int_\Omega\int_{(0,T]}|H_s(\omega)|^2\,ds\,dP(\omega)$ for its extended nonnegative time-and-sample energy.

For any measure $P$ and a real process $H$ with continuous paths for every sample,

$$e_T(D(H))\le T^2e_T(H).$$

Thus finite integrated second moment on a finite horizon is preserved by the actual ordinary primitive. The proof first bounds every squared primitive on that horizon by $T$ times the pathwise input energy, then integrates over time and samples. No exchange of an unmeasurable double integral is used. The bound includes $T=0$ and uses the extended nonnegative convention $0\cdot\infty=0$.
