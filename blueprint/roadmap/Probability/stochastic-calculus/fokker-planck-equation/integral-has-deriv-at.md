# Differentiation under the integral sign

Let $F, F' : \mathbb{R} \times \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ be jointly continuous, and suppose that for all $s, x \in \mathbb{R}$ the map $r \mapsto F(r, x)$ has derivative $F'(s, x)$ at $s$. Then for all $a, b, t \in \mathbb{R}$, the map $s \mapsto \int_a^b F(s, x) \, dx$ is differentiable at $t$ with

$$\frac{d}{ds}\Big|_{s = t} \int_a^b F(s, x) \, dx = \int_a^b F'(t, x) \, dx,$$

where $\int_a^b$ is the oriented interval integral.
