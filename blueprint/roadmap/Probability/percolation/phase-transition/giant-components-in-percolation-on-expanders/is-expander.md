# $(b, d)$-expander graph

Let $G$ be a simple graph on a finite vertex set $V$, $b \in \mathbb{R}$ and $d \in \mathbb{N}$. Then $G$ is a $(b, d)$-expander in the sense of Krivelevich, Lubetzky and Sudakov if every vertex has degree at most $d$ and every set $S \subseteq V$ with $|S| \le |V|/2$ satisfies

$$b\,|S| \le \sum_{x \in S} |N(x) \setminus S|,$$

where $N(x)$ is the set of neighbours of $x$; the right side is the number of edges from $S$ to $V \setminus S$.
