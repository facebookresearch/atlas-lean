# The key renewal hypothesis for a law with exponential tilt

Let $G$ be a measure on $\mathbb{R}$, $\theta \in \mathbb{R}$, and let $G_\theta$ be the normalised exponential tilt of $G$, the measure with density $e^{\theta x} / \int e^{\theta y}\, dG(y)$ with respect to $G$ (the zero measure when that integral is not finite and positive). Suppose $G_\theta = \operatorname{Exp}(1)$. Then every pair $(Y, y)$ admissible for the key renewal theorem that solves $Y(t) = y(t) + \int Y(t - x)\, dG_\theta(x)$ for every $t$ has $Y(t) \to \int_{(0,\infty)} y(s)\, ds / \int x\, dG_\theta(x)$. This is exactly the key renewal hypothesis of the Cramér–Lundberg constant identification. The normalisation matters: for $\theta = 0$ the measure $2 \operatorname{Exp}(1)$ satisfies the hypothesis, while its raw tilt has mass $2$. Under the root condition $\int e^{\theta y}\, dG(y) = 1$ the normalised and raw tilts coincide.
