# Strongly Rayleigh generating polynomials and determinantal cylinder laws

A finite law on binary configurations has the multiaffine generating polynomial obtained by multiplying the variables of its occupied sites and averaging that monomial under the law. The law is strongly Rayleigh when this polynomial is real stable. Its coefficients are real because they are probability masses, so the remaining test is that it has no zero when every argument has strictly positive imaginary part. For a countable index set require this for each finite marginal. A determinantal law is specified instead by the probability that every element of each finite subset is occupied: it equals the corresponding kernel principal minor. The kernel is Hermitian and its finite principal compressions are positive contractions when all the indicated finite matrices are positive semidefinite and bounded above by identity. On a countable group, law invariance under left shifts is distinct from translation invariance of the entries of the kernel; a pointwise equivariant IID factor is a measurable map of independent uniform $[0,1]$ labels, commuting with every left shift at every label configuration and having the prescribed pushforward law.
