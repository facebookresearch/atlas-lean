# Admissible pair for the key renewal theorem

A pair $(Y, y)$ of real functions is admissible for the key renewal theorem when:

- the input $y$ is measurable, vanishes on $(-\infty, 0)$, is continuous at almost every point, and on $[0, \infty)$ is dominated in absolute value by a nonincreasing majorant that is integrable on $[0, \infty)$, the standard sufficient condition for direct Riemann integrability;
- the candidate solution $Y$ is measurable, vanishes on $(-\infty, 0)$ and is bounded on every half-line $(-\infty, T]$.

This is a sufficient condition, not a definition of direct Riemann integrability. The predicate involves no renewal law and no renewal equation, so it does not make a solution unique by itself: for the input $0$ and the law $\delta_0$, both $Y = 0$ and the indicator of $[0, \infty)$ are admissible solutions of $Y = Y * \delta_0$.
