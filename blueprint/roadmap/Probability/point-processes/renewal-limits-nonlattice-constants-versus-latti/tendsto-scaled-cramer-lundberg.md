# Cramér–Lundberg constant

Let $G$ be a finite measure on $\mathbb{R}$ concentrated on $[0, \infty)$, typically a defective ladder-height measure, with a Cramér root $\theta > 0$, $\int e^{\theta y}\, dG(y) = 1$, and an integrable tilted mean $\int y\, e^{\theta y}\, dG(y)$. Let $Z$ be measurable and bounded, with $Z(t) = 0$ for $t < 0$, solving $Z(t) = \mathbf 1\{t \ge 0\}\, G(t, \infty) + \int Z(t - y)\, dG(y)$ for every real $t$; for the maximum $M$ of the ladder process, $Z(t) = \mathbb{P}(M > t)$ for $t \ge 0$. For a defective $G$ ($G(\mathbb{R}) < 1$) the vanishing and boundedness conditions pin $Z$ down: without them $Z + k e^{-\theta t}$ solves the same equation. Assume the key renewal theorem for the tilted law $e^{\theta y} dG(y)$, in the form that every admissible solution $Y$ of $Y(t) = y(t) + \int Y(t - x)\, e^{\theta x} dG(x)$ tends to $\int_{(0,\infty)} y / \int x\, e^{\theta x} dG(x)$; this is a hypothesis here. Then $e^{\theta t} Z(t) \to (1 - G(\mathbb{R})) / (\theta \int y\, e^{\theta y}\, dG(y))$.
