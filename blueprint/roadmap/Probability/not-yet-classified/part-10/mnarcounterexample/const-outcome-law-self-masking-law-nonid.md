# Explicit nonidentifiability of a missing-not-at-random mean

Let the response indicator $R$ be a fair coin. Under the first full-data law $Y = 1$ always; under the second, $Y = 1$ when $R$ is true and $Y = 0$ otherwise. The observed data $(R, Y \cdot [R])$ have the same law under both, but $\mathbb{E}[Y] = 1$ under the first and $\mathbb{E}[Y] = 1/2$ under the second; in particular the means differ.
