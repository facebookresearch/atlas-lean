# Almost-sure bootstrap validity for smooth finite-dimensional statistics

Let $X_0, X_1, \ldots$ be pairwise independent, identically distributed, square-integrable random vectors on a probability space, with values in a finite-dimensional real inner product space $E$. Let $Z$ be an almost-everywhere measurable random vector in $E$ on a probability space with $\langle Z, t\rangle \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \mathrm{Var}\langle X_0, t\rangle)$ for every $t \in E$. Let $F$ be a second-countable real normed space and $g : E \to F$ measurable and strictly differentiable at $\mathbb{E}X_0$ with derivative $L$. Then for almost every data sequence, the bootstrap law of $\sqrt{n}\,(g(\bar x^*_n) - g(\bar x_n))$, with $\bar x^*_n$ the mean of $n$ draws from the empirical law of the first $n$ observations, converges weakly to the law of $LZ$.
