# Spearman-Brown formula for the reliability of a sum

On a probability space, let $X_j = T + E_j$ for $j = 1, \dots, k$ with $k \ge 1$, where $T$ and every $E_j$ are square integrable, $\operatorname{Cov}(T, E_j) = 0$ for all $j$, $\operatorname{Cov}(E_i, E_j) = 0$ for $i \ne j$, $\operatorname{Var}(T) = v > 0$ and $\operatorname{Var}(E_j) = w \ge 0$. With the one-item reliability $\rho = v/(v + w)$,

$$\frac{\operatorname{Var}(k T)}{\operatorname{Var}\big(\sum_j X_j\big)} = \frac{k \rho}{1 + (k - 1)\rho}.$$

This is the Spearman-Brown prophecy formula for the reliability of a lengthened test: Spearman, \"Correlation calculated from faulty data\", British Journal of Psychology 3 (1910), 271-295, and Brown, \"Some experimental results in the correlation of mental abilities\", British Journal of Psychology 3 (1910), 296-322.
