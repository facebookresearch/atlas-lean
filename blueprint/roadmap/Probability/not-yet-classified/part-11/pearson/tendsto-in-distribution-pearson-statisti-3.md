# Pearson's chi-square theorem with estimated parameters

Let $X_1, X_2, \dots$ be i.i.d. with values in a finite set of $k$ cells, and let $p_\theta$, $\theta \in F$ with $F$ a real normed space of finite dimension $s$, be cell probabilities with $p_\theta(i) \ge 0$, measurable in $\theta$, $\sum_i p_\theta(i) = 1$ for $\theta$ near $\theta_0$, and differentiable at $\theta_0$ with derivative $D$. Assume $P(X_1 = i) = p_{\theta_0}(i) > 0$ for every cell, that the weighted Jacobian $J a = \big(D a_i / \sqrt{p_{\theta_0}(i)}\big)_i$ is injective, and that $L$ is linear with $J L$ the orthogonal projection onto the range of $J$. Let $N_i$ be the cell counts of the first $n$ observations, $r_n = \big((N_i - n p_{\theta_0}(i))/\sqrt{n p_{\theta_0}(i)}\big)_i$, and let the estimators $\hat\theta_n$ be almost everywhere measurable with $\sqrt n(\hat\theta_n - \theta_0) - L r_n \to 0$ in probability. If $s + 1 < k$, then the fitted Pearson statistic $\sum_i (N_i - n p_{\hat\theta_n}(i))^2/(n p_{\hat\theta_n}(i))$ converges in distribution to $\chi^2_{k-1-s}$.
