# Quasi-regular symmetric form

Let $X$ be a topological space with a measure $m$ and $\mathcal E$ a [symmetric
form](symmetric-form.md) on $L^2(X; m)$. Then $\mathcal E$ is quasi-regular if:

1. (QR1) there is an [$\mathcal E$-nest](is-nest.md) $(F_k)$ of compact sets;
2. (QR2) there is a subset of $D[\mathcal E]$, dense in $D[\mathcal E]$ for $\mathcal E_1$, each of
   whose elements has an [$\mathcal E$-quasi-continuous](is-quasi-continuous.md) $m$-version;
3. (QR3) there are $u_n \in D[\mathcal E]$ ($n \in \mathbb{N}$) with $\mathcal E$-quasi-continuous
   $m$-versions $\tilde u_n$ and an [$\mathcal E$-exceptional](is-exceptional.md) set $N$ such that
   $\{\tilde u_n\}$ separates the points of $X \setminus N$.

This is the notion of Albeverio and Ma (1991) and Ma and Röckner (1992), in the form of Röckner
and Schmuland's Definition 0.7, here for symmetric forms.

## Sources

- M. Röckner and B. Schmuland, *Quasi-regular Dirichlet forms: examples and counterexamples*, Canadian Journal of Mathematics 47 (1995), 165–200, Definition 0.7.
- Z.-M. Ma and M. Röckner, *Introduction to the Theory of (Non-Symmetric) Dirichlet Forms*, Universitext, Springer, 1992.
