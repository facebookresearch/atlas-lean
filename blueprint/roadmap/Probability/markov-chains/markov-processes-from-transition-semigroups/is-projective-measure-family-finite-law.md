# Finite Markov laws form a projective family

Let $(K_t)_{t \ge 0}$ be Markov kernels on a measurable space $E$ satisfying the Chapman–Kolmogorov
equations $K_{s+t}(x, A) = \int K_s(x, dy)\, K_t(y, A)$, and let $x \in E$. Then the [Markov laws
$\mu_J$ at finite sets of times](finite-law.md) $J \subset [0, \infty)$, started from $x$, form a
projective family: for finite sets $J \subseteq I$, the image of $\mu_I$ under the restriction
$E^I \to E^J$ is $\mu_J$. Projectivity is a consequence of the Chapman–Kolmogorov equations, not an
assumption.
