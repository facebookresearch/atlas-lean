# Transition matrix of a random walk on a group

Let $G$ be a group and $\mu : G \to \mathbb{R}$ any function; no nonnegativity or normalisation is assumed. Its matrix is

$$P(x, y) = \mu(x^{-1} y), \qquad x, y \in G.$$

When $\mu$ is a probability distribution on $G$ ($\mu \ge 0$ and $\sum_{g \in G} \mu(g) = 1$), $P$ is the transition matrix of the random walk that multiplies the current state on the right by an independent step of law $\mu$.
