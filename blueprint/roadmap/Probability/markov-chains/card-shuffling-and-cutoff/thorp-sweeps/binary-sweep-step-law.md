# Coordinate-fiber sweeps on the binary cube

The slots are $d$-bit words. A coordinate layer is the subgroup of permutations preserving each fiber of the other coordinates. For a binary fiber it consists of independently either switching or retaining each pair; its uniform law is therefore the product fair-bit law. A coordinate sweep composes such independent layers in the prescribed coordinate order with the earliest acting first. The full permutation law is not the same as its pushed-forward law of an injective marked list. When passing between physical shuffle and rotating-frame sweep models an explicit change of coordinates is required. Uniform transition kernels and Fourier averages use their action on the same finite carrier.
