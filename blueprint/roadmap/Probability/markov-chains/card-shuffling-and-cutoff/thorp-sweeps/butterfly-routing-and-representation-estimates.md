# Butterfly routing and representation estimates for coordinate sweeps

On $d$-bit words let $B_d$ be the butterfly permutation, obtained recursively by applying independent $B_{d-1}$ to the two first-bit fibers, then independently swapping pairs with the same tail. At $d=0$ it is the identity. All switch bits are independent and fair. The palindrome is $P_d=B_d\widetilde B_d^{-1}$ for independent butterflies. Write $N=2^d$, $L(N,k)=k(1+\log(N/k))$ with the totalized real convention giving zero at $k=0$. On any complex tabloid Hilbert Specht module $V_\mu$ of degree $D$ relabelled via a bijection from the $d$-cube, $T$ is the average of $\rho_\mu(B_d)$ or of $\rho_\mu(B_d^{-1})$ for a chosen orientation, and $K=\mathbb E\rho_\mu(P_d)$. Write $k=N-\mu_1$, with $D_{\rm tail}$ the Specht degree of the row-length tail after deleting the first row.

For injective lists of size $j$ let $A$ be the butterfly transition matrix, allowing any reindexing of the $d$ coordinates by conjugacy, and $H=AA^{\mathsf T}$ or $A^{\mathsf T}A$ according to orientation. There are $(N)_j$ such lists. Let $M_x(a)$ be the uniform mean over target lists $y$ of $[((N)_j)P_d(x,y)]^{1+a}$, where $P_d(x,y)$ denotes the probability of the indicated list image. For a palindrome realization with marked input list $x$, define the routing cost $C_d(x)$ recursively: first apply the root switch of the inverse butterfly to the marked list; partition by the switched first bit and recurse with its tails in the two child palindromes. At the current node add the number of cycles of the alternating permutation contained entirely in the switched list. With child palindrome permutations $p_0,p_1$, this alternating permutation takes $(0,y)$ to $(1,y)$ and $(1,y)$ to $(0,p_0^{-1}p_1y)$. The height of the root of a dimension-$h$ recursion is $h$. The cost is zero in dimension zero. At height $h>0$, a switch bit has either the root index given by an $(h-1)$-bit tail or a child index given by one first bit and a switch index of height $h-1$. Each child palindrome receives the two coin arrays restricted to that child. Count each distinct orbit set once, and only if the whole orbit is in the switched image of the marked list. For a label sent to child $b$, its tail in that child is its original tail: the second butterfly's root switch changes only the first bit. Distinct labels sent to the same child have different tails, since on each tail both bits are XORed with the same switch bit. Write $C_{\ge J}$ for the full natural cost minus the cost with only node contributions at heights $\le J-1$ retained; the two costs use exactly the same children and switches. This is the height-restricted sum for $J\ge1$.

The source asserts the following nine estimates. Constants are as quantified per item (not one common set of constants for all items).

1. **Adaptive:** There are $c,c',C,c_0>0$ such that for all $d,\mu$, bijections and either orientation, $\|TT^*\|\le e^{-cL(N,k)}$, $\operatorname{tr}((TT^*)^4)\le e^{-c'\log D+CL(N,k)}$, and $\|T\|\le e^{-c_0(\log D+L(N,k))}$.
2. **Casimir:** There are $a,C>0$ with $M_x(1/64)^{64/65}\le e^{CL(N,j)}$ for $1\le j\le N$ and every marked list $x$. For $k>0$, $\|K\|\le e^{-aL(N,k)}$ and $D\operatorname{tr}(K^{65})\le e^{CL(N,k)}$. There is a positive integer $l$ such that for every sequence of deterministic initial permutations the physical [pair-step shuffle](physical-thorp-step-and-specht-averages.md) at $ld$ steps has distance to uniform tending to zero.
3. **Dense:** For $j\ge1$ and fixed density $\varrho=j/N>0$, define $h_i=\sup_p\sum_{l\ge0}p_l/(l+1)$ over probability vectors with $p_l\le(l+1)2^{-(i-1)}$. Set $H_*(\varrho)=\tfrac12\sup\{\sum_{i\ge0}h_{i+1}a_i:0\le a_i\le1,\sum_{i\ge0}2^{-i-1}a_i\le\varrho\}$. For each fixed $\varrho,\epsilon>0$, some $c>0$ works for every $d\ge1$, $j$ with $j/N=\varrho$, and marked $x$: the probability that $C_d(x)+\log_2((N)_j/N^j)$ exceeds $j[H_*(\varrho)+(-\varrho-(1-\varrho)\log(1-\varrho))/(\varrho\log2)+\epsilon]$ is at most $e^{-cj}$. Here $0\log0=0$.
4. **Sparse contact:** Some $C_*,C>0$ work for $d\ge1$, $1\le k<N$ and both orientations: $\|T\|^2\le\min(1,(C_*dk/N)^{k/2})$, $T=0$ if $k=1$, and $\|T\|^2\le C^k(1+d)^{Ck}(k/N)^{k/2}$.
5. **Harmonic:** There exist positive $c,C,\zeta,c_{\rm tail},c_1,c_2$, $0<\delta<1$ and integer $p_0>0$. For $k>0$, $\|K\|\le e^{-cL}$, $\|K\|\le e^{CL}D_{\rm tail}^{-\zeta}$, $\|T\|\le e^{-c_{\rm tail}(L+\log D_{\rm tail})}$ for either orientation, and $\|K\|\le e^{-c_1L}D^{-c_2}$, with $L=L(N,k)$. For $1\le j\le N$, every ordered-coordinate reflected $H$ has each row's uniform $1+\delta$ moment of $(N)_jH(x,y)$ and $\operatorname{tr}(H^{p_0})$ at most $e^{CL(N,j)}$.
6. **Smoothing:** There exist integer $u\ge1$ and real $C$ with $(H^u)_{xy}\le e^{Cj}/(N)_j$ for all $j\le N$, all coordinate orders and orientations and all pairs of lists. In the full-list space the trace of $H^u$ for orientation $H=A^{\mathsf T}A$, with the same $u$, is at most $e^{CN}$ for all coordinate orders.
7. **Tail:** For one $1<p\le2$ and real $C_D,C$, both row and column $p$-moments in every reflected $H$ are at most $e^{C_Dj}$ under each normalization: $m^{-1}\sum_y(mH_{xy})^p$ and $m^{-1}\sum_y(mH_{yx})^p$ for $m=(N)_j$, and likewise for $m=N^j$ (the sums still run only over injective lists). For either orientation of every relabelled Specht sweep, $\|T\|^2\le e^{Ck}D_{\rm tail}^{-(p-1)/p}$.
8. **High-height:** There are $b,\delta,C>0$ and integer $J_0\ge1$ so that for every marked list of size $j$ and cutoff $J\ge J_0$, $\log_2\mathbb E2^{bC_{\ge J}}\le Cj2^{-bJ}$ and $\log_2\mathbb E2^{bC_d}\le Cj(j/N)^\delta$. In dimension $d=s+r$ the high-height bound also holds when the second butterfly and the $s$ low-coordinate stages of the first butterfly are fixed arbitrarily and the remaining $r$ stages of the first butterfly are independent fair switches, for every $J\ge J_0$ with $2s<J$ and every choice of the fixed second-butterfly and low-stage coins. The split writes a word as its first $r$ bits and its last $s$ bits. Within those first $r$ recursion levels, take the root-index switch from the high-stage array at the given $s$-bit tail and at the corresponding index in the height-$r$ recursion. After descending through all $r$ high bits, take the remaining switches from the low-stage array at the resulting $r$-bit prefix, using the height-$s$ index. Equivalently, the index assembly at $r=0$ uses the low-stage array and the unique empty prefix; at $r+1$ a root index splits its tail into $r$ and $s$ bits, and a child index prepends that child's bit to the prefix indexing low arrays and to the recursive child index indexing high arrays.
9. **Sparse saving:** Some $C>0$ and $d_0>0$ work for $1\le k<N$ with $d^{3/4}\le\log(N/k)$ and either orientation: $\|T\|^2\le e^{Ck}(k/N)^{k/4}$, and if $d\ge d_0$ the stronger bound $(k/N)^{k/4}$ holds.

**Conventions.** Use the recursively independent butterfly and palindrome, the exact list-image kernels, the two reflected orientations and normalization choices, the alternating-cycle cost, and the same Hilbert Specht action under arbitrary prescribed relabellings.

## Proof depends on

The routing cycle decomposition and moment bounds for arbitrary selected lists, plus the quantitative transfer from list density moments to Specht Fourier block estimates. The conditional high-height estimate needs its own proof under frozen choices rather than an application of the unconditional moment bound to a conditioned law.

## Expansion

Separate the nine blocks and their prerequisites, beginning with the routing/palindrome model and a single shared Specht carrier. Establish the two reflected normalizations and row/column estimates, then the dimension-sensitive transfers and finally any mixing deduction. For the conditional bound, identify the exact assembly of low and high bits and preserve the strict $J>2s$ condition. The result is the pinned nine-block version only.
