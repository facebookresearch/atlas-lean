# Uniqueness of the stationary distribution of a primitive chain

Let $S$ be a finite set and $P$ a stochastic $S \times S$ matrix (nonnegative entries, rows
summing to $1$) that is primitive: some power $P^k$ with $k > 0$ has all entries strictly
positive. Let $\pi$ be a stationary distribution ($\pi \ge 0$, $\sum_y \pi(y) = 1$,
$\pi P = \pi$) and let $\pi' : S \to \mathbb{R}$ be any vector with $\sum_y \pi'(y) = 1$ and
$\pi' P = \pi'$, not necessarily nonnegative. Then $\pi' = \pi$. In particular a primitive
stochastic matrix has at most one stationary distribution.
