# The contact process on homogeneous trees has two phase transitions

Let $T$ be the homogeneous tree in which every vertex has $n + 1$ neighbours, and run the contact process on $T$: infected vertices recover at rate $1$ and infect each neighbour at rate $\lambda$. Let $\lambda_1$ be the critical value for global survival (the process started from one infected vertex survives with positive probability) and $\lambda_2$ the critical value for local survival (the root is reinfected at arbitrarily large times with positive probability). Pemantle (1992, Theorem 2.1) proves that for all $n > 2$, $0 < \lambda_1 < \lambda_2 < \infty$, so for $\lambda_1 < \lambda < \lambda_2$ the process survives globally while every vertex is eventually free of infection (weak survival); for $n = 2$ the same holds except that the middle inequality may fail to be strict. His Theorem 2.2 gives explicit bounds on $\lambda_1$ and on an auxiliary critical value $\lambda_a$ that separate them.

## Expansion

1. The model: the graphical construction of the contact process on $T$, monotonicity, and the definitions of $\lambda_1$, $\lambda_2$ and global and local survival.
2. Bounds on $\lambda_1$ (Theorem 2.2 (i)–(ii)) from comparison with branching random walks and a supermartingale argument.
3. Bounds on $\lambda_a$ (Theorem 2.2 (iii)–(iv)) and the inequality $\lambda_a \le \lambda_2$, giving the separation for $n > 2$.
4. The case $n = 2$, completed by later work (Liggett; Stacey), as a separate statement.
