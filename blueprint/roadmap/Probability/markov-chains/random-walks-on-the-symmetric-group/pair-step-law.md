# Step law from a uniform pair of positions

Let $n \in \mathbb{N}$ and let $f$ map pairs $(i, j)$ of positions in $\{1, \dots, n\}$ to permutations of $\{1, \dots, n\}$. The pair step law of $f$ is the function on permutations given by

$$\mu_f(\sigma) = \frac{\#\{(i, j) \in \{1, \dots, n\}^2 : f(i, j) = \sigma\}}{n^2}$$

for every permutation $\sigma$. For $n > 0$ it is the law of $f(i, j)$ for a uniform pair $(i, j)$, a probability distribution on permutations. For $n = 0$ it is $0$, by the convention $x/0 = 0$, so it puts mass $0$ on the unique (empty) permutation and is not a probability distribution.
