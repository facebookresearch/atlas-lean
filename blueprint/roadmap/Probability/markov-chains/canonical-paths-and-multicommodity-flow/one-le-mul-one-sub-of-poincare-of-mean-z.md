# A Poincaré inequality bounds the spectral gap, for an eigenvector orthogonal to constants

Let $P$ have unit row sums and satisfy detailed balance with respect to a probability vector $\pi > 0$, and suppose that for every $\psi$

$$\sum_{x,y} (\psi(x)-\psi(y))^2\,\pi(x)\pi(y) \;\le\; B \sum_{x,y} (\psi(x)-\psi(y))^2\,\pi(x)P(x,y).$$

Then a nonzero real eigenvector $f$ with eigenvalue $\mu$ and $\sum_x \pi(x) f(x) = 0$ satisfies $1 \le B(1-\mu)$: for such $f$ the left side is $2\sum_x \pi(x)f(x)^2$ and the right side is $2B(1-\mu)\sum_x \pi(x)f(x)^2$. This is the step of the proof of Theorem 5 of A. Sinclair, *Combinatorics, Probability and Computing* 1 (1992), that inserts an eigenvector into the variational characterisation (5) of the spectral gap.
