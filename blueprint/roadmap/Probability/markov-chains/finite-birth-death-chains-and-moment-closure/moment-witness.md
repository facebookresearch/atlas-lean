# Two-point law on three states for the moment-closure counterexample

The vector `w` on the states `0, 1, 2` with `w(0) = w(2) = 1 / 2` and `w(1) = 0`. It is
[nonnegative](moment-witness-nonneg.md) and [sums to one](moment-witness-sum-one.md), so it is a
probability law: half mass at `0` and half at `2`.
