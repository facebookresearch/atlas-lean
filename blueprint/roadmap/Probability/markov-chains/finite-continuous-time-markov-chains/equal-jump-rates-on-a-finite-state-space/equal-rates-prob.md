# Transition probability under equal jump rates

For $n \in \mathbb{N}$ and real $\mu$ and $t$, write $m = n + 1$ and $c = e^{-m\mu t/n}$. The equal-rates matrix on $n + 1$ states has entry $\frac1m + \frac nm c$ if $i = j$ and $\frac1m - \frac1m c$ if $i \ne j$. For $n = 0$ the exponent is $0$ by the convention for division by zero. For a rate $\mu \ge 0$ and time $t \ge 0$ it is the transition matrix at time $t$ of the chain whose $n$ possible jumps are equally likely; for $\mu t < 0$ the off-diagonal entries are negative.
