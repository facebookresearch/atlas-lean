# Mean jump count recovered from the mismatch probability

For $n \in \mathbb{N}$ and a real $p$, write $m = n + 1$. The equal-rates distance is $-\frac{n}{m} \log\bigl(1 - \frac{m}{n} p\bigr)$. For $n > 0$ and $p$ the terminal mismatch probability $\mathbb{P}(X_t \ne X_0)$ of the equal-rates chain, below the saturation level $p < n/(n+1)$, it recovers the mean number of jumps $\mu t$. This is the probability that the state at time $t$ differs from the start, not the probability of having left the start at some time: the chain can leave and return. For $n = 0$ the expression is $0$ by the convention for division by zero, and at or above saturation the real logarithm of a nonpositive number is read as $\log|y|$ (with $\log 0 = 0$).
