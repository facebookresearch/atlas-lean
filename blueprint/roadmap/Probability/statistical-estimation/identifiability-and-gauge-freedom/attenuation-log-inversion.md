# For $I_0 > 0$, the log attenuation recovers $L$ from $I = I_0 e^{-L}$

For an incident intensity $I_0 > 0$ and every real $L$, the transmitted intensity $I = I_0 e^{-L}$
of a monochromatic beam under the Beer–Lambert law satisfies

$$-\log \frac{I}{I_0} = L \qquad \text{and equivalently} \qquad \log \frac{I_0}{I} = L ,$$

the two forms agreeing for all real arguments, since $\log(b / a) = -\log(a / b)$.

In the CT reading, $L$ is the line integral of the attenuation coefficient along the ray, so
$L \ge 0$ for an absorbing medium; the identities hold for every real $L$. Without a condition on
$I_0$ the identities can fail: at $I_0 = 0$ both quotients are $0 / 0 = 0$ by the convention
$x / 0 = 0$, and with $\log 0 = 0$ both left-hand sides are $0$, so the identities fail for
$L \ne 0$.
