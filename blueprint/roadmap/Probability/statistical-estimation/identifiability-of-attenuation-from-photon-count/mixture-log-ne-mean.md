# Equal mix of line integrals $0$ and $2$: log attenuation is not $1$

Consider a beam made of two monochromatic components of equal incident intensity, normalised so that
the total incident intensity is $1$, whose line integrals of attenuation are $0$ and $2$. Its
transmitted intensity is $(e^{0} + e^{-2}) / 2$, and the log attenuation of this transmission is not
the mean line integral $1$:

$$-\log \frac{e^{0} + e^{-2}}{2} \ne 1 .$$

So for a beam with several energies, the log attenuation of the total transmission need not be the
mean of the line integrals at the separate energies, unlike the
[single-energy identity](attenuation-log-inversion.md) $-\log(I/I_0) = L$.
