# Statistical dimension lower bounds statistical query complexity

If the decision problem $B(\mathcal{D}, D)$ (distinguish $D$ from a family $\mathcal{D}$) has $\mathrm{SDA}(B(\mathcal{D}, D), \gamma) \ge d$, that is, some finite witness family $\mathcal{H} \subseteq \mathcal{D}$ has $\mathrm{SDA}(\mathcal{H}, D, \gamma) \ge d$, then every randomized statistical algorithm that solves it with success probability $\alpha > 1/2$ for every input distribution, using queries to VSTAT$(1/(3\gamma))$, must make at least $(2\alpha - 1)d$ queries. In particular an algorithm that is always correct ($\alpha = 1$), such as a deterministic one, needs at least $d$ queries, while the bound degrades to $0$ as $\alpha$ approaches $1/2$. As the main application, for every constant $\delta > 0$, every planted bipartite clique size $k \le n^{1/2 - \delta}$ and every $r > 0$, every statistical algorithm that solves the distributional planted $k$-biclique problem with probability at least $2/3$, or distinguishes it from the uniform distribution on $\{0,1\}^n$, needs at least $n^{\Omega(\log r)}$ queries to VSTAT$(n^2/(rk^2))$. Hence no polynomial-time statistical algorithm solves it with queries to VSTAT$(o(n^2/k^2))$, and every statistical algorithm needs $n^{\Omega(\log n)}$ queries to VSTAT$(n^{2-\delta}/k^2)$; this is nearly optimal, since $n$ queries to VSTAT$(16n^2/k^2)$ recover the planted set. Most practical algorithms (moment methods, local search, convex-optimization methods, MCMC and simulated annealing) can be implemented with statistical queries.

## Proof sketch

Fix a witness family $\mathcal{H} \subseteq \mathcal{D}$ with $\mathrm{SDA}(\mathcal{H}, D, \gamma) \ge d$, and run a deterministic algorithm on $D$ with every query $h$ answered by its exact null value $\mathbb{E}_D[h]$. For a candidate $D_i \in \mathcal{H}$ with $p_i = \mathbb{E}_{D_i}[h]$, this answer is a valid response of VSTAT$(1/(3\gamma))$ on $D_i$ unless $|\mathbb{E}_D[h] - p_i|$ exceeds the tolerance $\max\{3\gamma, \sqrt{3\gamma\, p_i(1 - p_i)}\}$, which shrinks as $\gamma$ decreases. Each query rules out at most a $1/d$-fraction of $\mathcal{H}$: if it ruled out a larger subfamily $\mathcal{H}' \subseteq \mathcal{H}$, then by Cauchy–Schwarz the total discrepancy $\sum_{D_i \in \mathcal{H}'} |\mathbb{E}_D[h] - p_i|$ would be at most $|\mathcal{H}'|$ times $\sqrt{\rho(\mathcal{H}', D)}$ times the norm of $h$ under $D$, with $\rho(\mathcal{H}', D) \le \gamma$, which is too small for every member to exceed its tolerance (Feldman et al., Lemma 3.3). So the alternatives on which the algorithm is fooled by the null answers include all but a $q/d$-fraction of $\mathcal{H}$ after $q$ queries. Under the input distribution that equals $D$ with probability $1/2$ and a uniform element of the witness family $\mathcal{H}$ otherwise, an algorithm with success probability $\alpha$ must succeed on at least a $(2\alpha - 1)$-fraction of $\mathcal{H}$, which needs $(2\alpha - 1)d$ queries (Theorem 3.7). For planted bicliques, the correlation of two planted distributions grows exponentially in the overlap $|S \cap S'|$ of their planted sets, and most pairs of planted sets intersect little, which gives the statistical dimension $n^{\Omega(\log n)}$.
