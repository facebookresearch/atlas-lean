# Uniqueness of unbiased functions of a complete statistic

Let $(\Omega, \mathcal{F})$ and $\mathcal{T}$ be measurable spaces and $(P_\theta)_{\theta \in \Theta}$ a family of probability measures on $\Omega$. A statistic $S : \Omega \to \mathcal{T}$ is complete for $(P_\theta)$ if it is measurable and every measurable $a : \mathcal{T} \to \mathbb{R}$ such that $a(S)$ is $P_\theta$-integrable with $\mathbb{E}_{P_\theta}[a(S)] = 0$ for every $\theta$ satisfies $a(S) = 0$ $P_\theta$-almost everywhere for every $\theta$. Let $\tau : \Theta \to \mathbb{R}$ be a target, and call an estimator $W$ unbiased for $\tau$ if $W$ is $P_\theta$-integrable with $\mathbb{E}_{P_\theta}[W] = \tau(\theta)$ for every $\theta$. If $S$ is complete and $g, h : \mathcal{T} \to \mathbb{R}$ are measurable with $g(S)$ and $h(S)$ both unbiased for $\tau$, then $g(S) = h(S)$ $P_\theta$-almost everywhere for every $\theta$.
