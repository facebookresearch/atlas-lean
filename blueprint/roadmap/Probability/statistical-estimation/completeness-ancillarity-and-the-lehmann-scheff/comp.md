# Measurable functions of an ancillary statistic are ancillary

Let $(\Omega, \mathcal{F})$ and $\mathcal{T}$ be measurable spaces and $(P_\theta)_{\theta \in \Theta}$ a family of probability measures on $\Omega$. A statistic $V : \Omega \to \mathcal{T}$ is ancillary for $(P_\theta)$ if it is measurable and its law $P_\theta \circ V^{-1}$ is the same for every $\theta \in \Theta$. If $V$ is ancillary for $(P_\theta)$ and $W : \mathcal{T} \to \mathcal{S}$ is measurable, then $W \circ V$ is ancillary for $(P_\theta)$.

## Sources

- G. Casella and R. L. Berger, *Statistical Inference*, 2nd ed., Duxbury (2002), Definition 6.2.24.
