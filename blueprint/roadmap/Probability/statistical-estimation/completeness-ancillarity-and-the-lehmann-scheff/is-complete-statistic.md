# Complete statistic

Let $(\Omega, \mathcal{F})$ and $\mathcal{T}$ be measurable spaces and $(P_\theta)_{\theta \in \Theta}$ a family of probability measures on $\Omega$. A statistic $S : \Omega \to \mathcal{T}$ is complete for $(P_\theta)$ if $S$ is measurable and, for every measurable $a : \mathcal{T} \to \mathbb{R}$,

$$\Big(a(S) \in L^1(P_\theta) \text{ and } \mathbb{E}_{P_\theta}[a(S)] = 0 \text{ for every } \theta\Big) \implies a(S) = 0 \ \ P_\theta\text{-a.e. for every } \theta.$$

Both the hypothesis and the conclusion range over all parameters.

## Sources

- E. L. Lehmann and H. Scheffé, *Completeness, similar regions, and unbiased estimation. Part I*, Sankhyā 10 (1950), 305–340; Casella–Berger, *Statistical Inference*, 2nd ed., Definition 6.2.21.
