# An almost surely constant statistic is ancillary

Let $(\Omega, \mathcal{F})$ and $\mathcal{T}$ be measurable spaces and $(P_\theta)_{\theta \in \Theta}$ a family of probability measures on $\Omega$. A statistic $V : \Omega \to \mathcal{T}$ is ancillary for $(P_\theta)$ if it is measurable and its law $P_\theta \circ V^{-1}$ is the same for every $\theta \in \Theta$. If $V$ is measurable and there is $c \in \mathcal{T}$ with $V = c$ $P_\theta$-almost everywhere for every $\theta$, then $V$ is ancillary: each of its laws is the point mass at $c$.

## Sources

- G. Casella and R. L. Berger, *Statistical Inference*, 2nd ed., Duxbury (2002), Definition 6.2.24.
