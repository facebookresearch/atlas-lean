# Factorization implies sufficiency of a statistic

Let $(\Omega, \mathcal{F})$ be a measurable space, $T : \Omega \to \mathcal{T}$ a measurable statistic, $\mu$ a measure on $\mathcal{F}$ and $(P_\theta)_{\theta \in \Theta}$ finite measures with

$$P_\theta = \big(g_\theta(T)\, h\big) \cdot \mu$$

for measurable $g_\theta : \mathcal{T} \to [0, \infty]$ and $h : \Omega \to [0, \infty]$. If the measure $h \cdot \mu$ is σ-finite on the σ-algebra $\sigma(T)$ generated by $T$, then $\sigma(T)$ is [sufficient](is-sufficient.md) for $(P_\theta)$.

The σ-finiteness hypothesis on $h \cdot \mu$ restricted to $\sigma(T)$ is an extra, nonstandard assumption, and it does not follow from $\mu$ being σ-finite: restriction to a sub-σ-algebra need not preserve σ-finiteness, so it fails for example when $\mu$ is Lebesgue measure on $\mathbb{R}$, $h = 1$ and $T$ is constant. Nor does it force $\mu$ to be σ-finite (take $h = 0$). The statement is therefore the factorization criterion under that extra hypothesis, not the classical criterion as stated.
