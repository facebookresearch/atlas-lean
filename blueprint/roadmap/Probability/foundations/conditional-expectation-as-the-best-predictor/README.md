# Conditional expectation as the best predictor

Let $\mu$ be a finite measure on a measurable space $(\Omega, \mathcal{F})$, let $\mathcal{G} \subseteq \mathcal{F}$ be a sub-σ-algebra, and let $Y : \Omega \to \mathbb{R}$ be square-integrable with respect to $\mu$. Then the conditional expectation $\mathbb{E}[Y \mid \mathcal{G}]$ of $Y$ with respect to $\mu$ minimises the squared error among square-integrable $\mathcal{G}$-measurable functions: for every $\mathcal{G}$-measurable $g : \Omega \to \mathbb{R}$ that is square-integrable with respect to $\mu$, $\int (Y - \mathbb{E}[Y \mid \mathcal{G}])^2 \, d\mu \le \int (Y - g)^2 \, d\mu$. When $\mu$ is a probability measure, this says that $\mathbb{E}[Y \mid \mathcal{G}]$ has the smallest mean squared error among predictors of $Y$ that use only the information in $\mathcal{G}$.

## Idea

In the space of square-integrable functions, $\mathbb{E}[Y \mid \mathcal{G}]$ is the orthogonal projection of $Y$ onto the $\mathcal{G}$-measurable functions. The residual $Y - \mathbb{E}[Y \mid \mathcal{G}]$ is orthogonal to every square-integrable $\mathcal{G}$-measurable $h$, and Pythagoras gives

$$\int (Y - g)^2 \, d\mu = \int (Y - \mathbb{E}[Y \mid \mathcal{G}])^2 \, d\mu + \int (\mathbb{E}[Y \mid \mathcal{G}] - g)^2 \, d\mu,$$

so any other predictor adds a nonnegative error. With the trivial σ-algebra the predictors are constants and the theorem says that the mean minimises $\mathbb{E}[(Y - c)^2]$, the minimum being the variance. With $\mathcal{G}$ generated by an observed variable $X$, the predictors are the functions $h(X)$, and the regression function $\mathbb{E}[Y \mid X]$ is the best regressor under squared loss.
