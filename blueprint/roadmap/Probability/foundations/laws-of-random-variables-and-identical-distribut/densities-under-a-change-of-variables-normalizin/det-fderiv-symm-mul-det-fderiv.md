# Determinants of a derivative and its inverse multiply to one

Let $E$ be a real normed space and $T : E \to E$ a measurable bijection with measurable inverse. If $T$ has Fréchet derivative $A$ at $z$ and $T^{-1}$ has Fréchet derivative $B$ at $T(z)$, then

$$\det B \cdot \det A = 1.$$

The space $E$ may be infinite-dimensional. There the determinant of every linear endomorphism is defined to be $1$, so the identity holds trivially and $\det A$ is not a Jacobian determinant in the usual sense. In finite dimension it is the familiar relation between the Jacobians of a map and of its inverse.
