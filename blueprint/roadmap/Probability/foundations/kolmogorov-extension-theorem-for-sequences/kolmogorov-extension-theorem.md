# Kolmogorov's extension theorem for sequences

Let $\mathbb R^{\mathbb N} = \{(\omega_1, \omega_2, \dots) : \omega_i \in \mathbb R\}$, with
$\mathbb N = \{1, 2, \dots\}$, carry the product σ-field $\mathcal R^{\mathbb N}$ generated by the finite-dimensional sets
$\{\omega : \omega_i \in B_i \text{ for } 1 \le i \le n\}$, where $n \ge 1$ and $B_1, \dots, B_n \subseteq \mathbb R$ are
Borel. For each $n \ge 1$ let $\mu_n$ be a probability measure on the Borel sets of $\mathbb R^n$, and suppose the
family is consistent:

$$\mu_{n+1}\big((a_1, b_1] \times \cdots \times (a_n, b_n] \times \mathbb R\big) = \mu_n\big((a_1, b_1] \times \cdots \times (a_n, b_n]\big)$$

for every $n$ and all real $a_i < b_i$. Then there is a unique probability measure $P$ on
$(\mathbb R^{\mathbb N}, \mathcal R^{\mathbb N})$ with
$P(\{\omega : \omega_i \in (a_i, b_i] \text{ for } 1 \le i \le n\}) = \mu_n((a_1, b_1] \times \cdots \times (a_n, b_n])$
for every $n$ and all real $a_i < b_i$.

## Proof

Two finite measures on $\mathbb R^n$ that agree on the boxes $(a_1, b_1] \times \cdots \times (a_n, b_n]$ with
$a_i < b_i$ are equal, since the boxes with some $a_i = b_i$ are empty. Applied to $\mu_n$ and to the image of
$\mu_{n+1}$ under the projection of $\mathbb R^{n+1}$ onto the first $n$ coordinates, which is continuous, hence Borel
measurable, this extends consistency to all Borel sets: $\mu_{n+1}(B \times \mathbb R) = \mu_n(B)$ for every Borel
$B \subseteq \mathbb R^n$. By induction, $\mu_m(B \times \mathbb R^{m - n}) = \mu_n(B)$ for all $m \ge n$, where
$B \times \mathbb R^{m - n}$ is Borel, as the preimage of $B$ under the projection of $\mathbb R^m$ onto the first $n$
coordinates.

For $n \ge 1$ and a Borel set $B \subseteq \mathbb R^n$, write $[B] = \{\omega : (\omega_1, \dots, \omega_n) \in B\}$.
The sets $B \subseteq \mathbb R^n$ with $[B] \in \mathcal R^{\mathbb N}$ form a σ-field that contains the products
$B_1 \times \cdots \times B_n$ of Borel sets, hence the product σ-field of $\mathbb R^n$, which is its Borel σ-field. So
every $[B]$ lies in $\mathcal R^{\mathbb N}$, and for a probability measure $P$ on $\mathcal R^{\mathbb N}$ the law
$B \mapsto P([B])$ of $(\omega_1, \dots, \omega_n)$ is a probability measure on the Borel sets of $\mathbb R^n$. If $P$
has the stated values, this law agrees with $\mu_n$ on boxes, so it is $\mu_n$. A set $[B]$ with
$B \subseteq \mathbb R^n$ is also $[B \times \mathbb R^{m - n}]$ for every $m \ge n$, and if $[B] = [B']$ with
$B' \subseteq \mathbb R^m$ and $m \ge n$, then $B' = B \times \mathbb R^{m - n}$. So finitely many such sets can be
written with the same $n$, and they form an algebra $\mathcal A$: $\mathbb R^{\mathbb N} = [\mathbb R^n]$, the
complement of $[B]$ is $[\mathbb R^n \setminus B]$, and $[B] \cup [B'] = [B \cup B']$ for $B, B' \subseteq \mathbb R^n$.
By the extended consistency, $\nu([B]) = \mu_n(B)$ depends only on the set $[B]$. The set function $\nu$ is finitely
additive on $\mathcal A$, since disjoint sets $[B]$ and $[B']$ written with the same $n$ have disjoint bases; hence it
is monotone and finitely subadditive, and $\nu(\mathbb R^{\mathbb N}) = 1$. Every $P$ with the stated values agrees with
$\nu$ on $\mathcal A$.

The set function $\nu$ is continuous at $\emptyset$: if sets $C_1 \supseteq C_2 \supseteq \cdots$ of $\mathcal A$ have
empty intersection, then $\nu(C_j) \to 0$. Suppose instead that $\nu(C_j) \ge \delta$ for every $j$, for some
$\delta > 0$. Write $C_j = [B'_j]$ with $B'_j \subseteq \mathbb R^{m_j}$, and put $n_j = j + \max(m_1, \dots, m_j)$ and
$B_j = B'_j \times \mathbb R^{n_j - m_j}$, so that $C_j = [B_j]$ with $B_j \subseteq \mathbb R^{n_j}$ Borel, the
dimensions increase strictly, $n_1 < n_2 < \cdots$, and $n_j \ge j$. Since finite Borel measures on $\mathbb R^{n_j}$
are inner regular, there are compact sets $K_j \subseteq B_j$ with $\mu_{n_j}(B_j \setminus K_j) < \delta 2^{-j-1}$. Put
$E_j = [K_j]$ and $D_k = E_1 \cap \dots \cap E_k$, sets of $\mathcal A$ with $D_k \subseteq E_k \subseteq C_k$. For
$j \le k$, $C_k \subseteq C_j$, so $C_k \setminus D_k \subseteq \bigcup_{j \le k} (C_j \setminus E_j)$, where
$C_j \setminus E_j = [B_j \setminus K_j]$ has value $\mu_{n_j}(B_j \setminus K_j)$. Hence
$\nu(C_k \setminus D_k) < \sum_{j \le k} \delta 2^{-j-1} < \delta / 2$, so $\nu(D_k) > \delta / 2$ and $D_k$ is
nonempty; choose $\omega^{(k)} \in D_k$. For $k \ge j$, $\omega^{(k)} \in D_k \subseteq E_j$, so the vector
$(\omega^{(k)}_1, \dots, \omega^{(k)}_{n_j})$ lies in $K_j$.

Since a sequence in a compact set has a convergent subsequence, there are infinite sets
$\mathbb N \supseteq I_1 \supseteq I_2 \supseteq \cdots$ with $I_j \subseteq \{j, j + 1, \dots\}$ such that
$(\omega^{(k)}_1, \dots, \omega^{(k)}_{n_j})$ converges as $k \to \infty$ in $I_j$: given $I_{j-1}$, with
$I_0 = \mathbb N$, the vectors with $k \in I_{j-1}$ and $k \ge j$ lie in the compact set $K_j$, so a subsequence of them
converges, and $I_j$ is the set of its indices. Choose $k_1 < k_2 < \cdots$ with $k_l \in I_l$. For each $j$,
$k_l \in I_j$ for every $l \ge j$, so $(\omega^{(k_l)}_1, \dots, \omega^{(k_l)}_{n_j})$ converges as $l \to \infty$, to
a point of $K_j$, which is closed. Since $n_i \ge i$, the coordinate $i$ is among the first $n_i$, so $\omega^{(k_l)}_i$
converges to some $\omega_i$ for every $i$, and $(\omega_1, \dots, \omega_{n_j}) \in K_j$ for every $j$. Then
$\omega = (\omega_1, \omega_2, \dots)$ lies in every $E_j$, hence in every $D_k \subseteq C_k$, which contradicts
$\bigcap_k C_k = \emptyset$.

Continuity at $\emptyset$ makes $\nu$ countably additive on $\mathcal A$: if $A \in \mathcal A$ is the disjoint union of
$A_1, A_2, \dots \in \mathcal A$, the sets $A \setminus (A_1 \cup \dots \cup A_k)$ lie in $\mathcal A$ and decrease to
$\emptyset$, so $\nu(A) = \lim_k (\nu(A_1) + \dots + \nu(A_k))$. Hence $\nu$ is σ-subadditive on $\mathcal A$: if
$s_1, s_2, \dots \in \mathcal A$ have their union in $\mathcal A$, that union is the disjoint union of the sets
$s_i \setminus (s_1 \cup \dots \cup s_{i-1})$ of $\mathcal A$, whose values are at most $\nu(s_i)$. As
$\mathbb R^{\mathbb N} \in \mathcal A$ has value $1$, Carathéodory's extension theorem extends $\nu$ uniquely to a
measure on the σ-field that $\mathcal A$ generates. That σ-field is $\mathcal R^{\mathbb N}$, since
$\mathcal A \subseteq \mathcal R^{\mathbb N}$ and $\mathcal A$ contains the sets $[B_1 \times \cdots \times B_n]$ that
generate $\mathcal R^{\mathbb N}$. The extension is a probability measure with the stated values, and it is the only
one, since every such $P$ agrees with $\nu$ on $\mathcal A$.
