# Tightness plus finite-dimensional convergence gives weak convergence

Let $T > 0$, and let $\mu_n$ ($n \in \mathbb{N}$) and $\mu$ be probability measures on
$C([0, T], \mathbb{R})$ with the topology of uniform convergence. If the family $\{\mu_n\}$ is
tight and for every finite set $I \subseteq [0, T]$ the image measures of $\mu_n$ under
$f \mapsto (f(t))_{t \in I}$ converge weakly to the image of $\mu$, then $\mu_n \to \mu$ weakly.

This is a criterion, not a process convergence theorem: applications must separately prove
tightness and all finite-dimensional convergences for their laws.
