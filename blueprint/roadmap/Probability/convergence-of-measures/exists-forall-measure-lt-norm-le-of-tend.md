# Convergence in distribution implies boundedness in probability

Let $E$ be a finite-dimensional real inner product space with its Borel σ-algebra. Let $(\Omega, P)$ and $(\Omega', P')$ be probability spaces, let $X_n \colon \Omega \to E$ for $n \in \mathbb{N}$ and $Z \colon \Omega' \to E$ be a.e.-measurable, and suppose $X_n$ converges in distribution to $Z$. Then $(X_n)$ is bounded in probability: for every $\varepsilon \in (0, \infty]$ there is $K \in \mathbb{R}$ with

$$P\bigl(\lVert X_n \rVert > K\bigr) \le \varepsilon \quad \text{for all } n \in \mathbb{N}.$$
