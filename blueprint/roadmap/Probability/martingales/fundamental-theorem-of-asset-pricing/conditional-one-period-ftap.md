# Conditional one-period fundamental theorem of asset pricing

Let $(\Omega, \mathcal F, \mathbb P)$ be a probability space, $\mathcal G \subseteq \mathcal F$ a sub-$\sigma$-field, and $\Delta S$ an $\mathcal F$-measurable random vector in $\mathbb R^d$, the one-period discounted price increments of $d$ assets. Say that there is *no arbitrage given $\mathcal G$* if every $\mathcal G$-measurable $\xi$ with values in $\mathbb R^d$ and $\xi \cdot \Delta S \ge 0$ almost surely satisfies $\xi \cdot \Delta S = 0$ almost surely. Then the following are equivalent:

1. there is no arbitrage given $\mathcal G$;
2. there is an $\mathcal F$-measurable $Z > 0$ with $\mathbb E[Z \mid \mathcal G] = 1$, $\mathbb E[Z\, |\Delta S| \mid \mathcal G] < \infty$ and $\mathbb E[Z\, \Delta S \mid \mathcal G] = 0$ almost surely;
3. there is a probability measure $Q \sim \mathbb P$ with bounded density $dQ/d\mathbb P$ such that $\mathbb E_Q[|\Delta S|] < \infty$ and $\mathbb E_Q[\Delta S \mid \mathcal G] = 0$ almost surely.

The conditional normalization in 2 and the uniform bound in 3 cannot in general be imposed together: when $\mathcal G$ is generated by a countable partition, the conditional densities on the cells may be unbounded, and a bounded density is obtained by giving the cells new weights, so that $Q$ need not agree with $\mathbb P$ on $\mathcal G$. No finiteness of $\Omega$ is assumed.
