# Sanov's theorem

Let $X$ be a Polish space with its Borel $\sigma$-algebra and let $X_0, X_1, \dots$ be
independent random variables in $X$, each with law $\alpha$. The empirical measures

$$L_n = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i<n} \delta_{X_i},$$

viewed as random elements of the space of probability measures on $X$ with the topology of weak
convergence, satisfy the large deviation principle with the good rate function

$$\beta \mapsto \mathrm{KL}(\beta \,\|\, \alpha) = \int \log \frac{d\beta}{d\alpha} \, d\beta,$$

which is $\infty$ unless $\beta \ll \alpha$. By the law of large numbers $L_n \to \alpha$; Sanov's
theorem says that the probability of seeing an empirical distribution near $\beta$ instead is
about $e^{-n \, \mathrm{KL}(\beta \| \alpha)}$. This is how the Kullback–Leibler divergence of
information theory arises as a large deviation rate.

Varadhan's proof: for the upper bound on a small neighbourhood of $\beta$, choose $f$ nearly
optimal in the Donsker–Varadhan formula and apply Chebyshev's inequality to
$\exp(n \int f \, dL_n)$, whose expectation is $(\int e^f d\alpha)^n$; cover compact sets by finitely
many such neighbourhoods, and pass to closed sets by exponential tightness of $L_n$. For the lower
bound, change measure to the i.i.d. law $\beta^{\otimes n}$ and use the law of large numbers.

The statement also assumes that the open sets of the weak topology on probability measures are
measurable for the σ-algebra generated by the evaluations $\mu \mapsto \mu(B)$. This holds on
Polish spaces, where the weak topology is second countable and generated by such measurable maps.
