# Large deviations

Large deviation principles and rate functions, Cramér's theorem and Chernoff bounds for empirical means, log-moment generating functions and the Legendre–Fenchel transform used to build them.

The general principles come first: an LDP transfers along continuous maps (the [contraction principle](contraction-principle/README.md)), its exponential moments are computed by [Varadhan's integral lemma](varadhan-s-integral-lemma/README.md), and reweighting by $e^{nF}$ gives the [tilted LDP](tilted-large-deviation-principle/README.md). The basic examples are [Sanov's theorem](sanov-s-theorem/README.md) for empirical measures, whose rate is the relative entropy of the [Donsker–Varadhan variational formula](../../InformationTheory/information-inequalities/donsker-varadhan-variational-formula/README.md) (on a finite alphabet it follows by counting types, see the [method of types](../../InformationTheory/entropy-and-mutual-information/method-of-types/README.md)), and [Cramér's theorem](cram-r-s-theorem/README.md) for empirical means, whose rate is a Legendre transform; the [Gärtner–Ellis theorem](satisfies-ldp-of-tendsto-scaled-cgf.md) removes independence.
