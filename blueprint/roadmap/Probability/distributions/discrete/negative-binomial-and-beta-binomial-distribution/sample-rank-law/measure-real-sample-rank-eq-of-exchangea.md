# Beta-binomial law of the sample rank for exchangeable scores without ties

Let $m, N, r \in \mathbb{N}$ with $1 \le r \le m + 1$, and let $\mu$ be a probability measure on the score
space $\mathbb{R}^{\{0, \dots, m\} \sqcup \{1, \dots, N\}}$ that is invariant under every permutation
of the coordinates and under which the scores are almost surely pairwise distinct. Then the number
$J$ of synthetic scores below the $r$-th real score (the [sample rank](sample-rank.md)) has the
beta-binomial law: $\mu(J = j) = \mathrm{BB}(N; r, m + 2 - r)(j)$ for every $j \in \mathbb{N}$.
