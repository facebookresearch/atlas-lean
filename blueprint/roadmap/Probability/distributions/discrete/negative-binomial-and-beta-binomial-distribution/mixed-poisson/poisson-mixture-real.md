# Mixed Poisson mass function

For a measure $\nu$ on $\mathbb{R}$ (the mixing law of the rate) and $k \in \mathbb{N}$, the mixed Poisson
mass function is

$$p_\nu(k) = \int e^{-r} \frac{r^k}{k!}\, d\nu(r),$$

an integral of a non-integrable function being read as $0$. When $\nu$ is a probability measure
concentrated on $[0, \infty)$ the integrand lies in $[0, 1]$ $\nu$-almost everywhere, and $p_\nu$ is a
probability mass function on $\mathbb{N}$: the law of $N$ where $N \mid \Lambda \sim \operatorname{Poisson}(\Lambda)$
and $\Lambda \sim \nu$.

## Sources

- J. Grandell, *Mixed Poisson Processes*, Chapman & Hall, 1997, Chapter 2.
