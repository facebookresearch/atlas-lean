# Tail of a sum of independent Bernoulli variables is Poisson-binomial

Let $(\Omega, \mu)$ be a probability space and $X_0, \dots, X_{m-1}$ mutually independent measurable
$\mathbb{N}$-valued random variables, $X_i$ being Bernoulli with parameter $p_i \in [0, 1]$ (values
$0, 1$, with $\mu(X_i = 1) = p_i$). Then for every $k \in \mathbb{N}$,

$$\mu\Bigl(\sum_i X_i \ge k\Bigr) = T_k(p),$$

where $T_k(p)$ is the [Poisson-binomial upper tail](poisson-binomial-tail.md): the one-sided p-value
of a burden test.
