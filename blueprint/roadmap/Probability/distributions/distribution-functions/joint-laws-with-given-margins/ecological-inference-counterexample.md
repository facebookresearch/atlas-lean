# Group margins do not determine individual associations

Two populations of two individuals each, in one group, can have the same group-level margins of two binary characteristics $X, Y$ (one individual with $X = 1$ and one with $Y = 1$) but opposite individual-level associations. In the first population the individuals are $(X, Y) = (1, 1)$ and $(0, 0)$, so $\operatorname{Cov}(X, Y) = 1/2 - 1/4 = 1/4$. In the second they are $(1, 0)$ and $(0, 1)$, so $\operatorname{Cov}(X, Y) = 0 - 1/4 = -1/4$. Group margins alone therefore do not identify the individual-level joint distribution.
