# Integrals against the exponential law

Let $r > 0$. For every $f : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$,

$$\int f \, d\operatorname{Exp}(r) = \int_{(0, \infty)} r e^{-rx} f(x)\, dx,$$

where $\operatorname{Exp}(r)$ is the exponential law of density $r e^{-rx}$ on $[0, \infty)$. No
integrability hypothesis is needed: if $f$ is not integrable, both sides are $0$ by convention.
