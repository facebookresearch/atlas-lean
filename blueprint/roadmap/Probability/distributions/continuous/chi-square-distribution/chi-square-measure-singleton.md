# A chi-square law has no atoms

For $p \in \mathbb{N}$, let $\chi^2_p$ be the gamma law on $\mathbb{R}$ with shape $p/2$ and rate $1/2$, the measure with Lebesgue density $x \mapsto \frac{(1/2)^{p/2}}{\Gamma(p/2)} x^{p/2 - 1} e^{-x/2}$ for $x > 0$ and $0$ for $x < 0$ (for $p = 0$ this is the zero measure, with the conventions $\Gamma(0) = 0$ and division by $0$ giving $0$). For every $p \in \mathbb{N}$ and $x \in \mathbb{R}$,

$$\chi^2_p(\{x\}) = 0,$$

since $\chi^2_p$ has a density with respect to Lebesgue measure. In particular $\chi^2_1$ puts no mass at $0$, which separates it from the degenerate limit $\delta_0$.
