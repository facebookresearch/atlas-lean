# Has Deriv At std Normal cdf

Let $\Phi(x) = N(0, 1)((-\infty, x])$ be the standard normal cumulative distribution function. Then $\Phi$ is differentiable at every $x \in \mathbb{R}$ with derivative the standard normal density:

$$\Phi'(x) = \varphi(x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} e^{-x^2/2}.$$

This follows from the integral representation of $\Phi$, continuity of $\varphi$, and the fundamental theorem of calculus.
