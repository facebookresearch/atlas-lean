# Gamma law is concentrated on the nonnegative reals

For all real $a, b$, let $\Gamma(a, b)$ be the gamma measure on $\mathbb{R}$: the measure with density
$\max\bigl(0, \frac{b^a}{\Gamma(a)} x^{a-1} e^{-bx}\bigr)$ for $x \ge 0$ and $0$ for $x < 0$ with respect
to Lebesgue measure. Here $b^a$ and $x^{a-1}$ are real powers: the usual power of a positive base, $0^0 = 1$ and $0^s = 0$ for $s \ne 0$, and $x^s = |x|^s \cos(\pi s)$ for $x < 0$; $\Gamma$ is Euler's gamma function, taken
to be $0$ at $0, -1, -2, \dots$; and a quotient by $0$ is read as $0$. For $a, b > 0$ the expression in
the maximum is already positive for $x > 0$. For shape $a = -\frac12$ and rate $b = 1$ it is negative
there, and for $a = 0$, $b = 1$ it is $0$; in both cases $\Gamma(a, b)$ is the zero measure. For
$a = \frac13$ and $b = -1$, $b^a = \cos(\pi/3) = \frac12$, and the density is
$\frac{1}{2\Gamma(1/3)} x^{-2/3} e^{x}$ for $x > 0$. In every case $x \ge 0$ for $\Gamma(a, b)$-almost every $x$.
