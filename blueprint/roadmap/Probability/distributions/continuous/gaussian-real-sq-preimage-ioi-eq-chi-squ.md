For $p \in \mathbb{N}$, let $\chi^2_p$ be the gamma law on $\mathbb{R}$ with shape $p/2$ and rate $1/2$, the measure with Lebesgue density $x \mapsto \frac{(1/2)^{p/2}}{\Gamma(p/2)} x^{p/2 - 1} e^{-x/2}$ for $x > 0$ and $0$ for $x < 0$ (for $p = 0$ this is the zero measure, with the conventions $\Gamma(0) = 0$ and division by $0$ giving $0$). For every $c \in \mathbb{R}$, if $N(0,1)$ denotes the standard normal law on $\mathbb{R}$,

$$N(0, 1)\bigl(\{x : x^2 > c\}\bigr) = \chi^2_1\bigl((c, \infty)\bigr).$$

So the square of a standard normal variable exceeds $c$ with the same probability as a $\chi^2_1$ variable.
