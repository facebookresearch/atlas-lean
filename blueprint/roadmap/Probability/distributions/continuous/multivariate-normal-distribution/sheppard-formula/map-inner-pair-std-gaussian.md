# Two-dimensional marginal of the standard Gaussian

Let $r$ be a standard Gaussian vector in a finite-dimensional real inner product space and
$e_1, e_2$ orthonormal. Then $(\langle e_1, r\rangle, \langle e_2, r\rangle)$ has the law of two independent
standard normal variables. The proof maps $r$ to $\mathbb{R}^2$ and compares characteristic
functions: $\mathbb{E}\, e^{i \langle t, (\langle e_1, r\rangle, \langle e_2, r\rangle)\rangle} = e^{-\|t_1 e_1 + t_2 e_2\|^2/2} = e^{-\|t\|^2/2}$.
This is the rotation invariance that reduces questions about two directions to the plane.
