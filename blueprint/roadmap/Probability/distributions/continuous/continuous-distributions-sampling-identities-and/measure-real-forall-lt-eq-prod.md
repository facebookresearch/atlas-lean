# Joint survival of independent links is the product of survivals

Let $(T_i)_{i \in \mathbb{N}}$ be mutually independent real-valued functions on a measure space $(\Omega, P)$, independence meaning that the $\sigma$-algebras they generate are independent under $P$ (no further measurability of the $T_i$ is assumed), let $n \in \mathbb{N}$ and $x \in \mathbb{R}$. Then the outer mass of the event that each of the first $n$ link strengths exceeds the load $x$ is the product of the individual outer masses, where $P(A)$ is the outer measure of a set $A$ that need not be measurable, read as a real number. When these preimages are measurable, these are the joint and individual survival probabilities:

$$P\bigl( T_i > x \text{ for all } i < n \bigr) = \prod_{i=0}^{n-1} P(T_i > x).$$
