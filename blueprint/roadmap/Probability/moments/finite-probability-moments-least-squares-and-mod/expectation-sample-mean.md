# Expectation of the sample mean under a product weight

Let $\alpha$ be a finite set, $p$ a weight on $\alpha$ with $\sum_a p(a) = 1$, and $\iota$ a nonempty finite index set. Under the product weight $\omega \mapsto \prod_i p(\omega_i)$ on samples $\omega \in \alpha^\iota$, the expectation of the sample mean of $X(\omega_i)$ is $\mathbb{E}_p[X]$, for every $X : \alpha \to \mathbb{R}$. The product weight $\omega \mapsto \prod_i p(\omega_i)$ is the law of an i.i.d. sample only when $p$ is a probability law ($p \ge 0$ and $\sum_a p(a) = 1$); the identity itself is algebraic and does not use nonnegativity.
