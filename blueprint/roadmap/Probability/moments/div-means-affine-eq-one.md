# A ratio of means equal to one under an affine map

Let $X,Y$ be real-valued functions on a probability space with law $\mu$, and write $E[Z]=\int Z\,d\mu$ for the Bochner integral, taken as zero for nonintegrable functions. For real $a,b$, if $E[X]/E[Y]=1$ and $E[aY+b]\ne 0$, then

$$
\frac{E[aX+b]}{E[aY+b]}=1.
$$

The original ratio being one implies $E[Y]\ne 0$ by the convention $x/0=0$, and then $E[X]=E[Y]\ne 0$. Thus both $X$ and $Y$ are integrable. The slope $a$ may be zero.
