# Exact-size Neyman–Pearson test on a finite space

Let $\Omega$ be a finite nonempty set, $p, q : \Omega \to [0, \infty)$ with $\sum_x p(x) = \sum_x q(x) = 1$ (null and alternative), and $\alpha \in (0, 1)$. Then there exist $k \ge 0$, $\gamma \in [0, 1]$ and a test $\varphi^* : \Omega \to [0, 1]$ with $\varphi^* = 1$ where $k\,p < q$, $\varphi^* = 0$ where $q < k\,p$ and $\varphi^* = \gamma$ where $q = k\,p$, such that $\sum_x\varphi^*(x)p(x) = \alpha$ and $\sum_x\varphi(x)q(x) \le \sum_x\varphi^*(x)q(x)$ for every test $\varphi : \Omega \to [0, 1]$ with $\sum_x\varphi(x)p(x) \le \alpha$.

## Sources

- J. Neyman and E. S. Pearson (1933), DOI 10.1098/rsta.1933.0009; E. L. Lehmann and J. P. Romano, *Testing Statistical Hypotheses*, 3rd ed., Theorem 3.2.1 (i)–(ii).
