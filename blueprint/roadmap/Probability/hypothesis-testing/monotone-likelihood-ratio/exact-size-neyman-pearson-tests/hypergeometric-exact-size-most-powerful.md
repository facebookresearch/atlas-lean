# Exact-size most powerful test between two hypergeometric laws

Let $K_0, K_1, m \le N$ and $\alpha \in (0, 1)$, and let $p_0(j)$, $p_1(j)$ be the probabilities of $j \in \{0, \dots, m\}$ under the hypergeometric laws of the number of marked items in $m$ draws without replacement from $N$ items of which $K_0$, respectively $K_1$, are marked. Then, with $M = m$, there exist a threshold $k \ge 0$, a tie value $\gamma \in [0, 1]$ and a test $\varphi^* : \{0, \dots, M\} \to [0, 1]$ with $\varphi^*(j) = 1$ where $k\,p_0(j) < p_1(j)$, $\varphi^*(j) = 0$ where $p_1(j) < k\,p_0(j)$ and $\varphi^*(j) = \gamma$ where $p_1(j) = k\,p_0(j)$, such that $\sum_j\varphi^*(j)p_0(j) = \alpha$ and $\sum_j\varphi(j)p_1(j) \le \sum_j\varphi^*(j)p_1(j)$ for every test $\varphi : \{0, \dots, M\} \to [0, 1]$ with $\sum_j\varphi(j)p_0(j) \le \alpha$.
