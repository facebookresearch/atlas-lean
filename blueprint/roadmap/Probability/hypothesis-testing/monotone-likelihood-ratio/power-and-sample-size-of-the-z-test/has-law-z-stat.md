# Law of the z statistic under a normal sample

Let $n \ge 1$ and let $\mathcal N(\mu, v)^{\otimes n}$ denote the law of an i.i.d. sample of size $n$ from the normal law with mean $\mu$ and variance $v > 0$. For $x \in \mathbb{R}^n$, $\mu_0 \in \mathbb{R}$ and $v > 0$, write $z(x) = \sqrt n\,(\bar x - \mu_0)/\sqrt v$ for the known-variance z statistic, $\bar x = \frac1n\sum_i x_i$, and $\Phi$ for the standard normal distribution function. Then under $\mathcal N(\mu, v)^{\otimes n}$ the statistic $z$ has the normal law

$$\mathcal N\Big(\frac{\sqrt n\,(\mu - \mu_0)}{\sqrt v},\ 1\Big).$$
