# Cochran's Q is chi-square under homogeneity

Let $\mu$ be a probability measure on $\Omega$, $k \ge 2$, $\theta \in \mathbb{R}$, $v_1, \dots, v_k > 0$, and $X_1, \dots, X_k : \Omega \to \mathbb{R}$ mutually independent with $X_i \sim N(\theta, v_i)$. Then Cochran's statistic $Q = \sum_i v_i^{-1}(X_i - \sum_j w_j X_j)^2$, with the known variances $v_i$ and the inverse-variance weights $w$, has the chi-square law with $k - 1$ degrees of freedom.

## Sources

- W. G. Cochran, *The combination of estimates from different experiments*, Biometrics 10 (1954), 101–129.
