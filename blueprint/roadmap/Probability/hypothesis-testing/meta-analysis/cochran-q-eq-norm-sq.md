# Cochran's Q as a squared projection norm

Let $v_1, \dots, v_k > 0$, $\theta \in \mathbb{R}$ and $z \in \mathbb{R}^k$, and set $X_i = \theta + \sqrt{v_i}\, z_i$. Then Cochran's $Q$ of the estimates $X$ with variances $v$ is the squared distance from $z$ to the line spanned by $a = (v_i^{-1/2})_i$, that is, the squared Euclidean norm of the orthogonal projection of $z$ onto $a^\perp$:

$$Q = \|P_{a^\perp} z\|^2.$$

This is the algebraic core of Cochran's theorem for $Q$.
