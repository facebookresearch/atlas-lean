# Law of the observations with a change point

Let $P_0$ and $P_1$ be probability measures on a measurable space $\mathcal{X}$, and let the observations $X_1, X_2, \dots$ be the coordinates of $\omega \in \mathcal{X}^{\mathbb{N}}$. For a change point $\nu \ge 1$, the change-point law $\mathsf{P}_\nu$ is the product measure under which the observations are independent, $X_1, \dots, X_{\nu-1}$ have law $P_0$ and $X_\nu, X_{\nu+1}, \dots$ have law $P_1$ (so $X_\nu$ is the first post-change observation, the convention of Lai 2001, §3, and of Veeravalli–Banerjee, §III-A). For $\nu = 0$ every observation has law $P_1$.

## Sources

- Venugopal V. Veeravalli and Taposh Banerjee, *Quickest change detection*, arXiv:1210.5552v1, 2012, §III-A (the i.i.d. Bayesian setting).
- T. L. Lai, *Sequential analysis: some classical problems and new challenges*, Statistica Sinica 11 (2001) 303–408, §3, p. 315 (the model $P^{(\nu)}$).
