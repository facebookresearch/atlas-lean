# Cumulative log-likelihood ratio

Let $P_0$ and $P_1$ be mutually absolutely continuous probability measures on a measurable space $\mathcal{X}$, so that the likelihood ratio $L = dP_1/dP_0$ is positive and finite almost surely, and let $X_{i+1}(\omega) = \omega_i$ be the coordinates of $\omega \in \mathcal{X}^{\mathbb{N}}$. The cumulative log-likelihood ratio of the first $n$ observations is

$$\Lambda_n = \sum_{i=1}^n \log L(X_i), \qquad \Lambda_0 = 0,$$

the logarithm of the likelihood ratio $R_n = \prod_{i \le n} L(X_i)$; the Radon–Nikodym derivative is read as a real number and $\log 0 = 0$ by convention (both matter only on null sets).
