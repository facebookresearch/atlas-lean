# Benjamini–Hochberg over causal nulls in a randomized trial

On a probability space, let $T$ be a measurable treatment event with $0 < P(T) < 1$, independent of the potential outcomes of every causal-null endpoint (measurable and integrable). If the p-values are measurable, mutually independent and calibrated for the observed-data Horvitz–Thompson null, then for $q \in [0, 1]$ the Benjamini–Hochberg procedure has false discovery rate over the causal nulls at most $q|H_0|/m$.
