# Validity of the conditional binomial upper-tail p-value

Let $D$ and $B$ be random counts on a probability space with $D$ measurable, and $p \in [0, 1]$. Suppose that, for every $d$ with $P(D = d) > 0$, $B$ has law $\mathrm{Bin}(d, p)$ conditionally on $D = d$. Then $\omega \mapsto P_{\mathrm{Bin}(D(\omega), p)}\big(\{j : j \ge B(\omega)\}\big)$, the upper tail of $\mathrm{Bin}(D, p)$ at the observed count, is a valid p-value: $P(\text{p-value} \le t) \le t$ for every $t \in [0, 1]$.
