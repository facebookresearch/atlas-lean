# Disjoint-union family is no larger than the doubled family

Let $\alpha$ be a set and $\mathcal{A}, \mathcal{B}$ finite families of finite subsets of $\alpha$. The family of disjoint unions $\{S \cup T : S \in \mathcal{A},\ T \in \mathcal{B},\ S \cap T = \emptyset\}$ has at most as many members as the [doubled family](box-doubled.md) $\mathcal{A} \boxtimes \mathcal{B}$: distinct disjoint pairs can have the same union.
