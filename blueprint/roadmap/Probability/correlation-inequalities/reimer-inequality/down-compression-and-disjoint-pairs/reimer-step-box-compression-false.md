# Down-compression can shrink the disjoint-union family

There exist families $\mathcal{A}, \mathcal{B}$ of subsets of $\{0, 1\}$ and a coordinate $i \in \{0,1\}$ such that down-compressing both families at $i$ strictly decreases the size of the disjoint-union family:

$$|C_i\mathcal{A} \circ C_i\mathcal{B}| < |\mathcal{A} \circ \mathcal{B}|,$$

where $\mathcal{A} \circ \mathcal{B} = \{S \cup T : S \in \mathcal{A},\ T \in \mathcal{B},\ S \cap T = \emptyset\}$.
