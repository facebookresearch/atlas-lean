# Members of a disjoint-union family avoiding a coordinate

Let $\alpha$ be a set, $i \in \alpha$ and $\mathcal{A}, \mathcal{B}$ finite families of finite subsets of $\alpha$, and let $\mathcal{A} \circ \mathcal{B} = \{S \cup T : S \in \mathcal{A},\ T \in \mathcal{B},\ S \cap T = \emptyset\}$. The members of $\mathcal{A} \circ \mathcal{B}$ not containing $i$ are exactly the disjoint unions of members of $\mathcal{A}$ and of $\mathcal{B}$ not containing $i$:

$$\{U \in \mathcal{A} \circ \mathcal{B} : i \notin U\} = \{S \in \mathcal{A} : i \notin S\} \circ \{T \in \mathcal{B} : i \notin T\}.$$
