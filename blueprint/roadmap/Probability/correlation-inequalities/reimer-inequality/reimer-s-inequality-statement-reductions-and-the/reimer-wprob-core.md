# Statement of Reimer's inequality for product weights

The statement of Reimer's inequality for product probability weights on $\{0,1\}^n$: for every $n \in \mathbb{N}$, every weight $\varphi : \{0,\dots,n-1\} \times \{0,1\} \to \mathbb{R}$ with $\varphi_i(b) \ge 0$ and $\varphi_i(0) + \varphi_i(1) = 1$, and all events $A, B \subseteq \{0,1\}^n$,

$$P_\varphi(A \mathbin{\square} B) \le P_\varphi(A)\, P_\varphi(B), \qquad P_\varphi(S) = \sum_{\omega \in S} \prod_i \varphi_i(\omega_i).$$

This proposition (the van den Berg–Kesten conjecture, proved by Reimer in 2000) is the core case from which Reimer's inequality on an arbitrary finite set and for Bernoulli product measures is derived.
