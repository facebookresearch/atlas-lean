# Regularly varying tail integral

Let $\mu$ be a probability measure on $\mathbb{R}$ with right tail $R(x) = \mu((x, \infty))$ and
combined tail $T(x) = \mu((x, \infty)) + \mu((-\infty, -x))$. Let $0 < \alpha < 1$, suppose that
$T$ is regularly varying of index $-\alpha$, and that $\mu$ has tail balance $p$: $0 \le p \le 1$,
$T(x) > 0$ for all large $x$, and $R(x)/T(x) \to p$ as $x \to \infty$. Then for every $c > 0$,

$$\frac{\int_{(0, cx]} y \, d\mu(y)}{x\, T(x)} \to \frac{p\, \alpha\, c^{1 - \alpha}}{1 - \alpha} \qquad (x \to \infty).$$
