# Convolution powers of a Gaussian are Gaussian

For $\mu \in \mathbb{R}$, $v \ge 0$ and $n \in \mathbb{N}$, the $n$-fold convolution power of the
Gaussian law $N(\mu, v)$ is $N(n\mu, nv)$ (for $v = 0$, $N(\mu, 0) = \delta_\mu$, and the
$0$-fold power is $\delta_0$): the law of the sum of $n$ i.i.d. $N(\mu, v)$ variables is
$N(n\mu, nv)$.
