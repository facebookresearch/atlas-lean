# Mean step of three-series necessity

Let $\mu$ be a probability measure, let $Y_0, Y_1, \dots$ be independent measurable real random
variables and let $A \in \mathbb{R}$ with $|Y_n| \le A$ everywhere for every $n$. If
$\sum_n \operatorname{Var}(Y_n) < \infty$ and the partial sums $\sum_{i < n} Y_i$ converge almost
surely, then the partial sums $\sum_{k < n} \mathbb{E}[Y_k]$ of the means converge to a finite
limit. The proof centres the variables, applies the convergence theorem for centered sums with an
$L^1$ bound from the total variance, and reads the mean partial sums as the difference of the two
convergent series at a single sample point.
