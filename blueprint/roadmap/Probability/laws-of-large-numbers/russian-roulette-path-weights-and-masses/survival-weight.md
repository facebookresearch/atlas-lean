# Survival weight of a fixed-length path

For a real function $Q$ on $S \times S$ and a path $x = (x_0, \dots, x_n)$ of $n + 1$ states, the survival weight of $x$ is $\prod_{k<n} Q(x_k, x_{k+1})$, the product of the entries of $Q$ along its $n$ transitions. It is a raw real weight, with no sign or row condition: for one state with $Q(i,i) = -2$, the path $(i, i)$ has survival weight $-2$. Its reading as the probability of following $x$ without stopping, given its start, needs $Q$ to be a survival kernel: nonnegative entries and every row sum $\sum_y Q(x, y) \le 1$. Nonnegativity alone is not enough: for one state with $Q(i,i) = 2$, the path $(i, i)$ has survival weight $2$.
