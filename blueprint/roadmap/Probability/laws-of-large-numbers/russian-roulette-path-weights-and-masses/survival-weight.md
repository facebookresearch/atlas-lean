# Survival weight of a fixed-length path

For a real function $Q$ on $S \times S$ and a path $x = (x_0, \dots, x_n)$ of $n + 1$ states, the survival weight of $x$ is $\prod_{k<n} Q(x_k, x_{k+1})$, the product of the entries of $Q$ along its $n$ transitions. It is a raw real weight, with no sign or row condition: for one state with $Q(i,i) = -2$, the path $(i, i)$ has survival weight $-2$. When $Q$ is a survival kernel, with nonnegative entries and each row summing to at most $1$ (as $Q(i, j) = (1 - q(i))\,P(i, j)$ is for stop probabilities $q(i) \in [0, 1]$ and a stochastic $P$), it is the probability of following $x$ without stopping, given its start. Nonnegativity alone is not enough: for one state with $Q(i,i) = 2$, the path $(i, i)$ has survival weight $2$.
