# Expectation of a sample mean with a common mean

Let $(\Omega, \mu)$ be a measure space, $\iota$ a nonempty finite index set with $n$ elements, and $X_i$, $i \in \iota$, integrable real random variables with $\int X_i \, d\mu = m$ for every $i$. Then the sample mean has integral $m$: $\int \frac1n \sum_i X_i \, d\mu = m$. Neither independence nor a probability measure is assumed. In particular, for real random variables $X_0, X_1, \dots$ with $X_0$ integrable and every $X_i$ distributed as $X_0$, the mean $\frac1n \sum_{i<n} X_i$ of the first $n \ge 1$ observations has integral $\int X_0 \, d\mu$: identical distribution makes each $X_i$ integrable with the same integral.
