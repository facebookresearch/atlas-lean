# Partial sums of indicators

For sets $A_0, A_1, \dots$ in $\Omega$ and $n \in \mathbb{N}$, the partial sum $S_n = \sum_{i < n} \mathbf{1}_{A_i}$ is the real-valued function on $\Omega$ whose value at $\omega$ is the number of sets among $A_0, \dots, A_{n-1}$ that contain $\omega$.

## Idea

Counting how many events occur is the simplest sum of random variables. $S_n$ is nonnegative, nondecreasing in $n$ and at most $n$; for measurable sets and a probability measure it is square-integrable and, by linearity, $\mathbb{E}[S_n] = \sum_{i < n} P(A_i)$. Since $\mathbf{1}_A^2 = \mathbf{1}_A$, an indicator has variance $P(A)(1 - P(A)) \le P(A)$. Independent events have independent indicators, so under pairwise independence the covariances vanish and $\operatorname{Var}(S_n) \le \mathbb{E}[S_n]$: the count concentrates around its mean once the mean is large. This bound drives the strong law for pairwise independent events.
