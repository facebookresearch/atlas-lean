# Busy-period transform of the M/G/1 queue

In the M/G/1 queue customers arrive according to a Poisson process of rate $\lambda > 0$ and are served one at a time, first come first served, by a single server; the service times are i.i.d. copies of a nonnegative random variable $B$ with $E[B] > 0$, independent of the arrival process, with Laplace–Stieltjes transform $\tilde B(s) = E[e^{-sB}]$, and the occupation rate is $\rho = \lambda E[B] < 1$. The Laplace–Stieltjes transform $\widetilde{BP}(s) = E[e^{-s\,BP}]$ of the length of a busy period satisfies, for $s \ge 0$,

$$
\widetilde{BP}(s) = \tilde B\bigl(s + \lambda - \lambda\, \widetilde{BP}(s)\bigr),
$$

and it is the root of this equation with $0 \le \widetilde{BP}(s) \le 1$. The equation comes from the decomposition $BP = B_1 + BP_1 + \dots + BP_N$: the service time $B_1$ of the customer starting the busy period, followed by the i.i.d. busy periods generated by the $N$ customers who arrive during $B_1$, each distributed as $BP$. Differentiating at $s = 0$ gives the [mean busy period](mean-busy-period-of-the-mg1-queue.md) $E[B]/(1 - \rho)$. For exponential service times with rate $\mu$, $\widetilde{BP}(s) = \bigl(\lambda + \mu + s - \sqrt{(\lambda + \mu + s)^2 - 4\lambda\mu}\bigr)/(2\lambda)$.

## Expansion

This page becomes ready once these prerequisites are stated:

- the branching decomposition of a busy period into the busy periods started by the arrivals during the first service, in an order other than first come first served that leaves the busy period unchanged;
- uniqueness of the root in $[0, 1]$ of the functional equation for $s > 0$.
