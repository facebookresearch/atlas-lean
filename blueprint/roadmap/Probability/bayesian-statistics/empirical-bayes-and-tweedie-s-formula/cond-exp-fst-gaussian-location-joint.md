# Tweedie's formula, conditional expectation form

Let $g$ be a probability measure on $\mathbb R$ with $\int |\mu|\, g(d\mu) < \infty$ and let $v > 0$. Under the [joint law](gaussian-location-joint.md) of $(\mu, x)$ in the model $\mu \sim g$, $x \mid \mu \sim \mathcal N(\mu, v)$,

$$\mathbb E[\mu \mid x] = m(x) \quad \text{almost surely},$$

where $m$ is the Bayes ratio of the [posterior mean](gaussian-posterior-mean.md) and the conditional expectation is taken with respect to the $\sigma$-algebra generated by $x$. Combined with [Tweedie's formula](gaussian-posterior-mean-eq-add-mul-deriv.md), $\mathbb E[\mu \mid x] = x + v (\log f)'(x)$ almost surely: this is Efron (2011), equations (1.2)–(1.4), read as a statement about conditional expectations, with $\sigma^2 = v$.

The moment condition is needed for $\mathbb E[\mu \mid x]$ to exist as a conditional expectation of an integrable variable; the pointwise formula needs no moment.

The proof checks the defining property: for every measurable $A$, $\int_{x \in A} \mu = \int_{x \in A} m(x)$ under the joint law, by two applications of Fubini's theorem and $m(x) f(x) = \int \mu\, \varphi_v(x - \mu)\, g(d\mu)$.
