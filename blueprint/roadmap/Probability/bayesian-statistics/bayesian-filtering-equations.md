# Bayesian filtering equations

A *probabilistic state-space model* consists of states $x_0, x_1, \dots \in \mathbb{R}^n$ and measurements $y_1, y_2, \dots \in \mathbb{R}^m$, with the following densities with respect to fixed reference measures:
- a prior density $p(x_0)$;
- a *dynamic model* $p(x_k \mid x_{k-1})$;
- a *measurement model* $p(y_k \mid x_k)$.

The model satisfies two Markov properties:
- given $x_{k-1}$, the state $x_k$ is independent of $x_{0:k-2}$ and $y_{1:k-1}$;
- given $x_k$, the measurement $y_k$ is independent of $x_{0:k-1}$ and $y_{1:k-1}$.

The *filtering distribution* at step $k$ is the conditional law of $x_k$ given the whole measurement history $y_{1:k} = (y_1, \dots, y_k)$.

**Theorem.** The filtering densities satisfy the following recursion, starting from $p(x_0)$.
- *Prediction* (Chapman–Kolmogorov):

    $$
    p(x_k \mid y_{1:k-1}) = \int p(x_k \mid x_{k-1})\,p(x_{k-1} \mid y_{1:k-1})\,dx_{k-1}.
    $$

- *Update* (Bayes' rule): for every measurement $y_k$ whose normalizer $Z_k = \int p(y_k \mid x_k)\,p(x_k \mid y_{1:k-1})\,dx_k$ satisfies $0 < Z_k < \infty$,

    $$
    p(x_k \mid y_{1:k}) = \frac{1}{Z_k}\,p(y_k \mid x_k)\,p(x_k \mid y_{1:k-1}).
    $$

Integrals over discrete state components are sums. For linear dynamic and measurement models with Gaussian noise and a Gaussian prior, every filtering distribution is Gaussian, and the recursion reduces to the Kalman filter.

**Proof.** By the first Markov property,

$$
p(x_k, x_{k-1} \mid y_{1:k-1}) = p(x_k \mid x_{k-1})\,p(x_{k-1} \mid y_{1:k-1}),
$$

and integrating out $x_{k-1}$ gives the prediction. By the second Markov property, $p(y_k \mid x_k, y_{1:k-1}) = p(y_k \mid x_k)$, so Bayes' rule applied to $x_k$ given $y_{1:k-1}$ gives the update, with $Z_k = p(y_k \mid y_{1:k-1})$.

Without densities, the same recursion holds for regular conditional distributions given the $\sigma$-algebra generated by $y_{1:k}$. Closed forms beyond the linear-Gaussian case are rare, so nonlinear filters approximate this recursion.
