# Convergence of the projected power method for angular synchronization

In angular synchronization one observes $C = zz^* + \sigma W$, where $z \in \mathbb C^n$ has entries of modulus $1$, $\sigma \ge 0$, and $W$ is Hermitian with zero diagonal and independent entries $W_{kl}$, $k < l$, whose real and imaginary parts are independent $N(0, 1/2)$. Problem (P) maximizes $x^*Cx$ over vectors $x$ with $|x_k| = 1$; its maximizer is compared with others up to a global phase $e^{i\theta}$. The projected power method uses $\mathcal P(a)_k = a_k/|a_k|$ when $a_k \ne 0$ and $\mathcal P(a)_k = 1$ when $a_k = 0$, and iterates $x_{t+1} = \mathcal P(Cx_t)$ from a scaled leading eigenvector $x_0$ of $C$: a vector with $\|x_0\|_2^2 = n$ maximizing $x^*Cx$ among all such vectors. Call the method successful on $C$ if (P) has a maximizer $\hat x$ that is unique up to phase and, for every scaled leading eigenvector $x_0$, there are phases $\theta_t$ with $e^{i\theta_t}x_t \to \hat x$. The claim (Bandeira's Open Problem 10.1 at the noise scale of Zhong and Boumal) is that there is $c > 0$ such that whenever $0 \le \sigma_n \le c\sqrt{n/\log n}$ for all $n$, for any signals $z_n$, the probability (outer probability, if the event is not measurable) that the method is not successful on $C$ tends to $0$ as $n \to \infty$.
