# Tightness of the semidefinite relaxation of angular synchronization

In angular synchronization one observes $C = zz^* + \sigma W$, where $z \in \mathbb C^n$ has entries of modulus $1$, $\sigma \ge 0$, and $W$ is Hermitian with zero diagonal and independent entries $W_{kl}$, $k < l$, whose real and imaginary parts are independent $N(0, 1/2)$. Problem (P) maximizes $x^*Cx$ over vectors $x$ with $|x_k| = 1$; its maximizer is compared with others up to a global phase $e^{i\theta}$. The semidefinite relaxation maximizes $\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{tr}(CX)$ over Hermitian positive semidefinite $X$ with $X_{kk} = 1$; it is tight on $C$ if for some maximizer $\hat x$ of (P), unique up to phase, $\hat x\hat x^*$ is its unique maximizer. The claim (Bandeira's Open Problem 10.2 at the noise scale of Zhong and Boumal) is that there is $c > 0$ such that whenever $0 \le \sigma_n \le c\sqrt{n/\log n}$ for all $n$, for any signals $z_n$, the probability (outer probability, if the event is not measurable) that the relaxation is not tight tends to $0$.
