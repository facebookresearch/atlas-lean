# Maximum modulus of a polynomial with random coefficients

Let $\varepsilon_0, \varepsilon_1, \dots$ be real-valued functions on $\Omega$, let $\omega \in \Omega$ and $n \in \mathbb{N}$. The maximum on the unit circle of the polynomial of degree at most $n$ with coefficients $\varepsilon_k(\omega)$ is

$$M_n(\omega) = \sup_{z \in \mathbb{C},\ |z| = 1} \Big| \sum_{k=0}^{n} \varepsilon_k(\omega) z^k \Big|.$$

The coefficients are arbitrary real numbers: with $n = 1$, $\varepsilon_0 = 2$ and $\varepsilon_1 = 0$ the polynomial is the constant $2$. Its degree is exactly $n$ whenever the leading coefficient $\varepsilon_n(\omega)$ is nonzero. When the $\varepsilon_k$ are independent uniform random signs, as in Erdős's problem on its growth, each $\varepsilon_k$ is $\pm 1$ almost surely, so the polynomial is a random sign polynomial of degree exactly $n$ almost surely, though not necessarily at every outcome.
