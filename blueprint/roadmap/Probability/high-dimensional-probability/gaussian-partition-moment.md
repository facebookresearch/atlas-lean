# First moments of Gaussian partitions

In real Euclidean dimension $d$, use standard Gaussian probability $\gamma_d$. A partition into $k$ parts is a family of $k$ measurable sets such that almost every point is in exactly one. For each part put $b_i=\int 1_{A_i}(x)x\,d\gamma_d(x)$ (the Bochner integral). Its contribution is $\|b_i\|^2$, without division by the probability of the part. Empty parts contribute zero.
