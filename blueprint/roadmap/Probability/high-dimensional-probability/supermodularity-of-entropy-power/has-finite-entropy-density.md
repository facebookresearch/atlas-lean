# Real random variable with a finite-entropy density

Let $(\Omega, P)$ be a measure space, not necessarily a probability space, and $X : \Omega \to \mathbb{R}$ a function. The image measure $X_\# P$ is totalised: it is the usual pushforward when $X$ is almost everywhere measurable, the zero measure when $P = 0$, and otherwise an arbitrary Dirac mass. So it exists even when preimages under $X$ are not measurable, for example for a function taking the values $0$ and $1$ on a two-point probability space with the trivial $\sigma$-algebra. Let $g$ be the Radon–Nikodym derivative of $X_\# P$ with respect to Lebesgue measure, with values in $[0, \infty]$ and equal to $0$ when $X_\# P$ has no Lebesgue decomposition with respect to Lebesgue measure, and let $f = \tilde g$ be its real version, with $\tilde\infty = 0$. Then $X$ has a density of finite entropy if

- $X$ has a density with respect to Lebesgue measure: $X$ is almost everywhere measurable, so that $X_\# P$ is the usual pushforward, $X_\# P$ has a Lebesgue decomposition with respect to Lebesgue measure, and $X_\# P$ is absolutely continuous with respect to Lebesgue measure, hence equal to the measure with density $g$; and
- the real function $x \mapsto f(x) \log f(x)$ is Lebesgue integrable on $\mathbb{R}$, with $0 \log 0 = 0$.

The integrand uses the real version $f$, so a set where $g = \infty$ contributes nothing: for $P$ equal to Lebesgue measure with constant density $\infty$ and $X$ the identity, the first condition holds, $g = \infty$ almost everywhere and the integrand is zero. When the first condition holds and $P$ is a probability measure, $g$ is finite almost everywhere and $f$ is the usual probability density of the law of $X$. A probability law alone is not enough: for a constant $X$ the law is a point mass, the derivative $g$ is zero almost everywhere, and $f$ is not a probability density.
