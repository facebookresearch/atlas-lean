# Subsphere estimates and Gaussian streaming precision lower bounds

For the stopping experiment, use noiseless Gaussian observations and arbitrary PMFs of the (stop bit, state) initially and after each observed row and exact label, and an output probability measure on unit vectors depending on terminal time and state. A seed of arbitrary independent law selects the whole finite-state learner. Assume for almost every seed the transition atom probabilities are almost-everywhere measurable relative to Gaussian row times Lebesgue label, and for all radii the conditional success probabilities are almost-everywhere measurable in seed and row array at each fixed signal and jointly with a uniform sphere signal. The policy first maps a sampled initial (stop bit, state) to either an active state or a terminal tag with time zero. At step $t$ it leaves any tagged state fixed, and otherwise binds the row/label transition PMF, giving a stop at this step tag $(t,\mathrm{state})$. At the horizon tag every remaining active state with $T$ and bind the output law for the tag. The conditional success used here is the mass of the closed angular cap under that resulting law. The fixed-signal and uniform-prior success probabilities are lower integrals under the seed–row product and the sphere–(seed–row) product, respectively. In these two precision statements the completed transition condition and **both** experiment measurability fields apply even if only the prior-averaged lower bound on success is used.

* For any $M(d)=o(d^2)$ and all sufficiently large $d$, for $0<\varepsilon\le1/10$ and $2^{M(d)}$ states, angular success at least $2/3$ under uniform prior **or** separately at every unit signal implies $T\ge 2^{-16}d\log_2(1/\varepsilon)$. Under just the uniform prior, success at least $1/2$ implies $T\ge c d\log_2(1/\varepsilon)$ for some universal $c>0$, with the same subquadratic memory regime and sufficiently large dimension.
* For a test subsphere of dimension parameter $n$, choose a linear $n$-plane $V\subseteq\mathbb R^d$, $a\perp V$ and $r>0$ with $\|a\|^2+r^2=1$. Its law is $a+rU$ for $U$ uniform on the unit sphere of $V$. Define $\|g\|_{d,n}$ as the supremum over these tests of $r^{d/16}$ times the nonnegative lower integral of $g(a+rU)$. There are constants $C\ge1$ and a natural $d_0$ that work for all $d\ge d_0$ in both the block and terminal bounds that follow. For the block bound take any natural $d/2\le n\le d$, any finite nonempty index type of cardinal at most real $W\ge1$, measurable $0\le g_v\le1$ and nonnegative measurable transition weights $\kappa_v(X,y)$ with sum at most one, put $g(s)$ equal to the nonnegative lower integral in $X$ of $\sum_v\kappa_v(X,Xs)g_v(s)$ for $\lfloor d/16\rfloor$ standard Gaussian rows. Then $\|g\|_{d,n}\le W^{1/\lfloor d/16\rfloor}C^d\sup_v\|g_v\|_{d,n-2}$. For the terminal bound use the same $C$ and $d_0$: for every natural $d/2\le n\le d$, unit-sphere output probability $\pi$, and $\varepsilon>0$, the terminal function $s\mapsto\pi\{y:\arccos\langle s,y\rangle\le\varepsilon\}$ has norm at most $C^d\varepsilon^{d/16}$.
* For [fixed-horizon learners](models/subsphere-tests-and-fixed-horizon-streams.md) without a stop bit, the initial state and every next state have PMFs on a nonempty finite state type $J$ with measurable singletons, and each terminal state specifies a probability measure on the ambient Euclidean space. For the suffix bound require that for almost every seed all transition atom maps are Borel measurable in the observation, and that for every state its output law concentrates on unit vectors. There is no extra joint-success measurability premise. There is $d_0$ such that for $d\ge d_0$, $\lfloor(d+1)/2\rfloor+2k\le d$, and $|J|\le W$ with $W\ge1$ and $W^{1/\lfloor d/8\rfloor}\le2^d$, take a stream of $k\lfloor d/16\rfloor$ observations in $\mathbb R^d$. For each $\ell\le k$, each fixed starting state $v\in J$, and each affine subsphere above with $n\ge\lfloor(d+1)/2\rfloor+2\ell$, the suffix starts at the point mass at $v$, uses transition time $(k-\ell)\lfloor d/16\rfloor+t$ at step $t$, and has independently sampled Gaussian rows. Its angular success at $0<\varepsilon\le1/10$, taken by a nonnegative lower integral under the subsphere–(seed–rows) product law, is at most $(16(1000^{32})^\ell\varepsilon/r)^{2\lfloor d/32\rfloor}$.
* There exist $C\ge2$ and $d_0$ such that for all natural $d,j,m$ with $d\ge d_0$ and $d/2+2j\le m\le d$, and **every real $\eta>0$**, the following bound holds. Use a fixed-horizon stream in $\mathbb R^m$ with $2^{d^2}$ states and budget $j\lfloor d/32\rfloor$. Require all transition atom maps to be Borel measurable in the observation for almost every seed. The output probability laws are on $\mathbb R^m$, without a unit-norm restriction. Require almost-everywhere measurability of the conditional mass of the event $\|y-s\|\le\eta$ under the uniform sphere–(seed–rows) product law in dimension $m$. Its nonnegative lower integral under that product law is at most $(C^{j+1}\eta)^{d/32}$.

For the mixture clause let $E$ be a finite-dimensional real inner-product space, with its Borel measurable structure, $V\subseteq N$ subspaces, and $e$ a unit vector of $V$. Write $\sigma_E$ for sphere probability as a measure on $E$, and use normalized Haar orthogonals. Let $\lambda_{V,N}$ be the law of a uniform unit vector in $N$ rotated by the identity on $V$ and independent Haar on $V^\perp$. If $1\le\dim V<\dim N<\dim E$, define $\lambda_{\rm lat}$ by sampling $b\sim\operatorname{Beta}((\dim N-\dim V)/2,(\dim E-\dim N)/2)$, $z$ uniform on the unit sphere of $e^\perp$, and independent Haar on $V$. Apply that Haar (identity on $V^\perp$) to $\sqrt{1-b}\,e+\sqrt b\,z$. Then $\lambda_{V,N}\le 2^{\dim E}(\sigma_E+\lambda_{\rm lat})$ as measures. For $V=0$ and $\dim N\ge1$, $\lambda_{0,N}=\sigma_E$. For $N=E$ and $\dim E\ge1$, rotating any number $t$ of independent sphere draws by a **single shared** independent Haar on $V^\perp$ (identity on $V$) gives the product law $\sigma_E^{\otimes t}$.

**Conventions.** Use completed-rule, seeded finite-memory experiments, normalized sphere laws and orthogonal actions in subspaces, affine test spheres with their real radius, observation kernels on exact row/label pairs, and the Beta latitude law. All floor expressions are natural divisions, except explicit real exponents and real dimension inequalities.

## Proof depends on

The subspace and latitude disintegration bounds, radius-weighted block and terminal estimates, and a block-iteration argument preserving suffix state and time semantics. The uniform consequence at $1/2$ and the explicit $2/3$ bound require their own quantitative extraction from those estimates.

## Expansion

Separate mixture domination and its two boundary laws, then the weighted norm estimates, fixed-horizon suffix inequalities, and two seeded precision conclusions. Match the two notions of loss (chord distance and angle), use the specified width control, and retain the same shared rotation across the joint boundary experiment. Do not infer independence merely from identical one-coordinate marginals.
