# Hájek projection of a U-statistic

Let $\mu$ be a measure on $\alpha$, $h : \alpha^m \to \mathbb{R}$ a kernel and $x \in \alpha^n$ a sample. Let $\tilde h = (m!)^{-1} \sum_\sigma h(\cdot \circ \sigma)$ be the symmetrized kernel, $\tilde\theta = \int \tilde h \, d\mu^{\otimes m}$ and $\tilde h_1$ its first Hoeffding projection. The Hájek projection of the U-statistic with kernel $h$ is defined by the formula $\tilde\theta + \frac{m}{n} \sum_i (\tilde h_1(x_i) - \tilde\theta)$, with $m/n = 0$ when $n = 0$ by the convention for division by zero.

For an arbitrary measure this formula need not be a projection: on a one-point space with $\mu$ twice the unit point mass, $m = n = 2$ and the constant kernel $1$, the U-statistic is $1$ while the formula gives $4 + (2 - 4) + (2 - 4) = 0$. It is the orthogonal projection of the U-statistic onto sums of square-integrable functions of single observations when $\mu$ is a probability measure, the sample is i.i.d. with law $\mu$, $\tilde h$ is square integrable and $m \le n$. The U-statistic depends on $h$ only through $\tilde h$, which is why the formula is built from $\tilde h$; when $h$ is integrable, $\int \tilde h = \int h$, and when it is not, the integral is read as $0$ and the two can differ. For a symmetric kernel $\tilde h = h$. For $m > n$ the U-statistic is $0$ by convention and the formula is not its projection.
