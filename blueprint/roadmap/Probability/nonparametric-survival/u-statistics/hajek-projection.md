# Hájek projection of a U-statistic

Let $\mu$ be a measure on $\alpha$, $h : \alpha^m \to \mathbb{R}$ a kernel and $x \in \alpha^n$ a sample. Let $\tilde h = (m!)^{-1} \sum_\sigma h(\cdot \circ \sigma)$ be the symmetrized kernel, $\tilde\theta = \int \tilde h \, d\mu^{\otimes m}$ and $\tilde h_1$ its first Hoeffding projection. The Hájek projection of the U-statistic with kernel $h$ is $\tilde\theta + \frac{m}{n} \sum_i (\tilde h_1(x_i) - \tilde\theta)$, with $m/n = 0$ when $n = 0$ by the convention for division by zero.

The U-statistic depends on $h$ only through $\tilde h$, so building the projection and its centring from $\tilde h$ makes this the projection onto sums of functions of single observations for an arbitrary, not necessarily symmetric, kernel. When $h$ is integrable, $\int \tilde h = \int h$; when it is not, the integral is read as $0$ and the two can differ, which is why the centring uses $\tilde h$. For a symmetric kernel $\tilde h = h$. The intended regime is $m \le n$; for $m > n$ the U-statistic is $0$ by convention and this is not its projection.
