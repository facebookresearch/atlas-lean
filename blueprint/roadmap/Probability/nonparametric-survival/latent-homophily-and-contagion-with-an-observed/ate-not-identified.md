# The average effect is not identified from the observed law

In the two-mechanism example with latent coordinates (ego trait $a$, peer trait $b$, shared environment $e$) uniform on $\{0,1\}^3$, observed tie $\mathbf 1\{a = b\}$ and ego behavior $a \oplus e$, the homophily mechanism (peer behavior $b \oplus e$, unaffected by ego) and the contagion mechanism (on the tie the peer copies ego's intervention, off the tie it behaves as under homophily) induce the same joint law of the observed triple (tie, ego behavior, peer behavior) but different average effects of ego's behavior on the peer.

## Sources

- C. R. Shalizi and A. C. Thomas, "Homophily and contagion are generically confounded in observational social network studies", Sociological Methods & Research 40(2) (2011), 211–239.
