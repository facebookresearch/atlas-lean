# Delayed-entry product-limit estimate

Consider left-truncated survival data on increasing grid times $t_0 < \dots < t_{k-1}$: subject $i$ enters at a grid index $e_i$ (or before $t_0$), leaves at $t_{x_i}$ with $e_i < x_i$, with or without an event; $d_j$ and $c_j$ are the events and censorings at $t_j$ and $Y_j = \#\{i : e_i < j \le x_i\}$ the number at risk, with support $\mathrm{supp} = \{j : Y_j > 0\}$. The delayed-entry product-limit estimate is $\hat S(t) = \prod_{j\in\mathrm{supp},\,t_j\le t}(1 - d_j/Y_j)$, where grid times with nobody at risk are left out. This formula is defined for all data; only when the support is [gap-free](gap-free.md) is $\hat S$ an estimate of survival, conditional on reaching the first supported time and normalized there rather than at time zero. If the support has a gap, the product runs across disconnected stretches and has no survival interpretation.
