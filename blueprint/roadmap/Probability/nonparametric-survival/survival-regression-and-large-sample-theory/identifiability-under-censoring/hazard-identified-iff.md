# Characterization of hazard identifiability under interval censoring

Under interval censoring on a finite grid, an inspection set $S$ with law $p_S$ is independent of the event time $T \sim p_T$, and one observes $S$ together with the inspection times in $S$ at or after $T$. Say that a grid point $c$ is inspected with positive probability when $P(c \in S) > 0$. Write $h(j) = P(T = j) / P(T \ge j)$ for the [discrete hazard](../../censored-survival-data/hazard.md), with $0/0 = 0$, the value when $P(T \ge j) = 0$. The observed-data law determines the hazard at $j$ for every pair of event-time laws if and only if $P(j \in S) > 0$ or $j$ is the last grid point, and $P(j - 1 \in S) > 0$ or $j = 0$. At the last grid point the condition on $j - 1$ reflects only the convention: every law with $P(T \ge j) > 0$ has hazard $1$ there, and the two laws the observations fail to separate there, the point masses at $j - 1$ and $j$, differ only because the first has $P(T \ge j) = 0$.
