# The hazard is identified under an unknown censoring law

For finite measures $F, F', G, G'$ on $\mathbb{R}$, let the observed-data measures of $(F, G)$ and $(F', G')$, the images of $F \otimes G$ and $F' \otimes G'$ under $(t, c) \mapsto (t \wedge c, \mathbf 1\{t \le c\})$, both equal $P$. If $P([h, \infty) \times \{0, 1\}) \ne 0$, then $F$ and $F'$ have the same hazard measure on $(-\infty, h]$; the censoring measures $G$ and $G'$ may differ. When $G$ and $G'$ are probability laws, $P$ is the law of $(X, \delta) = (T \wedge C, \mathbf 1\{T \le C\})$ for independent $T$ and $C$, and the condition reads $P(X \ge h) \ne 0$.
