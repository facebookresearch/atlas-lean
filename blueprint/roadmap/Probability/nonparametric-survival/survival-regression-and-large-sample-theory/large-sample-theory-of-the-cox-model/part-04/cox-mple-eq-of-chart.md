# The Cox estimator is the chart root of the sample mean

Fix distinct grid times $t_1, \dots, t_k$ and distinct covariate levels $c_1, \dots, c_q$, and encode an observation $(T, d, z)$ by the indicators of an observed event at $t_j$ with covariate $c_a$ and of being at risk at $t_j$ with covariate $c_a$. For a vector $\mu$ of such coordinates let $S^{(0)}(\mu, b, j) = \sum_ae^{bc_a}\mu(\text{at risk}, j, a)$, $S^{(1)}(\mu, b, j) = \sum_ac_ae^{bc_a}\mu(\text{at risk}, j, a)$ and $\Psi(\mu, b) = \sum_{j,a}\mu(\text{event}, j, a)(c_a - S^{(1)}/S^{(0)})$, a quotient with $S^{(0)} = 0$ being $0$. Let $\varepsilon > 0$ and let $g$ be a chart around $\theta$: for every coordinate vector $\mu$ within $\varepsilon$ of $\theta$, $\Psi(\mu, g(\mu)) = 0$ and $g(\mu)$ is the only root of $\Psi(\mu, \cdot)$ within $\varepsilon$ of $g(\mu)$. If $n \ge 1$, the first $n$ observed times lie among the $t_j$, their covariates lie among the $c_a$, and their encoded mean $\bar x_n$ lies within $\varepsilon$ of $\theta$, then the Cox estimator of the first $n$ observations equals $g(\bar x_n)$.
