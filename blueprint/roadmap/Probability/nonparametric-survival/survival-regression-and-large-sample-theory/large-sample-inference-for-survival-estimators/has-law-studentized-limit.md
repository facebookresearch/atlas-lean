# The studentized Kaplan–Meier limit is standard normal

Let $p_T$ and $p_C$ be probability mass functions on $\{0, \dots, k-1\}$ and let $(T, C)$ have the independent censoring law (independent $T \sim p_T$ and $C \sim p_C$), with observed cell $(\min(T, C), \mathbf 1\{T \le C\})$ and cell indicator $e_{\mathrm{cell}(T, C)}$; let $p$ be its vector of cell probabilities, $e_j = p(j, 1)$ and $Y_j = \sum_{l\ge j}(p(l, 1) + p(l, 0))$, and suppose $P(\min(T, C) \ge m) \ne 0$. The Kaplan–Meier map at $m$ is $g(v) = \prod_{j\le m}\big(1 - v(j, 1)/\sum_{l\ge j}(v(l, 1) + v(l, 0))\big)$; $L$ denotes its derivative at $p$ and $\tau^2 = \sum_{j\le m}e_j/(Y_j(Y_j - e_j))$ the population Greenwood sum, a term with zero denominator being $0$. Let $G$ be a random vector in cell space every projection $\langle G, t\rangle$ of which is centered normal with variance $\mathrm{Var}\langle e_{\mathrm{cell}(T, C)}, t\rangle$; no sample of subjects is involved. If $S = P(T > m) \ne 0$ and $\tau^2 > 0$, then $LG/\sqrt{S^2\tau^2}$ is standard normal.
