# Studentized Kaplan–Meier limit

Let subjects $Z_i = (T_i, C_i)$ take values in $\{0, \dots, k-1\}^2$, with observed cell $(\min(T_i, C_i), \mathbf 1\{T_i \le C_i\})$; let $p$ be the vector of cell probabilities, $\hat p_n$ the empirical cell proportions of the first $n$ subjects, $e_j = p(j, 1)$ and $Y_j = \sum_{l\ge j}(p(l, 1) + p(l, 0))$. Suppose the $Z_i$ are independent with the independent censoring law (independent $T \sim p_T$ and $C \sim p_C$), and $P(\min(T, C) \ge m) \ne 0$. The Kaplan–Meier map at $m$ is $g(v) = \prod_{j\le m}\big(1 - v(j, 1)/\sum_{l\ge j}(v(l, 1) + v(l, 0))\big)$; $L$ denotes its derivative at $p$ and $\tau^2 = \sum_{j\le m}e_j/(Y_j(Y_j - e_j))$ the population Greenwood sum, a term with zero denominator being $0$. Let $\hat V_n(m)$ be Greenwood's variance estimate for the first $n$ subjects. If $S = P(T > m) \ne 0$, $\tau^2 > 0$ and $G$ is an almost everywhere measurable random vector in cell space whose projections $\langle G, t\rangle$ are centered normal with the variance of $\langle e_{\mathrm{cell}(Z_0)}, t\rangle$, then $\sqrt n(\hat S_n(m) - S)/\sqrt{n\hat V_n(m)} \Rightarrow LG/\sqrt{S^2\tau^2}$, where the ratio is $0$ whenever $n\hat V_n(m) = 0$ (division by zero gives zero). Positive $\tau^2$ does not exclude a vanishing sample variance.
