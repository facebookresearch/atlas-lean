# Marked compensator uniqueness for a single-jump history

On a finite measure space, let $X$ be a random time and $Z$ a process with each $Z_u$ measurable, with values in a measurable space with measurable singletons, such that $Z_u = z_0$ exactly when $u < X$ and $Z_u = Z_s$ whenever $X \le s \le u$. Let $\mathcal F_t = \sigma(Z_u : u \le t)$ be its natural history and $\mathcal F_{t-} = \bigvee_{s<t}\mathcal F_s$. A càdlàg compensator of a real process $N$ here is an $\mathcal F_{t-}$-measurable real process $A$ with right-continuous paths, almost every path of locally finite variation and tending to $0$ at $-\infty$, such that $N - A$ is a martingale. On a countably generated mark space $E$, let $A_t$ and $A'_t$ be finite measures on $E$ (for each $t$ and outcome), and for each measurable $B \subseteq E$ let $N(B)$ be a real process. If for every measurable $B$ both $t \mapsto A_t(B)$ and $t \mapsto A'_t(B)$ are càdlàg compensators of $N(B)$, so that $N(B) - A_\cdot(B)$ and $N(B) - A'_\cdot(B)$ are martingales, then almost surely $A_t = A'_t$ for all $t$.
