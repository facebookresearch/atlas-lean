# Dividing a centered Gaussian variable by a nonnegative constant

Let $(\Omega', P')$ be a measure space, $Z : \Omega' \to \mathbb{R}$, and $\sigma^2, v \ge 0$ real numbers. Write $N(0, s)$ for the centered Gaussian law on $\mathbb{R}$ with variance $s \ge 0$, where $N(0, 0)$ is the point mass at $0$. Say that a function $Y$ has law $\nu$ under $P'$ if $Y$ is almost everywhere measurable and the image measure of $P'$ under $Y$ is $\nu$.

If $Z$ has law $N(0, \sigma^2)$ under $P'$, then $\omega \mapsto Z(\omega)/v$ has law

$$
N\Bigl(0, \frac{\sigma^2}{v^2}\Bigr)
$$

under $P'$. Division uses $x/0 = 0$, so for $v = 0$ the variable $Z/v$ is identically $0$ and $\sigma^2/v^2 = 0$; the statement holds for every $v \ge 0$, including $v = 0$.
