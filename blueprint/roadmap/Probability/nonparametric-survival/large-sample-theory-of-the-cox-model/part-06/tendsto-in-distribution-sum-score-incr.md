# Score central limit theorem from counting-process conditions

In the discrete-time Cox counting-process model on a probability space, for each $n$ there are $n$ subjects with observed grid times $t_i \in \mathbb{N}$, event indicators $\delta_i$ and covariates $|z_i| \le B$ known at time $0$, observed through a filtration $(\mathcal F_k)$; subject $i$ is at risk at step $k$ when $k + 1 \le t_i$ ($Y_i(k)$, predictable) and has an event there when $t_i = k + 1$ and $\delta_i$ ($\Delta N_i(k)$, adapted); the intensity is $\mathbb{E}[\Delta N_i(k) \mid \mathcal F_k] = Y_i(k)e^{\beta_0z_i}\Delta\alpha_k$ with $\Delta\alpha_k \ge 0$; and almost surely at most one event occurs per step. Write $S^{(j)}(b, k) = \sum_lY_l(k)z_l^je^{bz_l}$ and $V(b, k) = S^{(2)}/S^{(0)} - (S^{(1)}/S^{(0)})^2$. Let $I^*_n(b) = \frac1n\sum_{k<K_n}\Delta\alpha_kV(b, k)S^{(0)}(\beta_0, k)$ be the normalized information compensator. If $I^*_n(\beta_0) \to v$ in probability, then $n^{-1/2}\sum_{k<K_n}$ (score increments at $\beta_0$) converges in distribution to $N(0, v)$.
