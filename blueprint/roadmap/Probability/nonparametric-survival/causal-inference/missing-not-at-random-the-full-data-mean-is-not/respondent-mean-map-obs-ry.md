# The respondents' mean is a functional of the observed-data law

Full-data laws are measures on $\{0,1\} \times \mathbb R$, a point $(r, y)$ recording the response indicator $r$ ($r = 1$ for a respondent) and the outcome $y$. The observed data of $(r, y)$ are $o(r, y) = (r, y)$ if $r = 1$ and $o(r, y) = (0, 0)$ if $r = 0$; the observed-data law of $P$ is the pushforward $P \circ o^{-1}$. For any measure $P$ on $\{0,1\} \times \mathbb R$, the respondents' mean $m(P) = \frac{\int_{\{r = 1\}} y \, dP(r, y)}{P(r = 1)}$ (with $x/0 = 0$, the mass $P(r = 1)$ read as a real number, $0$ when infinite, and the integral $0$ when the integrand is not integrable) is a functional of the observed-data law:

$$m(P) = m(P \circ o^{-1}).$$
