# Complete-case identification

Let $\mu$ be a probability measure on a measurable space $\Omega$, $R \subseteq \Omega$ measurable and $Y : \Omega \to \mathbb R$ measurable and integrable. Suppose $R$ is independent of $Y$, in the form: for every measurable $B \subseteq \mathbb R$, $\mu(R \cap \{Y \in B\}) = \mu(R)\, \mu(Y \in B)$, and $\mu(R) > 0$. Then the complete-case mean equals the full-data mean:

$$\frac{\int \mathbf 1_R\, Y \, d\mu}{\mu(R)} = \int Y \, d\mu.$$
