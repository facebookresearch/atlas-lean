# Completeness cannot be dropped

Let $\mathcal{Y}$ and $\mathcal{Z}$ be finite sets with measurable singletons, and let $P$ be a probability measure on $\{\mathrm{true}, \mathrm{false}\} \times \mathcal{Y} \times \mathcal{Z}$, whose coordinates are the response indicator $R$, the outcome $Y$ and the shadow variable $Z$. Write $p(r, y, z) = P(\{(r, y, z)\})$ for the point masses. The observed-data map sends $(r, y, z)$ to $(z, y)$ if $r = \mathrm{true}$ and to $(z, \ast)$ if $r = \mathrm{false}$, with values in $\mathcal{Z} \times (\mathcal{Y} \sqcup \{\ast\})$. Suppose $P$ satisfies the shadow assumption: for every measurable $B \subseteq \mathcal{Z}$ and every $y$, $P(R = \mathrm{true}, Z \in B, Y = y)\, P(Y = y) = P(R = \mathrm{true}, Y = y)\, P(Z \in B, Y = y)$; that $P(R = \mathrm{true}, Y = y) = \sum_{z} p(\mathrm{true}, y, z) \neq 0$ and $P(R = \mathrm{false}, Y = y) = \sum_{z} p(\mathrm{false}, y, z) \neq 0$ for every $y$; and that $P$ is not complete, so some nonzero $g : \mathcal{Y} \to \mathbb{R}$ has $\sum_{y} p(\mathrm{true}, y, z)\, g(y) = 0$ for all $z$. Then there is a probability measure $Q \neq P$ on the same space that satisfies the shadow assumption and has the same image measure under the observed-data map as $P$.
