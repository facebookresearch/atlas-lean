# Mean waiting time under a scaled deterministic hazard

For real functions $h : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ and a real scale $c$, the mean waiting time is $\int_{(0,\infty)} \exp\bigl(-c \int_0^t h(r)\, dr\bigr)\, dt$, a Bochner integral read as $0$ when the integrand is not integrable, where $\int_0^t h(r)\, dr$ is the interval integral, likewise read as $0$ when $h$ is not integrable on $[0, t]$. When $c\,h$ is a nonnegative, locally integrable hazard, the integrand is the survival function of that deterministic hazard, and when it is moreover integrable the value is the ordinary mean waiting time; for other inputs it need not be a survival function (for $h = 1$ and $c = -1$ the integrand is $e^t > 1$).
