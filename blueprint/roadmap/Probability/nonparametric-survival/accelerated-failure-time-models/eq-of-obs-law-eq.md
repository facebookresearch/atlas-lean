# Identifiability of the log-linear model for one covariate value

Let $\mu_0$ be a finite error measure on $\mathbb{R}$ whose survival function $z \mapsto \mu_0(z, \infty)$ is strictly decreasing, and let $\mu_C$ be a nonzero finite log-censoring measure on $\mathbb{R}$; the observed-data measure of a pair $(\nu, \mu)$ of measures on $\mathbb{R}$ is the image of $\nu \otimes \mu$ under $(a, c) \mapsto (\min(a, c), \mathbf 1\{a \le c\})$. For $\sigma, \sigma' > 0$ let $\nu$ and $\nu'$ be the log-linear measures, the images of $\mu_0$ under $e \mapsto \eta + \sigma e$ and $e \mapsto \eta' + \sigma' e$. If the observed-data measures of $(\nu, \mu_C)$ and $(\nu', \mu_C)$ agree, then $\eta = \eta'$ and $\sigma = \sigma'$. When $\mu_0$ and $\mu_C$ are probability laws, these are the laws of $(\min(\log T, \log C), \mathbf 1\{T \le C\})$ for $\log T = \eta + \sigma\varepsilon$, $\varepsilon \sim \mu_0$, and an independent $\log C \sim \mu_C$.
