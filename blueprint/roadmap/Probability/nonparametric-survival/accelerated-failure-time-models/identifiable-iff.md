# Full column rank characterizes identifiability

Let $\mu_0$ be a finite error measure whose survival function $z \mapsto \mu_0(z, \infty)$ is strictly decreasing, let each subject $i$ have covariates $x_i \in \mathbb{R}^p$ and a nonzero finite log-censoring measure $\mu_{C,i}$, and fix a common known scale $\sigma > 0$; the observed-data measure of a pair $(\nu, \mu)$ of measures on $\mathbb{R}$ is the image of $\nu \otimes \mu$ under $(a, c) \mapsto (\min(a, c), \mathbf 1\{a \le c\})$, and $\nu_i(\beta)$ is the image of $\mu_0$ under $e \mapsto \langle\beta, x_i\rangle + \sigma e$. The regression coefficients are identified, that is, agreement of the observed-data measures of $(\nu_i(\beta), \mu_{C,i})$ and $(\nu_i(\beta'), \mu_{C,i})$ for every subject implies $\beta = \beta'$, if and only if the design matrix $(x_i)$ has full column rank, i.e. $\beta \mapsto (\langle\beta, x_i\rangle)_i$ is injective. When $\mu_0$ and the $\mu_{C,i}$ are probability laws, the observed-data measures are the laws of $(\min(\log T_i, \log C_i), \mathbf 1\{T_i \le C_i\})$ for $\log T_i = \langle\beta, x_i\rangle + \sigma\varepsilon_i$ and independent $\log C_i \sim \mu_{C,i}$.
