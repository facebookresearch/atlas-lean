# The economic order quantity

Let demand occur at constant rate $D > 0$, with setup cost $K > 0$ per order, holding cost $h > 0$ per unit and unit time, instantaneous replenishment and no shortages. Ordering $Q > 0$ units whenever stock reaches zero gives cycles of length $Q/D$ in which stock falls linearly from $Q$ to $0$; each cycle costs $K + hQ^2/(2D)$, so the long-run average cost is $KD/Q + hQ/2$. This function of $Q > 0$ is strictly convex and is uniquely minimized at $Q^\star = \sqrt{2KD/h}$, with minimum $\sqrt{2KDh}$. Quantity discounts, finite replenishment rates and planned shortages are outside this statement.
