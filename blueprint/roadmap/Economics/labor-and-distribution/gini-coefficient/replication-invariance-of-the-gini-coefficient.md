# Replication invariance of the Gini coefficient

For $n \ge 1$ incomes $y_1, \dots, y_n \ge 0$ with mean $\mu > 0$, let $G(y) = \sum_{i,j}|y_i - y_j|/(2n^2\mu)$, the population-normalized Gini coefficient. Then $G(cy) = G(y)$ for every $c > 0$, and replicating the population $k \ge 1$ times (each income appearing $k$ times) leaves $G$ unchanged, since the double sum is multiplied by $k^2$ and $n^2$ becomes $k^2n^2$. The bias-corrected variant $\sum_{i,j}|y_i - y_j|/(2n(n-1)\mu)$, defined for $n \ge 2$, is scale invariant but not replication invariant: for $y = (0, 1)$ it is $1$, and for the twice-replicated $(0, 0, 1, 1)$ it is $2/3$.
