# Second-derivative test for the surplus

Let `A` and `L` be real functions of the rate and `S(r) = A(r) - L(r)` their surplus.
Derivatives here follow the convention that a function's derivative at a point where it is
not differentiable is `0`, so `S′` is defined everywhere and `S″(r₀)` is the derivative of
`S′` at `r₀`. If `S(r₀) = 0`, `S′(r₀) = 0`, `S″(r₀) > 0` and `S` is continuous at `r₀`, then
`S(r) ≥ 0` for all rates `r` sufficiently close to `r₀`. The hypotheses constrain only the
difference `A - L`, not `A` and `L` themselves: they do not make the asset or liability value
differentiable, so on their own they do not certify the Redington conditions on `A` and `L`.
