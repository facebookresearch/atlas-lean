# Costly information and strategic responses can make information harmful

(Costly information.) A decision maker faces a state $\theta \in \{0, 1\}$ with probability $1/2$ each and earns $1$ for choosing the action equal to $\theta$ and $0$ otherwise. Without information the expected payoff is $1/2$; free perfect information raises it to $1$; perfect information bought at price $c > 1/2$ lowers it to $1 - c < 1/2$.

(Strategic response.) A seller owns an asset worth $\theta \in \{0, 2\}$ to her and $\theta + 1$ to a buyer, with probability $1/2$ each. The buyer first chooses whether to offer to buy at price $1.5$; then the seller accepts or rejects. Solutions are sequential equilibria: the seller's acceptance decision is sequentially rational, and an uninformed seller's belief after an offer, on or off the equilibrium path, is the prior $1/2$, since the buyer has no signal about $\theta$. If the seller is uninformed, she accepts (since $1.5 > E[\theta] = 1$), the buyer expects $E[\theta] + 1 - 1.5 = 0.5 > 0$ and offers, and the seller's payoff is $1.5$. If it is common knowledge that the seller observes $\theta$, she accepts only when $\theta = 0$, the buyer's expected payoff from offering is $\tfrac12(1 - 1.5) = -0.25 < 0$, the buyer does not offer, and the seller's expected payoff is $E[\theta] = 1 < 1.5$. In each case this equilibrium is unique. Free information has nonnegative value to a single decision maker who can ignore it and whose information others do not respond to; these conditions are sufficient, not necessary.
