# Existence of market-clearing positive prices, assuming Brouwer

In an exchange economy with finite sets `I` of consumers and `L` of goods, with at least one good,
assume Brouwer's fixed-point property for the price simplex
`Δ = {p | p l ≥ 0 for every l, ∑ₗ p l = 1}`: every map `ℝ^L → ℝ^L` that is continuous on `Δ` and
maps `Δ` into `Δ` has a fixed point in `Δ`. Suppose each consumer's demand `xᵢ` is continuous on `Δ`
and exhausts the budget there: `p · xᵢ(p) = p · ωᵢ` for every `p ∈ Δ`. Suppose moreover that every
good with price zero is in strict excess demand: `p ∈ Δ` and `p l = 0` imply `z(p) l > 0`, where
`z(p) = ∑ᵢ (xᵢ(p) − ωᵢ)` is aggregate excess demand. Then some `p ∈ Δ` with `p l > 0` for every good
clears all markets: `z(p) = 0`.
