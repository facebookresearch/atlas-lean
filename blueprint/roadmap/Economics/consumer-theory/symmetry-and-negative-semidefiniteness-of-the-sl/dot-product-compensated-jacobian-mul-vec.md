# Negative semidefiniteness of the substitution matrix

For finitely many goods, let `e` be a function of prices and `h` a compensated demand function; let
`U` be an open set of prices containing `p`, and suppose Shephard's lemma holds on `U`: for every
`q ∈ U` the function `e` has derivative `v ↦ h(q) · v` at `q`, i.e. its gradient at `q` is the
compensated demand `h(q)`. Suppose `h` has derivative `Dh` (a linear map) at `p`. If moreover `e` is
concave on `U`, then the substitution matrix `S i j = (Dh eⱼ) i` is negative semidefinite:
`x · (S x) ≤ 0` for every vector `x`.
