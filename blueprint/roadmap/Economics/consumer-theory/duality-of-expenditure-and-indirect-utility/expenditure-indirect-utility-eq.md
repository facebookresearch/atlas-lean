# Expenditure at the indirect utility level equals wealth

Expenditure–utility duality. For finitely many goods, let the utility `u` be locally nonsatiated on
the consumption set `X` (near every bundle of `X`, in the sup distance, there is a bundle of `X` of
strictly higher utility), and let the utility-maximization problem at `(p, w)` attain its optimum at
some Marshallian demand `x`. Then `e(p, v(p, w)) = w`: the least cost
`inf {p · y | y ∈ X, u(y) ≥ v(p, w)}` of reaching the indirect utility level `v(p, w)` equals
wealth. No sign condition on prices is needed.
