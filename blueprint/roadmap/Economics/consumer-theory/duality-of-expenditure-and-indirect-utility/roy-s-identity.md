# Roy's identity

Let $u$ be a utility function on $\mathbb{R}^L_{\ge 0}$ and $v(p, w) = \sup\{u(x) : x \ge 0,\ p \cdot x \le w\}$ its indirect utility, for prices with all $p_\ell > 0$. If $\bar x$ attains $v(\bar p, \bar w)$ with $\bar p \cdot \bar x = \bar w$, $v$ is differentiable at $(\bar p, \bar w)$ and $\partial v/\partial w(\bar p, \bar w) \ne 0$, then $\bar x_\ell = -(\partial v/\partial p_\ell)/(\partial v/\partial w)$ for every good $\ell$; in particular the demand is single-valued there. Proof: $g(p) = v(p, p \cdot \bar x) - v(\bar p, \bar w) \ge 0$, since $\bar x$ remains affordable, with $g(\bar p) = 0$, so $\nabla g(\bar p) = 0$.
