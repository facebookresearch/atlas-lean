# Roy's identity

Let $u$ be a utility function on $\mathbb{R}^L_{\ge 0}$, $v(p, w) = \sup\{u(x) : x \ge 0,\ p \cdot x \le w\}$ the indirect utility, and $e(p, \bar u) = \inf\{p \cdot x : u(x) \ge \bar u\}$ the expenditure function, on prices with all $p_\ell > 0$. (Roy.) If $\bar x$ attains $v(\bar p, \bar w)$ with $\bar p \cdot \bar x = \bar w$, $v$ is differentiable at $(\bar p, \bar w)$ and $\partial v/\partial w(\bar p, \bar w) \ne 0$, then $\bar x_\ell = -(\partial v/\partial p_\ell)/(\partial v/\partial w)$ for every good $\ell$; in particular the demand is single-valued there. Proof: $g(p) = v(p, p \cdot \bar x) - v(\bar p, \bar w) \ge 0$, since $\bar x$ remains affordable, with $g(\bar p) = 0$, so $\nabla g(\bar p) = 0$. (Shephard.) If $\bar h$ attains $e(\bar p, \bar u)$ and $e(\cdot, \bar u)$ is differentiable at $\bar p$, then $\partial e/\partial p_\ell(\bar p, \bar u) = \bar h_\ell$, because $p \mapsto p \cdot \bar h - e(p, \bar u) \ge 0$ vanishes at $\bar p$.
