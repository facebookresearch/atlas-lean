# Conditional survival probabilities from a life table

Let $n \ge 0$ and let $l_0 \ge l_1 \ge \dots \ge l_n \ge 0$ be a finite life table of real numbers with $l_0 > 0$. Define a law with a death outcome in $(k, k+1]$ of mass $(l_k - l_{k+1})/l_0$ for each $k < n$ and one residual survivor outcome of mass $l_n/l_0$, placed after date $n$; with strict survival $\{T > t\}$, $P(T > k) = l_k/l_0$ for $k \le n$. Then for integer ages $x \le x + k \le n$ with $l_x > 0$, $P(T > x + k \mid T > x) = l_{x+k}/l_x$. The table makes no claim about survival beyond date $n$.
