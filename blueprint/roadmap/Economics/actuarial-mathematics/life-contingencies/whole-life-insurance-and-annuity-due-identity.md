# Whole life insurance and the annuity-due identity

With the notation of the finite identity, suppose $\sum_k v^kS_k < \infty$, $\sum_k v^{k+1}(S_k - S_{k+1}) < \infty$ and $v^nS_n \to 0$. Then the whole-life values, defined as the limits $A_\infty = \lim_n A(n)$ and $\ddot a_\infty = \lim_n \ddot a(n)$ of the finite expected present values, satisfy $A_\infty = 1 - (1 - v)\ddot a_\infty$. The summability and the vanishing of $v^nS_n$ are hypotheses: they are not automatic for every $v > 0$ (for example $v > 1$ with $S_k = 1$ for all $k$ fails them).
