# Lundberg's inequality for the classical risk process

Let $T_0, T_1, \dots$ be independent interarrival times, each with the exponential law of rate $\lambda > 0$, with arrival times $S_n = T_0 + \dots + T_{n-1}$ ($S_0 = 0$) and right-continuous counting process $N_t = \#\{n \ge 1 : S_n \le t\}$, so that the $n$-th claim is counted from its epoch $S_n$ on. Let the claims $X_1, X_2, \dots \ge 0$ be i.i.d. and independent of the interarrival times, and for an initial capital $u \ge 0$ and premium rate $c > 0$ let $U_t = u + c t - \sum_{i=1}^{N_t} X_i$ be the classical risk process. Under the hypotheses of the existence theorem for the adjustment coefficient, with adjustment coefficient $R > 0$, the probability of ruin satisfies

$$\psi(u) = P(U_t < 0 \text{ for some } t \ge 0) \le e^{-R u}.$$

With this convention $U_{S_n} = u + c S_n - \sum_{i=1}^{n} X_i$ includes the $n$-th claim, and since $U$ increases between claim epochs, ruin can occur only at a claim epoch. For exponential claims $\psi(u) = (\lambda E[X]/c)\, e^{-R u}$, so the exponent $R$ cannot be improved.

## Expansion

A sound expansion specifies the following prerequisites and links their statements.

- The claim-epoch process $M_n = e^{-R U_{S_n}}$, $n \in \mathbb{N}$, is a nonnegative martingale for the filtration generated by $T_0, \dots, T_{n-1}$ and $X_1, \dots, X_n$: its ratio $M_{n+1}/M_n = e^{R(X_{n+1} - c T_n)}$ is independent of the past with mean $M(R)\,\lambda/(\lambda + c R) = 1$, which is $h(R) = 0$.
- The continuous-time ruin event equals the event that $U_{S_n} < 0$ for some $n \ge 1$, using the right-continuous count above and the almost-sure finiteness of every $N_t$.
- Ville's maximal inequality for a nonnegative supermartingale, $P(\sup_n M_n \ge a) \le E[M_0]/a$, applied with $M_0 = e^{-R u}$ and $a = 1$.
