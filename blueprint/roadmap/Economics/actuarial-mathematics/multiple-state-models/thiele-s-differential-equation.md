# Thiele's differential equation for a deterministic two-state policy

Let $T > 0$, let $\delta, \mu, \pi, b$ be real functions continuous on $[0, T]$, and let $g$ be real. Put $F(t, s) = \exp(-\int_t^s(\delta + \mu))$ and $V(t) = \int_t^T F(t, s)(\mu(s)b(s) - \pi(s))\,ds + F(t, T)g$. Then $V(T) = g$, and for each $0 < t < T$, $V$ is differentiable at $t$ with $V'(t) = (\delta(t) + \mu(t))V(t) + \pi(t) - \mu(t)b(t)$: since $\partial_t F(t, s) = (\delta(t) + \mu(t))F(t, s)$ and $F(t, t) = 1$, differentiating under the integral and the fundamental theorem of calculus give $V'(t) = (\delta(t) + \mu(t))V(t) - (\mu(t)b(t) - \pi(t))$. The identity needs no sign condition on $\delta$ or $\mu$, so it covers the nonnegative interest rate and intensity of the insurance application. This has the form of Thiele's differential equation for the prospective reserve in the two-state (alive, dead) case: in the continuous-time insurance model of Coffie, Duedahl and Proske, take states alive and dead, interest rate $r = \delta$, exit intensity $\lambda = \mu$, payment stream $dB_{\mathrm{alive}} = -\pi\,dt$, death benefit $b$ and reserve $0$ after death. It is an analytic statement about the integral $V$: identifying $V$ with the expected present value of an insurance liability needs a lifetime law with hazard $\mu$ and an exchange of expectation and integral, which are not part of it.

## Sources

- E. Coffie, S. Duedahl and F. Proske, "Thiele's differential equation based on Markov jump processes with non-countable state space", preprint, 2021, arXiv:2102.10047, <https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/97192/1/Coffie_etal_arXiv_2021_Thieles_differential_equation_based_on_Markov_jump_processes.pdf>, §2.2, Theorem 2.2.1 and Example 2.2.2 (countable state space); the statement above is the analytic identity for the integral, derived here directly, while their theorem concerns the insurance reserve. The derivatives in their proof of Theorem 2.2.1 are computed from the exponential weights alone, with no sign condition on the rates, and their Definition 2.1.1 allows a discount rate of either sign.
