# Thiele's differential equation for a deterministic two-state policy

Let $T > 0$ and let $\delta, \mu, \pi, b$ be continuous on $[0, T]$ with $\mu \ge 0$ and $\delta \ge 0$, and let $g$ be real. Put $F(t, s) = \exp(-\int_t^s(\delta + \mu))$ and $V(t) = \int_t^T F(t, s)(\mu(s)b(s) - \pi(s))\,ds + F(t, T)g$. Then $V(T) = g$ and for each $0 < t < T$, $V'(t) = (\delta(t) + \mu(t))V(t) + \pi(t) - \mu(t)b(t)$: since $\partial_t F(t, s) = (\delta(t) + \mu(t))F(t, s)$ and $F(t, t) = 1$, differentiating under the integral and the fundamental theorem of calculus give $V'(t) = (\delta(t) + \mu(t))V(t) - (\mu(t)b(t) - \pi(t))$. This has the form of Thiele's differential equation for the prospective reserve in the two-state (alive, dead) case: in the continuous-time insurance model of Coffie, Duedahl and Proske, take states alive and dead, interest rate $r = \delta$, exit intensity $\lambda = \mu$, payment stream $dB_{\mathrm{alive}} = -\pi\,dt$, death benefit $b$ and reserve $0$ after death. It is an analytic statement about the integral $V$: identifying $V$ with the expected present value of an insurance liability needs a lifetime law with hazard $\mu$ and an exchange of expectation and integral, which are not part of it.
