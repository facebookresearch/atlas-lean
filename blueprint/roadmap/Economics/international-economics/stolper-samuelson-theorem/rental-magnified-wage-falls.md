# Magnification effect for the capital-intensive good

Let goods prices `p = (p₁, p₂)` determine factor prices `f(p) = (w, r)` through both
zero-profit conditions `c₁(f(p)) = p₁` and `c₂(f(p)) = p₂` for `p` near `p₀` (locally
diversified production), where `c₁` and `c₂` are the unit cost functions of the two
goods. Assume that at `f(p₀) = (w, r)` the unit bundle `(aLᵢ, aKᵢ)` is cost-minimizing for
`cᵢ` (`i = 1, 2`), that `c₁`, `c₂` are differentiable at `f(p₀)` and `f` at `p₀`, that
`w > 0`, `r > 0`, `aK₁ > 0`, `aL₂ > 0` and `aK₂ > 0`, and that good 1 is labor-intensive
at `p₀`: `aL₂ * aK₁ < aL₁ * aK₂`, that is `aL₂ / aK₂ < aL₁ / aK₁`.
Then a rise in the price of the capital-intensive good 2 raises the rental rate
more than proportionally and lowers the wage: `(p₂ / r) * ∂r/∂p₂ > 1` and
`∂w/∂p₂ < 0` at `p₀`.
