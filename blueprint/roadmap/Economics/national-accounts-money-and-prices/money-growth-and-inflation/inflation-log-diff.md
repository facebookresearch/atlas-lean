# Log-differenced inflation–money-growth relation

Let `M(s) * V = P(s) * Y(s)` for every `s`, with a single constant velocity
`V ≠ 0`. If `M`, `P` and `Y` are differentiable at `t` and `M(t)`, `P(t)` and
`Y(t)` are nonzero, then inflation equals money growth minus real-output growth:
`P'(t) / P(t) = M'(t) / M(t) - Y'(t) / Y(t)`. Velocity constancy is built in: `V`
is a constant, not a function of `t`.
