# GDP by production, income and expenditure agree

Consider an input–output table over a finite set of sectors, with gross outputs
`X(i)`, intermediate sales `Z(i, j)` from sector `i` to sector `j`, final
expenditures `F(i)` and incomes `W(j)`. If the table balances by rows,
`X(i) = ∑_j Z(i, j) + F(i)` for every `i` (each sector's gross output is sold to
other sectors or to final users), and by columns, `X(j) = ∑_i Z(i, j) + W(j)` for
every `j` (each sector's gross output pays for its intermediate inputs, and the
rest is income), then GDP measured by production, by income and by expenditure
is the same:

`∑_j (X(j) - ∑_i Z(i, j)) = ∑_j W(j) = ∑_i F(i)`.

The second equality is where the accounting matters: intermediate sales appear
in both balances and cancel in the totals.
