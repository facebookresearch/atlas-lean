# Infinite dividend-discount identity under the price recursion

Let `0 < 1 + r`, and let `P t` and `D t` be the price and the dividend at date `t`. If prices
satisfy the one-period recursion `P t = (D (t+1) + P (t+1)) / (1 + r)` for every `t`, the
discounted terminal price `P n / (1 + r)^n` tends to `0` as `n → ∞` (no bubble), and the
dividends `D t`, `t ≥ 1`, are nonnegative, then the series of discounted dividends
`∑_{t=1}^{∞} D t / (1 + r)^t` converges, with sum `P 0`, today's price. It follows from the
finite identity `P 0 = ∑_{t=1}^{n} D t / (1 + r)^t + P n / (1 + r)^n` by letting `n → ∞`.
