# Modigliani–Miller I: debt plus equity is priced as the unlevered firm

Modigliani and Miller 1958, Proposition I. Consider a one-period market with a finite, nonempty
set of states `Ω` and a finite set of securities `A`, with payoffs `X(a, ω)` and prices `p(a)`,
and a gross risk-free return `R > 0`. Assume no funded arbitrage: no portfolio `h` has funded
gain `∑_a h(a) * (X(a, ω) - R * p(a))` nonnegative in every state `ω` and positive in some state.
Let security `u` be the unlevered firm, with operating payoff `V(ω)`, and let securities `d` and
`e` be the debt with face value `F` and the equity of the levered firm, with payoffs
`min(V(ω), F)` and `max(V(ω) - F, 0)` in every state. Then the levered firm's value, debt plus
equity, equals the price of the unlevered firm: `p(d) + p(e) = p(u)`.
