# Pre-trade endowment swaps cannot make a whole coalition strictly better off

Suppose a nonempty coalition $S$ of agents permutes its endowments among its members before top trading cycles is run with truthful preferences. Then not all members of $S$ are strictly better off than under honest TTC; equivalently, under any nontrivial pre-trade permutation some agent who traded its endowment is no better off. In contrast, there are markets in which such a permutation makes all members of $S$ weakly better off and at least one strictly better off.

## Proof sketch

Consider the first TTC round of the honest run in which a member of $S$ leaves; in the manipulated run that member's honest house is still available when it points, so it cannot end strictly better unless another member loses its honest house.
