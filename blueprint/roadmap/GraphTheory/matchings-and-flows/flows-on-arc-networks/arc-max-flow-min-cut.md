# Max-flow min-cut theorem for arc networks

Let $V$ and $E$ be finite sets and let each arc $e \in E$ run from its tail $t(e) \in V$ to its head $h(e) \in V$; parallel arcs and loops are allowed. Let $B$ be the signed incidence matrix of the network, $B_{ve} = [h(e) = v] - [t(e) = v]$, where $[P]$ is $1$ if $P$ holds and $0$ otherwise, let $u : E \to \mathbb{R}$ be arc capacities with $u(e) \ge 0$ for every arc, and let $s, t \in V$ with $s \ne t$. Call $f : E \to \mathbb{R}$ arc-feasible if $0 \le f(e) \le u(e)$ for every arc and $(Bf)_v = 0$ for every $v \ne s, t$; its value is $-(Bf)_s$, the flow out of $s$ minus the flow into $s$. An $s$-$t$ cut is a set $S \subseteq V$ with $s \in S$ and $t \notin S$, and its capacity is $\sum_{e \,:\, t(e) \in S,\ h(e) \notin S} u(e)$.

Then there is an arc-feasible $f$ whose value is at least the value of every arc-feasible flow and at most the capacity of every $s$-$t$ cut, and there is an $s$-$t$ cut whose capacity equals the value of $f$. So the maximum value of an arc-feasible $s$-$t$ flow equals the minimum capacity of an $s$-$t$ cut; this holds also when $E$ is empty, with parallel arcs, loops and zero capacities.
