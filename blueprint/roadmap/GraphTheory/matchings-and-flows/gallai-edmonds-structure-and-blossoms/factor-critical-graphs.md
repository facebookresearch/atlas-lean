# Factor-critical graphs and near-perfect matchings

A finite simple graph $H$ is *factor-critical* if for every vertex $v$ of $H$ the graph $H - v$ has a perfect matching. A *near-perfect matching* of a graph on $2k + 1$ vertices is a matching with $k$ edges, that is, a matching missing exactly one vertex. A factor-critical graph has an odd number of vertices and is connected: if $C_1, C_2$ were distinct components, choosing $v \in C_2$ would force $C_1$ to have a perfect matching and choosing $v \in C_1$ would force $C_2$ to have one, while $|C_1| + |C_2|$ is odd. Every odd cycle and every complete graph on an odd number of vertices is factor-critical.
