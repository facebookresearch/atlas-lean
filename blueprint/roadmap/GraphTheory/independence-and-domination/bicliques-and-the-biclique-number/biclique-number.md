# Biclique number of a graph

Let $G$ be a simple graph on a finite vertex set $V$. The biclique number $\omega_b(G)$ is the supremum, in the natural numbers, of the integers $t$ for which there are $c, d \ge 1$ with $c + d = t$ such that $G$ contains a $(c, d)$-biclique (disjoint vertex sets of sizes $c$ and $d$ with every vertex of one adjacent to every vertex of the other).

Such $t$ are at most $|V|$, so when $G$ has an edge $\omega_b(G)$ is the largest such $t$ (and is at least $2$); when $G$ has no edge the set is empty and $\omega_b(G) = 0$, the convention for an empty supremum.
