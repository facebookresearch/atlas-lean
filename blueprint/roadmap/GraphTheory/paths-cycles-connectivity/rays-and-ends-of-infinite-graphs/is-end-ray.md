# Ray of an end

For a simple graph $G$ and an arbitrary sequence $r_0$ of vertices, a sequence $s$ is a ray of the end of $r_0$ when $s$ is a ray of $G$ (an injective sequence with consecutive vertices adjacent) and $r_0$ and $s$ are [equivalent](rays-equiv.md). The sequence $r_0$ need not be a ray: on the one-way infinite path, $r_0(n) = 2n$ is not a ray, yet it is equivalent to the ray $s(n) = n$. When $r_0$ is a ray, the rays $s$ are exactly the rays of the end of $r_0$: no finite vertex set separates the tails of $r_0$ and $s$.
