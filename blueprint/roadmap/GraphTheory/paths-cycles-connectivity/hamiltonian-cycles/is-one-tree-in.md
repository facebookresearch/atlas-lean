# 1-tree within an edge set

For a vertex $v_0$ and finite sets $A$ and $T$ of unordered pairs of $\{0, \dots, n-1\}$, $T$ is a 1-tree with special vertex $v_0$ within $A$ when $T \subseteq A$; the simple graph formed by the members of $T$ not containing $v_0$, restricted to the vertices other than $v_0$, is a tree; and exactly two members of $T$ contain $v_0$. Two conventions matter when $T$ contains diagonal pairs $\{x, x\}$. The simple graph drops them: with $n = 3$ and $v_0 = 0$, the set $A = T = \{\{0,1\}, \{0,2\}, \{1,2\}, \{1,1\}\}$ passes the test, although keeping the loop at $1$ would not give a tree. The count of members containing $v_0$ is taken on the raw set, where $\{v_0, v_0\}$ counts once. When $A$ consists of edges of $K_n$, as in the successive-shortest statements, this is the usual loop-free 1-tree.
