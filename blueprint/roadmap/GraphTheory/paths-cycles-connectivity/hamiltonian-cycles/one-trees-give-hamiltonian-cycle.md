# Goldengorin's question 3 (Hamiltonian cycles from shortest 1-trees)

The statement that there is a natural number $k$ such that for every $n$, every vertex $v_0$ and every assignment $w$ of positive real weights to the unordered pairs of $\{0, \dots, n-1\}$, whenever $T_0, \dots, T_{k-1}$ are successive shortest 1-trees with special vertex $v_0$ of the complete graph $K_n$ (each $T_i$ a 1-tree with special vertex $v_0$ avoiding the edges of $T_0, \dots, T_{i-1}$, of total weight at most that of every such 1-tree, chosen arbitrarily among the lightest), the graph on $\{0, \dots, n-1\}$ whose edges are those of $T_0, \dots, T_{k-1}$ has a Hamiltonian cycle; here a 1-tree with special vertex $v_0$ is a set of edges whose edges not at $v_0$ form a spanning tree of the other vertices, together with exactly two edges at $v_0$. Stated here as a proposition.
