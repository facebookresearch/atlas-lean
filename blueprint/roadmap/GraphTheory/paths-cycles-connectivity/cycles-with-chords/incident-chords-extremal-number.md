# Erdős Problem 767 (cycles with many chords at one vertex)

The statement that for every natural number $k \ge 1$ and all sufficiently large $n$, every simple graph on $\{0, \dots, n-1\}$ with no cycle having $k$ chords at one of its vertices has at most $(k+1)n - (k+1)^2$ edges, and some such graph has exactly $(k+1)n - (k+1)^2$ edges. Here a cycle through $v$ has $k$ chords at $v$ when $k$ distinct vertices of the cycle are adjacent to $v$ by edges that are not edges of the cycle. Stated here as a proposition; its resolution: [Resolution of Erdős Problem 767](incident-chords-extremal-number-holds.md).
