# Resolution of Erdős Problem 150

There is a real $\alpha < 2$ such that $c(n)^{1/n} \to \alpha$ as $n \to \infty$, where $c(n)$ is the largest number of minimal cuts of a simple graph on $\{0, \dots, n-1\}$, and a set $T$ of vertices of a simple graph $G$ is a minimal cut when the subgraph induced on the complement of $T$ is not connected (it has two vertices joined by no path; a graph with at most one vertex counts as connected) while for every proper subset $S \subsetneq T$ the subgraph induced on the complement of $S$ is connected. That is, the proposition [Erdős Problem 150 (growth of the number of minimal cuts)](minimal-cuts-exponential-rate-below-two.md) holds.
