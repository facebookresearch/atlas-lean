# The friendship paradox

Let $G$ be a finite graph with $n$ vertices and degrees $d_v$, with at least one edge. Choose an edge uniformly at random and then one of its two endpoints uniformly; the chosen vertex is $v$ with probability $d_v/\sum_w d_w$, so its expected degree is $\sum_v d_v^2/\sum_v d_v$. By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality this is at least the average degree $\sum_v d_v/n$, with equality exactly when all vertices have the same degree. This is the degree-biased mean of this sampling scheme; it is not the average over vertices of the mean degree of their neighbours.
