# Tiles of two-ended highly arc transitive digraphs need not be unions of complete bipartite graphs

It is not the case that for every digraph $D$ whose underlying undirected graph is connected and has exactly two ends, if $D$ is highly arc transitive (for every $s \ge 0$ the automorphisms act transitively on $s$-arcs, sequences $x_0 \to x_1 \to \dots \to x_s$ with $x_i \ne x_{i+2}$), then for every map $b : V \to \mathbb{Z}$ with finite level sets $\{v : b(v) = i\}$ and $b(v) = b(u) + 1$ for every arc $u \to v$, every integer $i$ and all vertices $u, u'$ at level $i$ and $v, v'$ at level $i + 1$: if $u \to v$, $u' \to v$ and $u \to v'$, then $u' \to v'$. That is, some such digraph has a tile, the bipartite digraph of arcs from one level to the next, containing arcs $u \to v$, $u' \to v$ and $u \to v'$ but not $u' \to v'$, so that tile is not a disjoint union of complete bipartite graphs. Hence the proposition [Cameron–Praeger–Wormald conjecture on tiles of two-ended highly arc transitive digraphs](two-ended-tiles-complete-bipartite.md) is false.
