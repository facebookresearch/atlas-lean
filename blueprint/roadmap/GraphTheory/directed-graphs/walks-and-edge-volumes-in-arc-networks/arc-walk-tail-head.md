# The first arc of a walk leaves its origin

Let $V$ and $E$ be sets and let each arc $e \in E$ run from its tail $t(e) \in V$ to its head $h(e) \in V$. A list of arcs is a walk from $o$ to $d$ if it is empty only when $o = d$, its first arc leaves $o$, the head of each arc is the tail of the next, and its last arc enters $d$. If $a :: l$, the list with first arc $a$ followed by $l$, is a walk from $o$ to $d$, then $t(a) = o$.
