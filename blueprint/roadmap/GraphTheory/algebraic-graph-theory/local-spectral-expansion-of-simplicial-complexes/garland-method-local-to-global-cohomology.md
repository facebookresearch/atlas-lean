# Garland's method: local spectral gaps force vanishing cohomology

If $X$ is a pure $d$-dimensional complex in which the $1$-skeleton of every link of a $(k-1)$-face has normalized spectral gap greater than $1 - \frac{1}{k+1}$, then $H^{k}(X; \mathbb{R}) = 0$. In particular quotients of Bruhat-Tits buildings of large thickness have vanishing real cohomology in the intermediate dimensions.

## Proof sketch

Decompose the norm of the coboundary $\|\delta f\|^2$ and of the codifferential $\|\delta^* f\|^2$ of a $k$-cochain $f$ into sums over links of $(k-1)$-faces. In each link the restricted cochain is a function on vertices, and the local spectral gap bounds its energy from below in terms of its variance. Summing, a harmonic cochain must vanish when the local gaps exceed the stated threshold.
