# Rank-three graph

A simple graph $G$ has rank $3$ when its automorphism group is transitive on the vertices, on the ordered pairs of adjacent vertices and on the ordered pairs of distinct non-adjacent vertices: for any two ordered pairs $(u, v)$, $(u', v')$ of the same kind (both diagonal, both adjacent, or both distinct and non-adjacent) some automorphism sends $u$ to $u'$ and $v$ to $v'$.

This predicate asks only for transitivity within each of the three classes of ordered pairs; it does not require the adjacent or the distinct non-adjacent class to be nonempty. So it agrees with the conventional notion of rank $3$ (exactly three orbits on ordered pairs) when $G$ has both an edge and a pair of distinct non-adjacent vertices; for example, $K_3$ satisfies it but has only two orbits on ordered pairs.
