# Resolution of Kourovka Problem 19.98(a)

It is not the case that for every finitely presented group $H$, every finite generating set $S$ of $H$ and every simple graph $\Delta$ on a finite vertex set, there are finitely many graphs $\Sigma_1, \dots, \Sigma_k$ such that every symmetrical extension of the Cayley graph of $(H, S)$ by $\Delta$ is isomorphic to one of them. Here the Cayley graph joins distinct $x, y \in H$ when $y = xs$ or $x = ys$ for some $s \in S$, and a simple graph $\Sigma$ is a symmetrical extension of a simple graph $\Gamma$ by a simple graph $\Delta$ when $\Sigma$ is connected and there are a group $G$ of automorphisms of $\Sigma$ acting transitively on its vertices and a $G$-invariant partition $\sigma$ of its vertices (an imprimitivity system) such that the quotient graph $\Sigma/\sigma$ (distinct blocks adjacent when an edge joins them) is isomorphic to $\Gamma$ and every block induces a subgraph of $\Sigma$ isomorphic to $\Delta$. That is, the proposition [Kourovka Problem 19.98(a) (Trofimov)](finitely-many-symmetrical-extensions.md) is false.
