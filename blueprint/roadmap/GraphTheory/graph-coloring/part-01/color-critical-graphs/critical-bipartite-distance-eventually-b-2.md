# Rödl–Tuza theorem on critical graphs close to bipartite

For every natural number $k \ge 4$ and all sufficiently large $n$, some $k$-critical simple graph on $\{0, \dots, n-1\}$ becomes bipartite after deleting $\binom{k-1}{2}$ edges, and no $k$-critical simple graph on $\{0, \dots, n-1\}$ becomes bipartite after deleting fewer: every $2$-colouring of its vertices leaves at least $\binom{k-1}{2}$ edges inside its colour classes. Here a simple graph $G$ on $\{0, \dots, n-1\}$ is $k$-critical when its chromatic number is $k$ and every subgraph of $G$ other than $G$ itself, viewed as a graph on its own vertex set, has chromatic number less than $k$. That is, the proposition [Rödl–Tuza value of the bipartite distance of critical graphs](critical-bipartite-distance-eventually-b.md) holds.
