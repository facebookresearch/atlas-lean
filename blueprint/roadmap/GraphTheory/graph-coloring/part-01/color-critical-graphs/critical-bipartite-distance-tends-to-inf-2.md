# Resolution of Erdős Problem 744

It is not the case that there is a natural number $K$ such that for every $k \ge K$ and every natural number $M$, for all sufficiently large $n$, every $k$-critical simple graph on $\{0, \dots, n-1\}$ needs at least $M$ edge deletions to become bipartite: every $2$-colouring of its vertices has at least $2M$ ordered pairs of adjacent vertices of equal colour. Here a simple graph $G$ on $\{0, \dots, n-1\}$ is $k$-critical when its chromatic number is $k$ and every subgraph of $G$ other than $G$ itself, viewed as a graph on its own vertex set, has chromatic number less than $k$. That is, the proposition [Erdős Problem 744 (distance of critical graphs from bipartite)](critical-bipartite-distance-tends-to-inf.md) is false.
