# Grünbaum's conjecture is false

It is not the case that the dual of every a simple loopless triangulation of a closed orientable surface, given as an oriented combinatorial map on a finite set $D$ of darts: a permutation $\alpha$ of $D$ that is an involution without fixed points (the two darts of an edge) and a permutation $\sigma$ (rotation of the darts around their vertex) such that the group generated by $\alpha$ and $\sigma$ acts transitively on $D$ (the surface is connected); $(\sigma\alpha)^3$ is the identity and $\sigma\alpha$ has no fixed point, so every face (cycle of $\sigma\alpha$) is a triangle; no dart $d$ has $\alpha(d)$ in its own $\sigma$-orbit (no loops); and two darts with the same $\sigma$-orbit whose reverses also share a $\sigma$-orbit are equal (no multiple edges). Vertices are the $\sigma$-orbits, edges the pairs $\{d, \alpha(d)\}$ and faces the cycles of $\sigma\alpha$, is $3$-edge-colourable: some colouring of its edges with three colours gives the three edges around every face distinct colours. Equivalently, some simple loopless triangulation of a closed orientable surface has a dual that is not $3$-edge-colourable.
