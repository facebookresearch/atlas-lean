# Simple loopless triangulation as an oriented map

For a set $D$ of darts (no finiteness is assumed), a pair of permutations $(\alpha, \sigma)$ of $D$ satisfies the test when: $\alpha$ is an involution without fixed points; the group generated by $\alpha$ and $\sigma$ acts transitively on $D$; $(\sigma\alpha)^3$ is the identity and $\sigma\alpha$ has no fixed point; no dart $d$ satisfies $\sigma^k(d) = \alpha(d)$ for a natural number $k \ge 0$; and whenever $\sigma^k(d) = d'$ and $\sigma^l(\alpha(d)) = \alpha(d')$ for natural numbers $k, l \ge 0$, then $d = d'$.

Read as an oriented combinatorial map, with vertices the $\sigma$-orbits, edges the pairs $\{d, \alpha(d)\}$ and faces the cycles of $\sigma\alpha$, these conditions say that the map is connected, every face is a triangle, and the underlying graph has no loops and no multiple edges. For a finite nonempty dart set this is a simple loopless triangulation of a closed connected orientable surface. Without finiteness the surface need not be closed: the darts of the regular triangular tiling of the plane, with $\alpha$ reversing each edge and $\sigma$ rotating the six darts at each vertex, pass the test. The empty dart set also passes it.
