# Cycle-plus-triangles theorem

For every natural number $n$ and all simple graphs $T$ and $C$ on a common vertex set $V$ with $3n$ elements, if $T$ is the disjoint union of $n$ triangles (for some bijection of $V$ with $\{0, \dots, n-1\} \times \{0, 1, 2\}$, two vertices are adjacent in $T$ exactly when they are distinct with the same first coordinate), $C$ is the image of the cycle graph on $\{0, \dots, 3n-1\}$ under a bijection onto $V$ (a Hamiltonian cycle when $n > 0$), and $T$ and $C$ have no common edge, then the union $T \cup C$ is properly $3$-colourable. That is, the proposition [Erdős Problem 842 (cycle plus triangles)](cycle-plus-triangles-three-colorable.md) holds.

For $n = 0$ the vertex set is empty, $T$ and $C$ are both the empty graph, the hypotheses hold, and the conclusion holds because the empty graph is $3$-colourable.
