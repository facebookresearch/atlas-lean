# Erdős Problem 631, second question (sharpness of 5)

The statement that some planar simple graph on $\{0, \dots, n-1\}$, for some $n$, has choice number exactly $5$: it is $5$-list-colourable and not $r$-list-colourable for any $r < 5$. Here a graph is $q$-list-colourable when for every type of colours and every assignment of lists of at least $q$ colours to its vertices it has a proper colouring from the lists, and planarity means that it has a straight-line drawing in the plane with distinct points for distinct vertices and no two edges meeting except at common endpoints, which for finite graphs is equivalent to planarity (Fáry's theorem). Stated here as a proposition; its resolution: [Some planar graph has choice number 5](planar-list-chromatic-five-attained-hold.md).
