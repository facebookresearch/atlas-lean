# Strongly k-colorable graph

Let $G$ be a simple graph on a finite vertex set $V$ and let $k$ be a natural number. $G$ is strongly $k$-colorable if for every partition $\mathcal{P}$ of $V$ into nonempty parts of size at most $k$ there is a proper coloring $c : V \to \{0, \dots, k-1\}$ of $G$ (adjacent vertices get different colors) that is injective on each part of $\mathcal{P}$.

The definition is meant for positive $k$. For $k = 0$ and $V$ nonempty, no partition of $V$ into nonempty parts has all parts of size at most $0$, so the condition holds vacuously for every graph.
