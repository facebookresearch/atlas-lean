# A graph of dimension four has at least nine edges, and nine suffice

The least number of edges of a simple graph on $\{0, \dots, n-1\}$, for some $n$, of dimension $4$ is $9$: some such graph has exactly $9$ edges and every such graph has at least $9$. Here the dimension of a graph is the least $d$ such that its vertices can be mapped injectively into $\mathbb{R}^d$ with every edge of length $1$. That is, the proposition [Erdős Problem 1007 (fewest edges in dimension four)](min-edges-dimension-four-eq-nine.md) holds.
