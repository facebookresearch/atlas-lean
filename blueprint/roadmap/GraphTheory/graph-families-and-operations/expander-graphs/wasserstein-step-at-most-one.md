# Lazy-walk coupling condition with cost at most one

Let $G$ be a simple graph on a finite vertex set $V$, with the lazy walk $P(x, y)$ equal to $1/2$ if $x = y$, to $1/(2\deg x)$ if $y$ is adjacent to $x$, and to $0$ otherwise. For vertices $x, y$, the condition holds when some coupling $\pi$ of the rows $P(x, \cdot)$ and $P(y, \cdot)$, a nonnegative function on $V \times V$ with row sums $P(x, \cdot)$ and column sums $P(y, \cdot)$, has $\sum_{u, v} \pi(u, v)\, d(u, v) \le 1$. Here $d$ is the graph distance with $d(u, v) = 0$ for vertices in different components, and the rows need not be probability vectors: the row of an isolated vertex has mass $1/2$, and if the two rows have different masses no coupling exists. Only for $x$ and $y$ in the same component with no isolated vertices is this the usual bound $W_1(P(x, \cdot), P(y, \cdot)) \le 1$ on the Wasserstein distance; for $x, y$ in two separate copies of $K_2$ the condition holds with cost $0$, since every cross-component distance is $0$.
