# Independence numbers of strong powers are at most $\vartheta^k$

For a finite graph $G$ and a graph $H$ on words $V^k$ as in [Feasible values multiply along strong powers](pow-mem-theta-feasible.md), for instance the [strong power](strong-power.md) $G^{\boxtimes k}$,

$$
\alpha(H) \le \vartheta(G)^k .
$$

For every feasible value $t$ of $G$, $t^k$ is feasible for $H$ and so dominates $\alpha(H)$ ([every feasible value dominates the independence number](../theta-function-of-a-graph/indep-num-le-of-mem-theta-feasible.md)); taking $k$-th roots, $\alpha(H)^{1/k} \le t$ for all feasible $t$, hence $\alpha(H)^{1/k} \le \vartheta(G)$.

## Sources

- L. Lovász, *On the Shannon capacity of a graph*, IEEE Trans. Inform. Theory 25 (1979), 1–7.
- D. E. Knuth, *The Sandwich Theorem*, Electron. J. Combin. 1 (1994), #A1, arXiv:math/9312214v1, §20.
