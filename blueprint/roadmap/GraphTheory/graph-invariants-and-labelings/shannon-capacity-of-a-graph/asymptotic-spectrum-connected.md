# Is the asymptotic spectrum of graphs connected? (Oberwolfach question)

The statement that the asymptotic spectrum of graphs is connected (nonempty and not a union of two disjoint nonempty relatively open sets) in the topology of pointwise convergence. The asymptotic spectrum of graphs $\Delta$ is the set of real-valued functions $f$ on finite simple graphs such that $f(G \sqcup H) = f(G) + f(H)$, $f(G \boxtimes H) = f(G) f(H)$, $f(G) \le f(H)$ whenever $G \le H$ (some map sends distinct non-adjacent vertices of $G$ to distinct non-adjacent vertices of $H$), and $f(K_1) = 1$; $\sqcup$ is the disjoint union and $\boxtimes$ the strong product. It carries the topology of pointwise convergence, as a subspace of the product space of all real-valued functions on finite simple graphs. Stated here as a proposition; its resolution: [The asymptotic spectrum of graphs is connected](asymptotic-spectrum-connected-holds.md).
