# Well-described polyhedra, centered convex bodies and the strong and weak oracle problems

A *well-described polyhedron* $(P; n, \varphi)$ is a polyhedron $P \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$ together with positive integers $n$ and $\varphi$ (with $\varphi \ge n + 1$, automatic unless $P = \mathbb{R}^n$) such that $P$ is the solution set of a finite system of linear inequalities $a^\top x \le \beta$ with $a \in \mathbb{Q}^n$ and $\beta \in \mathbb{Q}$, each whole inequality (the encoding of $(a, \beta)$, not of each coefficient separately) having encoding length at most $\varphi$; the encoding length of $(P; n, \varphi)$ is $n + \varphi$, so $n$ and $\varphi$ count in unary rather than through their bit lengths. The *strong separation problem* asks, for $y \in \mathbb{Q}^n$, to decide $y \in P$ or find $c \in \mathbb{Q}^n$ with $c^\top x < c^\top y$ for all $x \in P$; the *strong optimization problem* asks, for $c \in \mathbb{Q}^n$, to assert $P = \emptyset$, or find $x^\ast \in P \cap \mathbb{Q}^n$ maximizing $c^\top x$ over $P$, or find $z \in \mathbb{Q}^n$ in the recession cone of $P$ with $c^\top z > 0$. For a convex body $K \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$ and $\delta > 0$ let $S(K, \delta)$ be the points within distance $\delta$ of $K$ and $S(K, -\delta)$ the points whose $\delta$-ball lies in $K$. A *centered convex body* $(K; n, R, r, a_0)$ is a convex body $K$ with rationals $R, r > 0$ and $a_0 \in \mathbb{Q}^n$ such that $B(a_0, r) \subseteq K \subseteq B(0, R)$; its encoding length is $n + \langle R \rangle + \langle r \rangle + \langle a_0 \rangle$, where $\langle \cdot \rangle$ denotes binary encoding length. *Weak membership* asks, for $y \in \mathbb{Q}^n$ and rational $\delta > 0$, to assert $y \in S(K, \delta)$ or $y \notin S(K, -\delta)$; *weak optimization* asks, for $c \in \mathbb{Q}^n$ and rational $\delta > 0$, to find $y \in \mathbb{Q}^n \cap S(K, \delta)$ with $c^\top x \le c^\top y + \delta$ for all $x \in S(K, -\delta)$, or to assert that $S(K, -\delta)$ is empty. Oracles for these problems receive and return rational data. An algorithm is *oracle-polynomial* if its number of oracle calls and elementary steps is bounded by a polynomial in the encoding length of the polyhedron or body ($n + \varphi$, respectively $n + \langle R \rangle + \langle r \rangle + \langle a_0 \rangle$) plus the encoding length of the query data $y$, $c$, $\delta$.
