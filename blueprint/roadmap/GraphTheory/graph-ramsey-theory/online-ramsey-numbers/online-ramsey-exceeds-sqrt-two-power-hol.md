# Online Ramsey numbers exceed $(\sqrt 2 + \varepsilon)^n$

There are a real $\varepsilon > 0$ and a natural number $n_0$ such that for every $n \ge n_0$ and every natural number $k$, if Builder can force a monochromatic $K_n$ from the empty position within $k$ moves, then $(\sqrt{2} + \varepsilon)^n \le k$; that is, the diagonal online Ramsey number satisfies $\tilde r(n, n) \ge (\sqrt 2 + \varepsilon)^n$ for all large $n$. Builder can force a monochromatic $K_n$ from a position $P$ within $k$ moves when: for $k = 0$, $P$ already has a monochromatic $K_n$; for $k + 1$, either $P$ already has one, or there are distinct $x, y$ such that the edge $\{x, y\}$ does not occur in $P$ in either colour and, for both colours $b$, Builder can force a monochromatic $K_n$ within $k$ moves from the position obtained by adding $(\{x, y\}, b)$ to $P$. A position has a [monochromatic $K_n$](has-monochromatic-clique.md) when for some colour $b$ there is a set of $n$ distinct natural numbers with every pair of distinct members recorded in $P$ with colour $b$; loops $\{x, x\}$ in the list play no role, and for $n = 1$ the empty position already has one. That is, the proposition [Exponential lower bound for online Ramsey numbers (AIM question)](online-ramsey-exceeds-sqrt-two-power.md) holds.
