# Resolution of Erdős Problem 79

For every natural number $N$ there are $n \ge N$ and a simple graph $G$ on $\{0, \dots, n-1\}$ that is not Ramsey size linear while every subgraph of $G$ other than $G$ itself, viewed as a graph on its own vertex set, is Ramsey size linear. A finite simple graph $F$ is Ramsey size linear when there is a real $c > 0$ such that $R(F, H) \le c\, e(H)$ for every simple graph $H$ on $\{0, \dots, n-1\}$, for every $n$, in which every vertex has positive degree; here $e(H)$ is the number of edges of $H$ and $R(F, H)$ is the least $M$ such that every simple graph on $M$ vertices contains a copy of $F$ or has a complement containing a copy of $H$, and $0$ if there is no such $M$. That is, the proposition [Erdős Problem 79 (minimal non-Ramsey-size-linear graphs)](infinitely-many-minimal-non-ramsey-size.md) holds.
