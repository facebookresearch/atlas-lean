# Size Ramsey numbers of paths do not grow superlinearly

It is not the case that for every real $C$ and all sufficiently large $n$, every simple graph $G$ on $\{0, \dots, m-1\}$, for any $m$, that arrows $P_n$ has more than $C n$ edges. Here a simple graph $G$ arrows $P_n$, the path on $n$ vertices (that is, $G$ is [Ramsey for](is-ramsey-for.md) $P_n$), when for every colouring of the unordered pairs of its vertices with two colours, the edges of $G$ of some colour form a graph containing a copy of $P_n$, not necessarily induced. Equivalently, for some real $C$ there are infinitely many $n$ for which some graph arrowing $P_n$ has at most $C n$ edges. That is, the proposition [Size Ramsey numbers of paths grow superlinearly (Oberwolfach question, part (a))](size-ramsey-path-superlinear.md) is false.

Erdős Problem #720 (parts (a) and (b)) asks the same questions with the path indexed by its number of edges, so its $n$-th term is the term here for $n + 1$; since $(n+1)/n \to 1$, superlinear and subquadratic growth are unaffected. Source for that formulation: T. F. Bloom, Erdős Problems, Problem #720, https://www.erdosproblems.com/720.
