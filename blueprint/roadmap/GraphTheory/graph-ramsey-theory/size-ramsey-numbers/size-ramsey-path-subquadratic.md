# Size Ramsey numbers of paths are subquadratic (Oberwolfach question, part (b))

The statement that for every real $\varepsilon > 0$ and all sufficiently large $n$, some simple graph $G$ on $\{0, \dots, m-1\}$, for some $m$, arrows $P_n$ and has at most $\varepsilon n^2$ edges. Here a simple graph $G$ arrows $P_n$, the path on $n$ vertices (that is, $G$ is [Ramsey for](is-ramsey-for.md) $P_n$), when for every colouring of the unordered pairs of its vertices with two colours, the edges of $G$ of some colour form a graph containing a copy of $P_n$, not necessarily induced. Stated here as a proposition; its resolution: [Size Ramsey numbers of paths are subquadratic](size-ramsey-path-subquadratic-holds.md).

Erdős Problem #720 (parts (a) and (b)) asks the same questions with the path indexed by its number of edges, so its $n$-th term is the term here for $n + 1$; since $(n+1)/n \to 1$, superlinear and subquadratic growth are unaffected. Source for that formulation: T. F. Bloom, Erdős Problems, Problem #720, https://www.erdosproblems.com/720.
