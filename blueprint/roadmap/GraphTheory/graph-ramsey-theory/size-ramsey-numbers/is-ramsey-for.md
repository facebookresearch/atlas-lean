# Ramsey graphs for a graph

A simple graph $H$ is Ramsey for a simple graph $G$, on arbitrary vertex sets, when for every subgraph $R \le H$ (the red edges, with blue graph $H \setminus R$) the graph $G$ has a copy, not necessarily induced, in $R$ or in $H \setminus R$.

For $G$ the path $P_n$ on $n$ vertices this says that $H$ arrows $P_n$: for every colouring $c$ of the unordered pairs of vertices of $H$ with two colours, the edges of $H$ of some colour contain a copy of $P_n$. Indeed, the red edges of a colouring form a subgraph $R \le H$ whose blue edges in $H$ are exactly $H \setminus R$, and conversely every $R \le H$ arises by colouring according to membership in $R$; colours of pairs outside $H$ do not matter.
