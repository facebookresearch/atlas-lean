# Resolution of Erdős Problem 559

It is not the case that for every natural number $d$ there is a real $C$ such that every simple graph $G$ on $\{0, \dots, n-1\}$, for every $n$, with maximum degree at most $d$ satisfies $\hat R(G) \le C n$. Here the size Ramsey number $\hat R(G)$ of a simple graph $G$ is the least $m$ such that some simple graph on $\{0, \dots, N-1\}$, for some $N$, with exactly $m$ edges is Ramsey for $G$ (every red/blue colouring of its edges has a monochromatic copy of $G$, not necessarily induced), and $0$ if there is none, as for a graph on an infinite vertex set. Equivalently, for some $d$ and every real $C$ there are $n$ and a graph on $\{0, \dots, n-1\}$ with maximum degree at most $d$ has $\hat R(G) > C n$. That is, the proposition [Erdős Problem 559 (size Ramsey numbers of bounded-degree graphs)](size-ramsey-linear-bounded-degree.md) is false.
