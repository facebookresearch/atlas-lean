# Resolution of Erdős Problem 925

It is not the case that there are real numbers $\delta > 0$ and $c > 0$ such that for all sufficiently large $n$, every simple graph $G$ on the vertex set $\{0, \dots, n-1\}$ that is the union $G = G_1 \cup G_2$ of two triangle-free graphs on the same vertex set (equivalently, whose edges can be coloured with two colours with no monochromatic triangle) has an independent set of at least $c\, n^{1/3 + \delta}$ vertices. Equivalently, for all real $\delta > 0$ and $c > 0$ there are infinitely many $n$ for which some union of two triangle-free graphs on $\{0, \dots, n-1\}$ has no independent set of $c\, n^{1/3+\delta}$ or more vertices. That is, the proposition [Erdős Problem 925 (independent sets in graphs not Ramsey for the triangle)](non-ramsey-triangle-large-independent-se.md) is false.
