# The multicolour Ramsey number $R(3, 3, n)$

For a natural number $n$, $R(3, 3, n)$ is the least $m$ such that every colouring of the edges of $K_m$ with colours $0, 1, 2$ (a map from the unordered pairs of $\{0, \dots, m-1\}$ to $\{0, 1, 2\}$) has a triangle all of whose edges have colour $0$, or a triangle all of whose edges have colour $1$, or a set of $n$ vertices all of whose pairs of distinct members have colour $2$ (the map also colours the diagonal pairs $\{x, x\}$, which play no role); it is $0$ if there is no such $m$. Such an $m$ exists by Ramsey's theorem.
