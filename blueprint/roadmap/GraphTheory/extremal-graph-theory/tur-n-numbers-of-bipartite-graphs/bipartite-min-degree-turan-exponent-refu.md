# Resolution of Erdős Problem 147

It is not the case that for every nonempty finite set $V$ and every bipartite simple graph $H$ on $V$ with minimum degree $r \ge 2$ there is a real $\varepsilon > 0$ such that $n^{2 - 1/(r-1) + \varepsilon} = O(\mathrm{ex}(n; H))$ as $n \to \infty$, that is, $\mathrm{ex}(n; H) \ge c\, n^{2 - 1/(r-1) + \varepsilon}$ for some $c > 0$ and all large $n$. Here $\mathrm{ex}(n; H)$ is the extremal number, the largest number of edges of a simple graph on $\{0, \dots, n-1\}$ containing no copy of $H$ (not necessarily induced), and $0$ if every such graph contains $H$. Equivalently, some nonempty finite bipartite graph $H$ with minimum degree $r \ge 2$ has, for every $\varepsilon > 0$, $n^{2 - 1/(r-1) + \varepsilon} \ne O(\mathrm{ex}(n; H))$. That is, the proposition [Erdős Problem 147 (Turán exponents and minimum degree)](bipartite-min-degree-turan-exponent.md) is false.
