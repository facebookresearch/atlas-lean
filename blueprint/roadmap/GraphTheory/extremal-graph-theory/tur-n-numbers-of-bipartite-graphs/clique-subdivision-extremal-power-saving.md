# Erdős Problem 1021 (Turán numbers of subdivided cliques)

The statement that for every natural number $k \ge 3$ there is a real $c > 0$ with $\mathrm{ex}(n; G_k) = O(n^{3/2 - c})$ as $n \to \infty$, where $G_k$ is the $1$-subdivision of $K_k$ (the bipartite graph between $\{0, \dots, k-1\}$ and its two-element subsets, each subset joined to its two elements) and $\mathrm{ex}(n; H)$ is the extremal number, the largest number of edges of a simple graph on $\{0, \dots, n-1\}$ containing no copy of $H$ (not necessarily induced), and $0$ if every such graph contains $H$. Stated here as a proposition; its resolution: [Resolution of Erdős Problem 1021](clique-subdivision-extremal-power-saving-2.md).
