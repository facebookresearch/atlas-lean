# Resolution of Erdős Problem 59

It is not the case that for every natural number $k$, every simple graph $H$ on $\{0, \dots, k-1\}$ that contains a cycle, and every real $\varepsilon > 0$, for all sufficiently large $n$ the number of $H$-free simple graphs on $\{0, \dots, n-1\}$ is at most $2^{(1 + \varepsilon)\, \mathrm{ex}(n; H)}$, where $\mathrm{ex}(n; H)$ is the largest number of edges of an $H$-free simple graph on $n$ vertices. Equivalently, some finite graph $H$ containing a cycle and some $\varepsilon > 0$ admit infinitely many $n$ with more than $2^{(1 + \varepsilon)\, \mathrm{ex}(n; H)}$ $H$-free graphs on $\{0, \dots, n-1\}$. That is, the proposition [Erdős Problem 59 (the number of $H$-free graphs)](free-graph-count-extremal-exponent.md) is false.
