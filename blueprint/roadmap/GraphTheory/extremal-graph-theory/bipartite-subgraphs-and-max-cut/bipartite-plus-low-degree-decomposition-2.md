# Resolution of Erdős Problem 613

It is not the case that for every natural number $n \ge 3$, every simple graph $G$ on $\{0, \dots, m-1\}$, for any $m$, with exactly $\binom{2n+1}{2} - \binom{n}{2} - 1$ edges is the union $G = B \cup D$ of a bipartite graph $B$ and a graph $D$ on the same vertices in which every vertex has fewer than $n$ neighbours. Equivalently, for some $n \ge 3$ some finite simple graph with exactly $\binom{2n+1}{2} - \binom{n}{2} - 1$ edges is not such a union. That is, the proposition [Erdős Problem 613 (bipartite plus low-degree decompositions)](bipartite-plus-low-degree-decomposition.md) is false.
