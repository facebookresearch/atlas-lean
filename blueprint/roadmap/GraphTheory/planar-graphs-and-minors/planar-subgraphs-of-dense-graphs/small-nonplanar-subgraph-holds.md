# Resolution of Erdős Problem 1018

For every real $\varepsilon > 0$ there is a natural number $C$ such that for all sufficiently large $n$, every simple graph $G$ on $\{0, \dots, n-1\}$ with at least $n^{1+\varepsilon}$ edges has a set $S$ of at most $C$ vertices whose induced subgraph is not planar, planarity meaning a straight-line drawing in the plane with distinct points for distinct vertices and no two edges meeting except at common endpoints (for finite graphs, equivalent to planarity by Fáry's theorem). That is, the proposition [Erdős Problem 1018 (small non-planar subgraphs)](small-nonplanar-subgraph.md) holds.
