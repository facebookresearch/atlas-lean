# Resolution of Erdős Problem 1019

For every natural number $n \ge 1$, every simple graph $G$ on $\{0, \dots, n-1\}$ with exactly $\lfloor n^2/4 \rfloor + \lfloor (n+1)/2 \rfloor$ edges contains a copy (an injective vertex map carrying edges to edges) of a saturated planar graph on more than $3$ vertices: a planar simple graph $H$ on $\{0, \dots, m-1\}$ with $m > 3$ and exactly $3m - 6$ edges, planarity meaning a straight-line drawing in the plane with distinct points for distinct vertices and no two edges meeting except at common endpoints (for finite graphs, equivalent to planarity by Fáry's theorem). That is, the proposition [Erdős Problem 1019 (saturated planar subgraphs)](contains-saturated-planar-graph.md) holds.
