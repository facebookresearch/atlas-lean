# Four left turns, given that two corners force a self-intersection

Let $n \ge 1$ and let $d = (d_0, \dots, d_{n-1})$ be a cyclic sequence of directions $d_i \in \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$, indices taken modulo $n$. Direction $k$ is the unit step $s(k)$, with $s(0) = (1, 0)$ (east), $s(1) = (0, 1)$ (north), $s(2) = (-1, 0)$ (west), $s(3) = (0, -1)$ (south), and the walk with step directions $d$ has vertices $p_k = \sum_{0 \le i < k} s(d_i)$ for $k = 0, \dots, n-1$ (so $p_0 = 0$; vertex indices are also taken modulo $n$). Suppose the walk is closed, $\sum_{i} s(d_i) = 0$, its vertices are pairwise distinct, every turn $d_{i+1} - d_i$ is $0$ or $1$, and any two distinct indices $i \neq j$ with $d_i = d_j = 1$ and $d_{i+1} = d_{j+1} = 2$ force two vertices of the walk to coincide. Then exactly $4$ indices $i$ have $d_{i+1} - d_i = 1$.
