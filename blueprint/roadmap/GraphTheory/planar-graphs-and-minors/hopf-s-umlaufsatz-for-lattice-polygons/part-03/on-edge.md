# A lattice point lies on a walk step

Let $n \ge 1$ and let $d = (d_0, \dots, d_{n-1})$ be a cyclic sequence of directions $d_i \in \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$, indices taken modulo $n$. Direction $k$ is the unit step $s(k)$, with $s(0) = (1, 0)$ (east), $s(1) = (0, 1)$ (north), $s(2) = (-1, 0)$ (west), $s(3) = (0, -1)$ (south), and the walk with step directions $d$ has vertices $p_k = \sum_{0 \le i < k} s(d_i)$ for $k = 0, \dots, n-1$ (so $p_0 = 0$; vertex indices are also taken modulo $n$). For an index $k$ and a point $q \in \mathbb{Z}^2$, $q$ lies on step $k$ when $\min(x(p_k), x(p_{k+1})) \le x(q) \le \max(x(p_k), x(p_{k+1}))$ and $\min(y(p_k), y(p_{k+1})) \le y(q) \le \max(y(p_k), y(p_{k+1}))$, that is, when $q$ lies in the bounding box of $p_k$ and $p_{k+1}$ (the closed segment traversed by the step when $p_{k+1} = p_k + s(d_k)$).
