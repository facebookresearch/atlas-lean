# A west run from a highest vertex ends in a south step

Let $n \ge 1$ and let $d = (d_0, \dots, d_{n-1})$ be a cyclic sequence of directions $d_i \in \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$, indices taken modulo $n$. Direction $k$ is the unit step $s(k)$, with $s(0) = (1, 0)$ (east), $s(1) = (0, 1)$ (north), $s(2) = (-1, 0)$ (west), $s(3) = (0, -1)$ (south), and the walk with step directions $d$ has vertices $p_k = \sum_{0 \le i < k} s(d_i)$ for $k = 0, \dots, n-1$ (so $p_0 = 0$; vertex indices are also taken modulo $n$). Suppose the walk is closed, $\sum_{i} s(d_i) = 0$, every turn $d_{i+1} - d_i$ is $0$ or $1$, and $M$ is an index such that $p_M$ has the largest $y$-coordinate among all vertices and $d_M = 2$ (west). Then there is a natural number $J \ge 1$ with $d_{M+i} = 2$ for all $0 \le i < J$ and $d_{M+J} = 3$ (south).
