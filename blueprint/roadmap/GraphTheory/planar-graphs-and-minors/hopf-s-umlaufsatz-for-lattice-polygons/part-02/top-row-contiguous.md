# Contiguous top row of a walk

Let $n \ge 1$ and let $d = (d_0, \dots, d_{n-1})$ be a cyclic sequence of directions $d_i \in \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$, indices taken modulo $n$. Direction $k$ is the unit step $s(k)$, with $s(0) = (1, 0)$ (east), $s(1) = (0, 1)$ (north), $s(2) = (-1, 0)$ (west), $s(3) = (0, -1)$ (south), and the walk with step directions $d$ has vertices $p_k = \sum_{0 \le i < k} s(d_i)$ for $k = 0, \dots, n-1$ (so $p_0 = 0$; vertex indices are also taken modulo $n$). The top row of the walk is contiguous when there is an index $M$ such that $y(p_M) \ge y(p_j)$ for all $j$, and for all indices $a, b$ with $y(p_a) = y(p_b) = y(p_M)$ and every integer $x$ with $x(p_a) \le x \le x(p_b)$, some vertex $p_c$ equals $(x, y(p_M))$.
