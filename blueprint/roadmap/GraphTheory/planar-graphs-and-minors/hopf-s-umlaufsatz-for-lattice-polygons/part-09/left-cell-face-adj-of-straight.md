# Consecutive left cells on a straight step are face-adjacent

Let $m$ be a natural number, $n = m + 3$, and let $d = (d_0, \dots, d_{n-1})$ be a cyclic sequence of directions $d_i \in \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$, indices taken modulo $n$. Direction $k$ is the unit step $s(k)$, with $s(0) = (1, 0)$ (east), $s(1) = (0, 1)$ (north), $s(2) = (-1, 0)$ (west), $s(3) = (0, -1)$ (south), and the walk with step directions $d$ has vertices $p_k = \sum_{0 \le i < k} s(d_i)$ for $k = 0, \dots, n-1$ (so $p_0 = 0$; vertex indices are also taken modulo $n$). Suppose the walk is closed, $\sum_{i} s(d_i) = 0$, and its vertices are pairwise distinct. Its face graph is the graph on unit faces (labelled by lower left corners) in which two adjacent faces are joined unless the primal edge separating them is a step $\{p_i, p_{i+1}\}$ of the walk. For an index $k$ let $\lambda_k$ be the cell to the left of step $k$: $p_k$, $p_k - (1, 0)$, $p_k - (1, 1)$ or $p_k - (0, 1)$ according as $d_k = 0, 1, 2, 3$. If $d_{k+1} = d_k$, then the faces $\lambda_k$ and $\lambda_{k+1}$ are adjacent in the face graph.
