# Horizontally face-adjacent cells have the same ray parity

Let $m$ be a natural number, $n = m + 3$, and let $d = (d_0, \dots, d_{n-1})$ be a cyclic sequence of directions $d_i \in \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$, indices taken modulo $n$. Direction $k$ is the unit step $s(k)$, with $s(0) = (1, 0)$ (east), $s(1) = (0, 1)$ (north), $s(2) = (-1, 0)$ (west), $s(3) = (0, -1)$ (south), and the walk with step directions $d$ has vertices $p_k = \sum_{0 \le i < k} s(d_i)$ for $k = 0, \dots, n-1$ (so $p_0 = 0$; vertex indices are also taken modulo $n$). For integers $a, b$, the upward ray parity $\pi(a, b) \in \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ of the cell $[a, a+1] \times [b, b+1]$ is the number, modulo $2$, of indices $k$ with $d_k$ horizontal (east or west), $\min(x(p_k), x(p_{k+1})) = a$ and $y(p_k) > b$. Suppose the walk is closed, $\sum_{i} s(d_i) = 0$, and its vertices are pairwise distinct. Its face graph is the graph on unit faces (labelled by lower left corners) in which two adjacent faces are joined unless the primal edge separating them is a step $\{p_i, p_{i+1}\}$ of the walk. If the faces $(a, b)$ and $(a + 1, b)$ are adjacent in the face graph, then $\pi(a, b) = \pi(a + 1, b)$.
