# Face-graph adjacency as absence of a walk step between cells

Let $m$ be a natural number, $n = m + 3$, and let $d = (d_0, \dots, d_{n-1})$ be a cyclic sequence of directions $d_i \in \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$, indices taken modulo $n$. Direction $k$ is the unit step $s(k)$, with $s(0) = (1, 0)$ (east), $s(1) = (0, 1)$ (north), $s(2) = (-1, 0)$ (west), $s(3) = (0, -1)$ (south), and the walk with step directions $d$ has vertices $p_k = \sum_{0 \le i < k} s(d_i)$ for $k = 0, \dots, n-1$ (so $p_0 = 0$; vertex indices are also taken modulo $n$). Suppose the walk is closed, $\sum_{i} s(d_i) = 0$, and its vertices are pairwise distinct. Its face graph is the graph on unit faces (labelled by lower left corners) in which two adjacent faces are joined unless the primal edge separating them is a step $\{p_i, p_{i+1}\}$ of the walk. The walk has this property when, for all integers $a, b$: the faces $(a, b)$ and $(a + 1, b)$ are adjacent in the face graph if and only if no index $k$ with $d_k$ vertical and $x(p_k) = a + 1$ satisfies $\min(y(p_k), y(p_{k+1})) \le b < \max(y(p_k), y(p_{k+1}))$; and the faces $(a, b)$ and $(a, b + 1)$ are adjacent in the face graph if and only if no index $k$ with $d_k$ horizontal and $\min(x(p_k), x(p_{k+1})) = a$ has $y(p_k) = b + 1$.
