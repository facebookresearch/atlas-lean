# King-saturated set

A set $K \subseteq \mathbb{Z}^2$ is king saturated when, for every $(a, b) \in \mathbb{Z}^2$: if $(a, b), (a+1, b+1) \in K$, then $(a+1, b) \in K$ or $(a, b+1) \in K$; and if $(a+1, b), (a, b+1) \in K$, then $(a, b) \in K$ or $(a+1, b+1) \in K$. That is, whenever $K$ contains two opposite corners of a unit face, it contains one of the other two.
