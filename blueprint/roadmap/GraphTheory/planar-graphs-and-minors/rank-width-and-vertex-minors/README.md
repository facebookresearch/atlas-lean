# Rank-width and vertex-minors

The cut-rank of a vertex set $X$ of a graph is the rank, over the two-element field, of the
adjacency matrix between $X$ and its complement. Rank-width is the branch-width of the cut-rank
function: the least width of a tree with all internal nodes of degree three whose leaves are the
vertices, a tree edge having as width the cut-rank of the vertex set on one side. Local
complementation at a vertex complements the graph induced on its neighbourhood. It preserves the
cut-rank function (Oum), so rank-width is invariant under local equivalence and does not increase
under vertex-minors, which play for rank-width the role that minors play for tree-width.
Rank-width and clique-width are bounded on the same graph classes, since
$\mathrm{rw}(G) \le \mathrm{cw}(G) \le 2^{\mathrm{rw}(G)+1} - 1$ (Oum–Seymour), and rank-width is at
most tree-width plus one, while complete graphs have rank-width at most one. Pages follow S. Oum,
*Rank-width and vertex-minors*, J. Combin. Theory Ser. B 95 (2005); S. Oum and P. Seymour,
*Approximating clique-width and branch-width*, J. Combin. Theory Ser. B 96 (2006); and S. Oum,
*Rank-width: algorithmic and structural results*, Discrete Appl. Math. 231 (2017),
arXiv:1601.03800v2 (author's preprints, as listed on each page).

Rank-width and clique-width are recorded as predicates "at most $k$", with rank-width $0$ for
graphs with at most one vertex, as in the sources.
