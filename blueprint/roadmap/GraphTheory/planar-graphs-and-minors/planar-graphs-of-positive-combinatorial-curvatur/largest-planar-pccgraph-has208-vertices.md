# Largest planar graphs of positive curvature (DeVos–Mohar question)

The statement that every planar PCC graph $(G, \rho)$ on a finite vertex set has at most $208$ vertices, and some planar PCC graph has exactly $208$ vertices. Here a simple graph $G$ with a permutation $\rho$ of its darts is a planar PCC graph when $\rho$ is a rotation system of $G$, $G$ is connected with $|V| - |E| + F = 2$ (an embedding in the sphere), the combinatorial curvature $1 - \deg(v)/2 + \sum 1/|f|$ is positive at every vertex (the sum over the corners at $v$), every vertex has degree at least $3$, and $(G, \rho)$ is neither a prism nor an antiprism; a prism or antiprism of order $N$ is a graph on $2N$ vertices with two $N$-gonal faces and all other faces quadrilaterals (face vector $\{4, 4, N\}$) or triangles (face vector $\{3, 3, 3, N\}$). Stated here as a proposition; its resolution: [The largest planar graphs of positive curvature have 208 vertices](largest-planar-pccgraph-has208-vertices-2.md).
