# Whitney's cut–cycle duality

Whitney's cut–cycle duality for finite subgraphs of the square lattice: for every subgraph $H$ of the square lattice on $\mathbb{Z}^2$ with finitely many edges, every lattice edge $\{p, q\}$ not in $H$ and every pair of adjacent faces $f, g$ whose separating primal edge is $\{p, q\}$, the sites $p$ and $q$ are joined by a walk in $H$ if and only if $f$ and $g$ are not joined by a walk in the face-region graph of $H$ (adjacent faces joined unless their separating primal edge is an edge of $H$) with the edge $\{f, g\}$ deleted.

Both restrictions are needed. If $H$ is the horizontal axis with the edge $\{(0,0), (1,0)\}$ removed, $p = (0,0)$, $q = (1,0)$ and $f$, $g$ are the faces below and above that edge, then $p$ and $q$ are not joined in $H$, and $f$ and $g$ are not joined either once the edge $\{f, g\}$ is deleted, since every other crossing of the axis is blocked by an edge of $H$. If instead $H$ has only the two edges $\{(0,0), (3,3)\}$ and $\{(3,3), (1,0)\}$, which are not lattice edges, then $p$ and $q$ are joined in $H$, but no separating primal edge is an edge of $H$, so $f$ and $g$ stay joined after the deletion.
