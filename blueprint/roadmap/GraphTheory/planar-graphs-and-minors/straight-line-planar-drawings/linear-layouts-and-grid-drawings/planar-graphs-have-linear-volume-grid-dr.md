# Linear-volume 3D grid drawings of planar graphs (Felsner–Liotta–Wismath question)

The statement that there is a natural number $c$ such that every planar simple graph on a finite nonempty vertex set with $n$ vertices, planar meaning that it has a straight-line drawing in the plane with distinct points for distinct vertices and no two edges meeting except at common endpoints (for finite graphs, equivalent to planarity by Fáry's theorem), has a $3$-dimensional grid drawing of volume at most $c\,n$: an injective placement of the vertices at integer points of $\mathbb{R}^3$, edges drawn as straight segments that pass through no other vertex and meet only at common ends, inside an axis-aligned box with at most $c\,n$ integer points. Stated here as a proposition; its resolution: [Planar graphs have 3D grid drawings of linear volume](planar-graphs-have-linear-volume-grid-dr-2.md).
