# Does some planar graph have obstacle number greater than one?

The statement that some planar simple graph on $\{0, \dots, n-1\}$, for some $n$, has no obstacle representation with at most one obstacle (obstacle number greater than $1$), a graph being planar when it has a straight-line drawing in the plane with distinct points for distinct vertices and no two edges meeting except at common endpoints (for finite graphs, equivalent to planarity by Fáry's theorem). An obstacle representation with $h$ obstacles places the vertices at distinct points and chooses $h$ connected sets avoiding every vertex point and every edge segment, such that every non-edge segment meets one of them. Stated here as a proposition; its resolution: [Some planar graph has obstacle number greater than one](planar-graph-obstacle-number-above-one-h.md).
