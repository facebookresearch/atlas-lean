# Four equivalent characterizations of finite Galois extensions

A finite field extension is Galois exactly when it is the splitting field of a separable polynomial over the base field. Mathlib proves this as part of a list of four equivalent conditions: for fields $F$ and $E$ such that $E$ is an $F$-algebra of finite dimension over $F$, the following are equivalent: (1) $E$ is Galois over $F$, which Mathlib defines as separable (in particular algebraic) and normal; (2) the fixed field of the whole group $\operatorname{Gal}(E/F)$ of $F$-algebra automorphisms of $E$, a group defined for every extension, is the smallest intermediate field, namely the image of $F$ in $E$; (3) the number of elements of $\operatorname{Gal}(E/F)$ equals the degree $[E : F]$, the dimension of $E$ over $F$; (4) there is a polynomial $p \in F[X]$ that is separable, meaning that $p$ and its formal derivative are coprime, and such that $E$ is a splitting field of $p$ over $F$, meaning that $p$ splits into constant and monic linear factors in $E[X]$ and $E$ is generated as an $F$-algebra by the roots of $p$. Conditions (1) and (4) give the familiar equivalence.
