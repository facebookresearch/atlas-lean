# Irreducibility of binomials from a character of the unit group

Let $K$ be a field, $n \ge 1$ a natural number and $a \in K$ with $a \neq 0$. Let $\varphi : K^\times \to \mathbb{Z}$ be a group homomorphism from the unit group of $K$ to the additive group of integers. If $|\varphi(a)|$ is coprime to $n$, then $X^n - a$ is irreducible in $K[X]$.

No absolute value, ultrametric inequality or restriction on the characteristic of $K$ is assumed: $\varphi$ is an arbitrary homomorphism.

Equivalently, $\varphi$ may be given as a function $v : K \to \mathbb{Z}$ with $v(xy) = v(x) + v(y)$ for all nonzero $x, y \in K$, coprimality of $v(a)$ and $n$ taken in $\mathbb{Z}$: the value $v(0)$ plays no role, and $v$ restricted to $K^\times$ is such a homomorphism.
