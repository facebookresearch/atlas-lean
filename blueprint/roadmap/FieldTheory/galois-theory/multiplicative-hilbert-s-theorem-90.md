# Hilbert's Theorem 90 for the multiplicative group

Let $L/K$ be a finite extension of fields and $\mathrm{Aut}_K(L)$ its group of $K$-automorphisms, acting
on the multiplicative group $L^\times$. Let $f \colon \mathrm{Aut}_K(L) \to L^\times$ satisfy
$f(gh) = g(f(h)) \, f(g)$ for all $g, h \in \mathrm{Aut}_K(L)$. Then there is $\beta \in L^\times$ with
$g(\beta)/\beta = f(g)$ for every $g \in \mathrm{Aut}_K(L)$.

When $L/K$ is Galois, $\mathrm{Aut}_K(L)$ is its Galois group $G$, and the statement says
$H^1(G, L^\times) = 0$: every crossed homomorphism $G \to L^\times$ is principal. In this form it is
Noether's generalization of Hilbert's Theorem 90 (Milne, *Fields and Galois Theory*, Theorem 5.23). Milne
deduces Hilbert's original form: if $G$ is cyclic of order $m$, generated by $\sigma$, every
$\alpha \in L^\times$ with $\mathrm{Nm}_{L/K}(\alpha) = \alpha \, \sigma(\alpha) \cdots \sigma^{m-1}(\alpha) = 1$ is of
the form $\beta/\sigma(\beta)$ with $\beta \in L^\times$.
