# Separable extension

A field extension $K/F$ is separable when every element of $K$ is a root of some separable polynomial over $F$, that is, of a nonzero polynomial in $F[x]$ with no repeated roots in a splitting field. Mathlib defines, for a commutative ring $F$ and an $F$-algebra $K$, the algebra $K$ to be separable over $F$ when every element $x \in K$ is separable over $F$, meaning that the minimal polynomial of $x$ over $F$ is a separable polynomial, where a polynomial $f$ is called separable when $f$ and its formal derivative $f^\prime$ are coprime, $af + bf^\prime = 1$ for some polynomials $a$ and $b$; a field extension $K/F$ is the case in which $F$ and $K$ are fields, and no finiteness of the extension or perfectness of $F$ is assumed. When $F$ and $K$ are fields, the minimal polynomial of $x$ divides every polynomial over $F$ with root $x$ and a divisor of a separable polynomial is separable, while the minimal polynomial is itself a polynomial with root $x$ that is nonzero when separable, so $x$ is a root of some separable polynomial exactly when its minimal polynomial is separable. For a nonzero polynomial over a field $F$ whose image splits over a field $L$, Mathlib proves that it is separable exactly when its roots in $L$, counted with multiplicity, are distinct, which applies in particular in a splitting field.
