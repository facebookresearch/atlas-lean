# Every polynomial over a field has a splitting field

Every polynomial over a field has a splitting field: if $F$ is a field and $f \in F[X]$, there is an extension field of $F$ over which $f$ factors into linear factors and which is generated over $F$ by the roots of $f$. Mathlib gives an explicit witness: for every field $F$ and every polynomial $f \in F[X]$, with no condition on $f$, it constructs a field $\operatorname{SplittingField}(f)$ with an $F$-algebra structure and proves that it is a splitting field of $f$ over $F$. Here, for a field $L$ that is an $F$-algebra, Mathlib's statement that $L$ is a splitting field of $f$ over $F$ consists of two conditions: the image of $f$ in $L[X]$ under the structure map $F \to L$ splits, that is, it is a product of constant polynomials and monic linear polynomials $X + c$ with $c \in L$; and the $F$-subalgebra of $L$ generated by the set of roots of $f$ in $L$ is all of $L$. This set of roots is the set of distinct roots in $L$ of the image of $f$; for the zero polynomial it is empty, although every element of $L$ is a zero of that polynomial, because Mathlib takes the multiset of roots of the zero polynomial to be empty. So for a constant polynomial, including the zero polynomial, the base field $F$ itself is a splitting field: the polynomial is a product of constants, its set of roots is empty, and the subalgebra generated by the empty set is $F$.
