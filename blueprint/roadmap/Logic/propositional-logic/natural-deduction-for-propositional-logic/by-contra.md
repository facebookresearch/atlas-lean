# Proof by contradiction

Propositions over an atom type with decidable equality are built from atoms by $\wedge$, $\vee$ and $\to$; there is no primitive $\bot$ or $\top$. A theory $T$ is a set of propositions, and $T$ derives a sequent $\Gamma \vdash A$, for a finite set $\Gamma$ of propositions, by natural deduction with the members of $T$ as axioms. A designated bottom atom $\bot$ is fixed, and $\neg A$ is $A \to \bot$. A theory $T$ is classical when it derives $\neg\neg A \to A$ for every $A$. For a classical theory $T$, a derivation of $\bot$ from $\Gamma \cup \{\neg A\}$ in $T$ yields a derivation of $A$ from $\Gamma$ in $T$.
