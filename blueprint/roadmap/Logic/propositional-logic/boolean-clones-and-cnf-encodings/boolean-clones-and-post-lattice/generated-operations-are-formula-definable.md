# Operations computed by circuits over a family lie in its generated clone

Let $G$ be a family of positive-arity Boolean operations. Every $n$-ary operation that is computed by a circuit over $G$ whose inputs are the variables $x_1,\dots,x_n$ and whose gates are operations from $G$, in particular every operation computed by a formula over the signature with one operation symbol for each member of $G$ and input leaves $x_1,\dots,x_n$, belongs to the clone generated by $G$.

## Proof sketch

Induction on the circuit: an input leaf $x_i$ computes the projection $\pi^n_i$, which lies in every clone, and a gate labelled by $g \in G$ of arity $k$ computes the composition of $g$ with the $k$ operations computed at its children, which lies in every clone containing $G$ and those operations. (Derived, not stated by the sources: conversely, the operations computed by formulas over $G$ with input leaves $x_1,\dots,x_n$, for all $n$, contain the projections and are closed under composition, so they form a clone containing $G$; hence an $n$-ary operation belongs to the clone generated by $G$ if and only if it is computed by such a formula, and a complete family yields, for every $n \ge 1$, formulas (and hence circuits) over $G$ for every $n$-ary Boolean function, unary constant functions needing an input leaf.)
