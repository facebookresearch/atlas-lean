# Unit resolution and SAT-solver states

Let $\Delta$ be a [CNF formula](../conjunctive-normal-form/cnf.md). *Unit resolution* repeatedly forms [resolvents](../resolution/resolvent.md) of a unit clause with the clauses containing the complement of its literal. Write $\Delta \vdash \ell$ when unit resolution derives the unit clause $\ell$ from $\Delta$, and $\Delta \vdash \mathrm{false}$ when it derives the empty clause; $\Delta$ is *1-inconsistent* if $\Delta \vdash \mathrm{false}$ and *1-consistent* otherwise.

A *decision sequence* is a sequence $D = \langle \ell_1, \dots, \ell_k \rangle$ of literals, $\ell_i$ being the decision at level $i$, and $D_m$ is its prefix of length $m$. A *SAT state* is a triple $S = (\Delta, \Gamma, D)$ of CNF formulas $\Delta$ (the input) and $\Gamma$ (the learned clauses) with $\Delta \models \Gamma$ and a decision sequence $D$; $S$ is 1-inconsistent when $\Delta \wedge \Gamma \wedge D$ is, and $S_k$ is $(\Delta, \Gamma, D_k)$. A literal $\ell$ is *implied at level* $k$, $S \vdash_k \ell$, if $k$ is the least integer with $\Delta \wedge \Gamma \wedge D_k \vdash \ell$. A state is *normal* if, for every $i \le k$, $S_{i-1}$ is 1-consistent and neither $\ell_i$ nor its complement is implied by $S_{i-1}$: no decision is made in a conflict or on an assigned variable. A triple need not be normal: $(\{p\}, \mathrm{true}, \langle \neg p \rangle)$ is a state, but its decision $\neg p$ is falsified at level $0$. The *assignment trail* of a normal state $S$ assigns literals level by level: at level $0$ unit propagation on $\Delta \wedge \Gamma$ repeatedly assigns the remaining literal $\ell$ of a clause whose other literals are all false and whose variable is unassigned; at level $i \ge 1$ the decision $\ell_i$ is assigned and propagation continues; and the trail stops at the first *conflict*, a clause all of whose literals are false. Each variable is assigned at most once, so a literal is *true* on the trail, *false* on it (its complement is true), or unassigned, and the *implication level* of a variable, and of both its literals, is the level at which it was assigned. Normality makes this possible: each decision is unassigned when it is made, and no decision follows a conflict. Below the first 1-inconsistent level the trail assigns exactly the literals that unit resolution derives, so a variable assigned at level $i < k$ has implication level the least $i$ with $\Delta \wedge \Gamma \wedge D_i \vdash \ell$ for one of its literals $\ell$; at the last level, which literals the trail assigns before the conflict depends on the order of propagation. A decision $\ell_i$ is true at level $i$. Derivability alone does not give an assignment: with $\Delta = \{p,\ \bar a \vee u,\ \bar a \vee \bar u \vee \bar p\}$ and the decision $a$, unit resolution derives $p$ at level $0$ and also $\bar p$ at level $1$, while the trail assigns $p$ at level $0$, then $a$ and $u$ at level $1$, and stops at the conflict on $\bar a \vee \bar u \vee \bar p$, with $p$ still true.
