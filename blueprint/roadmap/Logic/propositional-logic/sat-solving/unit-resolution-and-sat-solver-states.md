# Unit resolution and SAT-solver states

Let $\Delta$ be a CNF formula. *Unit resolution* repeatedly resolves a unit clause against the clauses containing the complement of its literal. Write $\Delta \vdash \ell$ when unit resolution derives the unit clause $\ell$ from $\Delta$, and $\Delta \vdash \mathrm{false}$ when it derives the empty clause; $\Delta$ is *1-inconsistent* if $\Delta \vdash \mathrm{false}$ and *1-consistent* otherwise.

A *decision sequence* is a sequence $D = \langle \ell_1, \dots, \ell_k \rangle$ of literals, $\ell_i$ being the decision at level $i$, and $D_m$ is its prefix of length $m$. A *SAT state* is a triple $S = (\Delta, \Gamma, D)$ of CNF formulas $\Delta$ (the input) and $\Gamma$ (the learned clauses) with $\Delta \models \Gamma$ and a decision sequence $D$; $S$ is 1-inconsistent when $\Delta \wedge \Gamma \wedge D$ is, and $S_k$ is $(\Delta, \Gamma, D_k)$. A literal $\ell$ is *implied at level* $k$, $S \vdash_k \ell$, if $k$ is the least integer with $\Delta \wedge \Gamma \wedge D_k \vdash \ell$. The *implication level* of a variable is the least $k$ such that $\Delta \wedge \Gamma \wedge D_k$ derives one of its two literals by unit resolution, and both literals of the variable have this level. Each variable thus has at most one level, even when unit resolution derives both of its literals: with $\Delta = \{p,\ \bar a \vee u,\ \bar a \vee \bar u \vee \bar p\}$ and the decision $a$, unit resolution derives $p$ at level $0$ and also $\bar p$ at level $1$, and the variable $p$ has level $0$. A decision $\ell_i$ is implied at level $i$, so in a normal state a literal falsified by the decision $\ell_i$ has implication level $i$. A state is *normal* if, for every $i \le k$, $S_{i-1}$ is 1-consistent and neither $\ell_i$ nor its complement is implied by $S_{i-1}$: no decision is made in a conflict or on an assigned variable.
