# Substituting for the outermost bound variable of a term

Let $L$ be a first-order language, $s$ an $L$-term with free variables in $\alpha$ and one bound
variable, and $t$ an $L$-term with free variables in $\alpha$ and $m + 1$ bound variables
$0, \dots, m$. Substituting $s$ for the bound variable $0$ of $t$ gives again a term with free
variables in $\alpha$ and bound variables $0, \dots, m$: every occurrence of the bound variable $0$
in $t$ is replaced by $s$, whose bound variable becomes the bound variable $0$ and whose free
variables stay free, and every other variable of $t$ is unchanged.
