# Herbrand's theorem

Let $L$ be a first-order language that has at least one ground term, and let $\varphi(x)$ be a quantifier-free formula of $L$ with one free variable $x$. If the sentence $\exists x\, \varphi$ holds in every nonempty $L$-structure, then there are finitely many ground terms $t_1, \dots, t_r$ of $L$ such that the disjunction $\varphi(t_1) \vee \dots \vee \varphi(t_r)$ holds in every nonempty $L$-structure. The hypothesis on ground terms excludes the case of a language without constants, where the empty disjunction would be needed. Buss states the theorem for several existential quantifiers and for consequences of universal theories, where the disjunction is a tautological consequence of finitely many ground instances of the axioms.
