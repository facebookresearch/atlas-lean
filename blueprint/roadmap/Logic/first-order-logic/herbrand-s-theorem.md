# Herbrand's theorem

Let $L$ be a first-order language without equality that has at least one constant symbol, and let $\varphi(y_1, \dots, y_k)$ be a quantifier-free formula of $L$ with no free variables other than $y_1, \dots, y_k$. If the sentence $\exists y_1 \cdots \exists y_k\, \varphi$ is valid, then there are finitely many tuples $(t_{i,1}, \dots, t_{i,k})$, $1 \le i \le r$, of ground terms of $L$ such that the disjunction $\bigvee_{i=1}^{r} \varphi(t_{i,1}, \dots, t_{i,k})$ is a tautology. Conversely, if such a disjunction is a tautology, the existential sentence is valid. More generally, the same holds with validity replaced by consequence from a theory axiomatized by universal sentences, with the disjunction then a tautological consequence of finitely many ground instances of the quantifier-free matrices of those axioms.
