# Logic

Logic studies formal languages, proof, models, semantics, consistency, and the foundations of mathematical reasoning.

[Modal logic](modal-logic/README.md) is the largest field, with more than a third of the subject's articles. The
systems K, T, S4 and S5 are
[sound and strongly complete](modal-logic/soundness-and-completeness-of-normal-modal-logic/README.md) for Kripke
models whose accessibility relation is arbitrary, reflexive, reflexive and transitive, or an equivalence relation, and
[filtration](modal-logic/filtration-and-the-finite-model-property/README.md) gives each of them the finite model
property. The field also has the [modal μ-calculus](modal-logic/modal-calculus/README.md), with least and greatest
fixed points, and its [model-checking game](modal-logic/model-checking-game-for-the-modal-calculus/README.md), a parity
game between Verifier and Refuter.

[Propositional logic](propositional-logic/README.md) covers natural deduction and its completeness,
[Craig interpolation](propositional-logic/craig-interpolation/README.md) and satisfiability, from the
[Tseitin transformation](propositional-logic/tseitin-transformation/README.md) and resolution to the DPLL procedure
and binary decision diagrams. [First-order logic](first-order-logic/README.md) centers on
[Gödel's completeness theorem](first-order-logic/g-del-s-completeness-theorem/README.md) in its strong form, built
from Henkin witnesses, and in [temporal logic](temporal-logic/README.md) every formula of linear temporal logic has a
Büchi automaton accepting exactly the words that satisfy it, the basis of
[LTL model checking](temporal-logic/model-checking-for-linear-temporal-logic/README.md).

[Proof theory](proof-theory/README.md) has
[cut elimination for classical linear logic](proof-theory/cut-elimination-for-classical-linear-logic/README.md) and
abstract forms of [Gödel's incompleteness theorems](proof-theory/g-del-s-incompleteness-theorems/README.md), and
[model theory](model-theory/README.md) has Tarski's
[quantifier elimination for the real field](model-theory/quantifier-elimination-for-real-closed-fields/README.md),
Morley's categoricity theorem and [Wilkie's conjecture](model-theory/wilkie-s-conjecture/README.md) for
$\mathbb{R}_{\exp}$, which bounds the number of algebraic points of degree at most $g$ and height at most $H$ on the
transcendental part of a definable set by a polynomial in $g$ and $\log H$.
