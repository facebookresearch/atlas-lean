# Satisfiability of ATLsc under partial observation is undecidable

With partial observation, satisfiability of ATL with strategy contexts is undecidable even over turn-based game structures, although under full observation turn-based satisfiability is decidable.

**Theorem (Laroussinie, Markey and Sangnier).** A *concurrent game structure with partial observation* (CGSO) is a finite-state concurrent game structure with a finite nonempty set of moves, together with an equivalence relation $\sim_a$ on states for each agent $a$; histories of equal length are $a$-equivalent if they are pointwise $\sim_a$-equivalent, and strategies of $a$ must give the same move after $a$-equivalent histories. It is *turn-based* (a TBGSO) if each state has an owner whose move alone determines the successor. Interpret [$\mathrm{ATL}^*_{sc}$, $\mathrm{ATL}_{sc}$, $\mathrm{ATL}^*_{sc,0}$ and $\mathrm{ATL}_{sc,0}$](atl-with-strategy-contexts.md) over CGSOs with strategy quantifiers ranging over such observation-respecting strategies (memoryless ones in the two variants with subscript $0$). For each of these four logics, the problem of deciding, given a formula $\Phi$, whether there exist a CGSO, an observation relation and a state satisfying $\Phi$ is undecidable, even when restricted to turn-based structures. This is Theorem 16 of the source, which reuses the reduction of Troquard and Walther from satisfiability over finite models of the product modal logic $\mathrm{S5} \times \cdots \times \mathrm{S5}$ ($n$ factors, written $\mathrm{S5}_n$ there) to $\mathrm{ATL}_{sc}$ satisfiability.

Partial observation changes satisfiability: the formula $\mathbf{AX}(\langle{\cdot} a_1 {\cdot}\rangle\mathbf X f) \wedge \neg\langle{\cdot} a_1 {\cdot}\rangle\mathbf{X}\mathbf X f$ is satisfiable with partial observation but not with full observation. Model checking $\mathrm{ATL}^*_{sc}$ over CGSOs with uniform observation (all agents sharing one equivalence) is decidable (Corollary 11 of the source).

## Expansion

The proof reuses the reduction of Troquard and Walther from formulas $\Phi$ of the $n$-fold product $\mathrm{S5} \times \cdots \times \mathrm{S5}$, written $\mathrm{S5}_n$ there (Section 4.1 of Troquard and Walther; not the multi-agent logic usually written $\mathrm{S5}_n$, whose satisfiability is decidable), in the form given by Laroussinie and Markey. With $\widehat P = \langle{\cdot}\emptyset{\cdot}\rangle\mathbf X P$ for atomic $P$, $\widehat\Phi$ commuting with $\neg$ and $\wedge$, and $\widehat{\Diamond_i\psi} = \langle{\cdot} a_i {\cdot}\rangle\widehat\psi$, the formula $\Phi$ has a finite model iff $\langle{\cdot}\mathrm{Agt}{\cdot}\rangle\widehat\Phi$ is satisfiable in a finite structure, where $\mathrm{Agt} = \{a_1, \ldots, a_n\}$ (Proposition 16 of Laroussinie and Markey, finite case; the source writes this coalition as the complement of $\emptyset$). The outer quantifier over all agents fixes a whole move vector, a world of the product model; the inner $\langle{\cdot}\emptyset{\cdot}\rangle\mathbf X P$ tests the successor this context selects, and $\langle{\cdot} a_i {\cdot}\rangle$ changes the $i$-th coordinate. With $\langle{\cdot}\emptyset{\cdot}\rangle$ outside instead, the equivalence fails: $p \wedge \Diamond_1\neg p$ holds at $(0,0,0)$ in the product $\{0,1\} \times \{0\} \times \{0\}$ with $p$ true only there, but with no move fixed the first conjunct requires every successor to satisfy $p$. Troquard and Walther's own formula is $\langle{\cdot}\Sigma_\Phi{\cdot}\rangle(\bot\,\mathbf U\,\mathrm{tr}(\Phi))$, with $\Sigma_\Phi$ the agents occurring in $\Phi$, $\mathrm{tr}(p) = \langle{\cdot}\emptyset{\cdot}\rangle\mathbf X p$ and $\mathrm{tr}(\Diamond_i\varphi) = \langle{\cdot} a_i {\cdot}\rangle(\bot\,\mathbf U\,\mathrm{tr}(\varphi))$, satisfiable iff $\Phi$ has a finite model (Lemma 1); its converse direction builds a product model whose $i$-th factor is the finite set of moves of agent $i$, and the CGSOs here have finitely many states and moves. The formulas use only Boolean operators, strategy quantifiers, non-nested $\mathbf X$, and $\bot\,\mathbf U\,\psi$, which holds iff $\psi$ holds at the current position; hence every strategy quantifier is evaluated at a single state and only the moves at that state matter, so adding observation relations or restricting to memoryless strategies does not change their meaning. A satisfying concurrent structure is converted into a turn-based one with partial observation (Theorem 6 of the source), and conversely a model with partial observation yields an $\mathrm{S5}_n$ model as in the full-observation case. A good expansion links pages for the undecidability of satisfiability over finite models of the product $\mathrm{S5} \times \cdots \times \mathrm{S5}$ for $n \ge 3$ (Theorem 1 (ii) of Troquard and Walther; this differs from plain satisfiability because the product lacks the finite model property for $n \ge 3$) and for the reduction: $\Phi$ has a finite model iff $\langle{\cdot}\mathrm{Agt}{\cdot}\rangle\widehat\Phi$ is satisfiable in a finite structure (Proposition 16 of Laroussinie and Markey; Lemma 1 of Troquard and Walther in their own form).
