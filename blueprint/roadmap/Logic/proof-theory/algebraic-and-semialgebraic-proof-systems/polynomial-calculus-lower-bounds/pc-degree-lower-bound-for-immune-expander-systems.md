# Polynomial calculus degree lower bounds for immune polynomials on expanders

If polynomial constraints are placed on the rows of a boundary expander and each constraint is $\ell$-immune, meaning it has no nonzero semantic consequence of degree below $\ell$, then every polynomial calculus refutation over any field has degree linear in the expansion size; this criterion goes beyond binomial ideals.

Polynomials are multilinear over a field $\mathbb{F}$ and semantic implication $f_1, \dots, f_k \models g$ means that $g$ vanishes on every common $0$-$1$ root of the $f_i$. Polynomial calculus is the multilinear variant of the linked definition: lines are multilinear polynomials, the rules are $f, g \vdash \alpha f + \beta g$ and $f \vdash f \cdot x$ with the product multilinearized, a refutation derives $1$, and its degree is the maximum degree of an inference.

- **Immunity.** A polynomial $f$ is $\ell$-immune if for every nonzero polynomial $g$, $f \models g$ implies $\deg(g) \ge \ell$. A Boolean function is $\ell$-immune over $\mathbb{F}$ if its associated multilinear polynomial is.
- **Expander.** For an $m \times n$ $0$-$1$ matrix $A$ let $J_i(A) = \{j : a_{ij} = 1\}$ and $X_i(A) = \{x_j : j \in J_i(A)\}$. The boundary $\partial_A(I)$ of a set $I$ of rows is the set of columns $j$ such that $\{a_{ij} : i \in I\}$ contains exactly one $1$. $A$ is an $(r, s, c)$-expander if $|J_i(A)| \le s$ for all $i$ and $|\partial_A(I)| \ge c|I|$ for every $I \subseteq [m]$ with $|I| \le r$.

**Theorem.** Let $A$ be an $(r, s, c)$-expander, let $\ell$ be an integer with $s \le r(\ell/4 - (s - c))$, and let $f_1, \dots, f_m$ be $\ell$-immune polynomials over an arbitrary field with $\mathrm{Vars}(f_i) \subseteq X_i(A)$. Then every polynomial calculus refutation of $f_1 = \dots = f_m = 0$ has degree greater than $r(\ell/4 - (s - c))$.

**Maximal-immunity case.** If $A$ is an $(r, s, c)$-expander and $\epsilon(i) \in \{0,1\}^{X_i(A)}$, then every polynomial calculus refutation of $\{\chi_{\epsilon(i)}(X_i(A)) : i \in [m]\}$ has degree greater than $rc/2$, where $\chi_{\epsilon}(\vec{v})$ is the multilinear polynomial equal to $1$ if $\vec{v} = \epsilon$ and $0$ otherwise.

## Proof sketch

Immunity is preserved under restrictions up to the number of fixed variables: if $f$ is $\ell$-immune then $f|_\rho$ is $(\ell - |\rho|)$-immune. The core is a locality theorem: for a partition $Y, V, Z$ of the variables, $\bar{P}$ over $Y \cup V$ and a $(2|V|+1)$-immune $Q$ over $V \cup Z$, a $Z$-free term reducible modulo the ideal of $\bar{P}, Q$ is already reducible modulo $\bar{P}$. Using the expansion one then builds a linear operator that acts on low-degree terms like reduction modulo the ideal generated by a small, locally determined set of rows, and Razborov's criterion turns such an operator into a degree lower bound. The maximal-immunity case covers clause polynomials, giving degree bounds for random $k$-CNFs; other applications are mod-$p$ Tseitin formulas over fields of characteristic different from $p$ and flow tautologies based on majority over every field.
