# Ideal Proof System

The Ideal Proof System (IPS) certifies that polynomial equations $F_1 = \dots = F_m = 0$ have no common root by an algebraic circuit $C(\vec{x}, \vec{y})$ in new placeholder variables $y_i$ that vanishes when every $y_i$ is set to $0$ and becomes the constant polynomial $1$ when each $y_i$ is replaced by $F_i$.

Let $\mathbb{F}$ be a field and $F_1, \dots, F_m \in \mathbb{F}[x_1, \dots, x_n]$. An **IPS certificate** that the system $F_1(\vec{x}) = \dots = F_m(\vec{x}) = 0$ is unsatisfiable over the algebraic closure $\overline{\mathbb{F}}$ is a polynomial $C(\vec{x}, \vec{y}) \in \mathbb{F}[x_1, \dots, x_n, y_1, \dots, y_m]$ such that

$$
C(x_1, \dots, x_n, 0, \dots, 0) = 0 \qquad \text{and} \qquad C(x_1, \dots, x_n, F_1(\vec{x}), \dots, F_m(\vec{x})) = 1 .
$$

The first condition says that $C$ lies in the ideal of $\mathbb{F}[\vec{x}, \vec{y}]$ generated by $y_1, \dots, y_m$; together the two conditions give $1 \in \langle F_1, \dots, F_m \rangle$, so the system has no common root. An **IPS proof** is an $\mathbb{F}$-algebraic circuit with inputs $x_1, \dots, x_n, y_1, \dots, y_m$ that computes an IPS certificate; its size is the size of the circuit. For a class $\mathcal{C}$ of algebraic circuits one speaks of $\mathcal{C}$-IPS proofs of a family of systems; IPS without qualification means $\mathrm{VP}$-IPS, i.e. families of circuits of polynomial size and polynomial degree.

For propositional proof complexity one adds the Boolean axioms with their own placeholders: an IPS refutation of $f_1, \dots, f_m$ over $\{0,1\}^n$ is a circuit $C(\vec{x}, \vec{y}, \vec{z})$ with $C(\vec{x}, \vec{0}, \vec{0}) = 0$ and $C(\vec{x}, f_1(\vec{x}), \dots, f_m(\vec{x}), x_1^2 - x_1, \dots, x_n^2 - x_n) = 1$.

## Proof sketch

By the weak Nullstellensatz, a certificate exists exactly when the system has no common root over $\overline{\mathbb{F}}$, so IPS is sound and complete. The two defining conditions are polynomial identities, so an IPS proof is verified by polynomial identity testing, which runs in randomized polynomial time. The key difference from rule-based algebraic systems such as Nullstellensatz and Polynomial Calculus is that intermediate gates of an IPS proof need not compute polynomials in the ideal $\langle F_1, \dots, F_m \rangle$; only the final certificate is constrained, so an IPS proof is an ordinary algebraic circuit and depth-reduction and other circuit transformations apply to proof size directly. Previously studied algebraic systems are complexity measures on certificates of special shape; for instance Nullstellensatz degree is the least degree of a certificate of the form $\sum_i y_i G_i(\vec{x})$.
