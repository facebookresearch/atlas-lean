# Gentzen's cut elimination for first-order logic

The following holds. Let $L$ be a first-order language and $X$ an infinite set of variables. For every finite list $\Gamma$ of $L$-formulas and every $L$-formula $\varphi$ with free variables in $X$: if the sequent $\Gamma \Rightarrow \varphi$ is derivable in the classical sequent calculus with cut, then it is derivable in the same calculus without the cut rule.

The calculus has the rules assumption, ex falso, $\to$ right and left, $\forall$ right (with the eigenvariable condition) and left, reductio ad absurdum, reflexivity of equality and substitution of equals, plus, in the calculus with cut, the rule: from $\Gamma \Rightarrow \varphi$ and $\varphi, \Gamma \Rightarrow \psi$ infer $\Gamma \Rightarrow \psi$.
