# Reverse mathematics

This node covers reverse mathematics: determining which set-existence axioms are needed to prove
theorems of ordinary mathematics, by proving equivalences between theorems and axioms over a weak base
theory. The formal setting is second-order arithmetic and its subsystems. The base theory is
$\mathsf{RCA}_0$; the other four of the "Big Five" subsystems, $\mathsf{WKL}_0$, $\mathsf{ACA}_0$,
$\mathsf{ATR}_0$ and $\Pi^1_1\text{-}\mathsf{CA}_0$, are proper extensions of $\mathsf{RCA}_0$ whose
characteristic axioms are weak König's lemma, arithmetical comprehension, arithmetical transfinite
recursion and $\Pi^1_1$ comprehension, and each is equivalent over $\mathsf{RCA}_0$ to many theorems of
analysis, algebra, infinitary combinatorics, logic and topology. Each system proves theorems that the
preceding one does not. For example, weak König's lemma, the statement that every infinite tree
$T \subseteq 2^{<\mathbb{N}}$ has a path, is equivalent over $\mathsf{RCA}_0$ to the Heine–Borel
covering theorem.

## Expansion

A sound expansion fixes the language of second-order arithmetic and its semantics, the axioms of
$\mathsf{RCA}_0$ (including the coding of finite sequences and of real numbers it supports), and the
Big Five systems as sets of axioms; then it states individual equivalences over $\mathsf{RCA}_0$, each
with its own source and its own coding of the mathematical objects, and the strictness of the
hierarchy as separate pages.
