# Deriv

A sequent $\Gamma \vdash \Delta$ is a pair of finite lists of propositional formulas; $A, \Gamma$ denotes the list $\Gamma$ with $A$ added at the head. Derivable sequents of this variant of Gentzen's calculus LK for classical propositional logic are generated by the following rules, for all lists $\Gamma, \Delta$ and formulas $A, B$.

- Axiom: $\Gamma \vdash \Delta$ whenever some formula $A$ occurs in both $\Gamma$ and $\Delta$.
- Implication: from $A, \Gamma \vdash B, \Delta$ infer $\Gamma \vdash A \to B, \Delta$; from $\Gamma \vdash A, \Delta$ and $B, \Gamma \vdash \Delta$ infer $A \to B, \Gamma \vdash \Delta$.
- Conjunction: from $\Gamma \vdash A, \Delta$ and $\Gamma \vdash B, \Delta$ infer $\Gamma \vdash A \wedge B, \Delta$; from $A, B, \Gamma \vdash \Delta$ infer $A \wedge B, \Gamma \vdash \Delta$.
- Disjunction: from $\Gamma \vdash A, B, \Delta$ infer $\Gamma \vdash A \vee B, \Delta$; from $A, \Gamma \vdash \Delta$ and $B, \Gamma \vdash \Delta$ infer $A \vee B, \Gamma \vdash \Delta$.
- Negation: from $A, \Gamma \vdash \Delta$ infer $\Gamma \vdash \neg A, \Delta$; from $\Gamma \vdash A, \Delta$ infer $\neg A, \Gamma \vdash \Delta$.
- Weakening: from $\Gamma \vdash \Delta$ infer $\Gamma \vdash A, \Delta$ and $A, \Gamma \vdash \Delta$.
- Contraction: from $\Gamma \vdash A, A, \Delta$ infer $\Gamma \vdash A, \Delta$; from $A, A, \Gamma \vdash \Delta$ infer $A, \Gamma \vdash \Delta$.
- Exchange: from $\Gamma \vdash \Delta$ infer $\Gamma' \vdash \Delta$ and $\Gamma \vdash \Delta'$ for any permutations $\Gamma'$ of $\Gamma$ and $\Delta'$ of $\Delta$.
- Cut: from $\Gamma \vdash A, \Delta$ and $A, \Gamma \vdash \Delta$ infer $\Gamma \vdash \Delta$.

Rules with two premises share the side formulas $\Gamma$ and $\Delta$.
