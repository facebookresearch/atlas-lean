# DAG-like resolution derivations

A clause is a finite list of literals $(x, s)$, a variable $x \in \mathbb{N}$ with a sign $s$, repeats allowed, and a CNF formula $\varphi$ is a list of clauses. The resolvent of clauses $C$ and $D$ on the pivot $x$ is the list $C$ with every occurrence of the literal $x$ removed, followed by the list $D$ with every occurrence of $\neg x$ removed. A DAG-like resolution derivation from $\varphi$ is a list of clauses, built from the empty list (which counts as a derivation) by appending one clause at a time, where each appended clause is either an input clause of $\varphi$ or the resolvent on some pivot $x$ of two clauses $C_1$, $C_2$ already in the list with $x \in C_1$ and $\neg x \in C_2$. Earlier clauses may be used any number of times; there is no weakening rule.
