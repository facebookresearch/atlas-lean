# Width of a tree derivation

A clause is a finite list of literals $(x, s)$, a variable $x \in \mathbb{N}$ with a sign $s$, repeats allowed, and a CNF formula $\varphi$ is a list of clauses. The resolvent of clauses $C$ and $D$ on the pivot $x$ is the list $C$ with every occurrence of the literal $x$ removed, followed by the list $D$ with every occurrence of $\neg x$ removed. The width of a [tree derivation](tree-derivation.md) is the largest length of a clause occurring in it, where the length of a clause is the length of its list, counting repeated literals. For example a tree whose clauses contain a repeated literal can have width $4$ while its largest number of distinct literals in a clause is $3$. This differs from the width of the separate resolution calculus with weakening, which counts distinct literals.
