# The deep-inference system SKS in the calculus of structures

SKS is Brünnler's deep-inference proof system for classical propositional logic in the calculus of structures: its rules rewrite subformulas at any depth inside a formula, and its structural rules (identity, cut, weakening, contraction) are atomic.

*Formulas* are built from the units $\mathsf f$ and $\mathsf t$, atoms $a,\bar a,b,\bar b,\dots$ (negation $\bar\cdot$ is an involution on atoms), formula variables, disjunction $[\alpha\vee\beta]$ and conjunction $(\alpha\wedge\beta)$; the De Morgan dual $\bar\alpha$ swaps $\vee$ and $\wedge$ and negates atoms and variables. A *context* $\xi\{\ \}$ is a formula with one hole. An inference *rule* $\nu$ is a pair $\alpha/\beta$ (premise over conclusion); an *inference step* replaces $\xi\{\gamma\}$ by $\xi\{\delta\}$ for an instance $\gamma/\delta$ of the rule obtained by renaming and substitution. The *equality* $=$ is the congruence generated by commutativity and associativity of $\vee$ and $\wedge$ and by the unit laws $\alpha\vee\mathsf f=\alpha$, $\alpha\wedge\mathsf t=\alpha$, $\mathsf t\vee\mathsf t=\mathsf t$, $\mathsf f\wedge\mathsf f=\mathsf f$; it is decidable in time $O(n^2)$.

A *derivation* in a system $S$ (a finite set of rules) alternates $=$-steps with steps of rules of $S$; a *proof* is a derivation with premise $\mathsf t$; size counts occurrences of units, atoms and variables. The rules of SKS are

$$
\mathsf{ai}{\downarrow}\ \frac{\mathsf t}{a\vee\bar a}\qquad
\mathsf{aw}{\downarrow}\ \frac{\mathsf f}{a}\qquad
\mathsf{ac}{\downarrow}\ \frac{a\vee a}{a}\qquad
\mathsf s\ \frac{A\wedge[B\vee C]}{(A\wedge B)\vee C}\qquad
\mathsf m\ \frac{(A\wedge B)\vee(C\wedge D)}{[A\vee C]\wedge[B\vee D]}
$$

(atomic identity, weakening, contraction, switch, medial), which form system KS, together with their duals

$$
\mathsf{ai}{\uparrow}\ \frac{a\wedge\bar a}{\mathsf f}\qquad
\mathsf{aw}{\uparrow}\ \frac{a}{\mathsf t}\qquad
\mathsf{ac}{\uparrow}\ \frac{a}{a\wedge a}
$$

(atomic cut, coweakening, cocontraction). The system SKSg replaces the atomic structural rules by their general versions $\mathsf i{\downarrow}$, $\mathsf w{\downarrow}$, $\mathsf c{\downarrow}$, $\mathsf i{\uparrow}$, $\mathsf w{\uparrow}$, $\mathsf c{\uparrow}$ on arbitrary formulas $A$ and has no medial; KSg is its fragment $\{\mathsf i{\downarrow},\mathsf w{\downarrow},\mathsf c{\downarrow},\mathsf s\}$. Systems without $\mathsf i{\uparrow}$, $\mathsf{ai}{\uparrow}$, $\mathsf w{\uparrow}$, $\mathsf{aw}{\uparrow}$ are *analytic*.

Facts from the source: SKSg, KSg, SKS and KS are complete, and SKSg and SKS are implicationally complete (Brünnler); KS and KSg are p-equivalent, and SKS and SKSg are p-equivalent; all implicationally complete systems of the calculus of structures are p-equivalent.

## Expansion

Locality is what deep inference buys: with medial, contraction can be reduced to atoms, so every rule of SKS acts on a bounded amount of material, which is impossible in the sequent calculus. Analytic deep inference is also more efficient than analytic sequent calculus: the Statman tautologies $S_n$ require cut-free Gentzen proofs of exponential size, but KS proves them with proofs whose size is quadratic in the size of $S_n$, using switch and identity steps just below the surface of the formula.
