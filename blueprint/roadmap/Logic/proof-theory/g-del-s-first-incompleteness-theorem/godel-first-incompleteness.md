# Gödel's first incompleteness theorem

The following holds. If Peano arithmetic is consistent ($\mathrm{PA} \nvdash \bot$), then there is a sentence $\sigma$ of the language of arithmetic such that

$$\mathbb{N} \models \sigma, \qquad \mathrm{PA} \nvdash \sigma, \qquad \mathrm{PA} \nvdash \neg \sigma.$$

Here $\mathrm{PA} \vdash \varphi$ denotes derivability from the Peano axioms in the classical natural deduction calculus for first-order logic, with sentences regarded as formulas over the infinite set $\mathbb{N}$ of variable names, and $\mathbb{N} \models \sigma$ means $\sigma$ is true in the standard model.
