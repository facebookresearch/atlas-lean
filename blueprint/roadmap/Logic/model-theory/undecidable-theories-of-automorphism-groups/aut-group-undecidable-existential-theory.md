# Kourovka 12.14: automorphism groups with undecidable theory

Question (M. Giraudet and A. M. W. Glass, Kourovka Notebook 12.14), in the strong form of its answer. Statement: let $L$ be a first-order language with countably many symbols and let $T$ be an $L$-theory that has an infinite model. Then $T$ has a model $A$ such that the existential theory of the group $\mathrm{Aut}(A)$ is undecidable: the set of finite systems of group equations $u = 1$ and inequations $v \ne 1$ (words coded as lists of variable-sign pairs) that are solvable in $\mathrm{Aut}(A)$ is not computable.

Since an existential sentence of the language of groups is effectively equivalent to a finite disjunction of such systems, this is the same as undecidability of the existential theory of $\mathrm{Aut}(A)$, and it implies that the full theory of $\mathrm{Aut}(A)$ is undecidable. It holds; the resolution is [Kourovka 12.14: automorphism groups with undecidable theory exist](aut-group-undecidable-existential-theory-2.md).
