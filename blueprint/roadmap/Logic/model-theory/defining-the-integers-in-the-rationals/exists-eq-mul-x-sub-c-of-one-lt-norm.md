# A trace outside $\mathbb{Z}_p$ gives two distinct roots

Let $p$ be a prime and let
$s \in \mathbb{Q}_p$ with $\lVert s \rVert > 1$, that is, $s \in \mathbb{Q}_p \setminus
\mathbb{Z}_p$. Then there are $r_1, r_2 \in \mathbb{Q}_p$ with

$$x^2 - sx + 1 = (x - r_1)(x - r_2)$$

in $\mathbb{Q}_p[x]$, $r_1 \neq r_2$, $\lVert r_1 \rVert = \lVert s \rVert$ and
$\lVert r_2 \rVert = \lVert s \rVert^{-1}$.

## Sources

- B. Poonen, "Characterizing integers among rational numbers with a universal-existential formula", Amer. J. Math. 131 (2009), 675–682, proof of Lemma 2.1(ii), read in the author's version with the corrections of April 15, 2019, p. 3: "If $s \in \mathbb{Q}_p - \mathbb{Z}_p$, then $x^2 - sx + 1$ is a product of two distinct factors, by the theory of Newton polygons". That Newton polygon has vertices $(0, 0)$, $(1, v_p(s))$ and $(2, 0)$, so its slopes are $v_p(s)$ and $-v_p(s)$; the valuations $v_p(r_1) = v_p(s)$ and $v_p(r_2) = -v_p(s)$ of the roots are minus these slopes.
