# The elementary-equivalence criterion for wreath products fails

This result refutes the proposition stated as [Kourovka Problem 2.20: elementary equivalence of wreath products](wreath-elementary-equivalence-iff.md). The statement is false: there are nontrivial groups $A$, $B$, $A_1$, $B_1$ for which the equivalence

$$A \wr B \equiv A_1 \wr B_1 \iff A \equiv A_1 \text{ and } B \equiv B_1$$

fails.

Here $A \wr B$ is the restricted (standard) wreath product: the subgroup of the regular wreath product $A^B \rtimes B$ consisting of the elements whose base component $B \to A$ has finite support; for finite $B$ it is all of $A^B \rtimes B$. Elementary equivalence $\equiv$ is with respect to the first-order language of groups (multiplication, inversion and the identity). With a trivial factor the question degenerates ($1 \wr C_2 \cong C_2 \cong C_2 \wr 1$), which is why all four groups are nontrivial. The analogous statement with universal equivalence in place of elementary equivalence is a different statement and is not the one meant here.
