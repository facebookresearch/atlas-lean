# Pheidas' question: additive polynomials are Diophantine over $\mathbb{F}_p[Z]$

Question (T. Pheidas), positive answer. For a prime $p$, call a polynomial in $\mathbb{F}_p[Z]$ additive if it has the form $\alpha_0 Z + \alpha_1 Z^p + \cdots + \alpha_n Z^{p^n}$ with $\alpha_i \in \mathbb{F}_p$.

Statement: for every prime $p$, the set $\mathcal{A}_p$ of additive polynomials is Diophantine over $\mathbb{F}_p[Z]$ with coefficients in $\mathbb{F}_p[Z]$: there are $n \in \mathbb{N}$ and finitely many polynomials $P_1, \dots, P_m$ in variables $x, y_1, \dots, y_n$ with coefficients in $\mathbb{F}_p[Z]$ such that for every $f \in \mathbb{F}_p[Z]$,

$$f \in \mathcal{A}_p \iff \exists\, y_1, \dots, y_n \in \mathbb{F}_p[Z] :\ P_j(f, y_1, \dots, y_n) = 0 \text{ for all } j.$$

Since the coefficients range over $\mathbb{F}_p[Z]$, the constant $Z$ may be used. It holds; the resolution is [Additive polynomials are Diophantine over $\mathbb{F}_p[Z]$](additive-polynomials-diophantine-holds.md).
