# Metric Wu planes and ordered origami planes

A metric Wu plane is a two-sorted structure of points and lines with incidence and orthogonality satisfying Wu's axioms of orthogonal geometry: incidence, the parallel axiom, an axiom of infinity, Desargues' axioms, orthogonality axioms and the existence of axes of symmetry. Adding betweenness and origami axioms gives ordered and origami planes.

**Vocabularies.** Points $P, Q, \dots$ and lines $\ell, m, \dots$ are two sorts (definable from incidence). The vocabulary $\tau_{wu}$ has the incidence relation $P \in \ell$ and the orthogonality relation $\ell \perp m$; the vocabulary $\tau_{o\text{-}wu}$ adds the betweenness relation $Be(P_1, P_2, P_3)$.

**Axioms.** For distinct points $A, B$ write $AB$ for the line through them, and call lines parallel, $\ell \parallel m$, if they are equal or have no common point. A $\tau_{wu}$-structure is a *metric Wu plane* (Definitions 1–3 of the source) if it satisfies:

- Hilbert's incidence axioms: (I-1) any two distinct points lie on a unique line; (I-2) every line contains at least two distinct points; (I-3) there are three distinct points not all on one line;
- the parallel axiom (ParAx): through a point $A$ not on a line $\ell$ there is exactly one line that does not meet $\ell$;
- the infinity axiom schema (InfLines): given non-collinear points $A, B, C$ and a line $\ell$ with $A \in \ell$, $B, C \notin \ell$ and $BC \parallel \ell$, let $A_1$ be the intersection of $\ell$ with the line through $C$ parallel to $AB$, and inductively $A_{n+1}$ the intersection of $\ell$ with the line through $C$ parallel to $A_nB$; then the points $A_1, A_2, \dots$ are pairwise distinct (one first-order sentence for each pair of indices);
- Desargues' axioms: (De-1) if the corresponding sides of two triangles $ABC$ and $A'B'C'$ are pairwise parallel, $AB \parallel A'B'$, $AC \parallel A'C'$, $BC \parallel B'C'$, then the lines $AA'$, $BB'$, $CC'$ are parallel to each other or concurrent; (De-2) if $AB \parallel A'B'$ and $AC \parallel A'C'$, and the lines $AA'$, $BB'$, $CC'$ are distinct and either concurrent or parallel to each other, then $BC \parallel B'C'$;
- the orthogonality axioms: (O-1) $\ell_1 \perp \ell_2$ iff $\ell_2 \perp \ell_1$; (O-2) given a point $O$ and a line $\ell_1$, exactly one line $\ell_2$ with $\ell_1 \perp \ell_2$ passes through $O$; (O-3) if $\ell_1 \perp \ell_2$ and $\ell_1 \perp \ell_3$, then $\ell_2 \parallel \ell_3$; (O-4) through every point passes a line $\ell$ with $\ell \not\perp \ell$ (lines with $\ell \perp \ell$ are *isotropic*); (O-5) the three altitudes of a triangle meet in one point;
- the axiom of symmetric axes (AxSymAx): any two intersecting non-isotropic lines have a symmetric axis, in the following sense.

*Symmetric points and axes.* For distinct points $A, B$ on a line $\ell$, choose $E \notin \ell$, let $D$ be the intersection of the line through $E$ parallel to $\ell$ with the line through $B$ parallel to $AE$, and let $C$ be the intersection of $\ell$ with the line through $D$ parallel to $EB$; by Desargues' axioms $C$ does not depend on $E$, and it is the *symmetric point of $A$ with respect to $B$* (and $P$ is its own symmetric point with respect to $P$). For points $A, B$ on a line $\ell$, choose a line $\ell' \ne \ell$ through $A$ and a point $M' \ne A$ on it, let $B'$ be the symmetric point of $A$ with respect to $M'$, and let $M$ be the intersection of $\ell$ with the line through $M'$ parallel to $B'B$; $M$ does not depend on the choices and is the *midpoint* of $A$ and $B$ (the midpoint of $A$ and $A$ is $A$). For distinct $A, B$, the *perpendicular bisector* of $A, B$ is the line through their midpoint perpendicular to $AB$; if $\ell$ is the perpendicular bisector of $A, B$, then $A$ is the *symmetric point of $B$ with respect to $\ell$*, written $\mathit{Sym}(A, \ell, B)$, and every point of $\ell$ is its own symmetric point with respect to $\ell$. For a non-isotropic line $\ell$, the points symmetric with respect to $\ell$ to the points of a line $\ell_1$ lie on a unique line $\ell_2$, the *symmetric line* of $\ell_1$ with respect to $\ell$, and $\ell$ is then a *symmetric axis* of $\ell_1$ and $\ell_2$.

The source prints (InfLines) without the hypothesis $BC \parallel \ell$. Without it the construction can cycle in the real plane: with $\ell$ the $x$-axis, $A = (0,0)$, $B = (1,1)$ and $C = (0,-1)$ it gives $A_1 = (1,0)$, $A_2 = (0,0)$, $A_3 = (1,0)$, so the printed schema has no model. With the hypothesis, the construction gives $A_n = A + n(C - B)$ in the plane over a field, so in the plane over a field the schema holds exactly when the field has characteristic $0$, which is the role the source gives it.

**Order and origami axioms.** An *ordered metric Wu plane* (Definition 9) is a $\tau_{o\text{-}wu}$-structure whose $\tau_{wu}$-reduct is a metric Wu plane and which satisfies the betweenness axioms, where $Be(A, B, C)$ means that $A, B, C$ are three distinct collinear points with $B$ between $A$ and $C$: (B-1) for distinct collinear $A, B, C$, if $Be(A, B, C)$ then $Be(C, B, A)$; (B-2) for distinct points $A, C$ there are points $B$ and $D$ on the line $AC$ with $Be(A, B, C)$ and $Be(A, C, D)$; (B-3) of three distinct collinear points exactly one lies between the other two; (B-4) (Pasch) if $A, B, C$ are not collinear, none of them lies on $\ell$, and $\ell$ meets $AB$ in a point $D$ with $Be(A, D, B)$, then $\ell$ contains a point $D'$ with $Be(A, D', C)$ or $Be(B, D', C)$. A point $A$ is *closer to a line $\ell$ than to a point $B$* if there are points $H \in \ell$ and $B'$ and a line $m \ni A$ with $\mathit{Sym}(H, m, B')$ and either $Be(A, B', B)$ or $B = B'$; a *fold placing a point $P$ onto a line $\ell$* is a line $m$ such that the symmetric point of $P$ with respect to $m$ lies on $\ell$. A *Euclidean ordered metric Wu plane* (Definition 10) is an ordered metric Wu plane satisfying (H*5): given points $P_1, P_2$ and a line $\ell_1$, if $P_2$ is closer to $\ell_1$ than to $P_1$, then some fold placing $P_1$ onto $\ell_1$ passes through $P_2$. An *ordered origami plane* (Definition 12, an ordered Wu origami plane in the source) is an ordered metric Wu plane satisfying (H*5) and (H*6): given points $P_1, P_2$ and lines $\ell_1, \ell_2$ with $P_1 \notin \ell_1$, $P_2 \notin \ell_2$, $\ell_1$ and $\ell_2$ not parallel, and the points distinct or the lines distinct, some fold places $P_1$ onto $\ell_1$ and $P_2$ onto $\ell_2$.

**The real plane.** $\Pi_\mathbb{R}$ is the plane over $\mathbb{R}$, with the usual points, lines, incidence, orthogonality ($a_0 a_1 + b_0 b_1 = 0$ for lines $a_i x + b_i y + c_i = 0$) and betweenness; it is an ordered origami plane.

## Expansion

Metric Wu planes are exactly the coordinate planes over Pythagorean fields of characteristic $0$, fields in which every sum of two squares is a square: the source proves that the theory of metric Wu planes is bi-interpretable with the theory of Pythagorean fields of characteristic $0$ (its Theorem 1 in Section 5; Makowsky's Theorem 13 stated a weaker form, without the definable isomorphisms), and that ordered metric Wu planes are bi-interpretable with ordered Pythagorean fields (Theorem 4) and Euclidean ordered metric Wu planes with Euclidean fields (Theorem 7), formally real Pythagorean fields in which every element is a square or minus a square.
