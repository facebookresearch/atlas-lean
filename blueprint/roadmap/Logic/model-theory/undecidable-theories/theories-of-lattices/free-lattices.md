# Free lattices

The free lattice $\mathbf{F}_\kappa$ on a set of $\kappa$ generators is the lattice generated by $\kappa$ elements that satisfy no relations beyond the lattice axioms; every map of the generators into a lattice extends to exactly one lattice homomorphism.

**Definition.** A *lattice* is a partially ordered set $(L, \leq)$ in which every two elements $x, y$ have a least upper bound $x \vee y$ and a greatest lower bound $x \wedge y$; equivalently, an algebra $(L, \vee, \wedge)$ in which both operations are associative, commutative and idempotent and satisfy the absorption laws $x \vee (x \wedge y) = x = x \wedge (x \vee y)$. For a cardinal $\kappa$, the *free lattice* $\mathbf{F}_\kappa$ is a lattice together with a set $X$ of $\kappa$ elements generating it such that every map $f : X \to L$ into a lattice $\mathbf{L}$ extends to exactly one lattice homomorphism $\mathbf{F}_\kappa \to \mathbf{L}$. Such a lattice exists and is determined up to isomorphism; its elements are the lattice terms over $X$ modulo the equations valid in all lattices. A lattice is *free* if it is isomorphic to some $\mathbf{F}_\kappa$, and *finitely generated free* if $\kappa$ is finite.

Lattices are treated as structures in the language $\{\leq\}$ of posets, in which $\vee$ and $\wedge$ are definable.

## Expansion

$\mathbf{F}_1$ has one element and $\mathbf{F}_2$ has four, while $\mathbf{F}_\kappa$ is infinite for $\kappa \geq 3$; $\mathbf{F}_3$ already contains $\mathbf{F}_\omega$ as a sublattice. Whitman's solution of the word problem decides $s \leq t$ for lattice terms recursively; its key clause, Whitman's condition, says that $s_1 \wedge s_2 \leq t_1 \vee t_2$ holds iff $s_1 \leq t_1 \vee t_2$, or $s_2 \leq t_1 \vee t_2$, or $s_1 \wedge s_2 \leq t_1$, or $s_1 \wedge s_2 \leq t_2$. Nation and Paolini showed that the universal theory of infinite free lattices is decidable.
