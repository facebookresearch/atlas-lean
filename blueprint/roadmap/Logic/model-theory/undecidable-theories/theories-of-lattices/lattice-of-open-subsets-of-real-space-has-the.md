# The lattice of open subsets of ℝⁿ has the theory of second-order arithmetic

For every n ≥ 2, the first-order theory of the lattice of open subsets of ℝⁿ under inclusion is many-one equivalent to second-order arithmetic, so it is far from decidable; for n = 1 the theory is decidable.

**Theorem (Henson, Jockusch, Rubel and Takeuti; reproved by Kudinov and Selivanov).** Let $\boldsymbol\Sigma^0_1(\mathbb R^n)$ be the lattice of all open subsets of $\mathbb R^n$ ordered by inclusion and $\mathrm{FO}(\boldsymbol\Sigma^0_1(\mathbb R^n))$ its first-order theory in the signature $\{\subseteq\}$. For every $n \ge 2$,

$$
\mathrm{FO}(\boldsymbol\Sigma^0_1(\mathbb R^n)) \equiv_m \mathrm{FO}(\mathbb N_2),
$$

where $\mathrm{FO}(\mathbb N_2)$ is the set of true sentences of second-order arithmetic and $\equiv_m$ is many-one equivalence (Theorem 3.2 of Kudinov and Selivanov).

Grzegorczyk had interpreted first-order arithmetic in the lattice of closed subsets of $\mathbb R^n$ for $n \ge 2$, showing undecidability; for $n = 1$ the theory is decidable, a consequence of Rabin's decidability of the monadic second-order theory of the binary tree. The lattices of closed and of open sets are anti-isomorphic, so the results transfer.

## Expansion

*Upper bound.* For an analytically representable locally compact space, open sets are coded by subsets of $\mathbb N$ through a numbering of a base, and inclusion of open sets becomes a relation definable in second-order arithmetic (Proposition 3.1).

*Lower bound.* Grzegorczyk's interpretation of $\mathbb N$ in $\boldsymbol\Sigma^0_1(\mathbb R^n)$ is extended to an interpretation of the two-sorted structure $\mathbb N_2$ of numbers and sets of numbers, which gives $\mathrm{FO}(\mathbb N_2) \le_m \mathrm{FO}(\boldsymbol\Sigma^0_1(\mathbb R^n))$.
