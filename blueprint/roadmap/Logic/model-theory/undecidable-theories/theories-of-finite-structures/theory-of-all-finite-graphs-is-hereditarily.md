# The theory of all finite graphs is hereditarily undecidable

The first-order theory of the class of all finite graphs is hereditarily undecidable: every theory into whose models the finite graphs are uniformly interpretable is undecidable.

**Theorem (Lavrov).** Let $\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{fin}}$ be the class of all finite graphs. Then $\mathrm{Th}(\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{fin}})$ is hereditarily undecidable in the interpretability sense of [hereditarily undecidable theories](interpretability-and-hereditary-undecidability.md).

According to the source, in all results known to its author, hereditary undecidability of a pseudovariety $\mathcal V$ of finite algebras is established by showing $\mathrm{Th}(\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{fin}}) \leq \mathrm{Th}(\mathcal{V})$.

## Expansion

Lavrov's paper shows, for certain elementary theories (by its title, as the source's bibliography gives it), that the sets of sentences true in all models and of sentences refuted in some finite model are effectively inseparable; that graphs are among these theories is the source's citation of Lavrov for $\mathcal G_{\mathrm{fin}}$ (Section 1). For graphs this yields the hereditary undecidability. The copy read cites the result without proof; a good expansion links the reduction from the halting problem or from Trakhtenbrot's theorem, neither of which is given in the copy read.
