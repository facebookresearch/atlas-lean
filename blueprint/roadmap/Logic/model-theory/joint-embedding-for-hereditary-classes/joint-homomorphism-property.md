# Joint homomorphism property

A class of finite structures has the joint homomorphism property if any two of its members map homomorphically into a common member.

**Definition.** Let $\mathcal C$ be a class of finite structures in a relational language, closed under isomorphism and under induced substructures (a *hereditary* class). The class $\mathcal C$ has the *joint homomorphism property* (JHP) if for all $A, B \in \mathcal C$ there is $C \in \mathcal C$ admitting homomorphisms from $A$ and from $B$.

## Depends on

None.

## Proof depends on

None.

## Expansion

Unlike the joint embedding property, the JHP is not preserved under changing to a quantifier-free interdefinable language: in a language with relations for both edges and non-edges, homomorphisms between graphs are embeddings. The JHP is of interest for infinite-domain constraint satisfaction problems (Bodirsky).
