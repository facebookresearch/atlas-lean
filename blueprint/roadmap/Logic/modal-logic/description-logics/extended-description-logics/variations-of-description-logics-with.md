# Variations of description logics with abstraction and refinement are undecidable

Satisfiability in description logics with abstraction and refinement is decidable, but it becomes undecidable under any of three seemingly harmless changes: repetition-free ensembles, abstraction levels forming a DAG, or quantified variables in the queries, already for the lightweight base logic EL.

**Theorem (Lutz and Schulze).** For the logics and variations of the page on [description logics with abstraction and refinement](description-logics-with-abstraction-and.md):

1. under the repetition-free semantics, satisfiability is undecidable in $\mathcal{ALC}^{abs}[ca]$, $\mathcal{ALC}^{abs}[ra]$, $\mathcal{EL}^{abs}[rr, ca]$ and $\mathcal{EL}^{abs}[rr, ra]$ (Theorem 6 of the source);
2. under the DAG semantics, satisfiability is undecidable in $\mathcal{ALC}^{abs}[ca, cr]$ and $\mathcal{EL}^{abs}[ca, cr, rr]$ (Theorem 7);
3. in the extension with quantified variables, satisfiability is undecidable in $\mathcal{ALCI}^{abs}[ca, cr]$ and $\mathcal{EL}^{abs}[ca, cr, rr]$ (Theorem 8).

The KR 2023 version of the paper states part 3 for $\mathcal{ALC}^{abs}[ca, cr]$, without inverse roles. The version read withdraws that claim: its footnote 2 says the conference construction contains a mistake and that it is not known whether the result holds for $\mathcal{ALC}^{abs}[ca, cr]$. Part 3 is therefore stated here for $\mathcal{ALCI}^{abs}[ca, cr]$ rather than $\mathcal{ALC}^{abs}[ca, cr]$.

Under the original semantics, satisfiability in $\mathcal{ALCHI}^{abs}$ is decidable in 2EXPTIME (Theorem 2 of the source). With only concept and role refinement, satisfiability under the repetition-free semantics coincides with satisfiability under the original semantics (Proposition 1), so abstraction statements are needed for part 1.

## Expansion

All three results reduce the complement of the halting problem for deterministic Turing machines on the empty tape, with a one-sided infinite tape. On one abstraction level an infinite path generated by a role $t$ with infinite $c$-paths branching off every node is built, and refinement and abstraction statements, whose ensembles may not overlap, close it into a grid on which the computation is simulated. Under the DAG semantics four coarser levels $L_1, \ldots, L_4$ are used so that ensembles for different levels may overlap. Inclusions $C \sqsubseteq_L \bot$ and $C \sqsubseteq_L \forall r.D$ are simulated in $\mathcal{EL}^{abs}$ with additional abstraction levels (Section 6.1), which transfers the results to EL. A good expansion links the undecidability of halting of deterministic Turing machines on the empty tape.
