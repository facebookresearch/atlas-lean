# Model checking probabilistic knowledge under synchronous perfect recall is undecidable

No algorithm decides, given a finite partially observed Markov chain, an atomic proposition $p$ and a rational threshold $c$, whether, from every state of positive initial probability, some continuation reaches a time at which agent $i$, remembering all its observations, assigns probability greater than $c$ to $p$.

**Setting (van der Meyden and Patra).** A finite *partially observed discrete-time Markov chain* (PO-DTMC) for agents $1, \dots, n$ is $M = (S, PI, PT, O_1, \dots, O_n, \pi)$: a finite [discrete-time Markov chain](../../temporal-logic/probabilistic-model-checking/discrete-time-markov-chains.md) with states $S$, initial distribution $PI$ and transition probabilities $PT$, observation functions $O_i : S \to \mathcal{O}$ and a valuation $\pi : S \to 2^{\mathit{Prop}}$. Its infinite paths, with the usual measure, are the runs of a synchronous probabilistic interpreted system; under the *synchronous perfect recall* semantics the local state of agent $i$ at time $m$ is its sequence of observations $O_i(s_0) \cdots O_i(s_m)$, so two points are indistinguishable to $i$ exactly when they have the same time and the same observation history ([knowledge in runs of a distributed system](knowledge-in-runs-of-a-distributed-system.md)). The probability $\Pr_i(\varphi)$ that agent $i$ assigns at a point is the conditional probability of $\varphi$ given the agent's indistinguishability class, and $M \models^{\mathrm{spr}} \varphi$ means that $\varphi$ holds at time $0$ of every run (Section 4 of the source). The path quantifier keeps the prefix up to the current time, so at time $0$ it keeps the initial state: $M \models^{\mathrm{spr}} \mathrm{EF}\,(\Pr_i(p) > c)$ holds when, from every state of positive initial probability, some continuation reaches a time at which the agent's posterior on $p$ exceeds $c$. One reachable point with that posterior does not suffice: with two absorbing initial states of probability $\tfrac12$ each, different observations, $p$ true only in the first and $c = \tfrac12$, the runs from the second state never reach posterior above $c$, so the formula fails.

**Theorem (van der Meyden and Patra).** It is undecidable, given a PO-DTMC $M$ with rational initial and transition probabilities (the reduction from probabilistic automata with rational transition probabilities produces such chains), an atomic proposition $p$ and a rational $c$, whether $M \models^{\mathrm{spr}} \mathrm{EF}\,(\Pr_i(p) > c)$ (Theorem 10 of arXiv:1511.03003v1).

This contrasts with decidable cases for temporal operators of finite reach under perfect recall. The same paper also shows two undecidability results for extensions of the probabilistic monadic logic of order of Beauquier, Rabinovich and Slissenko, which is decidable except for a set of parameters of arbitrarily small measure and is close to the clock semantics: in the weak monadic logic of order with probability and knowledge, model checking a PO-DTMC is undecidable for formulas with linear combinations of probability terms and second-order quantification into the scope of probability operators (Theorem 11), and there is a fixed PO-DTMC with $4$ states for which model checking mixed-time polynomial atomic probability formulas $\exists t_1 \cdots t_n\, \bigl(f(\Pr(p_1(t_{j_1})), \dots, \Pr(p_m(t_{j_m}))) = 0\bigr)$, with $f$ a rational polynomial, is undecidable (Theorem 12), by a reduction from Hilbert's tenth problem through the [Davis–Putnam–Robinson–Matiyasevich theorem](../../../TheoryOfComputation/undecidable-problems/diophantine-sets-and-hilbert-s-tenth-problem/dprm-theorem.md).

## Expansion

The proof reduces from the emptiness problem for probabilistic finite automata with a cutpoint (Paz): from an automaton with cutpoint $\lambda$ one builds a single-agent PO-DTMC whose observation sequences correspond to input words and in which the agent's posterior probability of an accepting-state proposition after a word equals the acceptance probability of that word, so some word is accepted with probability above $\lambda$ exactly when $\mathrm{EF}(\Pr_i(p) > \lambda)$ holds. For Theorem 11, second-order quantification into probability operators expresses perfect recall, so Theorem 10 transfers; for Theorem 12, a fixed four-state chain realizes $t \cdot 2^{-t}$ and $2^{-t}$ as probabilities of propositions at time $t$, so a polynomial combination of such probabilities at existentially quantified times vanishes exactly when a corresponding integer polynomial has a zero in the natural numbers.
