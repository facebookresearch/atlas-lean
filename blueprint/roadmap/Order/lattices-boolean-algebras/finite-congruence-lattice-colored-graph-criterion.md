# A colored-graph criterion for finite congruence lattices

Let $L$ be a finite lattice with bottom. It is order-isomorphic to all congruences of a nonempty finite algebra with finitely many finitary operations, ordered by inclusion, if and only if there is a nonempty finite set $X$ and coloring $c:X\times X\to L$ meeting the following conditions:

- $c(x,y)=c(y,x)$, and $c(x,y)=\bot$ exactly for $x=y$.
- $c(x,y)\le c(x,z)\vee c(z,y)$ for all $x,y,z$.
- For $a\not\le b$ some pair has color at most $a$ but not at most $b$.
- If $S$ is a nonempty finite set of ordered seed pairs and $c(x,y)\le\bigvee_{(v,w)\in S}c(v,w)$, then $x,y$ are in the equivalence relation generated by the pairs $(f(v),f(w))$ for $(v,w)\in S$, with $f:X\to X$ ranging over maps satisfying $c(f(v'),f(w'))\le c(v',w')$ for every pair $(v',w')$.

A congruence is an equivalence relation preserved by every operation coordinatewise. This criterion by itself asserts neither undecidability nor that all finite lattices are representable.

## Depends on

- Finite universal algebras, their compatible setoids, and finite join in the color lattice.

## Proof depends on

- Representation in both directions with exact separation and saturation by non-color-increasing self-maps.

## Expansion

From congruences color a pair by its least containing congruence. Prove the required seed saturation, then recover the congruence lattice from a finite witness, preserving inclusion and proving surjectivity onto all congruences.
