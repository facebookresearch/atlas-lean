# The word problem for the free modular lattice on five generators is unsolvable

No algorithm decides, for two lattice terms in five variables, whether they are equal in the free modular lattice on five generators, that is, whether the identity they form holds in every modular lattice.

The *free modular lattice* $\mathrm{FM}(n)$ on generators $x_1, \dots, x_n$ is the modular lattice generated by $x_1, \dots, x_n$ in which two lattice terms $s(x_1, \dots, x_n)$ and $t(x_1, \dots, x_n)$, built from the generators with $\vee$ and $\wedge$, are equal exactly when the identity $s = t$ holds in every [modular lattice](modular-lattices-and-subspace-lattices.md). The *word problem* for $\mathrm{FM}(n)$ asks, given two such terms, whether they are equal in $\mathrm{FM}(n)$.

**Theorem (Freese).** The word problem for $\mathrm{FM}(5)$ is unsolvable.

Conventions: lattices here have no constants $0, 1$. By the definition of $\mathrm{FM}(5)$, the theorem says that the set of lattice identities in five variables valid in all modular lattices is not decidable. Freese's proof associates a projective modular lattice with each finitely presented group, and his result holds for every variety of modular lattices that contains all infinite modular lattices of height $6$. Herrmann later proved that the word problem for $\mathrm{FM}(4)$ is unsolvable as well. For three generators the word problem is solvable, by Dedekind's description of $3$-generated modular lattices; the jump is from three to four generators.

## Expansion

The proof uses von Neumann frames, which freely generate projective modular lattices (Huhn), and the coordinate rings attached to them. On the model side, a construction of Dilworth and Hall glues an upper section of one modular lattice to a lower section of another, here height-$2$ intervals in subspace lattices of $4$-dimensional vector spaces over skew fields, so that results of Cohn and McIntyre on finitely presented skew fields with unsolvable word problem can be used to capture a finitely presented group with unsolvable word problem inside the units of a coordinate ring. On the syntactic side, terms in $\mathrm{FM}(5)$ produce a configuration of frames; reduction of frames forces relations such as a characteristic, and elements that are stable under reduction and under change of the reference frame serve as group generators on which the group relations are forced. Deciding equality in $\mathrm{FM}(5)$ would then decide the word problem of that group.

A good expansion adds and links pages for von Neumann frames and their coordinate rings, the Dilworth–Hall gluing construction, and the finitely presented skew fields with unsolvable word problem of Cohn and McIntyre.
