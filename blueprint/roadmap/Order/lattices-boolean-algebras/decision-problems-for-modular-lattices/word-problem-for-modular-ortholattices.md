# A finitely presented modular ortholattice with unsolvable word problem

There is a modular ortholattice with three generators and finitely many defining relations whose word problem is unsolvable; the source remarks that the construction works in every variety of modular ortholattices containing the orthocomplemented projective geometries of dimension 14.

An *ortholattice* is a bounded lattice $(L; \vee, \wedge, 0, 1)$ with an operation $x \mapsto x'$ satisfying $x \vee x' = 1$, $x \wedge x' = 0$, $(x \vee y)' = x' \wedge y'$, $(x \wedge y)' = x' \vee y'$ and $x'' = x$; it is a *modular ortholattice* if its lattice reduct is a [modular lattice](../modular-lattice.md). The [subspace lattice](submodule.md) of a finite-dimensional vector space with an anisotropic hermitian form, with $U' = U^\perp$, is an example. For a variety $\mathcal{V}$ of such algebras, a *finite presentation* $(G, \mathrm{Rel})$ is a finite set $G$ of generators and a finite set $\mathrm{Rel}$ of equations between terms in $G$; the algebra it *presents* in $\mathcal{V}$ is the free algebra on $G$ in $\mathcal{V}$ modulo $\mathrm{Rel}$, and its *word problem* asks, given an equation between terms in $G$, whether it holds in that algebra.

**Theorem (Roddy).** There is a finitely presented modular ortholattice with three generators whose word problem is unsolvable.

Conventions: two-generated modular ortholattices have solvable word problem, since the free modular ortholattice on two generators is finite, so three generators is the least possible number. The source remarks that the construction works for every variety of modular ortholattices containing the orthocomplemented projective geometries of dimension $14$, and leaves open whether the word problem of the free modular ortholattice (with no relations) is solvable.

## Expansion

The proof adapts Freese's method for the free modular lattice. A modular ortholattice generated by an orthogonal $n$-frame is coordinatized by a regular ring with involution together with a hermitian form, and such an ortholattice is generated by $3$ elements. Using constructions of Cohn and Bergman, the source builds a skew field with involution whose multiplicative group contains a copy of a six-generator, thirty-six-relator group with involution that has unsolvable word problem, encoded in the entries $\alpha_2, \dots, \alpha_{14}$ of a hermitian form in dimension $14$. The free modular ortholattice on an orthogonal $14$-frame modulo the corresponding finitely many relations, $\mathrm{Frm}(14, \Phi)$, then has unsolvable word problem: a solution would solve the word problem of that group. Since it is $3$-generated, this gives the theorem.

A good expansion adds and links pages for the coordinatization of modular ortholattices by rings with involution and for the group with involution that carries the unsolvability.
