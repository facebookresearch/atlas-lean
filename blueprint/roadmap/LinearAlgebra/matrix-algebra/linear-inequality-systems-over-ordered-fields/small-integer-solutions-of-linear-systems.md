# Small integer solutions of linear systems (Siegel-type bounds)

Let $A\in\mathbb{Z}^{p\times q}$ with $p<q$, let $r=\operatorname{rank}A$, and let $M$ be the largest absolute value of an $r\times r$ minor of $A$ ($M=1$ when $r=0$). Then $Ax=0$ has a nonzero integer solution with $|x_j|\le\max(1,M)$ for every $j$. If all entries of $A$ have absolute value at most $\alpha\ge1$, Hadamard's inequality gives $M\le r^{r/2}\alpha^r$, so $|x_j|\le\max(1,p^{p/2}\alpha^p)$.

Moreover, let $b\in\mathbb{Z}^p$ and let $\Delta$ be the largest absolute value of a subdeterminant (the determinant of a square submatrix) of $[A\ b]$. If $Ax=b$ has a solution $x\in\mathbb{Z}^q$ with $x\ge0$, it has one with $0\le x_j\le(q+1)\Delta$ for every $j$ (Schrijver, Theorem 17.1, applied to the polyhedron $\{x : Ax=b,\ x\ge0\}$). If all entries of $[A\ b]$ have absolute value at most $\alpha\ge1$, then $\Delta\le p^{p/2}\alpha^p$, so the bound has bit length polynomial in $p$, $q$ and $\log\alpha$.

## Proof sketch

If $r=0$ take a unit vector. Otherwise choose $r$ linearly independent rows and a nonsingular $r\times r$ submatrix $B$ of them; since $r\le p<q$ there is a column $j$ outside $B$, and Cramer's rule gives a kernel vector supported on the columns of $B$ and $j$ whose entries are $\pm$ $r\times r$ minors, with $\pm\det B\ne0$ at $j$. For nonnegative solutions, the integer hull of $P=\{x : Ax=b,\ x\ge0\}$ is the convex hull of finitely many integral points plus the cone generated by finitely many integral vectors, all with entries at most $(q+1)\Delta$ in absolute value: Cramer's rule bounds the vertices of $P$ and the generators of its recession cone by $\Delta$, and every integral point of $P$ is such a point plus an integral combination of the generators. Subdeterminants of the inequality system $Ax\le b$, $-Ax\le-b$, $-x\le0$ are, up to sign, subdeterminants of $[A\ b]$.
