# Lieb's concavity theorem, case `r = 0`

For a complex $m \times n$ matrix $K$ and $0 \le q \le 1$, the map $(A, B) \mapsto \operatorname{Re} \operatorname{tr}(K^* A^q K B^0)$ is jointly concave on pairs of positive definite matrices, $A$ of size $m$ and $B$ of size $n$. This is the instance $r = 0$ of Lieb's concavity theorem (E. A. Carlen, *Trace inequalities and quantum entropy: an introductory course*, lecture-notes draft of May 3, 2009, Theorem 6.1), in the form of the [joint statement](lieb-concavity-trace-rpow.md); since $B^0 = 1$, it reduces to the concavity of $A \mapsto \operatorname{Re} \operatorname{tr}(K^* A^q K)$.

This is a restricted case: with $r = 0$ the map depends on one matrix only, so its joint concavity is one-variable concavity. The joint concavity for $q, r > 0$ is the substance of Lieb's theorem; this case is its boundary $r = 0$.
