# Araki–Lieb–Thirring inequality

For positive semidefinite complex matrices $A$ and $B$ and reals $q \ge 0$, $r \ge 1$, $\operatorname{tr}\bigl((A^{1/2} B A^{1/2})^{rq}\bigr) \le \operatorname{tr}\bigl((A^{r/2} B^r A^{r/2})^q\bigr)$
(H. Araki, Lett. Math. Phys. 19 (1990) 167–170; K. M. R. Audenaert, arXiv:math/0701129,
Theorem 1, the case $r \ge 1$). Both traces are real because both matrices are positive semidefinite.

With $q = 1$ and $r = t$ this is the Lieb–Thirring trace inequality $\operatorname{tr}(A^{1/2} B A^{1/2})^t \le \operatorname{tr}(A^{t/2} B^t A^{t/2})$ for $t \ge 1$, first proved in Lieb and Thirring's 1976 paper on moments of Schrödinger eigenvalues (Carlen, Theorem 7.4 and the remark after it; Carlen writes it with the roles of $A$ and $B$ exchanged). Araki extended it to every $q \ge 0$. Together with the Lie–Trotter product formula the Lieb–Thirring case gives the Golden–Thompson inequality.

Matrix powers are defined by the functional calculus, $A^p = f(A)$ with $f(x) = x^p$ on the spectrum and the convention $0^0 = 1$; at $q = 0$ both sides equal the dimension.
