# Van der Waerden permanent bound

For every integer $n \ge 1$ and every doubly stochastic real $n \times n$ matrix $A$, with nonnegative entries and every row and column summing to $1$, the permanent of $A$ is at least $n!/n^n$.

## Idea

The permanent is the determinant without signs, $\operatorname{per} A = \sum_\sigma \prod_i A_{i\sigma(i)}$. Van der Waerden conjectured in 1926 that among doubly stochastic matrices it is smallest for the matrix with all entries $1/n$, whose permanent is $n!/n^n$. Egorychev and Falikman proved the conjecture independently in 1981, Egorychev using the Alexandrov–Fenchel inequality for mixed discriminants; Gurvits later gave a short proof through real stable polynomials. Applied to the biadjacency matrix of a $d$-regular bipartite graph with $n + n$ vertices, divided by $d$, the bound shows that the graph has at least $n!\,(d/n)^n$ perfect matchings. The proofs of 1981 also show that equality holds only for the matrix with all entries $1/n$.
