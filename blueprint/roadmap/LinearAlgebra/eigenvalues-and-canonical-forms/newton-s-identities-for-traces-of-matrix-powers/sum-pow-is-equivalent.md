# Asymptotic formula for sums of powers

Let $k \ge 1$ be an integer. As $n \to \infty$, the sum $1^k + 2^k + \cdots + n^k$ is asymptotically equivalent, that is, has ratio tending to $1$, to

$$\frac{k (n+1)^{k+1}}{2} \sum_{r=0}^{\lfloor k/2 \rfloor} \frac{(-1)^r}{(k - r)(2r + 1)} \binom{k - r}{r} \binom{2r}{r}^{-1}.$$

The difference of the two sides need not tend to $0$.

## Sources

Peiran Ding, Yanjun Zhu and Lei Wang, "A New Formula for the Sum of Integer Powers Based on Elementary Matrix Theory", INTEGERS 25 (2025), Article A98, Theorem 3.
