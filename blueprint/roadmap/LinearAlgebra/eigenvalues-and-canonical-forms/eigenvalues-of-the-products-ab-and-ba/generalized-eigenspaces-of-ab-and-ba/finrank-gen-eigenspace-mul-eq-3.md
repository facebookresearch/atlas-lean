# Generalized eigenspaces of AB and BA have equal dimension

Let $\mathbb{K}$ be a field, let $A$ be an $m \times n$ matrix and $B$ an $n \times m$ matrix over
$\mathbb{K}$, with rows and columns indexed by finite sets, let $\mu \in \mathbb{K}$ be nonzero, and
let $k \in \mathbb{N} \cup \{\infty\}$. Then the generalized $\mu$-eigenspaces of exponent $k$ of
$AB$ in $\mathbb{K}^m$ and of $BA$ in $\mathbb{K}^n$ have the same dimension. Here the generalized
$\mu$-eigenspace of exponent $k$ of a square matrix $C$ is $\ker (C - \mu I)^k$ for finite $k$, and
the union of these kernels over all finite $k$ for $k = \infty$.

The hypothesis $\mu \ne 0$ is needed: for $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ and
$B = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$, at exponent $k = 1$, $AB = B$ has a
one-dimensional kernel while $BA = 0$ has a two-dimensional one.
