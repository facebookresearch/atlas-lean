# Spectral theorem for normal matrices

Let $M$ be a complex square matrix indexed by a finite set, with conjugate transpose $M^*$. If $M$ is normal, that is, $M^* M = M M^*$, then there are a unitary matrix $P$ ($P^*P = PP^* = I$) and complex numbers $(d_i)$ such that $P^* M P$ is the diagonal matrix with entries $d_i$.

## Idea

A normal matrix has an orthonormal basis of eigenvectors. The proof starts from Schur's theorem, which makes $M$ upper triangular by a unitary change of basis; a triangular matrix that commutes with its conjugate transpose must be diagonal, as one sees by comparing the diagonal entries of $T^*T$ and $TT^*$ row by row. Hermitian, skew-Hermitian and unitary matrices are normal, with real, imaginary and unit-modulus eigenvalues respectively. The theorem is the finite-dimensional model of the spectral theorem for normal operators on Hilbert spaces and is the basis of functional calculus for matrices.
