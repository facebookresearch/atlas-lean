# Fixed point and shifted resolvent

Let $K$ be a real positive semidefinite $n \times n$ matrix, let $\lambda > 0$ and let
$Y \in \mathbb{R}^n$. A vector $z \in \mathbb{R}^n$ satisfies the fixed-point equation
$z = \lambda K (Y - z)$ if and only if it solves the linear system $(I + \lambda K) z = \lambda K Y$,
if and only if $z = (\lambda^{-1} I + K)^{-1} K Y$; moreover
$(\lambda^{-1} I + K)^{-1} K = K (K + \lambda^{-1} I)^{-1}$. The first equivalence holds for every
$K$ and $\lambda$, and the commutation whenever $\lambda^{-1} I + K$ is invertible; positive
semidefiniteness and $\lambda > 0$ make $\lambda^{-1} I + K$ invertible, which the equivalence with
the resolvent formula needs.
