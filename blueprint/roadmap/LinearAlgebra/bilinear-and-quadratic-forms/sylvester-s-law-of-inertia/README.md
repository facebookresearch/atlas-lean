# Sylvester's law of inertia

Let $A$ and $B$ be real symmetric square matrices indexed by a finite set, and suppose that $B = P^{\mathsf T} A P$ for some invertible real matrix $P$. Then $A$ and $B$ have the same number of positive eigenvalues, the same number of negative eigenvalues and the same number of zero eigenvalues, each counted with multiplicity.

## Idea

A real quadratic form can be brought to a sum of squares $\pm x_1^2 \pm \dots$ by many different changes of variables, and the coefficients vary with the choice. The law of inertia says that the number of plus signs and the number of minus signs do not: they are invariants of the form, its signature. The proof compares the dimensions of the largest subspaces on which the form is positive definite or negative definite, which no change of basis can alter. For example, $x^2 - y^2$ and $2xy$ are congruent and both have one positive and one negative eigenvalue. Over the complex numbers only the rank survives.
