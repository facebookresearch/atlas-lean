# Selected Eisenstein primes in the additive set E

Let $\omega = e^{2\pi i/3}$ and $E = \{a + b\omega : a, b \in \mathbb{N}\}$, an additive set of Eisenstein integers: it is closed under addition but not under multiplication, since $\omega \in E$ while $\omega^2 = -1 - \omega \notin E$. A pair $(a, b)$ of natural numbers, standing for $a + b\omega$, is a selected prime of $E$ when $b = 0$ and $a$ is a prime $\equiv 2 \pmod 3$, or $a = 0$ and $b$ is a prime $\equiv 2 \pmod 3$, or $a, b > 0$ and $a^2 - ab + b^2$ is prime.

Every selected prime is an Eisenstein prime, and these three types are the primes used in the cited proof, but they are not all the Eisenstein primes lying in $E$: $2 + 2\omega = -2\omega^2$ is an associate of the Eisenstein prime $2$, yet it has two nonzero coordinates and norm $4$, so it is not selected.
