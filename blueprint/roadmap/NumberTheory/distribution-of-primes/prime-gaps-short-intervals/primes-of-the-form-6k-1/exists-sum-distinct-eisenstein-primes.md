# Sums of distinct Eisenstein primes

Every element $a + b\omega$ of $E$ outside the exceptional set $B = A \cup A\omega \cup \{1 + \omega, 2 + 2\omega, 2 + 4\omega, 4 + 2\omega\}$ with $A = \{1, 3, 4, 6, 8, 9, 10, 12, 14, 15, 20, 21, 26, 27, 32, 37, 38, 44, 50, 67, 79\}$ is the sum of a finite set (possibly empty) of distinct selected primes of $E$; here $E = \{a + b\omega : a, b \in \mathbb{N}\}$ with $\omega = e^{2\pi i/3}$ is an additive set of Eisenstein integers, and a pair $(a, b)$ of natural numbers, standing for $a + b\omega$, is a selected prime of $E$ when $b = 0$ and $a$ is a prime $\equiv 2 \pmod 3$, or $a = 0$ and $b$ is a prime $\equiv 2 \pmod 3$, or $a, b > 0$ and $a^2 - ab + b^2$ is prime. The selected primes are the primes used in the cited proof, not all the Eisenstein primes lying in $E$.
