# Selberg's $\Lambda^2$ inequality, pointwise

Let $P \ne 0$ and let $\lambda : \mathbb N \to \mathbb R$ satisfy $\lambda_1 = 1$. For every $n \in \mathbb N$,

$$\mathbf 1_{\gcd(P, n) = 1} \le \Bigl(\sum_{d \mid P,\ d \mid n} \lambda_d\Bigr)^2 .$$

If $\gcd(P, n) = 1$ the only common divisor is $d = 1$ and the right side is $\lambda_1^2 = 1$; otherwise the right side is a square. This is the computation after display (2.1) of Heath-Brown's lectures, which shows that the $\Lambda^2$ weights $\mu^+(d) = \sum_{[d_1, d_2] = d} \lambda_{d_1} \lambda_{d_2}$ satisfy the upper-bound sieve condition (1.6). Heath-Brown takes $P = \Pi(\mathcal P, z)$ squarefree; the inequality holds for every $P \ne 0$.
