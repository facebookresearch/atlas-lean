# Window bounds for weighted Chebyshev sums of cyclic families of characters

This node is still to be broken down. It covers the bound that the
[zero-free half-plane for primitive Dirichlet L-functions](../../zeta-l-functions/dirichlet-l-functions/primitive-dirichlet-l-functions-zero-free-half-plane.md)
feeds into the
[criterion on windows](../../zeta-l-functions/dirichlet-l-functions/zeros-of-dirichlet-l-functions/zero-free-half-planes-from-chebyshev-bounds-on-windows.md), and its derivation
from [Green's description of the cuspidal characters of $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{F}_p)$](../../../GroupTheory/characters-of-finite-groups/green-cuspidal-characters-of-gl-n.md),
the [window bound for Tate–Shafarevich groups](../../algebraic-number-theory/galois-representations/tate-shafarevich-window-bound-over-cyclotomic-subfields.md)
and the [definite-side hypotheses](../../../RepresentationTheory/automorphic-representations/definite-side-hypotheses-for-character-families.md).

The bound reads as follows. Let $\psi$ be a primitive Dirichlet character of order $o$, and let
$K_\psi$ be the set of the $o$ primitive characters that induce the powers $\psi^k$,
$0 \le k < o$; it contains $\psi$ and the trivial character modulo $1$. With $G_\chi$ the weighted
Chebyshev functions of the criterion, let

$$G_\psi^{\mathrm{cyc}}(y) = G_{\mathbf 1}(y) + \frac{1}{3o + 1} \sum_{\chi \in K_\psi,\ \chi \ne \mathbf 1} \operatorname{Re} G_\chi(y),$$

where $\mathbf 1$ is the trivial character modulo $1$. Then for every $\theta$ with
$1/2 < \theta < 1$ and every $Y_0 \ge 1$ there is $Y \ge Y_0$ with $G_\psi^{\mathrm{cyc}}(y) \le y^\theta$
for all $y \in [Y, Y^{3/2}]$.

Its derivation passes through two intermediate statements about auxiliary number fields, the
hosts, attached to scales and subject to many arithmetic conditions, and about families of hosts
along windows of scales. The first, from Green's description and the definite-side hypotheses,
says that whenever hosts meeting a given requirement exist at all large scales of a set of windows,
and the members of all families of such hosts satisfy the Tate–Shafarevich bound, some of these
families satisfy, at the scales of their windows, a chain of conditions (the "ties"). The second
supplies, for every primitive $\psi$ and every small $\varepsilon > 0$, hosts meeting the requirement
attached to $\psi$ at all large scales of a set of windows of exponent $3/2$. That requirement
combines the Tate–Shafarevich bound for the host field with conditions relating the host to $K_\psi$
at the scale $x$. The frame of the host specifies a finite abelian group, the frame group, with a
distinguished element $a_0$, two sets $X_+$ and $X_-$ of characters of the host and a distinguished
character. The cyclic subgroup generated by $a_0$ has index $o$ in the frame group, where $o$ is the
order of $\psi$; each character of $K_\psi$ is the
primitive character of some character in $X_+$; the distinguished character is not kept, that is,
its primitive character is not in $K_\psi$; a share condition holds on the frame; and for every
character in $X_+$ whose primitive character is not in $K_\psi$, and every character in $X_-$, the
primitive character $\chi$ satisfies $|G_\chi(x)| \le x^{1/2 + \varepsilon}$ and
$|L'(1, \chi)/L(1, \chi)| \le 4 \log x$. The step from the ties to the bound above uses the ties
together with these conditions, for every member of the families at all large scales.

The window bound for Tate–Shafarevich groups is conditional: it concludes for prime pairs $(q, d)$
that satisfy its hypotheses. The second intermediate statement therefore also needs an independent
supply of good prime pairs: for every modulus $M \ge 1$, every small $\varepsilon > 0$, every $A \ge 1$
and every large $Y$ there are primes $q$ and $d$ satisfying those hypotheses, with $qd$ prime to $M$,
such that every primitive character $\chi$ that agrees, on the integers prime to $2Mqd$, with a character modulo
$M$ times the Legendre symbol modulo $q$, the one modulo $d$ or their product satisfies
$|G_\chi(y)| \le y^{1/2 + \varepsilon/2}$ and $|L'(1, \chi)/L(1, \chi)| \le 4 \log y$ for
$Y \le y \le Y^A$, and such that the same twists of the real primitive characters of conductor
dividing $8M$ have no zero with real part greater than $1 - 1/400$ up to an explicit height. It is
proved by showing that most primes $q$ in the relevant range are good and that, for each such $q$,
most of the candidate primes $d$ are good, with the bound on the logarithmic derivative at $1$ from
a separate estimate. A precise statement of the two intermediate results requires definitions of the hosts, families,
scales and ties, which are given by long lists of conditions specific to this proof.

A good expansion has definition pages for the scales, the hosts, the families of hosts along
windows and the ties, a page for each of the two intermediate statements (with Green's
description and the definite-side hypotheses as the inputs of the first, and the Tate–Shafarevich
window bound and the supply of good prime pairs as the inputs of the second), a page for the supply
of good prime pairs, and a page for the step from the ties to the bound above. The host supply also
uses a construction of Behrend's type, which a good expansion states and links: there is $C > 0$
such that for all $n \ge 1$ and $L \ge 2$ some set $T$ of at least $n e^{-C \sqrt{\log n \log L}} / L$
integers in $[0, n/4L)$ has no three distinct elements $s_1, s_2, s_3$ with
$j(s_2 - s_1) = i(s_3 - s_1)$ for distinct nonzero integers $i, j$ with $|i|, |j| \le L$. It is built
from [Behrend's lemma](../../../Combinatorics/additive-combinatorics/progressions-dense-sets/bounds-for-roth-numbers/behrend-large-sphere.md)
that some sphere centred at the origin contains many of the lattice points of a cube. On the route
through Green's description the same construction is only present in a bundle of conditions, whose
consumer on that route projects another of its components.

## Expansion

A good expansion also states and links the following, each as Alpöge's library states it at the
locators under Sources:

- the supply of good prime pairs $(q, d)$ described above, for the second intermediate statement, beside the window bound for Tate–Shafarevich groups
- the construction of Behrend's type described above, of large sets of integers without the relations $j(s_2 - s_1) = i(s_3 - s_1)$, built from Behrend's lemma, for the host supply of the second intermediate statement
