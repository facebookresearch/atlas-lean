# Finitely many n have n − 2x² prime for all small x

It is not the case that there are infinitely many natural numbers $n \ge 1$ such that $n - 2x^2$ is prime for every integer $x \ge 0$ with $2x^2 < n$.

This refutes [Erdős Problem 1140 (integers n with n − 2x² prime for all small x)](infinitely-many-twice-square-shifts-prim.md), the statement recorded as a proposition; the refutation is due to Epure and Gica (2010), with a result of Mollin and Williams, cited under Sources.

An even such $n$ is $2$. Theorem 4.1 of Epure and Gica shows that the only such $n \equiv 1 \pmod 4$ are $5, 13, 61, 181$. Their theorem does not cover $n \equiv 3 \pmod 4$: there their method together with the result of Mollin and Williams on real quadratic fields of class number one leaves only $7, 31, 199$ and at most one more, so at most nine $n$ qualify.
