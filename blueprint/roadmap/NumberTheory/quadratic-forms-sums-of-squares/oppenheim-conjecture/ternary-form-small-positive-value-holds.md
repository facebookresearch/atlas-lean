# Small values of x² + y² − α z² for positive irrational α

For every irrational $\alpha > 0$ and every $\varepsilon > 0$ there are positive integers $x, y, z$ with $|x^2 + y^2 - \alpha z^2| < \varepsilon$.

Problem 496 as printed asks this for every irrational $\alpha$. For negative $\alpha$ the form is positive definite and $x^2 + y^2 - \alpha z^2 > 2$ at positive integers (at $\alpha = -\sqrt2$ and $\varepsilon = 1$ no positive $x, y, z$ work), so this is the version for positive irrational $\alpha$. Margulis's theorem gives a nonzero integer vector with $|x^2 + y^2 - \alpha z^2| < \varepsilon$; positive coordinates follow from the remark of Dani after his Theorem 4.1: for at least three variables, a nondegenerate indefinite form that is not a multiple of a rational form, and whose null cone contains a vector with all coordinates positive, has a dense set of values at points with positive integer coordinates, and $(1, 1, \sqrt{2/\alpha})$ is such a null vector.

This resolves [Erdős Problem 496 for positive α (small values of x² + y² − α z²)](ternary-form-small-positive-value.md), the statement recorded as a proposition, in the affirmative; the resolution is due to Margulis (1987), cited under Sources.
