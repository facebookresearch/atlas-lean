# Pencils of quadrics in eight variables with a common zero, as a statement

The statement that for every prime $p$, every finite extension $k$ of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ and all symmetric $8 \times 8$ matrices $M_1, M_2$ over $k$ such that the intersection of the quadrics $x^{\mathsf T} M_1 x = 0$ and $x^{\mathsf T} M_2 x = 0$ in $\mathbb{P}^7$ is a smooth complete intersection of codimension two and has a nonzero common zero in $k^8$, there are $a, b \in k$ with $\det(aM_1 + bM_2) \ne 0$ such that the quadratic form $x^{\mathsf T}(aM_1 + bM_2)x$ vanishes on a $3$-dimensional subspace of $k^8$ (it contains three hyperbolic planes); here being a smooth complete intersection of codimension two is expressed by the rank test: at every nonzero common zero $x$ over an algebraic closure the vectors $M_1 x$ and $M_2 x$ are linearly independent.

Question 3(b) as printed does not assume a common zero over $k$. This is the conditional variant with a nonzero common zero in $k^8$, the situation of the known local theorems; it does not settle Question 3(b) as printed.

This page records the statement as a proposition. Its resolution: [Pencils of p-adic quadrics in eight variables with a common zero split three hyperbolic planes](pencil-with-common-zero-splits-three-hyp-2.md).
