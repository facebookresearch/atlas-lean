# A primitive form with positive leading coefficient is determined by its root

Let
$f = (a, b, c)$ and $g = (a', b', c')$ be integer triples with
$\gcd(a, b, c) = \gcd(a', b', c') = 1$,
$a > 0$ and $a' > 0$. If some $\tau$ in the upper half-plane is a root of both
$a\tau^2 + b\tau + c$ and $a'\tau^2 + b'\tau + c'$, then $f = g$.

## Sources

- Andrew V. Sutherland, *18.783 Elliptic Curves*, lecture notes, MIT OpenCourseWare, Fall 2025, Lecture 17, proof of Theorem 17.10 (by Gauss's lemma, the integer polynomials vanishing at $\tau$ are the multiples of the primitive $ax^2 + bx + c$).
