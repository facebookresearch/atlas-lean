# A translate of a form is properly equivalent to it

Let $(a, b, c)$ be a form whose
discriminant $D = b^2 - 4ac$ is not the square of an integer, so that $a \ne 0$. If $b'$ is an
integer with $b' \equiv b \pmod{2a}$, then $4a$ divides $b'^2 - D$, the form
$(a, b', c')$ with $c' = (b'^2 - D)/(4a)$ (a translate of $(a, b, c)$) has discriminant $D$, and
it is properly equivalent to $(a, b, c)$.

## Sources

- Anthony W. Knapp, *Advanced Algebra*, digital second edition, 2016, Chapter I, §4, p. 26, the paragraph before Lemma 1.10 ("the number $c' = (b'^2 - D)/(4a)$ is an integer", "the translate $(a, b', c')$ is properly equivalent to $(a, b, c)$"), and §3, p. 14 ($a \ne 0$).
