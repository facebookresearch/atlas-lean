# Proper equivalence preserves the discriminant, primitivity and positive definiteness

For
integer triples $(a, b, c)$, standing for the forms $ax^2 + bxy + cy^2$:

1. proper equivalence is an equivalence relation;
2. properly equivalent forms have the same discriminant $b^2 - 4ac$;
3. a form properly equivalent to a primitive form ($\gcd(a, b, c) = 1$) is primitive;
4. if $(a, b, c)$ and $(a', b', c')$ are properly equivalent and their discriminant is negative,
   then $a' > 0$ if and only if $a > 0$.

## Sources

- Anthony W. Knapp, *Advanced Algebra*, digital second edition, 2016, Chapter I, §3: p. 13, (i)–(iii) (the identity matrix acts as the identity, a product of matrices acts as the two substitutions in turn, and each member of $\mathrm{GL}(2, \mathbb{Z})$ preserves the discriminant; proper equivalence classes are the orbits of $\mathrm{SL}(2, \mathbb{Z})$), and p. 14 (for $D < 0$ "any form equivalent to a positive definite form is again positive definite"). Knapp assumes throughout §3 that forms are primitive (p. 12); item 3 records that proper equivalence keeps primitivity.
