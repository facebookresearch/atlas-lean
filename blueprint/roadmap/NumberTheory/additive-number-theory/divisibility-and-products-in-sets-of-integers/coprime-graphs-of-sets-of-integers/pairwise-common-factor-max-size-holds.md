# Largest sets with pairwise common factors containing N

For every $N \ge 2$ there is a prime factor $q$ of $N$ such that every set $A \subseteq \{1, \dots, N\}$ containing $N$ with $\gcd(a, b) > 1$ for all distinct $a, b \in A$ has at most as many elements as the set of integers in $\{1, \dots, N\}$ divisible by $q_1 \cdots q_j$ or by one of $2q_1, \dots, 2q_j$, where $q_1 < \dots < q_j$ are the prime factors of $N$ that are at most $q$.

This resolves [Erdős Problem 534 (largest sets with pairwise common factors containing N)](pairwise-common-factor-max-size.md), the statement recorded as a proposition, in the affirmative; the resolution is due to Ahlswede and Khachatrian (1996), cited under Sources.

- R. Ahlswede and L. H. Khachatrian, Sets of integers with pairwise common divisor and a factor from a specified set of primes, Acta Arith. (1996), 259–276.
