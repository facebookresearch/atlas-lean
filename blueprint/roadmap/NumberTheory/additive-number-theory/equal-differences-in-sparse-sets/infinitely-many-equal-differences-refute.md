# Square-root dense sets need not share infinitely many differences

It is not the case that for all sets $A$ and $B$ of natural numbers with square-root growth, there are infinitely many quadruples $(a_1, a_2, b_1, b_2)$ with $a_i \in A$, $b_i \in B$ and $a_1 - a_2 = b_1 - b_2 \ne 0$; here a set $A$ of natural numbers has square-root growth when there is $c > 0$ with $|A \cap \{1, \dots, N\}| \ge c\sqrt N$ for all sufficiently large $N$.
