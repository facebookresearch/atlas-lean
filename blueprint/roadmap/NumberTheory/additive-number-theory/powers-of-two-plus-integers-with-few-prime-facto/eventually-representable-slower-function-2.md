# Not every large integer is 2^k + m with Ω(m) = o(log log m)

It is not the case that for some $g : \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{R}$ with $g(m) = o(\log\log m)$, every sufficiently large natural number $n$ is $2^k + m$ for some $k \ge 0$ and positive integer $m$ with $\Omega(m) < g(m)$, where $\Omega(m)$ is the number of prime factors of $m$ counted with multiplicity.
