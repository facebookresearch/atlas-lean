# Sets of density zero need not have thin bases

It is not the case that every set $A$ of natural numbers with $|A \cap \{1, \dots, N\}|/N \to 0$ is contained in $B + B$ for some set $B$ of natural numbers with $|B \cap \{1, \dots, N\}| = o(N^{1/2})$.

This refutes [Erdős Problem 333 (thin bases for sets of density zero)](density-zero-has-thin-basis.md), the statement recorded as a proposition; the refutation is due to Erdős and Newman (1977), cited under Sources.

## Sources

- T. F. Bloom, Erdős Problems, Problem #333, https://www.erdosproblems.com/333.
- P. Erdős and D. J. Newman, Bases for sets of integers, J. Number Theory (1977), 420–425.
