# Sets with no square product of an odd number of elements are not almost full

It is not the case that $F_{2k+1}(N) \sim N$ as $N \to \infty$ for every $k \ge 2$, where $F_k(N)$ is the largest size of a set $A \subseteq \{1, \dots, N\}$ such that no product of $k$ distinct elements of $A$ is a perfect square.

This refutes [Erdős Problem 121, general question](odd-product-free-almost-full.md), the statement recorded as a proposition; the refutation is due to Tao (2024), cited under Sources.

## Sources

- T. F. Bloom, Erdős Problems, Problem #121, https://www.erdosproblems.com/121.
- T. Tao, On product representations of squares, arXiv:2405.11610 (2024), Theorem 1.2, https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.11610; published in Acta Mathematica Hungarica 175 (2025), 142–157, https://doi.org/10.1007/s10474-025-01505-7.
