# Some permutations have quadratically many distinct consecutive sums

It is not the case that for every real $\varepsilon > 0$ and all sufficiently large $n$, every permutation $\pi$ of $\{1, \dots, n\}$ has $S(\pi) \le \varepsilon n^2$, where for a permutation $\pi$ of $\{1, \dots, n\}$, $S(\pi)$ is the number of distinct sums $\pi(u) + \pi(u+1) + \dots + \pi(v)$ with $1 \le u \le v \le n$.

This refutes [Erdős Problem 34 (distinct consecutive sums of permutations)](consecutive-sum-count-little-o.md), the statement recorded as a proposition; the refutation is the published work cited under Sources.
