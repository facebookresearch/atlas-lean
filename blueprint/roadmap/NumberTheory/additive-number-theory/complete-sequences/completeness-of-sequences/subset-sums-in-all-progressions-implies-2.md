# Subset sums in every progression without completeness

It is not the case that for every strictly increasing sequence $1 \le a_0 < a_1 < \cdots$ of integers with $a_{i+1}/a_i \to 1$ such that every infinite arithmetic progression $r, r + d, r + 2d, \dots$ ($d \ge 1$) contains infinitely many sums of distinct terms, every sufficiently large integer is a sum of distinct terms.

This refutes [Erdős Problem 253 (subset sums in every progression)](subset-sums-in-all-progressions-implies.md), the statement recorded as a proposition; the refutation is due to Cassels (1960), cited under Sources.

## Sources

- T. F. Bloom, Erdős Problems, Problem #253, https://www.erdosproblems.com/253.
- J. W. S. Cassels, On the representation of integers as the sums of distinct summands taken from a fixed set, Acta Sci. Math. (Szeged) 21 (1960), 111–124.
