# Largest sets avoiding a given subset sum

For every real $\varepsilon > 0$ and all sufficiently large $n$, some $k \ge (1/2 - \varepsilon)\, n/\log n$ and no $k > (1/2 + \varepsilon)\, n / \log n$ has the following property: for every integer $m \ge 1$ there is a $k$-element set $S \subseteq \{1, \dots, n\}$, depending on $m$, no subset of which sums to $m$ (natural logarithm). That is, the largest such $k$ is $(1/2 + o(1))\, n/\log n$.

This resolves [Erdős Problem 771 (large sets avoiding a given subset sum)](subset-sum-avoiding-asymptotic.md), the statement recorded as a proposition, in the affirmative; the resolution is due to Alon and Freiman (1988), cited under Sources.
