# Erdős Problem 771 (large sets avoiding a given subset sum)

The statement that for every real $\varepsilon > 0$ and all sufficiently large $n$, some $k \ge (1/2 - \varepsilon)\, n/\log n$ and no $k > (1/2 + \varepsilon)\, n / \log n$ has the following property: for every integer $m \ge 1$ there is a $k$-element set $S \subseteq \{1, \dots, n\}$, depending on $m$, no subset of which sums to $m$ (natural logarithm). That is, the largest such $k$ is $(1/2 + o(1))\, n/\log n$.

This page records the statement as a proposition. Its resolution: [Largest sets avoiding a given subset sum](subset-sum-avoiding-asymptotic-holds.md).
