# Largest sets whose subset sums of different sizes are disjoint

For every real $\varepsilon > 0$ and all sufficiently large $N$, some subset of $\{1, \dots, N\}$ with at least $(2 - \varepsilon)\sqrt N$ elements has disjoint subset sum levels, and no subset of $\{1, \dots, N\}$ with more than $(2 + \varepsilon)\sqrt N$ elements has them; here a finite set $A$ of natural numbers has disjoint subset sum levels when any two nonempty subsets of $A$ of different sizes have different sums. That is, the largest size $k(N)$ of such a set satisfies $k(N) \sim 2\sqrt N$.

This resolves [Erdős Problem 874 (Erdős–Straus problem on disjoint subset sum levels)](disjoint-subset-sum-levels-asymptotic.md), the statement recorded as a proposition, in the affirmative; the resolution is due to Deshouillers and Freiman (1999), cited under Sources.
