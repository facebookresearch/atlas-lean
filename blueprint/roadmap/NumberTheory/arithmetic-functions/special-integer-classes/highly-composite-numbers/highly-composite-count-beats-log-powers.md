# Erdős Problem 381 (counting highly composite numbers)

The statement that for every $k \ge 1$ there is a real $c > 0$ with $Q(x) \ge c (\log x)^k$ for all sufficiently large real $x$, where for a real $x$, $Q(x)$ is the number of highly composite numbers $n \le x$, a positive integer $n$ being highly composite when every positive integer $m < n$ has fewer divisors than $n$.

This page records the statement as a proposition. Its refutation: [The count of highly composite numbers does not dominate every power of log](highly-composite-count-beats-log-powers-2.md).
