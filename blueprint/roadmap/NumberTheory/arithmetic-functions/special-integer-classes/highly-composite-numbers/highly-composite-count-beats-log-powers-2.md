# The count of highly composite numbers does not dominate every power of log

It is not the case that for every $k \ge 1$ there is a real $c > 0$ with $Q(x) \ge c (\log x)^k$ for all sufficiently large real $x$, where for a real $x$, $Q(x)$ is the number of highly composite numbers $n \le x$, a positive integer $n$ being highly composite when every positive integer $m < n$ has fewer divisors than $n$.

This refutes [Erdős Problem 381 (counting highly composite numbers)](highly-composite-count-beats-log-powers.md), the statement recorded as a proposition; the refutation is due to Nicolas (1971), cited under Sources, whose polylogarithmic upper bound on $Q(x)$ is a stronger result that implies this negation; the statement here asserts only that for some $k$ no positive multiple of $(\log x)^k$ is an eventual lower bound.

- J.-L. Nicolas, Répartition des nombres hautement composés de Ramanujan, Canadian J. Math. (1971), 116–130.
