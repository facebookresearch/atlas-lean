# Möbius inversion formula for divisor sums

For complex-valued functions $f$ and $F$ on the positive integers, $F(n) = \sum_{d \mid n} f(d)$ for every positive integer $n$ if and only if $f(n) = \sum_{d \mid n} \mu(d) F(n/d)$ for every positive integer $n$, where $\mu$ is the Möbius function and the sums run over the positive divisors $d$ of $n$. Mathlib proves this more generally for functions $f, g \colon \mathbb{N} \to R$ with values in a non-associative ring $R$, that is, a ring with identity whose multiplication need not be associative or commutative: $\sum_{d \mid n} f(d) = g(n)$ for every integer $n > 0$ if and only if $\sum_{uv = n} \mu(u)\, g(v) = f(n)$ for every integer $n > 0$, where the first sum runs over the positive divisors $d$ of $n$, the second sum runs over the ordered pairs $(u, v)$ of positive integers with $uv = n$, and the integer $\mu(u)$, mapped into $R$, multiplies $g(v)$ on the left. Both conditions concern only $n > 0$, so the values $f(0)$ and $g(0)$ play no role.
