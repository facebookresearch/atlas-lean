# Non-pretentious arithmetic functions

An arithmetic function $f : \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{C}$ is non-pretentious when for every modulus $q \ge 1$, every Dirichlet character $\chi$ modulo $q$ and every real $t$, the real numbers $\bigl(1 - \operatorname{Re}(f(p)\overline{\chi(p)} p^{it})\bigr)/p$, indexed by the primes $p$, are not absolutely summable. No bound on $f$ is assumed, so this is weaker than divergence of the series over the primes in increasing order: if $f$ takes the values $0$ and $2$ alternately on successive primes, then at the trivial character and $t = 0$ the ordinary series is the convergent alternating series of the reciprocals of the primes, yet for every $\chi$ and $t$ the absolute sum already diverges on the primes where $f = 0$. When $|f| \le 1$ the terms are nonnegative, and the condition says that the series diverges.
