# Normal order of chains of divisors congruent to one

There are reals $c > 0$ and $C$ such that the natural numbers $n$ with $c \log_* n \le h(n)$ and $H(n) \le C \log_* n$ have natural density $1$ (the proportion of them among $0, 1, \dots, b-1$ tends to $1$ as $b \to \infty$); here $h(n)$ is the largest $\ell$ for which there are primes $p_1 < \dots < p_\ell$ dividing $n$ with $p_{i+1} \equiv 1 \pmod{p_i}$ for every $i < \ell$, $H(n)$ is the largest $u$ for which there are positive integers $d_1 < \dots < d_u$ dividing $n$ with $d_{i+1} \equiv 1 \pmod{d_i}$ for every $i < u$ (for $n \ge 1$ these lengths are bounded; for $n = 0$ every natural number divides $n$, so chains of every length exist and $H(0)$ is $0$ by the convention for the supremum of an unbounded set of natural numbers), and $\log_* x$ is the least number $k$ of applications of the natural logarithm to $x$ giving a value at most $1$.

This resolves [Erdős Problem 696 (chains of divisors congruent to one)](divisor-chain-length-normal-order-log-st.md), the statement recorded as a proposition, in the affirmative; the resolution is the one recorded in Erdős Problems, Problem #696.
