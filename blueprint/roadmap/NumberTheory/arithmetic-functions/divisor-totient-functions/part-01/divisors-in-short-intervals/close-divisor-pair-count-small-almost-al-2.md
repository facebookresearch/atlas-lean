# Pairs of close divisors are not rare among divisors

It is not the case that for every real $\varepsilon > 0$, the natural numbers $n$ with $r(n) < \varepsilon\, \tau(n)$ have natural density $1$: the proportion of them among $0, 1, \dots, b-1$ tends to $1$ as $b \to \infty$; here $r(n)$ is the number of pairs $(d_1, d_2)$ of divisors of $n$ with $d_1 < d_2 < 2 d_1$ and $\tau(n)$ is the number of divisors of $n$.

This refutes [Erdős Problem 449 (pairs of close divisors)](close-divisor-pair-count-small-almost-al.md), the statement recorded as a proposition; the refutation is due to Hall and Tenenbaum (1988), cited under Sources.

- R. R. Hall and G. Tenenbaum, Divisors, Cambridge University Press (1988), Section 4.6.
