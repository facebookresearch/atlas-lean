# Ford's theorem on integers with a divisor in a dyadic interval

There is $\delta : \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{R}$ such that for every $n$ the integers with a divisor in $(n, 2n)$ have natural density $\delta(n)$, and $\delta(n) \asymp (\log n)^{-\alpha} (\log\log n)^{-3/2}$ as $n \to \infty$ (bounded above and below by positive constant multiples for large $n$), where $\alpha = 1 - (1 + \log\log 2)/\log 2 = 0.086\ldots$.

This resolves [Erdős Problem 446, growth of the density](dyadic-divisor-density-growth.md), the statement recorded as a proposition, in the affirmative; the resolution is due to Ford (2008), cited under Sources.
