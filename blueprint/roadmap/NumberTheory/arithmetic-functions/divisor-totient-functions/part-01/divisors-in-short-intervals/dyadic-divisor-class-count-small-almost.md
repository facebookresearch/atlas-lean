# Erdős Problem 448 (divisors in dyadic intervals)

The statement that for every real $\varepsilon > 0$, the natural numbers $n$ with $\tau^+(n) < \varepsilon\, \tau(n)$ have natural density $1$ (the proportion of them among $0, 1, \dots, b-1$ tends to $1$ as $b \to \infty$); here $\tau^+(n)$ is the number of $k$ such that $n$ has a divisor in $[2^k, 2^{k+1})$ and $\tau(n)$ is the number of divisors of $n$.

This page records the statement as a proposition. Its refutation: [Divisors are not almost always concentrated in few dyadic intervals](dyadic-divisor-class-count-small-almost-2.md).
