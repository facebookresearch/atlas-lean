# Divisors are not almost always concentrated in few dyadic intervals

It is not the case that for every real $\varepsilon > 0$, the natural numbers $n$ with $\tau^+(n) < \varepsilon\, \tau(n)$ have natural density $1$ (the proportion of them among $0, 1, \dots, b-1$ tends to $1$ as $b \to \infty$); here $\tau^+(n)$ is the number of $k$ such that $n$ has a divisor in $[2^k, 2^{k+1})$ and $\tau(n)$ is the number of divisors of $n$.

This refutes [Erdős Problem 448 (divisors in dyadic intervals)](dyadic-divisor-class-count-small-almost.md), the statement recorded as a proposition; the refutation is due to Erdős and Tenenbaum (1981), cited under Sources.
