# Sums of ratios of consecutive divisors tend to infinity for almost all integers

For every real $K$, the natural numbers $n$ with $G(n) > K$ have natural density $1$ (the proportion of them among $0, 1, \dots, b-1$ tends to $1$ as $b \to \infty$), where for a natural number $n$ with divisors $1 = d_1 < \dots < d_{\tau(n)} = n$, $G(n) = \sum_{i < \tau(n)} d_i/d_{i+1}$.

This resolves [Erdős Problem 673 (sums of ratios of consecutive divisors)](divisor-ratio-sum-tends-to-infinity-almo.md), the statement recorded as a proposition, in the affirmative; Erdős (1982, §6) already states that $G(n) \to \infty$ for almost all $n$ is trivial; T. Tao's observation that $\tau(n/m)/m \le G(n) \le \tau(n)$ for every divisor $m > 1$ of $n$, recorded in Erdős Problems, Problem #673, gives an argument for it.
