# The derivative of the gamma function at 1 is minus Euler's constant

The complex gamma function is complex differentiable at $s = 1$, with derivative $\Gamma'(1) = -\gamma$, where
$\gamma = \lim_{n \to \infty} \bigl(1 + \tfrac{1}{2} + \dots + \tfrac{1}{n} - \log n\bigr)$ is Euler's constant.

## Sources

- F. W. J. Olver et al. (eds.), *NIST Digital Library of Mathematical Functions*, Version 1.2.8 of 2026-09-15,
  <https://dlmf.nist.gov/5.4>: equations 5.4.1 ($\Gamma(1) = 1$) and 5.4.12 ($\psi(1) = -\gamma$), where
  $\psi = \Gamma' / \Gamma$, so $\Gamma'(1) = \Gamma(1) \psi(1) = -\gamma$.
