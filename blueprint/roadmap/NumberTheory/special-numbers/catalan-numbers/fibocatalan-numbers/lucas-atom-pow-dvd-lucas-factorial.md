# Exact power of a Lucas atom dividing a Lucas factorial

Let $\{n\}$ denote the Lucas polynomials in $s$ and $t$ with integer coefficients, $\{0\} = 0$, $\{1\} = 1$ and $\{n\} = s\{n-1\} + t\{n-2\}$, and let $\{n\}! = \{1\}\{2\} \cdots \{n\}$ be the Lucas factorial. Let $P : \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{Z}[s, t]$ be any function with $\{n\} = \prod_{d \mid n} P(d)$ for every $n \ge 1$, the product over the positive divisors of $n$. Then:

1. for all natural numbers $d \ge 2$ and $n$, the power $P(d)^{\lfloor n/d \rfloor}$ divides $\{n\}!$ and $P(d)^{\lfloor n/d \rfloor + 1}$ does not;
2. for all integers $m$, $n$ and $d$, $\lfloor m/d \rfloor + \lfloor n/d \rfloor \le \lfloor (m+n)/d \rfloor$, the quotients taken in the rationals, where division by $0$ gives $0$.

Kendra Killpatrick, "Super FiboCatalan Numbers and Their Lucas Analogues", Journal of Integer Sequences 28 (2025), Article 25.5.8, Lemma 2 (quoted from Sagan and Tirrell, Lemma 3.1).
