# Unbounded prime-power exponent in every interior coefficient

The statement that for every natural number $s$ there are infinitely many $n \ge 2$ such that every binomial coefficient $\binom{n}{k}$ with $1 \le k < n$ is divisible by $p^s$ for some prime $p$ (depending on $k$). Equivalently, $\limsup_{n} S(n) = \infty$, where $S(n)$ is the largest $s$ with this property for $n$.

The prime may differ from one coefficient to the next; no prime power need divide all of them. In row $6$, for instance, every interior coefficient has a prime factor, yet $\gcd(6, 15, 20) = 1$.

This page records the statement as a proposition.
