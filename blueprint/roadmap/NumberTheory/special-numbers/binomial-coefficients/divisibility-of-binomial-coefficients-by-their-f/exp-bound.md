# Exponential bound for n_k, as a statement

The statement that there is $f : \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{R}$ with $f(k) \to 0$ and $n_k \le e^{(1 + f(k))k}$ for every $k$, where for a natural number $k$, $n_k$ is the least $n \ge 2k$ such that all but one of the numbers $n - i$, $0 \le i < k$, divide $\binom{n}{k}$: exactly one $i_0 < k$ has $n - i_0 \nmid \binom{n}{k}$ ($0$ if there is no such $n$).
