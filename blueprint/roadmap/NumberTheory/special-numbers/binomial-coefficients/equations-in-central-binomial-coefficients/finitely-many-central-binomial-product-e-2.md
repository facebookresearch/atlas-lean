# Infinitely many equal products of central binomial coefficients

It is not the case that only finitely many pairs $(M, N)$ of disjoint nonempty finite sets of positive integers satisfy $\prod_{m \in M} \binom{2m}{m} = \prod_{n \in N} \binom{2n}{n}$. Equivalently, infinitely many such pairs exist.

This refutes [Erdős Problem 397 (equal products of central binomial coefficients)](finitely-many-central-binomial-product-e.md), the statement recorded as a proposition; the refutation, recorded in Erdős Problems, Problem #397, is a construction generated by ChatGPT and posted for verification by N. Somani; for every $a \ge 2$, with $c = 8a^2 + 8a + 1$, it gives $\binom{2a}{a}\binom{4a+4}{2a+2}\binom{2c}{c} = \binom{2a+2}{a+1}\binom{4a}{2a}\binom{2c+2}{c+1}$.
