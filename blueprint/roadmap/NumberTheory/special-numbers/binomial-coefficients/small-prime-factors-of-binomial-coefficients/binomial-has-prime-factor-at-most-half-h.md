# Binomial coefficients have a prime factor at most n/2 (non-strict bound)

For all natural numbers $n$ and $k$ with $1 < k < n - 1$ and $(n, k) \ne (7, 3), (7, 4)$, the binomial coefficient $\binom{n}{k}$ has a prime factor $p$ with $2p \le n$.

Problem 384 as printed asks for a prime factor $p < n/2$. With the strict bound the statement fails, for instance $\binom{4}{2} = 6 = 2 \cdot 3$, so this is the corrected statement with the non-strict bound $p \le n/2$, as in Ecklund's theorem.

This resolves [Erdős Problem 384 with the non-strict bound p ≤ n/2 (small prime factors of binomial coefficients)](binomial-has-prime-factor-at-most-half.md), the statement recorded as a proposition, in the affirmative; the resolution is due to Ecklund (1969), cited under Sources.
