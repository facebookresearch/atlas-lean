# Becher's question on random Poisson generic sequences

The statement that every Martin-Löf random binary sequence is Poisson generic, where a binary sequence $x$ is Poisson generic when for every real $\lambda > 0$ and every $k \ge 0$, the proportion of the $2^n$ binary words $w$ of length $n$ occurring exactly $k$ times in $x$ at positions $i < \lfloor \lambda 2^n \rfloor$ tends to $e^{-\lambda}\lambda^k/k!$ as $n \to \infty$, and a binary sequence $x$ is Martin-Löf random when for every Martin-Löf test $U$, a uniformly computably enumerable sequence $U_0, U_1, \dots$ of sets of finite binary strings such that the sequences with a prefix in $U_m$ have fair-coin measure at most $2^{-m}$, some $U_m$ contains no prefix of $x$.

This page records the statement as a proposition. Its resolution: [Martin-Löf random sequences are Poisson generic](martin-lof-random-is-poisson-generic-hol.md).
