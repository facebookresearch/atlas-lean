# Rising-product series can be rational

It is not the case that for every $f : \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{N}$ with $f(n) \to \infty$, the sum $\sum_{n \ge 1} \frac{1}{(n+1)(n+2)\cdots(n+f(n))}$ is irrational.

This refutes [Erdős Problem 270 (irrationality of rising-product series)](rising-product-series-irrational.md), the statement recorded as a proposition; the refutation is due to Crmarić and Kovač (2025), cited under Sources.

## Sources

- T. F. Bloom, Erdős Problems, Problem #270, https://www.erdosproblems.com/270.
- T. Crmarić and V. Kovač, On the irrationality of certain super-polynomially decaying series, arXiv:2504.18712 (2025), https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.18712; published in Colloquium Mathematicum (2025), https://doi.org/10.4064/cm9628-5-2025.
