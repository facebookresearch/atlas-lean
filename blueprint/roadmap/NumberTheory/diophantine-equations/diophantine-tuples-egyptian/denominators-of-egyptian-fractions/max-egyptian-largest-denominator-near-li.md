# Erdős Problem 305 (largest denominators of Egyptian fractions)

The statement that for every real $\varepsilon > 0$ there is a real $C$ with $D(b) \le C\, b\, (\log b)^{1 + \varepsilon}$ for every $b \ge 2$, where $D(b) = \max_{1 \le a < b} D(a, b)$ ($0$ for $b \le 1$) and for natural numbers $a$ and $b$, $D(a, b)$ is the least $N$ such that $a/b = 1/n_1 + \dots + 1/n_k$ for distinct positive integers $n_1 < \dots < n_k \le N$ (the least possible largest denominator; $0$ if there is no such $N$).

This page records the statement as a proposition. Its resolution: [Egyptian fractions with denominators at most b (log b)^(1 + o(1))](max-egyptian-largest-denominator-near-li-2.md).
