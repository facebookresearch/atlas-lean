# Rationals with a two-term odd greedy expansion

A rational $q$ has a two-term odd greedy expansion if and only if $q = n/\bigl(n\,(\prod_{p \mid r} p^{a_p})(1 + 2t) - r\bigr)$ for some even $n$, integer $0 < r < 2n$ with $\nu_2(2r) \le \nu_2(n)$, and natural number $t$, where $a_p = \max\bigl(\lceil (\nu_p(2r) - \nu_p(n))/2 \rceil, 0\bigr)$ and $\nu_p$ is the $p$-adic valuation; here the odd greedy choice at a positive rational $r$ is the least positive odd integer $d$ with $1/d \le r$, and $q$ has a two-term odd greedy expansion when, with $x_1$ the odd greedy choice at $q$ and $x_2$ the odd greedy choice at $q - 1/x_1$, $q = 1/x_1 + 1/x_2$.
