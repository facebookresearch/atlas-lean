# No solutions of $(5^n)^x + (4^m p + 1)^y = z^2$ for $m \ge 1$

For all natural numbers $p$, $m$, $n$, $x$, $y$ and $z$ with $m \ge 1$,

$$(5^n)^x + (4^m p + 1)^y \neq z^2.$$

No primality of $p$ and no condition on $n$ is assumed: both bases are congruent to $1$ modulo $4$. The hypothesis $m \ge 1$ is needed, since $5^1 + (4^0 \cdot 3 + 1)^1 = 3^2$.

## Sources

Suton Tadee, "On the Diophantine Equation $n^x + (5p)^y = z^2$", INTEGERS 24 (2024), Article A68, Corollary 2 (credited there to Moonchaisook et al.).
