# Arithmetic polygons

Let $a$ be a nonnegative integer and $N$ a natural number. An arithmetic polygon with side lengths $a + 1, \dots, a + N$, all integers, is an $N$-periodic sequence of points
$v_0, v_1, \dots$ in the plane $\mathbb{C}$, side $k$ joining $v_k$ to $v_{k+1}$ and $O = v_0$ being the
special vertex, such that

1. $N \ge 3$ and $v_0, \dots, v_{N-1}$ are pairwise distinct;
2. for each $k < N$, side $k$ has length $a + 1 + k$;
3. for each side, some line perpendicular to it passes through $O$ and one endpoint of the side:
   $O - v_k$ or $O - v_{k+1}$ is orthogonal to $v_{k+1} - v_k$; and
4. no vertex has angle $0$ or $\pi$: consecutive sides $v_{k+1} - v_k$ and $v_{k+2} - v_{k+1}$ are
   never parallel.

Self-intersecting polygons are allowed.
