# A small value of $M$ in the family of a good fraction when $a^2 + ab - b^2 < 0$

For integers $a$ and $b$, a rational number $x$ is good for $(a, b)$ when $1 - x - x^2 \ne 0$ and $G^{(a,b)}(x) = \frac{(b - a)x + a}{1 - x - x^2}$ is an integer. For a pair of integers $(p, q)$, its family consists of the consecutive pairs $(H_n, H_{n+1})$, $n \in \mathbb{Z}$, of the two-sided integer sequence with $H_0 = p$, $H_1 = q$ and $H_{n+2} = H_{n+1} + H_n$ for every integer $n$. For integers $a, b, p, q$ put

$$M(a, b, p, q) = \frac{(2p + q)\big((b - a)p + aq\big)}{q^2 - pq - p^2} - a + b,$$

a rational number; the denominator vanishes only for $(p, q) = (0, 0)$, where the quotient is read as $0$.

Let $a$ and $b$ be integers with $a^2 + ab - b^2 < 0$, and let $p$ and $q$ be coprime integers with $q \ne 0$ such that $p/q$ is good for $(a, b)$. Then some pair $(r, s)$ in the family of $(p, q)$ satisfies $M(a, b, r, s)^2 \le |a^2 + ab - b^2|$. The pair may have $s = 0$; without such pairs the bound fails, for instance for $(a, b) = (1, 11)$ and $p/q = 0$.
