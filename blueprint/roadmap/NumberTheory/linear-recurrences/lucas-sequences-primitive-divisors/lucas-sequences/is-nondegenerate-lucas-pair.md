# Nondegenerate Lucas pair

A pair of integers $(P, Q)$ is nondegenerate when $Q \ne 0$ and,
for all complex numbers $\alpha$ and $\beta$ with $\alpha + \beta = P$ and $\alpha \beta = Q$, the
quotient $\alpha / \beta$ is not a root of unity. Such $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are the two roots of
$x^2 - P x + Q$, in either order.

## Sources

- Chris Smyth, *The Terms in Lucas Sequences Divisible by Their Indices*, Journal of Integer Sequences 13 (2010), Article 10.2.4, <https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/journals/JIS/VOL13/Smyth/smyth2.tex>: Section 7 (proof of Theorem 22) calls a Lucas sequence degenerate if $Q = 0$ or $\alpha / \beta$ is a root of unity, where $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are the two roots of $x^2 - Px + Q$ (Section 1), so that $\alpha + \beta = P$ and $\alpha \beta = Q$.
