# Guzmán Sánchez–Luca lemma: $L < 2^r H (\log H)^r$

Let $r$, $H$ and $L$ be real numbers with $r \ge 1$, $H > (4r^2)^r$ and $H > L/(\log L)^r$, where
$\log$ is the natural logarithm. Then $L < 2^r H (\log H)^r$.

No condition on $L$ is imposed. Since $H > (4r^2)^r \ge 4$, the right-hand side exceeds $8$, so the
conclusion holds for every $L \le 1$ whatever value is given to $L/(\log L)^r$ there; the
hypothesis $H > L/(\log L)^r$ matters only for $L > 1$. The source also assumes $H > 0$, which
follows from $H > (4r^2)^r$.

## Sources

- Prasanta Kumar Ray and Kisan Bhoi, *Narayana Numbers Which Are Concatenations of Two Base b Repdigits*, INTEGERS 24 (2024), Article A20, Lemma 3.
