# Van der Laan numbers that are not sums of two prime powers

Let $(V_n)_{n \ge 0}$ be the Van der Laan sequence, given by $V_0 = 1$, $V_1 = 0$, $V_2 = 1$ and $V_{n+3} = V_{n+1} + V_n$. The following holds: there are infinitely many $k \in \mathbb{N}$ such that $V_k$ is not a sum of two prime powers, and moreover there is $K \in \mathbb{N}$ such that for every $k \ge K$ with

$$k \equiv 45752 \pmod{967206240},$$

$V_k$ is not a sum of two prime powers.

An integer $n$ is a sum of two prime powers if $n = p^a + q^b$ for some primes $p, q$ and some $a, b \in \mathbb{N}$; since exponents may be $0$, the number $1 = p^0$ counts as a prime power.

## Sources

- Baczkowski and Keener, *Third-Order Recurrence Sequences with Infinitely Many Terms not Equal to the Sum of Two Prime Powers*, Journal of Integer Sequences 29 (2026), Article 26.2.3, Theorem 4.
