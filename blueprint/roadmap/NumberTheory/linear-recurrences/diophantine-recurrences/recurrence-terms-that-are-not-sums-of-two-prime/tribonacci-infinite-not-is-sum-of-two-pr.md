# Infinitely many Tribonacci numbers are not sums of two prime powers

Let $(T_n)_{n \ge 0}$
be the Tribonacci sequence, given by $T_0 = 0$, $T_1 = 0$, $T_2 = 1$ and
$T_{n+3} = T_{n+2} + T_{n+1} + T_n$. There are infinitely many natural numbers $k$ such that
$T_k$ is not a sum of two prime powers.

The paper uses the initial values $0, 1, 1$, so its $k$-th term is $T_{k+1}$, and proves the
stronger statement that its $k$-th term is not a sum of two prime powers for every sufficiently
large $k \equiv 3817 \pmod{163843680}$.

## Sources

- Daniel Baczkowski and Bruce Keener, *Third-Order Recurrence Sequences with Infinitely Many Terms not Equal to the Sum of Two Prime Powers*, Journal of Integer Sequences 29 (2026), Article 26.2.3, Theorem 2 (`jis-2026-26.2.3-thm-2`).
