# Bumby's theorem on arithmetic progressions in recurrences

Let $w$ satisfy $w_{n+2} = aw_{n+1} + bw_n$ for integers $a, b$, let $p$ be a prime with $p \nmid b$, and suppose $w$ is uniformly distributed modulo $p$. If $c$ is a multiple of $\lambda_w(p)/p$ with $\gcd(c, p) = 1$, then for every $s \ge 0$ there are integers $A$ and $B$ with $p \nmid B$ and $w_{s+ic} \equiv A + iB \pmod p$ for $0 \le i < p$. Here, for $m \ge 1$, $w$ is uniformly distributed modulo $m$ when $\lambda_w(m) > 0$ and every residue modulo $m$ occurs exactly $\lambda_w(m)/m$ times among $w_0, \dots, w_{\lambda_w(m)-1}$, and $\lambda_w(m)$ is the least $\ell > 0$ with $w_{n+\ell} \equiv w_n \pmod m$ for all $n$ ($0$ if none).

## Sources

- L. Somer and M. Křížek, Generalization of a theorem of Vélez on uniform distribution in second-order linear recurrences, Integers 25 (2025), Article A1, Theorem 1.2 (Bumby).
