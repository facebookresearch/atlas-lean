# The product $\prod_{n \ge 0} (1 - a q^n)$ at $a = 2$, $q = 1$

The infinite product $\prod_{n \ge 0} (1 - a q^n)$, with the value $1$ by convention when it
does not converge unconditionally, equals $1$ at $a = 2$ and $q = 1$.

Every factor $1 - 2 \cdot 1^n$ equals $-1$, so the finite partial products alternate between $-1$
and $1$ and the product does not converge; the value $1$ comes from the convention, not from a
limit of partial products. The $q$-Pochhammer symbol $(a;q)_\infty$ is defined only for
$|q| < 1$.
