# Membership in a subgroup joined with a central involution

Let $G$ be a group, $\Gamma$ a subgroup of $G$ and $c$ an element of the centre of $G$ with
$c^2 = 1$. Then an element $g \in G$ lies in the subgroup generated by $\Gamma$ and $c$ if and only
if $g \in \Gamma$ or $cg \in \Gamma$.

For a subgroup $\Gamma$ of $\mathrm{SL}(2, \mathbb{Z})$ and $c = -1$ this says that an element lies in
the subgroup generated by $\Gamma$ and $-1$ exactly when it or its negative lies in $\Gamma$, which is
the computation behind "up to sign" arguments.
