# Length $p^k$ when the modulus has a single factor $p$

For integers $m \ge 1$ and $n \ge 1$, let $D$ be the Ducci map on $\mathbb{Z}_m^n$, $D(x_1, \dots, x_n) = (x_1 + x_2, x_2 + x_3, \dots, x_n + x_1)$, and let $L_m(n)$ be the length of the basic Ducci sequence: the least $\alpha \ge 0$ such that $D^{\alpha + \beta}(u) = D^{\alpha}(u)$ for some $\beta \ge 1$, where $u = (0, \dots, 0, 1)$. Let $n$ be even and let $p$ be a prime with $n = p^k n_1$ and $m = p\, m_1$, where $k \ge 1$, $\gcd(n_1, m_1) = 1$, $p \nmid n_1$ and $p \nmid m_1$. Then $L_m(n) = p^k$.

## Sources

- Mark L. Lewis and Shannon M. Tefft, *The Maximum Length for Ducci Sequences on $\mathbb{Z}_m^n$ when $n$ is Even*, INTEGERS 26 (2026), Article A17, Theorem 1, part 2.
