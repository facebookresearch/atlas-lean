# Length one when the modulus is coprime to the even length

For integers $m \ge 1$ and $n \ge 1$, let $D$ be the Ducci map on $\mathbb{Z}_m^n$, $D(x_1, \dots, x_n) = (x_1 + x_2, x_2 + x_3, \dots, x_n + x_1)$, and let $L_m(n)$ be the length of the basic Ducci sequence: the least $\alpha \ge 0$ such that $D^{\alpha + \beta}(u) = D^{\alpha}(u)$ for some $\beta \ge 1$, where $u = (0, \dots, 0, 1)$. If $n$ is even, $m \ge 2$ and $\gcd(n, m) = 1$, then $L_m(n) = 1$. The hypothesis $m \ge 2$ is needed: for $m = 1$ the basic tuple is $0$, already on its cycle, and $L_1(n) = 0$.

## Sources

- Mark L. Lewis and Shannon M. Tefft, *The Maximum Length for Ducci Sequences on $\mathbb{Z}_m^n$ when $n$ is Even*, INTEGERS 26 (2026), Article A17, Theorem 1, part 1.
