# Erdős Problem 440, second question

The statement that (i) for every strictly increasing sequence $a$ of positive integers and every $\varepsilon > 0$, $A(x) \le (1 + \varepsilon)\sqrt{x}$ for infinitely many $x$, and (ii) some strictly increasing sequence $a$ of positive integers has, for every $\varepsilon > 0$, $A(x) \ge (1 - \varepsilon)\sqrt{x}$ for all sufficiently large $x$; here for a strictly increasing sequence $a_0 < a_1 < \cdots$ of positive integers and $x \in \mathbb{N}$, $A(x)$ is the number of indices $i$ with $\operatorname{lcm}(a_i, a_{i+1}) \le x$. So the largest possible value of $\liminf_x A(x)/\sqrt{x}$ is $1$.

This page records the statement as a proposition. Its resolution: [The largest liminf of the consecutive least common multiple count](max-liminf-lcm-count-ratio-eq-one-holds.md).
