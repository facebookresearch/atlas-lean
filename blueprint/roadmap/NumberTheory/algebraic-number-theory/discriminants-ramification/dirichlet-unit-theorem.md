# Dirichlet's unit theorem: the logarithmic image of the units is a full lattice

Let $K$ be a number field with $r$ real and $s$ complex places, let $S_\infty$ be its set of infinite
places, $U_K$ the unit group of its ring of integers and $\mu(K)$ the finite cyclic group of roots of unity
in $K$. Let

$$\lambda \colon U_K \to \mathbb{R}^{S_\infty}, \qquad u \mapsto (\log |u|_v)_{v \in S_\infty},$$

where $|\cdot|_v$ is the normalized absolute value at $v$ (the square of the usual one at a complex place).
Then the kernel of $\lambda$ is $\mu(K)$, and its image is a full lattice in the hyperplane
$H = \{ x \in \mathbb{R}^{S_\infty} : \sum_v x_v = 0 \}$, of dimension $r + s - 1$: a subgroup generated by a
basis of $H$. Consequently $U_K$ is finitely generated and $U_K \cong \mu(K) \times \mathbb{Z}^{r+s-1}$.

For any infinite place $v_0$, leaving out the coordinate of $v_0$ is a linear isomorphism of $H$ onto
$\mathbb{R}^{S_\infty \setminus \{v_0\}}$, with inverse $x \mapsto (x, -\sum_{v \ne v_0} x_v)$. So the statement
is equivalent to: the image of $U_K$ under $u \mapsto (\log |u|_v)_{v \ne v_0}$ is a full lattice in
$\mathbb{R}^{S_\infty \setminus \{v_0\}}$. This is the form Mathlib proves, with the logarithmic embedding
$x \mapsto (m_v \log w_v(x))_{v \ne v_0}$, $m_v \in \{1, 2\}$ the multiplicity of the place, which is the
same map.

Milne's proof: $\lambda(U_K)$ meets every bounded set in finitely many points, since there are finitely
many algebraic integers of bounded degree whose conjugates are all bounded, so it is a lattice in $H$ and
the kernel is finite, hence $\mu(K)$ (Proposition 5.8). Minkowski's lattice-point theorem gives, for each
infinite place $v$, a unit $u_v$ with $|u_v|_w < 1$ for every infinite place $w \ne v$, and such units
[generate a subgroup of finite index](finite-index-closure-range-of-lt-one-off.md); so the lattice spans $H$
(Theorem 5.9).
