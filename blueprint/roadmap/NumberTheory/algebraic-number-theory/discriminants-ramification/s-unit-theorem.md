# Dirichlet's S-unit theorem

Let $K$ be a number field, let $S$ be a finite set of places of $K$ containing every infinite place, and let

$$U(S) = \{ a \in K^\times : \mathrm{ord}_{\mathfrak{p}}(a) = 0 \text{ for every finite place } \mathfrak{p} \notin S \}$$

be the group of $S$-units of $K$. Let $\mu(K)$ be the group of roots of unity in $K$, the torsion subgroup of
$K^\times$. Then $U(S)$ is finitely generated, its torsion subgroup is $\mu(K)$, and its rank is $|S| - 1$;
since $S$ contains an infinite place, $|S| \ge 1$. Moreover, for $a \in U(S)$ let

$$\lambda(a) = (\log |a|_v)_{v \in S} \in \mathbb{R}^S,$$

where $|\cdot|_v$ is the normalized absolute value at $v$: $|a|_v = |a|_w^{m_w}$ at an infinite place $w$, with
$|\cdot|_w$ the absolute value induced by an embedding of $K$ into $\mathbb{C}$ and $m_w = 1$ if $w$ is real and
$m_w = 2$ if $w$ is complex, and $|a|_v = N(\mathfrak{p}_v)^{-\mathrm{ord}_{\mathfrak{p}_v}(a)}$ at a finite place
$v$ with prime ideal $\mathfrak{p}_v$ of norm $N(\mathfrak{p}_v)$. Then $\lambda(a) = 0$ if and only if
$a \in \mu(K)$, and the image of $\lambda$ is the subgroup of $\mathbb{R}^S$ generated by $|S| - 1$ vectors that
are linearly independent over $\mathbb{R}$ and lie in the hyperplane

$$H = \{ x \in \mathbb{R}^S : \sum_{v \in S} x_v = 0 \}.$$

As $H$ has dimension $|S| - 1$, these vectors form a basis of $H$: the image of $\lambda$ is a full lattice in
$H$. Since $U(S)$ is a finitely generated abelian group, its torsion subgroup $\mu(K)$ is finite, hence cyclic,
as is every finite subgroup of the multiplicative group of a field, and the first part says that
$U(S) \cong \mu(K) \times \mathbb{Z}^{|S| - 1}$.

For $S$ the set of infinite places, $U(S)$ is the unit group of the ring of integers and this is
[Dirichlet's unit theorem](dirichlet-unit-theorem.md): the logarithmic image of the units of the ring of
integers is a full lattice in the trace-zero hyperplane of $\mathbb{R}^{S_\infty}$, which leaving out one
coordinate identifies with $\mathbb{R}^{r+s-1}$ if $K$ has $r$ real and $s$ complex places (Milne,
Proposition 5.8 and Theorem 5.9). Milne states the rank for $S$ given by the infinite places together with a
finite set of $t$ prime ideals, as $r + s + t - 1$, and proves it from that case and the finiteness of the
class group: if $h$ is the class number, $\mathfrak{p}^h$ is principal for every prime ideal $\mathfrak{p}$, so
the valuations at the finite places of $S$ map $U(S)$ onto a subgroup of finite index of $\mathbb{Z}^t$, with
kernel the units of the ring of integers (Milne, Theorem 5.11). The image of $\lambda$ lies in $H$ by the
[product formula](../adeles-class-field-theory/adeles-and-ideles/adelic-norm-principal-embedding.md), since
$|a|_v = 1$ at every place $v \notin S$.
