# Abelian extensions of the rationals with finitely many ramified primes have undecidable first-order theory

If $A$ is an abelian, possibly infinite, extension of $\mathbb{Q}$ in which only finitely many rational primes ramify, then $\mathbb{Z}$ is first-order definable both in the field $A$ and in its ring of integers, so both have undecidable first-order theories in the language of rings.

Let $A$ be an algebraic extension of $\mathbb{Q}$, possibly of infinite degree, that is Galois over $\mathbb{Q}$ with abelian Galois group, and such that only finitely many rational primes ramify in $A$ (that is, in the number fields contained in $A$). Let $\mathcal{O}_A$ be the integral closure of $\mathbb{Z}$ in $A$. Work in the language of rings $\{+, \cdot, 0, 1\}$ with equality.

**Theorem (Shlapentokh).**

1. $\mathbb{Z}$ is first-order definable in the field $A$, and the first-order theory of $A$ is undecidable.
2. $\mathbb{Z}$ is first-order definable in the ring $\mathcal{O}_A$, and the first-order theory of $\mathcal{O}_A$ is undecidable.

Examples are the fields obtained from $\mathbb{Q}$ by adjoining all $\ell^n$-th roots of unity, $n \ge 1$, for $\ell$ in a fixed finite set of primes, and their subfields. By the Kronecker–Weber theorem such an $A$ is a subfield of a cyclotomic field generated by roots of unity of order divisible only by primes from a finite set.

## Expansion

By Kronecker–Weber, $A$ lies in a cyclotomic extension in which the degrees of all finite subextensions are divisible only by primes from a finite set; so for an odd prime $q$ outside that set, $A$ is $q$-bounded: along every path of the factor tree of every prime, the local degrees have bounded $q$-adic order, and every prime of $\mathbb{Q}$ is completely $q$-bounded. Over $q$-bounded fields the source defines the integral closures of small rings of $\mathcal{S}$-integers, and in particular $\mathcal{O}_A$, by norm equations in the style of Rumely, Videla and Fukuzaki. By earlier existential results, $\mathbb{Z}$ is existentially definable in the integral closure of $\mathcal{O}_{A_0,\mathcal{S}}$ in $A$ for any number field $A_0 \subseteq A$ and any finite nonempty set $\mathcal{S}$ of its primes. Combining the two definitions gives a first-order definition of $\mathbb{Z}$ in $A$ and in $\mathcal{O}_A$, and the undecidability of the theory of $\mathbb{Z}$ transfers.
