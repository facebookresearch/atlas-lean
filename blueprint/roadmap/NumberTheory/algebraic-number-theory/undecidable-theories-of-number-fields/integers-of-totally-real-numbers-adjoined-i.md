# The ring of integers of the totally real numbers adjoined i has undecidable theory

The ring of totally real algebraic integers is existentially definable in the ring of integers of $\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{tr}}(i)$, where $\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{tr}}$ is the field of totally real algebraic numbers; in particular the first-order theory of that ring of integers is undecidable.

An algebraic number is *totally real* when every root of its minimal polynomial over $\mathbb{Q}$ is real. Let $\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{tr}}$ be the field of all totally real algebraic numbers, $\mathbb{Z}^{\mathrm{tr}}$ its ring of integers, $i = \sqrt{-1}$, and $\mathcal{O}$ the ring of integers (integral closure of $\mathbb{Z}$) of the field $\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{tr}}(i)$. Work in the language of rings $\{+, \cdot, 0, 1\}$ with equality.

**Theorem (Springer).**

1. $\mathbb{Z}^{\mathrm{tr}}$ is existentially definable in $\mathcal{O}$: there is an existential formula $\varphi(x)$ of the language of rings such that an element of $\mathcal{O}$ satisfies $\varphi$ in $\mathcal{O}$ exactly when it lies in $\mathbb{Z}^{\mathrm{tr}}$.
2. The first-order theory $\operatorname{Th}(\mathcal{O})$ is undecidable.

The field $\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{tr}}(i)$ itself, like $\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{tr}}$, has a decidable theory, so the ring of integers of $\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{tr}}(i)$ is not first-order definable in its fraction field. A remark in an earlier survey had listed this ring as decidable; that claim was based on a result about the overrings $\mathcal{O}[1/p]$.

## Expansion

A totally real field $K$ is closed under square roots when it contains every totally real $\alpha$ with $\alpha^2 \in K$; $\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{tr}}$ has this property. Springer proves that for such $K$ and any totally imaginary quadratic extension $L$ of $K$, the ring $\mathcal{O}_K$ is existentially definable in $\mathcal{O}_L$ (Theorem 1.3 of arXiv:2207.00140v3). The method works with the unit group $R^\times$ of an existentially definable non-maximal order $R \subseteq \mathcal{O}_L$ whose only roots of unity are $\pm 1$: then $(R^\times)^2$ is a set of totally real elements, and sums and differences of such units, together with the fact that in these infinite extensions many algebraic integers are sums of a bounded number of units, give an existential description of $\mathcal{O}_K$ inside $\mathcal{O}_L$. Working with $R$ instead of $\mathcal{O}_L$ is what allows $L$ to contain infinitely many roots of unity, as $\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{tr}}(i)$ does. Clause 1 is the case $K = \mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{tr}}$, $L = \mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{tr}}(i)$. Clause 2 follows: relativizing quantifiers to the existential definition of $\mathbb{Z}^{\mathrm{tr}}$ translates sentences about $\mathbb{Z}^{\mathrm{tr}}$ into sentences about $\mathcal{O}$, and Julia Robinson proved $\operatorname{Th}(\mathbb{Z}^{\mathrm{tr}})$ undecidable.

A good expansion adds and links pages for Springer's existential definition of $\mathcal{O}_K$ in $\mathcal{O}_L$ for totally real $K$ closed under square roots and totally imaginary quadratic $L/K$ (Theorem 1.3 of arXiv:2207.00140v3), and for the transfer of undecidability along an existential definition.
