# For every d ≥ 2 the abelian part of the compositum of all fields of degree at most d has undecidable first-order theory

For every $d \ge 2$, the field $\mathbb{Q}^{(d)}_{\mathrm{ab}}$, the maximal abelian subextension of the compositum of all extensions of $\mathbb{Q}$ of degree at most $d$, has undecidable first-order theory in the language of rings.

For a number field $F$ and $d \ge 2$, let $F^{(d)}$ be the compositum, in a fixed algebraic closure, of all extensions of $F$ of degree at most $d$, and $F^{(d)}_{\mathrm{ab}}$ the maximal abelian subextension of $F \subseteq F^{(d)}$. The fields $\mathbb{Q}^{(d)}_{\mathrm{ab}}$ are undecidable for all $d \ge 2$ (example (1) of Section 4.1 of the source): each is a totally imaginary quadratic extension of a totally real field to which the main theorem of the source applies.

The case $d = 2$, the compositum $\mathbb{Q}^{(2)}$ of all quadratic fields, is due to Martínez-Ranero, Utreras and Videla.

## Expansion

The family of polynomials $x^2 - p(a) x - 1$, with $p(t) \in \mathbb{Z}[t]$ nonconstant, has unit constant term and totally real roots generating real quadratic fields. Applied with the totally real subfield of $\mathbb{Q}^{(d)}_{\mathrm{ab}}$ that contains all these roots, the main theorem of the source (Theorem 3.6: totally imaginary quadratic extensions of infinite totally real fields inside some $F^{(d)}_{\mathrm{ab}}$ containing all roots of such a family are undecidable) gives undecidability of $\mathbb{Q}^{(d)}_{\mathrm{ab}}$.
