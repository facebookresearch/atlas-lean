# The j-invariant of a proper ideal generates the ring class field

Let $K \subset \mathbb{C}$ be an imaginary quadratic field, $\mathcal{O}$ an order in $K$, and
$\mathfrak{a}$ a proper fractional $\mathcal{O}$-ideal, that is, a lattice that is a
[proper ideal](../../../../Analysis/special-functions/elliptic-functions/complex-multiplication-of-period-lattices/is-proper-ideal.md) of $\mathcal{O}$, with $j$-invariant $j(\mathfrak{a})$. Then $j(\mathfrak{a})$ is an
algebraic integer, and

$$K(j(\mathfrak{a})) = L,$$

the [ring class field](ring-class-field.md) of $\mathcal{O}$: the single value $j(\mathfrak{a})$ already
generates the field generated by all of them.

For $\mathcal{O} = \mathcal{O}_K$ this is the generation of the Hilbert class field by the
$j$-invariant of a fractional ideal
([maximal-order case](../hilbert-class-field/hilbert-class-field-generated-by-j.md)).

## Expansion

A good expansion has pages for the modular polynomial $\Phi_N \in \mathbb{Z}[X, Y]$ (Sutherland,
Lecture 20, which recalls its integrality from Theorem 19.17 of Lecture 19, and Theorem 20.3, which says that $\Phi_N(j_1, j_2) = 0$ exactly
when $j_1$ and $j_2$ are the $j$-invariants of curves related by a cyclic isogeny of degree $N$); for the
leading term $-X^{2p}$ of $\Phi_p(X, X)$ for $p$ prime (Lecture 20, Lemma 20.9); and for the integrality
of $j(\mathfrak{a})$, a root of $\Phi_p(X, X)$ for $p$ the norm of a principal $\mathcal{O}$-ideal of
prime norm (Lecture 20, Theorem 20.12 and Corollary 20.13). With the
[Galois group of the ring class field](ring-class-field-galois-group.md), Galois theory then gives
$K(j(\mathfrak{a})) = L$ (Lecture 21, Corollary 21.2). Each page states its result precisely, with its
locator in those notes.
