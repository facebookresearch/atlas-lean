# Global Artin map

Let $K$ be a number field with idèle group $\mathbb{I}_K$, and let $K^{\mathrm{ab}}$ be the maximal abelian
extension of $K$ in a fixed algebraic closure. For a place $v$ of $K$ let
$\iota_v \colon K_v^\times \to \mathbb{I}_K$ put an element at $v$ and $1$ at the other places. For a
finite abelian extension $L/K$ and a place $w$ of $L$ above $v$, restriction to $L$ identifies
$\mathrm{Gal}(L_w/K_v)$ with the decomposition group $D(w) \subseteq \mathrm{Gal}(L/K)$, which depends only
on $v$ since $L/K$ is abelian; let $\phi_v \colon K_v^\times \to \mathrm{Gal}(L_w/K_v) = D(w)$ be the local
Artin map of $L_w/K_v$: for $v$ finite, the [local Artin map](../../../p-adic-numbers/local-class-field-theory.md)
of $K_v$ followed by restriction to $L_w$; for $v$ real and $L_w = \mathbb{C}$, the surjection
$\mathbb{R}^\times \to \mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{R})$ with kernel $\mathbb{R}_{>0}$; and the trivial
map when $L_w = K_v$ is archimedean.

Then there is a unique continuous homomorphism

$$\phi_K \colon \mathbb{I}_K \to \mathrm{Gal}(K^{\mathrm{ab}}/K)$$

such that for every finite extension $L \subseteq K^{\mathrm{ab}}$ of $K$, every place $v$ of $K$ and every
$x \in K_v^\times$, the restriction $\phi_K(\iota_v(x))|_L$ is $\phi_v(x) \in D(w) \subseteq \mathrm{Gal}(L/K)$.
Writing $\phi_{L/K}(a) = \phi_K(a)|_L$, one has

$$\phi_{L/K}(a) = \prod_v \phi_v(a_v) \qquad (a \in \mathbb{I}_K),$$

a finite product, because $\phi_v(a_v) = 1$ whenever $v$ is finite and unramified in $L$ and $a_v$ is a
unit; and $\phi_{L'/K}(a)|_L = \phi_{L/K}(a)$ for $L \subseteq L'$. The map $\phi_K$ is the global Artin map
of $K$, and $\phi_{L/K}$ the global Artin map of $L/K$.

Milne and Sutherland define $\phi_{L/K}$ by the product formula; the local Artin maps of the finite
abelian subextensions are compatible with restriction, so the $\phi_{L/K}$ form a compatible system and
define $\phi_K$. It is continuous because each local Artin map is continuous and the units at the
unramified places lie in the kernel of $\phi_{L/K}$; it is unique because $\mathbb{I}_K$ is the closure of
the subgroup generated by the images of the $\iota_v$.

## Depends on

- [Topological idele group](../adeles-and-ideles/topological-idele-group.md)
- [Local class field theory: the local Artin map](../../../p-adic-numbers/local-class-field-theory.md), at the finite places; the proof also uses the triviality of the units on unramified extensions and the continuity of the local Artin maps

## Proof depends on

- [Local class field theory: the local Artin map](../../../p-adic-numbers/local-class-field-theory.md), for the local Artin maps at the finite places: as restrictions of the one map $\mathrm{rec}_{K_v}$ they are compatible with restriction to subextensions, they are trivial on the units when $L_w/K_v$ is unramified, and they are continuous
- [Only finitely many primes ramify in a number field extension](../../discriminants-ramification/ramification-in-extensions-of-number-fields/finite-ramified-primes.md), for the finiteness of the product
- [Topological idele group](../adeles-and-ideles/topological-idele-group.md), for $a_v \in \mathcal{O}_v^\times$ at almost all $v$ and for the restricted-product topology: a product of open subgroups $U_v \subseteq K_v^\times$ with $U_v = \mathcal{O}_v^\times$ for almost all $v$ is open, which gives the continuity, and the images of the $\iota_v$ generate a dense subgroup, which gives the uniqueness
