# Global duality for Galois groups with restricted ramification

This node is still to be broken down. It covers the cohomology of the $S$-units and $S$-idèle classes of
the maximal extension $F_S$ of a number field $F$ unramified outside a set $S \supseteq S_\infty$ of places,
the global duality theorem for $G_S = \operatorname{Gal}(F_S/F)$ that comes from the class formation of the
$S$-idèle classes, and the auxiliary results through which Neukirch–Schmidt–Wingberg and Milne derive from
it the finiteness theorems, Poitou–Tate duality, the nine-term sequence and the global Euler characteristic
formula. It follows Neukirch–Schmidt–Wingberg, Chapter VIII, §§3–7, and Milne's *Arithmetic Duality
Theorems*, Chapter I, §§4–5.

A good expansion has pages for:

1. the roots of unity of order in $\mathbb{N}(S)$ in $F_S$ and the $\mathbb{N}(S)$-divisibility of the
   $S$-units $\mathcal{O}_S^\times$ (NSW (8.3.4));
2. the cohomology of the $S$-idèles $I_S$, of the $S$-idèle classes $C_S$ and of $\mathcal{O}_S^\times$
   (NSW (8.3.5)–(8.3.12); Milne, Lemma I.4.1, Propositions I.4.2–4.3, Lemmas I.4.4–4.5), including that
   $(G_S, C_S)$ is a class formation for the primes in $\mathbb{N}(S)$ (NSW (8.3.9); Milne, Proposition I.4.2);
3. the global duality theorem for $\operatorname{Mod}_S(G_S)$ (NSW (8.4.1)–(8.4.5); Milne, Theorem I.4.6 and
   Corollary I.4.7);
4. the topologies on the local and global cohomology groups and the properties of the Shapiro maps
   $\mathrm{sh}^i$ (NSW (8.5.1)–(8.5.5));
5. the auxiliary results of §6: (8.6.3)–(8.6.6) (the class group description of
   $\text{Ш}^1(G_S, \mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})$, the boundary map $C_S(A)^{G_S} \to H^1(G_S, A')$ and the
   finiteness of $\text{Ш}^1(G_S, A')$, and the compatibility diagrams), (8.6.11) (properness of
   localization), (8.6.12) and (8.6.13) (the Euler characteristic quotient $\varphi(G_S, A)$ and the exactness
   criterion for the Poitou–Tate complex); Milne, Lemmas I.4.8–4.9 and I.4.12–4.13;
6. the Galois module structure of the $S$-units used for the Euler characteristic (NSW (8.7.1)–(8.7.3)).

## Proof depends on

- [Fundamental class of a finite Galois extension of number fields](../../adeles-class-field-theory/reciprocity-law/fundamental-class.md), for the class formation of the idèle classes and their invariant maps, from which item 2 builds that of the $S$-idèle classes
- [S-unit theorem](../../discriminants-ramification/s-unit-theorem.md), for the finite generation and rank of the $S$-units used in items 2 and 6
- [Local Tate duality](../galois-cohomology-of-local-fields/local-tate-duality.md), for the local duality in items 4 and 5
- [Tate duality for the real numbers](../galois-cohomology-of-local-fields/tate-duality-for-the-real-numbers.md), for the archimedean places in items 3 and 5
- [Local Euler characteristic formula](../galois-cohomology-of-local-fields/local-euler-characteristic-formula.md), for the product formula behind (8.6.13)
- [Cohomology of profinite groups](../../../../RepresentationTheory/group-cohomology/cohomology-of-profinite-groups.md), for the abstract duality relative to a class formation (Tate–Nakayama) used in item 3
