# Conductor of the ℓ-adic representation of a newform

This node is still to be broken down. It covers clause (d) of the theorem of Darmon, Diamond and Taylor that
lists the properties of the $\ell$-adic representation attached to a newform of weight two: its conductor is
the prime-to-$\ell$ part of the level. They derive this clause from Carayol's Théorème (A), the compatibility
of that representation with the local components of the automorphic representation of the newform at every
prime $p \neq \ell$.

Let $N \ge 1$. On the space $S_2(\Gamma_1(N))$ of cusp forms of weight $2$ for $\Gamma_1(N)$, with
$q$-expansions $f = \sum_{n \ge 1} a_n q^n$, $q = e^{2\pi i \tau}$, Darmon, Diamond and Taylor use the
Petersson scalar product $\langle f, g \rangle = \int_{\mathcal{H}/\Gamma_1(N)} f(\tau)\overline{g(\tau)}\,dx\,dy$
($\tau = x + iy$), the diamond operators
$\langle d \rangle f(\tau) = (c\tau + d)^{-2} f\bigl(\tfrac{a\tau + b}{c\tau + d}\bigr)$ for
$\left(\begin{smallmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{smallmatrix}\right) \in \Gamma_0(N)$, which depend only on
$d \in (\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^\times$, and the Hecke operators
$T_p f(\tau) = \frac{1}{p}\sum_{i=0}^{p-1} f\bigl(\tfrac{\tau + i}{p}\bigr) + p\,\langle p \rangle f(p\tau)$ for
primes $p \nmid N$ and $U_q f(\tau) = \frac{1}{q}\sum_{i=0}^{q-1} f\bigl(\tfrac{\tau + i}{q}\bigr)$ for primes
$q \mid N$. A **newform of weight $2$ and level $N$** is an $f \in S_2(\Gamma_1(N))$ with $a_1 = 1$ that is an
eigenvector of every $T_p$ ($p \nmid N$), $U_q$ ($q \mid N$) and $\langle d \rangle$
($d \in (\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^\times$), and that lies in the new subspace of $S_2(\Gamma_1(N))$: the
orthogonal complement, for the Petersson scalar product, of the span of the forms $g(a\tau)$ with
$g \in S_2(\Gamma_1(M))$, $M < N$ and $aM \mid N$.

Let $f = \sum_{n \ge 1} a_n q^n$ be a newform of weight $2$ and level $N$, let $K_f \subset \mathbb{C}$ be the
number field generated by the $a_n$, and let its character
$\psi \colon (\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^\times \to K_f^\times$ send $d$ to the eigenvalue of $\langle d \rangle$ on
$f$. Let $\ell$ be a prime, $\lambda$ a prime of $K_f$ above $\ell$, and $K_{f,\lambda}$ the completion, into
which $K_f$ embeds, so that $a_p$ and $\psi(p)$ are elements of $K_{f,\lambda}$. In particular $f$ is a
normalized eigenform of weight $2$ with nebentypus $\psi$, in the convention of
[Galois representations attached to normalized eigenforms](../../not-yet-classified/exists-galois-rep-charpoly-frob.md),
which gives an irreducible continuous representation $\rho \colon G_{\mathbb{Q}} \to \mathrm{GL}_2(K_{f,\lambda})$,
unramified at every prime $p \nmid \ell N$, such that every arithmetic Frobenius at a prime above such a $p$
has characteristic polynomial $X^2 - a_p X + \psi(p)\,p$.

**Theorem.** Let $\rho \colon G_{\mathbb{Q}} \to \mathrm{GL}_2(K_{f,\lambda})$ be any representation with
these three properties: continuous, irreducible over $K_{f,\lambda}$, and unramified at every prime
$p \nmid \ell N$ with that characteristic polynomial of Frobenius. Then the
[conductor](conductor-of-an-l-adic-representation.md) $N(\rho) = \prod_{p \neq \ell} p^{m_p(\rho)}$ of $\rho$
is the prime-to-$\ell$ part of $N$, and indeed

$$m_p(\rho) = v_p(N) \qquad \text{for every prime } p \neq \ell.$$

Darmon, Diamond and Taylor print the first form, $N(\rho)$ equal to the prime-to-$\ell$ part of $N$. The
second implies it, and the first implies the second because each $m_p(\rho)$ is a nonnegative integer, as they
note on p. 54 (citing Serre, *Corps locaux*, Chapter VI); the derivation from Carayol's theorem gives the
second prime by prime.

They state clause (d) for one representation $\rho_f$, the pushforward of the action of
$G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ on the rational $\ell$-adic Tate module of the abelian variety $A_f$ that Shimura attaches to
$f$, with values in $\mathrm{GL}_2(K'_f)$, where $K'_f \subset \overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell$ is generated by the
$a_n$ over a fixed finite extension $K$ of $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$ and fixed embeddings of $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ into
$\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell$ and $\mathbb{C}$. The theorem above is the same statement. Choose the embedding
of $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ into $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell$ that induces $\lambda$ and take $K = \mathbb{Q}_\ell$;
then $K'_f = K_{f,\lambda}$. By their clause (a), $\rho_f$ has the listed properties, and it is absolutely
irreducible by their clause (c). Any $\rho$ as in the theorem has the Frobenius traces of $\rho_f$ at the
primes $p \nmid \ell N$ and is semisimple, being irreducible, so it is isomorphic to $\rho_f$
(Darmon–Diamond–Taylor, Proposition 2.6 (c), which rests on the Chebotarev density theorem); conductors
depend only on the isomorphism class. For another $K$, $\rho_f$ is the extension of scalars of this one to
$K'_f$, which changes no codimension of a fixed subspace.

## Expansion

A good expansion has pages for:

1. newforms of weight two and level $N$: the operators $T_p$, $U_q$ and $\langle d \rangle$ and the Petersson
   scalar product on $S_2(\Gamma_1(N))$ (Darmon–Diamond–Taylor, §1.2, pp. 26–27, and §1.3, pp. 28–29), the old
   and new subspaces (Definition 1.21), and the decomposition of $S_2(\Gamma_1(N))$ into the spaces of forms
   that share the $T_p$-eigenvalues ($p \nmid N$) of a newform of level dividing $N$ (Theorem 1.22);
2. the step from any $\rho$ as in the theorem to $\rho_f$: a semisimple $\ell$-adic representation is
   determined by its Frobenius traces outside a finite set of primes (Darmon–Diamond–Taylor,
   Proposition 2.6 (c)), and $\rho_f$ has properties (a) and (c) of their Theorem 3.1;
3. the automorphic representation $\pi$ of $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})$ attached to $f$ and its
   local components $\pi_p$;
4. Carayol's Théorème (A) for $F = \mathbb{Q}$ and weight $2$: there are a finite extension $E$ of the field
   of rationality $\mathbb{Q}(\pi)$ and a strictly compatible system $\{\sigma^{\lambda'}\}$ of continuous
   two-dimensional $E_{\lambda'}$-adic representations of $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ such that, for every prime $p$ and
   every finite place $\lambda'$ of $E$ of residue characteristic different from $p$, the restriction of
   $\sigma^{\lambda'}$ to the local Weil group $W_p$ is equivalent to the $\lambda'$-adic realization
   $\sigma^{\lambda'}(\pi_p)$ of the
   Weil–Deligne representation $\sigma(\pi_p)$. Carayol normalizes class field theory so that geometric
   Frobenius elements correspond to uniformizers, writes $\omega_p$ for the character $\lvert\cdot\rvert_p$, and
   his "Hecke" correspondence $\pi_p \mapsto \sigma(\pi_p)$ differs from the Langlands correspondence by the
   twist by $\omega_p^{1/2}$ followed by the contragredient;
5. the identification of $\rho$ with Carayol's $\sigma^{\lambda'}$, for the place $\lambda'$ of $E$ above $\ell$
   that the chosen embeddings determine, after extension of scalars to a common field: at the primes
   $p \nmid \ell N$ both have Frobenius trace $a_p$, and $\rho$ is absolutely irreducible (item 2), so the
   semisimplification of $\sigma^{\lambda'}$, which has the same traces, is irreducible, hence so is
   $\sigma^{\lambda'}$, and the two are isomorphic by Proposition 2.6 (c);
6. the conductor of a Weil–Deligne representation $(r, \mathcal{N})$ of $W_p$ on $V$, the codimension of
   $(\ker \mathcal{N})^{r(I_p)}$ plus the Swan conductor of $r$, and the identity of $m_p(\rho)$ with the
   conductor of the Weil–Deligne representation of $\rho|_{G_p}$ for $p \neq \ell$: $\rho^{I_p}$ is the
   $I_p$-invariant part of $\ker \mathcal{N}$, and $\rho$ and $r$ agree on the groups $I_p^u$, $u > 0$, so that
   $\int_0^\infty \operatorname{codim} \rho^{I_p^u}\,du$ is the Swan conductor;
7. the invariance of the conductor of a Weil–Deligne representation under twists by unramified characters, such
   as $\omega_p^{1/2}$, and under passage to the contragredient, so that through Carayol's theorem and the local
   Langlands correspondence the conductor of the Weil–Deligne representation of $\rho|_{G_p}$ is the conductor of
   $\pi_p$, the exponent read off its $\varepsilon$-factor;
8. the theory of local newforms, by which the conductor of $\pi_p$ is $v_p(N)$ for a newform of level $N$;
9. the integrality of $m_p(\rho)$ (Darmon–Diamond–Taylor, p. 54), which makes the two forms of the theorem
   equivalent; at finite level it is the
   [integrality of Artin and Swan conductors](../../../AlgebraicGeometry/not-yet-classified/ramification-conductors/finite-image-artin-swan-integral.md).

## Proof depends on

- [Chebotarev density theorem](../adeles-class-field-theory/chebotarev-density-theorem.md), for the density of Frobenius elements in the finite Galois extensions of $\mathbb{Q}$, which with continuity gives Darmon–Diamond–Taylor's Proposition 2.6 (c), used to compare $\rho$ with $\rho_f$ and with Carayol's $\sigma^{\lambda'}$
- [Weil–Deligne representation of an ℓ-adic representation](weil-deligne-representations/weil-deligne-representation-of-an-l-adic-representation.md), for the Weil–Deligne representation of $\rho|_{G_p}$, $p \neq \ell$, and the equivalence through which Carayol's $\lambda'$-adic realization $\sigma^{\lambda'}(\pi_p)$ corresponds to $\sigma(\pi_p)$
- [Local Langlands correspondence for GLₙ](../../../RepresentationTheory/reductive-groups-over-local-fields/representations-of-p-adic-gl-n/local-langlands-correspondence-for-gl-n.md), for the preservation of $\varepsilon$-factors of twists by characters and the compatibility with twists and contragredients, which relate Carayol's "Hecke" correspondence at $p$ to $\mathrm{rec}_2$
- [L- and ε-factors of pairs for GLₙ and of Weil–Deligne representations](../../../RepresentationTheory/reductive-groups-over-local-fields/representations-of-p-adic-gl-n/l-and-epsilon-factors-of-pairs.md), for the $\varepsilon$-factors of $\pi_p$ and of Weil–Deligne representations and the conductor of $\pi_p$
