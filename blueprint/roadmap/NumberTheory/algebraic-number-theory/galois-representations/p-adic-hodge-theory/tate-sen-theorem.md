# Tate–Sen theorem on the Galois cohomology of C(i)

Let $K$ be a $p$-adic field in the sense of Fontaine–Ouyang, a complete discretely valued field of characteristic $0$ whose residue field is perfect of characteristic $p$ (for instance a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$), $C$ the completion of an algebraic closure $\bar K$, with its
continuous action of $G_K$, and $\chi$ the $p$-adic cyclotomic character. For a continuous character
$\eta \colon G_K \to \mathbb{Z}_p^\times$ let $C(\eta)$ be $C$ with $G_K$ acting by $g \cdot c = \eta(g) g(c)$, and
$C(i) = C(\chi^i)$. Then

$$C(\eta)^{G_K} = \begin{cases} 0 & \text{if } \eta(I_K) \text{ is infinite}, \\
\text{a one-dimensional } K\text{-vector space} & \text{if } \eta(I_K) \text{ is finite}; \end{cases}$$

in particular $C^{G_K} = K$ and $C(i)^{G_K} = 0$ for $i \ne 0$. For continuous cohomology,
$H^1(G_K, C(i)) = 0$ for $i \ne 0$, and $H^1(G_K, C)$ is a one-dimensional $K$-vector space. If $K$ is a finite
extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$, then moreover $H^n(G_K, C(i)) = 0$ for all $i$ and all $n \ge 2$, and $H^1(G_K, C)$ is
generated by the class of $\log \chi \colon G_K \to \mathbb{Z}_p \subset K$.

## Proof depends on

- [Structure of the rings of periods](structure-of-the-period-rings.md), for its item 1, in two parts. For the cyclotomic $\mathbb{Z}_p$-extension: Tate's normalized traces and Sen's method, through which Fontaine–Ouyang prove $C(\eta)^{G_K} = 0$ for infinite $\eta(I_K)$ (Corollary 4.45, by Proposition 1.104 (3)) and compute the cohomology of the twists $C(i)$ (proof of Proposition 4.46: the vanishing of $H^n(H_K, C(i))$ for $n \ge 1$ and the procyclic computation on $\hat K_\infty(i)$), and through which Brinon–Conrad compute $H^1(G_K, C)$ (proof of Theorem 14.3.4), with the inflation isomorphisms of Brinon–Conrad, Proposition 14.3.3, in place of a Hochschild–Serre sequence with the coefficients $C(i)$. For the other branches of Brinon–Conrad's proof of Theorem 14.3.4: the Tate–Sen formalism for the splitting tower of an infinitely ramified $\eta$ (§14.1, of which the cyclotomic tower is one example, with Lemma 14.1.9 and Proposition 14.3.3), and, for finite $\eta(I_K)$, the passage to a finite Galois extension $K'/K$ over which $\eta$ is unramified, though not necessarily trivial: in degree $0$, completed unramified descent (Lemma 3.2.6) makes $C(\eta)^{G_{K'}}$ one-dimensional over $K'$ and finite Galois descent returns to $K$; in degree $1$, restriction identifies $H^1(G_K, C(\eta))$ with the $\mathrm{Gal}(K'/K)$-invariants $H^1(G_{K'}, C(\eta))^{\mathrm{Gal}(K'/K)}$, completed unramified descent identifies $C(\eta)$ with $C$ as $C$-representations of $G_{K'}$, so that $H^1(G_{K'}, C(\eta))$ is one-dimensional over $K'$ by the case $\eta = 1$, and finite Galois descent for the $K'$-semilinear action of $\mathrm{Gal}(K'/K)$ returns to $K$
- [Cohomology of profinite groups](../../../../RepresentationTheory/group-cohomology/cohomology-of-profinite-groups.md), for the cohomology of finite groups, which vanishes in positive degrees on $\mathbb{Q}$-vector spaces by restriction and corestriction (its item 3), used to replace $K$ by a finite extension (Brinon–Conrad, proof of Theorem 14.3.4) and to pass from $\Gamma_{K_m}$ to $\Gamma_K$ (Fontaine–Ouyang, proof of Proposition 4.46); the continuous cohomology with the coefficients $C(i)$ comes from item 1 of the structure of the rings of periods
