# Conductor of the ℓ-adic representation of a newform

This page states clause (d) of the theorem of Darmon, Diamond and Taylor that lists the properties of the
$\ell$-adic representation attached to a newform of weight two: its conductor is the prime-to-$\ell$ part of the
level. They derive this clause from Carayol's Théorème (A), the compatibility of that representation with the
local components of the automorphic representation of the newform at every prime $p \neq \ell$.

Let $N \ge 1$. On the space $S_2(\Gamma_1(N))$ of cusp forms of weight $2$ for $\Gamma_1(N)$, with
$q$-expansions $f = \sum_{n \ge 1} a_n q^n$, $q = e^{2\pi i \tau}$, Darmon, Diamond and Taylor use the
Petersson scalar product $\langle f, g \rangle = \int_{\mathcal{H}/\Gamma_1(N)} f(\tau)\overline{g(\tau)}\,dx\,dy$
($\tau = x + iy$), the diamond operators
$\langle d \rangle f(\tau) = (c\tau + d)^{-2} f\bigl(\tfrac{a\tau + b}{c\tau + d}\bigr)$ for
$\left(\begin{smallmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{smallmatrix}\right) \in \Gamma_0(N)$, which depend only on
$d \in (\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^\times$, and the Hecke operators
$T_p f(\tau) = \frac{1}{p}\sum_{i=0}^{p-1} f\bigl(\tfrac{\tau + i}{p}\bigr) + p\,\langle p \rangle f(p\tau)$ for
primes $p \nmid N$ and $U_q f(\tau) = \frac{1}{q}\sum_{i=0}^{q-1} f\bigl(\tfrac{\tau + i}{q}\bigr)$ for primes
$q \mid N$. A **newform of weight $2$ and level $N$** is an $f \in S_2(\Gamma_1(N))$ with $a_1 = 1$ that is an
eigenvector of every $T_p$ ($p \nmid N$), $U_q$ ($q \mid N$) and $\langle d \rangle$
($d \in (\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^\times$), and that lies in the new subspace of $S_2(\Gamma_1(N))$: the
orthogonal complement, for the Petersson scalar product, of the span of the forms $g(a\tau)$ with
$g \in S_2(\Gamma_1(M))$, $M < N$ and $aM \mid N$.

Let $f = \sum_{n \ge 1} a_n q^n$ be a newform of weight $2$ and level $N$, let $K_f \subset \mathbb{C}$ be the
number field generated by the $a_n$, and let its character
$\psi \colon (\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^\times \to K_f^\times$ send $d$ to the eigenvalue of $\langle d \rangle$ on
$f$. Let $\ell$ be a prime, $\lambda$ a prime of $K_f$ above $\ell$, and $K_{f,\lambda}$ the completion, into
which $K_f$ embeds, so that $a_p$ and $\psi(p)$ are elements of $K_{f,\lambda}$. In particular $f$ is a
normalized eigenform of weight $2$ with nebentypus $\psi$, in the convention of
[Galois representations attached to normalized eigenforms](../../not-yet-classified/exists-galois-rep-charpoly-frob.md),
which gives an irreducible continuous representation $\rho \colon G_{\mathbb{Q}} \to \mathrm{GL}_2(K_{f,\lambda})$,
unramified at every prime $p \nmid \ell N$, such that every arithmetic Frobenius at a prime above such a $p$
has characteristic polynomial $X^2 - a_p X + \psi(p)\,p$.

**Theorem.** Let $\rho \colon G_{\mathbb{Q}} \to \mathrm{GL}_2(K_{f,\lambda})$ be any representation with
these three properties: continuous, irreducible over $K_{f,\lambda}$, and unramified at every prime
$p \nmid \ell N$ with that characteristic polynomial of Frobenius. Then the
[conductor](conductor-of-an-l-adic-representation.md) $N(\rho) = \prod_{p \neq \ell} p^{m_p(\rho)}$ of $\rho$
is the prime-to-$\ell$ part of $N$, and indeed

$$m_p(\rho) = v_p(N) \qquad \text{for every prime } p \neq \ell.$$

Darmon, Diamond and Taylor print the first form, $N(\rho)$ equal to the prime-to-$\ell$ part of $N$. The
second implies it, and the first implies the second because each $m_p(\rho)$ is a nonnegative integer, as they
note on p. 54 (citing Serre, *Corps locaux*, Chapter VI); the derivation from Carayol's theorem gives the
second prime by prime.

They state clause (d) for one representation $\rho_f$, the pushforward of the action of
$G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ on the rational $\ell$-adic Tate module of the abelian variety $A_f$ that Shimura attaches to
$f$, with values in $\mathrm{GL}_2(K'_f)$, where $K'_f \subset \overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell$ is generated by the
$a_n$ over a fixed finite extension $K$ of $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$ and fixed embeddings of $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ into
$\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell$ and $\mathbb{C}$. The theorem above is the same statement. Choose the embedding
of $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ into $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell$ that induces $\lambda$ and take $K = \mathbb{Q}_\ell$;
then $K'_f = K_{f,\lambda}$, and step 1 below shows that any $\rho$ as in the theorem is isomorphic to $\rho_f$;
conductors depend only on the isomorphism class. For another $K$, $\rho_f$ is the extension of scalars of this one to
$K'_f$, which changes no codimension of a fixed subspace.

The proof follows Carayol. Fix a prime $p \neq \ell$.

1. The representation $\rho$ is absolutely irreducible. Let
   $\rho_f \colon G_{\mathbb{Q}} \to \mathrm{GL}_2(K_{f,\lambda})$ be
   [Darmon, Diamond and Taylor's representation](absolute-irreducibility-of-the-l-adic-representations-of-a-newform.md),
   for embeddings of $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ into $\mathbb{C}$ and $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell$ that induce $\lambda$: it is
   unramified at every prime $p' \nmid \ell N$, $\rho_f(\mathrm{Frob}_{p'})$ has characteristic polynomial
   $X^2 - a_{p'} X + \psi(p')\,p'$, and it is absolutely irreducible. So $\rho$ and $\rho_f$ have the same
   characteristic polynomials of Frobenius at the primes not dividing $\ell N$, and both are semisimple, $\rho$ being
   irreducible; hence $\rho$ is
   [isomorphic to $\rho_f$](deligne-serre-theorem-on-weight-one-forms/semisimple-galois-representations-with-equal-frobenius-polynomials.md)
   (as in Darmon–Diamond–Taylor, Proposition 2.6 (c)) and absolutely irreducible.
2. Let $\pi$ be the [automorphic representation of $f$](automorphic-representation-of-a-weight-two-newform.md),
   with $\pi_q \cong \mathrm{Ind}(\xi_1, \xi_2)$ for $q \nmid N$, where
   $\xi_1(q)q^{1/2}$ and $\xi_2(q)q^{1/2}$ are the roots $\alpha_q, \beta_q$ of $X^2 - a_q X + \psi(q)\,q$, and with
   conductors $\mathfrak{c}(\pi_q) = q^{v_q(N)}\mathbb{Z}_q$ for every prime $q$. It is cuspidal with
   $\pi_\infty \cong D_{2,0}$ (expansion item 1), so
   [Carayol's theorem](carayol-s-theorem-on-hilbert-modular-forms.md), for $F = \mathbb{Q}$, $k_1 = 2$ and $w = 0$, gives
   a finite extension $E \subset \mathbb{C}$ of $\mathbb{Q}(\pi)$ and continuous representations
   $\sigma^{\lambda'} \colon G_{\mathbb{Q}} \to \mathrm{GL}_2(E_{\lambda'})$ with $\sigma^{\lambda'}|_{W_q} \cong \sigma^{\lambda'}(\pi_q)$
   for every prime $q$ and every finite place $\lambda'$ of $E$ of residue characteristic other than $q$. Take for
   $\lambda'$ the place above $\ell$ induced by an embedding of $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ into $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell$ that
   induces $\lambda$ on $K_f$, and let $L \subset \overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell$ be a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$
   containing $E_{\lambda'}$ and $K_{f,\lambda}$.
3. For $q \nmid \ell N$, [Carayol's Hecke correspondence](carayol-s-hecke-correspondence.md) gives
   $\sigma(\pi_q) = \xi_1^{-1}\omega_q^{-1/2} \oplus \xi_2^{-1}\omega_q^{-1/2}$, so $\sigma^{\lambda'}$ is unramified at $q$ and a
   geometric Frobenius element acts on it with eigenvalues $q/\alpha_q$ and $q/\beta_q$; an arithmetic Frobenius
   element acts with eigenvalues $\alpha_q/q$ and $\beta_q/q$, as on $\rho \otimes \epsilon^{-1}$, where $\epsilon$ is the
   [$\ell$-adic cyclotomic character](cyclotomic-character-and-tate-twists.md), which is unramified at $q$ and takes
   the value $q$ on $\mathrm{Frob}_q$. So $\sigma^{\lambda'} \otimes L$ and $(\rho \otimes \epsilon^{-1}) \otimes L$ have the
   same Frobenius traces at all primes $q \nmid \ell N$. The second is irreducible by step 1, hence semisimple,
   so the semisimplification of the first is
   [isomorphic to the second](deligne-serre-theorem-on-weight-one-forms/semisimple-galois-representations-with-equal-frobenius-polynomials.md) and irreducible; hence
   $\sigma^{\lambda'} \otimes L$ is irreducible and $\sigma^{\lambda'} \otimes L \cong (\rho \otimes \epsilon^{-1}) \otimes L$.
4. Since $\epsilon$ is trivial on $I_p$, the representations $\rho$ and $\rho \otimes \epsilon^{-1}$ agree on the groups
   $I_p^u$, and extension of scalars changes no codimension of a fixed subspace, so $m_p(\rho) = m_p(\sigma^{\lambda'})$.
   By [the comparison of conductors](conductor-through-the-weil-deligne-representation.md), $m_p(\sigma^{\lambda'})$ is
   the conductor of the Weil–Deligne representation of $\sigma^{\lambda'}|_{G_p}$. That representation is determined
   by $\sigma^{\lambda'}|_{W_p} \cong \sigma^{\lambda'}(\pi_p)$, the $\lambda'$-adic realization of a model of $\sigma(\pi_p)$
   over $E$, so it is that model with scalars extended to $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell$, by the uniqueness of the
   [Weil–Deligne representation of an ℓ-adic representation](weil-deligne-representations/weil-deligne-representation-of-an-l-adic-representation.md);
   its [conductor](conductor-of-a-weil-deligne-representation.md) is $a(\sigma(\pi_p))$.
5. Finally $a(\sigma(\pi_p))$ is the exponent $f(\pi_p)$ of the $\varepsilon$-factor of $\pi_p$ (expansion item 2);
   by [Casselman's theorem](local-newforms-for-gl-2.md) that exponent is the exponent of $\mathfrak{c}(\pi_p)$, which is
   $v_p(N)$ by step 2. So $m_p(\rho) = v_p(N)$.

## Expansion

This node is still to be broken down: steps 2 and 5 use two results that no page states yet, for want of a source
that states them in the form used. A good expansion states and links:

1. that the automorphic representation $\pi$ of step 2 is as in Carayol's (0.3) for $F = \mathbb{Q}$, $k_1 = 2$ and
   $w = 0$: cuspidal, with $\pi_\infty \cong D_{2,0}$, the essentially square-integrable representation of
   $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{R})$ that occurs in the unitarily induced representation $\mathrm{Ind}(\mu, \nu)$ of
   $\mu(t) = \lvert t \rvert^{1/2}$ and $\nu(t) = \lvert t \rvert^{-1/2}$ (Carayol, §0.2, p. 409). Carayol says that the
   representations with these archimedean components correspond to holomorphic Hilbert modular forms of the given
   weight (§0.3, p. 409, citing [Oh]); Loeffler and Weinstein place $\varphi_f$ in their space $\mathcal{A}_0(\omega)$ (§2.1,
   p. 1181) and describe $\pi_{f,\infty}$ through a representation of $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{R})$ induced from the Borel subgroup
   (§1.1, p. 1179, citing Gelbart, Proposition 5.21; not read here);
2. the conductor of the Weil–Deligne representation that Carayol's Hecke correspondence attaches to an irreducible
   admissible infinite-dimensional representation $\pi$ of $\mathrm{GL}_2(F)$, $F$ a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$:
   $a(\sigma(\pi)) = f(\pi)$, where $\varepsilon(\pi, \psi, s) = \varepsilon(\pi, \psi, 0)\, q^{-s(f(\pi) + 2c(\psi))}$ and $c(\psi)$ is the
   largest integer $r$ such that $\psi$ is trivial on $\mathfrak{p}_F^{-r}$; or the same for $\mathrm{rec}_2(\pi)$, from which Carayol's
   relation $\sigma(\pi) \cong (\mathrm{rec}_2(\pi) \otimes \omega^{1/2})^\vee$ passes to $\sigma(\pi)$. The sources read give it only in
   part. Carayol gives the identities $\varepsilon(\chi.\check\sigma(\pi_\mathfrak{p})) = \varepsilon(\chi.\omega_\mathfrak{p}^{1/2}.\pi_\mathfrak{p})$ (§0.5,
   p. 410). Wedhorn concludes that $\mathrm{rec}_n$ preserves conductors on supercuspidal representations ((4.2.5), from
   (2.5.4) and (3.2.2), which defines the conductor of an irreducible Weil–Deligne representation). Bushnell, Henniart
   and Kutzko prove $f(\pi) = f(\sigma^F_n(\pi))$ for supercuspidal $\pi$ (Corollary to Theorem 4.1, p. 549), and the
   equality of the exponents of pairs for all irreducible representations through the extended correspondences of
   their §3.9 (Remark 2, p. 549), for maps $\sigma^F_n$ with the properties of their §3.1, which they check for
   those of Harris (§6).
