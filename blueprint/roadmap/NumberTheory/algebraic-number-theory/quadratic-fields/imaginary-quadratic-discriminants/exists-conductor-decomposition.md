# Every imaginary quadratic discriminant has a conductor decomposition

Every imaginary
quadratic discriminant $D$, that is, every integer $D < 0$ with $D \equiv 0$ or
$1 \pmod 4$, has a conductor decomposition: there are an integer $f \ge 1$ and a fundamental
imaginary quadratic discriminant $D_K$ with $D = f^2 D_K$.

Fowler takes $D_K$ to be the discriminant of $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D})$.

## Sources

- Guy Fowler, *Some uniform effective results on André–Oort for sums of powers in* $\mathbb{C}^n$, Math. Proc. Cambridge Philos. Soc. 180 (2026), 607–641, proof of Proposition 2.7, p. 615, https://doi.org/10.1017/S0305004125101825 ("Write $\Delta = f^2 D$, where $D$ is the discriminant of $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{\Delta})$").
