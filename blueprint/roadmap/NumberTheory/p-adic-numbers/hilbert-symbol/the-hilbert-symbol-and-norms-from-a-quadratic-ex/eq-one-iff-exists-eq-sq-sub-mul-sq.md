# The norm form criterion for the Hilbert symbol

Let $F$ be a field in which $2 \neq 0$ and
let $a, b \in F$ be nonzero. Then the Hilbert symbol $(a, b)$ equals $1$ if and only if
$a = z^2 - b y^2$ for some $y, z \in F$. When $b$ is not a square, $z^2 - b y^2$ is the norm of
$z + y \sqrt b$ from $F(\sqrt b)$ (J.-P. Serre, *A Course in Arithmetic*, Chapter III, §1.1,
Proposition 1 and its proof, p. 19; Serre takes $F = \mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{Q}_p$).
