# The norm criterion for the Hilbert symbol

Let $F$ be a field in which $2 \neq 0$, let
$a, b \in F$ be nonzero, and let $\beta$ be an element with $\beta^2 = b$ of a field extension $L$
of $F$, so that $F(\beta) \subseteq L$ is the field $F(\sqrt b)$. Then the Hilbert symbol $(a, b)$
equals $1$ if and only if $a$ is the norm $N_{F(\beta)/F}(\xi)$ of a nonzero element $\xi$ of
$F(\beta)$. If $b$ is a square in $F$, then $F(\beta) = F$, every $a$ is such a norm, and
$(a, b) = 1$ for every $a$ (J.-P. Serre, *A Course in Arithmetic*, Chapter III, §1.1,
Proposition 1, p. 19; Serre takes $F = \mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{Q}_p$).
