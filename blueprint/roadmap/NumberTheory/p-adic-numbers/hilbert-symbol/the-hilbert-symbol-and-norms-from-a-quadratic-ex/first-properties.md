# First properties of the Hilbert symbol

Let $F$ be a field in which $2 \neq 0$ and let
$a, a', b, c \in F$ be nonzero. Then the Hilbert symbol satisfies:

1. $(a, b) = (b, a)$ and $(a, c^2) = 1$;
2. $(a, -a) = 1$, and $(a, 1 - a) = 1$ if $a \neq 1$;
3. if $(a, b) = 1$, then $(a a', b) = (a', b)$;
4. $(a, b) = (a, -a b)$, and $(a, b) = (a, (1 - a) b)$ if $a \neq 1$.

(J.-P. Serre, *A Course in Arithmetic*, Chapter III, §1.1, Proposition 2, p. 19, with its proof
on pp. 19–20; Serre takes $F = \mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{Q}_p$.)
