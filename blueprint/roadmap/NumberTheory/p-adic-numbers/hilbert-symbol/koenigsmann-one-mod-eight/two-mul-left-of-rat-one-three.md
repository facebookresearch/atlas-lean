# Koenigsmann: $(2p, q)_2 = -1$ and $(-2p, q)_2 = 1$

Let $p, q$ be rational numbers with
$p \equiv 1$ and $q \equiv 3 \pmod{8\mathbb{Z}_{(2)}}$, that is, $p = 1 + 8x$ and $q = 3 + 8y$ for
rationals $x, y$ with odd denominators. Then the $2$-adic Hilbert symbols of the images in
$\mathbb{Q}_2$ are $(2p, q)_2 = -1$ and $(-2p, q)_2 = 1$. Here `a`, `a'` and `b` are the units of
$\mathbb{Q}_2$ with values $2p$, $-2p$ and $q$.

The source takes $p, q \in \mathbb{Q}^\times$; the congruences already force $p, q \neq 0$, so that
hypothesis is not stated.

## Sources

- J. Koenigsmann, "Defining $\mathbb{Z}$ in $\mathbb{Q}$", Ann. of Math. 183 (2016), 73–93, proof of Proposition 16(a), p. 85: "Since $p \equiv 1 \pmod{8\mathbb{Z}_{(2)}}$ and $q \equiv 3 \pmod{8\mathbb{Z}_{(2)}}$, we have, by Observation 5, $(2p,q)_2 = -1$"; the value $(-2p, q)_2 = 1$ gives $2 \notin \mathbb{P}(p, q) = \Delta_{-2p,q} \cap \Delta_{2p,q}$ (Definition 9(c), p. 80), as Proposition 16(b) uses with Lemma 14 (p. 83).
