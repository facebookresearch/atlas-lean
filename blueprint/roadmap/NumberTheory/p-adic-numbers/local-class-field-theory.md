# Local class field theory: the local Artin map

Let $K$ be a non-archimedean local field of characteristic $0$, that is, a finite extension of
$\mathbb{Q}_p$ for a prime $p$, with ring of integers $\mathcal{O}_K$ and residue field $k$ of $q$
elements. Fix a separable closure $K^{\mathrm{sep}}$ of $K$, and let $K^{\mathrm{ab}}$ and $K^{\mathrm{ur}}$
be the maximal abelian and the maximal unramified extensions of $K$ in it. A finite unramified extension
$L/K$ is Galois with cyclic Galois group generated by its Frobenius element $\mathrm{Frob}_{L/K}$, the
unique $\sigma \in \mathrm{Gal}(L/K)$ with $\sigma(x) \equiv x^q$ modulo the maximal ideal of
$\mathcal{O}_L$ for all $x \in \mathcal{O}_L$. Then there is a unique homomorphism

$$\mathrm{rec}_K \colon K^\times \to \mathrm{Gal}(K^{\mathrm{ab}}/K)$$

with the following two properties.

- **Normalization.** For every uniformizer $\pi$ of $K$ and every finite unramified extension $L/K$,
  $\mathrm{rec}_K(\pi)$ acts on $L$ as $\mathrm{Frob}_{L/K}$.
- **Reciprocity.** For every finite abelian extension $L/K$ in $K^{\mathrm{sep}}$, the norm group
  $\mathrm{Nm}_{L/K}(L^\times)$ is contained in the kernel of $a \mapsto \mathrm{rec}_K(a)|_L$, and the
  induced homomorphism

    $$\mathrm{rec}_{L/K} \colon K^\times / \mathrm{Nm}_{L/K}(L^\times) \to \mathrm{Gal}(L/K)$$

    is an isomorphism. In particular $(K^\times : \mathrm{Nm}_{L/K}(L^\times)) = [L : K]$.

The map $\mathrm{rec}_K$ is the local Artin map, or local reciprocity map, of $K$, and $\mathrm{rec}_{L/K}$
the local Artin map of $L/K$. By the normalization, $\mathrm{rec}_K(\pi)$ acts on $K^{\mathrm{ur}}$ as its
arithmetic Frobenius automorphism, which induces $x \mapsto x^q$ on the residue field; and every unit
$u \in \mathcal{O}_K^\times$ acts trivially on $K^{\mathrm{ur}}$, since $u\pi$ and $\pi$ are both
uniformizers. Since each norm group $\mathrm{Nm}_{L/K}(L^\times)$ has finite index, it is open in
$K^\times$, so $\mathrm{rec}_K$ is continuous.

This is the arithmetic normalization. The reciprocal map $a \mapsto \mathrm{rec}_K(a)^{-1}$ sends a
uniformizer to the inverse of the arithmetic Frobenius, a geometric Frobenius element, on
$K^{\mathrm{ur}}$; it is the Artin map $\mathrm{Art}_K$ of the local Langlands literature, for instance of
Wedhorn's notes, which characterize $\mathrm{Art}_K$ by this property of uniformizers and by its
triviality on norms.

Milne and Sutherland state the theorem for every non-archimedean local field, including the finite
extensions of $\mathbb{F}_p((T))$; the global theory of number fields uses only the case above.

- [Residue field of a local ring](../../RingTheory/local-rings/local-rings-and-residue-fields/residue-field-local-ring.md), for the residue field $k$ of $\mathcal{O}_K$
- [Partial residue-field equivalence for complete finite DVR extensions](../../RingTheory/ramification/unramified-extensions-and-residue-field-extensio/residue-field-partial-equivalence-packag.md), for a finite unramified extension $L/K$ being Galois: $\mathcal{O}_L$ is a complete discrete valuation ring, finite over $\mathcal{O}_K$ of rank $[L : K]$, which equals the residue degree as $L/K$ is unramified, and finite residue fields are separable; its item (i) with $B_1 = B_2 = \mathcal{O}_L$ lifts each of the $[L : K]$ automorphisms of the cyclic residue field extension to an $\mathcal{O}_K$-algebra endomorphism of $\mathcal{O}_L$, an automorphism since the inverse lifts too, which extends to $L$; so $L/K$ has $[L : K]$ automorphisms
- [Existence and uniqueness of the Frobenius element under trivial inertia](../../RingTheory/ramification/frobenius-elements/frobenius-elements-under-trivial-inertia/exists-unique-of-inertia-eq-bot.md), for the Frobenius element $\mathrm{Frob}_{L/K}$ of a finite unramified extension, whose inertia group is trivial
- [The Frobenius element generates the decomposition group at an unramified prime](../../RingTheory/ramification/frobenius-elements/frobenius-elements-and-complete-splitting/arith-frob-at-generates-stabilizer-of-is.md), for $\mathrm{Gal}(L/K)$ being generated by $\mathrm{Frob}_{L/K}$: the maximal ideal of $\mathcal{O}_L$ is its only nonzero prime, so its decomposition group is the whole Galois group
