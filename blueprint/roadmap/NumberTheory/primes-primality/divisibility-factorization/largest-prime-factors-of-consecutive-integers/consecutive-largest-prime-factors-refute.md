# Largest prime factors of n and n + 1 cannot be prescribed

It is not the case that for any two distinct primes $p$ and $q$ there is a natural number $n$ with $P(n) = p$ and $P(n + 1) = q$, where $P(m)$ denotes the largest prime factor of $m$ (and $0$ if $m$ has none). For instance no $n$ has $P(n) = 2$ and $P(n+1) = 7$.

This refutes [Erdős Problem 649 (prescribed largest prime factors of n and n + 1)](consecutive-largest-prime-factors.md), the statement recorded as a proposition; the refutation, recorded in Erdős Problems, Problem #649, is the obstruction $2^k \not\equiv -1 \pmod 7$.
