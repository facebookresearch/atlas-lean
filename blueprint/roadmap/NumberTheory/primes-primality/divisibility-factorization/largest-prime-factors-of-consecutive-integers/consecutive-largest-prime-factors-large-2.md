# Largest prime factors of n and n + 1 cannot be prescribed even for large primes

It is not the case that there is $N$ such that for any two distinct primes $p, q \ge N$ there is a natural number $n$ with $P(n) = p$ and $P(n + 1) = q$, where $P(m)$ denotes the largest prime factor of $m$ (and $0$ if $m$ has none).

This refutes [Erdős Problem 649 for large primes](consecutive-largest-prime-factors-large.md), the statement recorded as a proposition; the refutation, recorded in Erdős Problems, Problem #649, is A. Tong's quadratic-reciprocity argument.
