# Fourier integral for the Riemann ξ function

For every complex number $z$,

$$
8 H_0(z) = \xi\Bigl(\frac12 + \frac{iz}{2}\Bigr),
$$

where $H_0(z) = \int_0^\infty \Phi(u) \cos(zu)\,du$ is the de Bruijn–Newman deformation at time
$0$, $\Phi$ is Newman's kernel, and $\xi(s) = \tfrac12 s (s - 1) \pi^{-s/2} \Gamma(s/2) \zeta(s)$ is
the Riemann $\xi$ function, extended to $s = 0$ and $s = 1$ by its value $1/2$ there.

## Sources

- B. Rodgers and T. Tao, *The de Bruijn–Newman constant is non-negative*, Forum Math. Pi 8 (2020), e6, arXiv:1801.05914v3, §1: equation (1) defines $H_0(z) = \tfrac18 \xi(1/2 + iz/2)$ with $\xi$ as in (2), and the next sentence gives the Fourier representation $H_0(z) = \int_0^\infty \Phi(u) \cos(zu)\,du$ with $\Phi$ as in (3) as a classical fact, citing E. C. Titchmarsh, *The theory of the Riemann zeta-function*, 2nd ed., p. 255.
