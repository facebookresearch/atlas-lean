# Classical zero-free region for the Riemann zeta function

There is a constant $c > 0$ such that $\zeta(s) \ne 0$ for every complex number $s \ne 1$ with

$$
\operatorname{Re} s > 1 - \frac{c}{\log(2 + |\operatorname{Im} s|)}.
$$

The point $s = 1$, where $\zeta$ has its pole, is excluded.

## Sources

- T. Tao, *254A, Notes 2: Complex-analytic multiplicative number theory* (2014), Proposition 38, <https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2014/12/09/254a-notes-2-complex-analytic-multiplicative-number-theory/>. Its proof combines the inequality $3 + 4 \cos \theta + \cos 2\theta \ge 0$ with the approximate formula for $\zeta'/\zeta$ near a zero (Proposition 19 there), which rests on Jensen's formula and the Borel–Carathéodory theorem.
