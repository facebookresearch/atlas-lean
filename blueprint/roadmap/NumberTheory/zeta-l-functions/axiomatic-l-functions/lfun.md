# L-function of an L-datum

For an L-datum and $s \in \mathbb{C}$, $L(s) = \prod_i E_i(N_i^{-s})^{-1}$ (an unconditional product, $1$ when it does not converge), with norms $N_i$ and Euler polynomials $E_i$.

Inversion is in $\mathbb{C}$ with $0^{-1} = 0$: a factor whose Euler polynomial value is $0$ contributes $0$, not a pole. This is separate from the convention for the product itself: for a single index with the zero Euler polynomial the product is the finite product $0$, and the fallback value $1$ for a nonconvergent product does not apply. The norms are natural numbers and $n^{-s}$ is the complex power, so for $n = 0$ it is $0^0 = 1$ when $s = 0$ and $0^{-s} = 0$ otherwise, unlike exponentiation of a positive norm.
