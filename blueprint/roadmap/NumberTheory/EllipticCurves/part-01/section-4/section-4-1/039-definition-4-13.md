# Definition 4.13

Let $C_{1}$ and $C_{2}$ be plane projective curves over $k$ defined by
 $f_{1}(x, y, z)=0$ and $f_{2}(x, y, z)=0$, respectively. A rational map
 $\psi: C_{1} \rightarrow C_{2}$ is an equivalence class of triples
 $(\psi_{x}: \psi_{y}: \psi_{z})$ of homogeneous polynomials in $k[x, y, z]$
 of the same degree, not all of which lie in $(f_{1})$, such that
 $f_{2}(\psi_{x}, \psi_{y}, \psi_{z}) \in (f_{1})$. Triples $(\psi_{x}:
 \psi_{y}: \psi_{z})$ and $(\psi_{x}^{\prime}: \psi_{y}^{\prime}:
 \psi_{z}^{\prime})$ are equivalent whenever $\psi_{x}
 \psi_{y}^{\prime}-\psi_{x}^{\prime} \psi_{y}$ and $\psi_{x}
 \psi_{z}^{\prime}-\psi_{x}^{\prime} \psi_{z}$ and $\psi_{y}
 \psi_{z}^{\prime}-\psi_{y}^{\prime} \psi_{z}$ all lie in $(f_{1})$. The
 rational map $\phi$ is defined at $P \in C_{1}(\bar{k})$ if any of
 $\psi_{x}(P), \psi_{y}(P), \psi_{z}(P)$ is nonzero, in which case
 $(\psi_{x}(P): \psi_{y}(P): \psi_{z}(P) \in C_{2}(\bar{k}))$.
