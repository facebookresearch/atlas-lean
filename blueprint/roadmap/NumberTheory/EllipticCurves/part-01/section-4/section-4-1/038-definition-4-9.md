# Definition 4.9

Let $C_{1}$ and $C_{2}$ be plane projective curves defined over $k$. A
 rational map $\phi: C_{1} \rightarrow C_{2}$ is a projective triple
 $(\phi_{x}: \phi_{y}: \phi_{z}) \in \mathbb{P}^{2}(k(C_{1}))$, such that for
 every $P \in C_{1}(\bar{k})$ where $\phi_{x}, \phi_{y}, \phi_{z}$ are
 defined and not all zero, the projective point $(\phi_{x}(P): \phi_{y}(P):
 \phi_{z}(P))$ lies in $C_{2}(\bar{k})$. The map $\phi$ is defined (or
 regular) at $P$ if there exists $\lambda \in k(C_{1})^{\times}$ such that
 $\lambda \phi_{x}, \lambda \phi_{y}, \lambda \phi_{z}$ are all defined at
 $P$ and not all zero at $P$.
