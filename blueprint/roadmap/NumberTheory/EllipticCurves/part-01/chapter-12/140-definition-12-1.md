# Definition 12.1

An anti-homomorphism $\varphi: R \rightarrow S$ of rings is a homomorphism of
 their additive groups that satisfies $\varphi(1_R) = 1_S$ and
 $\varphi(\alpha\beta) = \varphi(\beta)\varphi(\alpha)$ for all $\alpha,
 \beta \in R$. An involution (or anti-involution) is an anti-homomorphism
 $\varphi: R \rightarrow R$ that is its own inverse: $\varphi \circ \varphi$
 is the identity map.
