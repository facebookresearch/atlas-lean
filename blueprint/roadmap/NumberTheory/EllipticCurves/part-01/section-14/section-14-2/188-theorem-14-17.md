# Theorem 14.17

Let $\gamma$ be a simple closed curve with positive orientation, let $f(z)$
 be a function that is meromorphic on an open set $\Omega$ containing
 $\gamma$ and its interior $\Gamma$, with no zeros or poles on $\gamma$, and
 let $g(z)$ be a nonzero function that is holomorphic on $\Omega$.
 $$\frac{1}{2\pi i} \int_{\gamma} g(z) \frac{f'(z)}{f(z)} dz=\sum_{w \in
 \Gamma} g(w) \operatorname{ord}_{w}(f)$$ When $g(z)=1$, the RHS is the
 difference between the number of zeros and poles that $f(z)$ has in $\Gamma$
 (counted with multiplicity), which is the usual argument principle.
