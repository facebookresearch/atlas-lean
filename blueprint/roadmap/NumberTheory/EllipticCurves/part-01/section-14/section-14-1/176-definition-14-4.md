# Definition 14.4

A function $f: \Omega \rightarrow \mathbb{C}$ defined on an open neighborhood
 $\Omega$ of a point $z_{0} \in \mathbb{C}$ is said to be holomorphic at
 $z_{0}$ if the derivative $f^{\prime}\left(z_{0}\right)\coloneqq\lim_{z
 \rightarrow z_{0}} \frac{f(z)-f\left(z_{0}\right)}{z-z_{0}}$ exists. We say
 that $f$ is holomorphic on an open set $\Omega$ if it is holomorphic at
 every $z_{0} \in \Omega$. Functions that are holomorphic on all of
 $\mathbb{C}$ are simply said to be holomorphic or entire.
