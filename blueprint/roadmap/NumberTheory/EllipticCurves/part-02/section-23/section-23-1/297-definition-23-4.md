# Definition 23.4

A discrete valuation on a field $F$ is a surjective homomorphism $v:
 F^{\times} \rightarrow \mathbb{Z}$ that satisfies the inequality $v(x+y)
 \geq \min(v(x), v(y))$ for all $x, y \in F^{\times}$. If $v$ is a discrete
 valuation on $F$, then the subring $R\coloneqq\{x \in F \mid v(x) \geq 0\}$
 is a PID with the unique maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}\coloneqq\{x \in R \mid
 v(x) \geq 1\}$. Every nonzero ideal $(x)$ of $R$ is then of the form
 $\mathfrak{m}^{n}$, where $n=v(x)$. Any $u \in F$ for which $v(u)=1$
 generates $\mathfrak{m}$ and is called a uniformizer for $\mathfrak{m}$.
