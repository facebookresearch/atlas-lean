# {−1, m}-perfect numbers of the form 2^k p

Let $m \ge 1$, $p$ an odd prime, and $\beta = \nu_2(m + 1)$ is the exponent of $2$ in $m + 1$. (1) If $k, \alpha \ge 1$ and both $2^k p$ and $2^{k+\alpha} p$ are $\{-1, m\}$-perfect, then $2^\alpha \equiv 1 \pmod{(m + 1)/2^\beta}$. (2) If $k \ge \beta$, $2^k p$ is $\{-1, m\}$-perfect and $2^\alpha \equiv 1 \pmod{(m + 1)/2^\beta}$, then $2^{k+\alpha} p$ is $\{-1, m\}$-perfect. Here for a set $S$ of integers, an integer $n$ with $|n| > 1$ is $S$-perfect when $n = 1 + \sum_d \lambda_d d$, the sum over the divisors $d$ of $|n|$ with $1 < d < |n|$, for some weights $\lambda_d \in S$.
