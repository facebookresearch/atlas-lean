# Construction of {−1, m}-perfect numbers of the form 2^k p

Let $m \ge 1$, $\beta = \nu_2(m + 1)$ is the exponent of $2$ in $m + 1$, and $\alpha > \beta$ with $2^\alpha \equiv 1 \pmod{(m + 1)/2^\beta}$. Then for every prime $p \equiv 2(2^{\alpha+1} - 1) - 1 \pmod{2(m + 1)}$ there is $k \ge \alpha$ with $2^k p$ $\{-1, m\}$-perfect. Here for a set $S$ of integers, an integer $n$ with $|n| > 1$ is $S$-perfect when $n = 1 + \sum_d \lambda_d d$, the sum over the divisors $d$ of $|n|$ with $1 < d < |n|$, for some weights $\lambda_d \in S$.
