# The comma map between danger intervals

For natural numbers $b, d, u, k, u'$, let $T = (d+1)b^k$ if $d < b - 1$ and $T = b^{k+1}$ otherwise; the comma map sends $(d, u, k)$ to $u'$ when $0 < u' \le b^2$, the base-$b$ comma sequence from $d b^k - u$ reaches $T - u'$ after zero or more comma successor steps, and $T - u'$ either has no comma successor in base $b$ or has only comma successors at least $T$. Both subtractions $d b^k - u$ and $T - u'$ are truncated natural subtractions, with value $0$ when the subtrahend is larger: for $(b, d, u, k) = (2, 1, 2, 0)$ the sequence starts at $0$, not at $-1$.
