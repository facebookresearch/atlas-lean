# Erdős Problem 1189 (divisors forming irreducible covering sets)

The statement that there are infinitely many natural numbers $n$ such that the divisors of $n$ greater than $1$ form an irreducible covering set: they form a covering set, and no proper subset does; here a finite set $S$ of integers greater than $1$ is a covering set when residues $a_n$ ($n \in S$) can be chosen so that every integer satisfies $x \equiv a_n \pmod n$ for at least one $n \in S$.

This page records the statement as a proposition. Its resolution: [Infinitely many integers whose divisors form an irreducible covering set](divisors-irreducible-covering-infinitely-2.md).
