# Almost covering systems need moduli spread over a long range

It is not the case that there is a real $C > 1$ such that for every real $\varepsilon > 0$ and every integer $N \ge 1$ there are a finite set $S$ of distinct moduli, all in $[N, CN]$, and residues $a_n$ ($n \in S$) such that the integers satisfying none of the congruences $x \equiv a_n \pmod n$ have density at most $\varepsilon$ (the density of this periodic set being its proportion in one period $\prod_{n \in S} n$).

This refutes [Erdős Problem 27 (almost covering systems with moduli in a short range)](almost-covering-in-short-range.md), the statement recorded as a proposition; the refutation is due to Filaseta, Ford, Konyagin, Pomerance and Yu (2007), cited under Sources.
