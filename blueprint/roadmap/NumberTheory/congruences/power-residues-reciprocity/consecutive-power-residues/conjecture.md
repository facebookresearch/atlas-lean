# Erdős Problem 436, first question (consecutive power residues)

The statement that for every $k \ge 2$ there are natural numbers $B$ and $N$ such that for every prime $p \ge N$ some $r$ with $1 \le r \le B$ has both $r$ and $r + 1$ being $k$-th power residues modulo $p$, where a natural number $r$ is a $k$-th power residue modulo $p$ when $r \equiv x^k \pmod p$ for some $x \not\equiv 0 \pmod p$.
