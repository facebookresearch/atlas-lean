# An odd prime non-residue (Robinson's Lemma 5)

For every prime `p` with `p ≡ 1 (mod 4)`
there is an odd prime `q` that is a quadratic non-residue modulo `p`, that is, whose Legendre
symbol `(q / p)` is `-1` (`legendreSym p q = -1`).

## Sources

- J. Robinson, "Definability and decision problems in arithmetic", J. Symbolic Logic 14 (1949), 98–114, Lemma 5, p. 108: "If p is a prime ≡ 1 (mod 4), there is an odd prime q such that (q/p) = −1." It supplies the prime `q` with which her Lemma 4 is applied in the proof of her Theorem 3.1(i), p. 109. The proof follows hers: a non-residue `s` modulo `p` with `0 ≤ s < p`, or `s + p`, is odd, and an odd non-residue has an odd prime factor that is a non-residue.
