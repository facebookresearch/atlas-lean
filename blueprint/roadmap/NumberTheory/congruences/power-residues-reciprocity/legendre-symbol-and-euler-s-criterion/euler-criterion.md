# Euler's criterion

For an odd prime $p$ and an integer $a$ not divisible by $p$, $a^{(p-1)/2}$ is congruent modulo $p$ to the Legendre symbol $\left(\frac{a}{p}\right)$. Mathlib proves this more generally, for every prime $p$, including $p = 2$, and every integer $a$, including multiples of $p$, as an equality in the ring $\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$: the residue modulo $p$ of the integer $\left(\frac{a}{p}\right)$ equals the residue of $a$ raised to the power $\lfloor p/2 \rfloor$. Here $\left(\frac{a}{p}\right)$ is Mathlib's Legendre symbol, defined for every prime $p$ as the integer that is $0$ if $p \mid a$, $1$ if $a$ is a nonzero square modulo $p$ and $-1$ otherwise; the exponent $\lfloor p/2 \rfloor$ is the natural-number quotient of $p$ by $2$, which equals $(p-1)/2$ for odd $p$ and equals $1$ for $p = 2$. When $p \mid a$, both sides are $0$.
