# Harmonic numbers of order two modulo $m$

For natural numbers $m$ and $k$, the
harmonic number $H_{k,2}$ of order two modulo $m$ is the residue $\sum_{i=1}^{k} (i^2)^{-1}$
in $\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}$, where $(i^2)^{-1}$ is the inverse of $i^2$ modulo $m$ when $i$ is coprime to $m$.
Here $x^{-1}$ denotes the following total inverse on $\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}$. For $m \ge 1$, let
$\bar x \in \{0, \dots, m - 1\}$ represent $x$; then $x^{-1}$ is the residue of the Bézout
coefficient $a$, in $a \bar x + b m = \gcd(\bar x, m)$, computed by the extended Euclidean
algorithm, so that $x x^{-1} = \gcd(\bar x, m)$ and $x^{-1}$ is the usual inverse when $x$ is a
unit. For $m = 0$, where $\mathbb{Z}/0\mathbb{Z} = \mathbb{Z}$, $x^{-1}$ is the sign of $x$. In particular $H_{k,2}$ modulo $0$ is $k$.
