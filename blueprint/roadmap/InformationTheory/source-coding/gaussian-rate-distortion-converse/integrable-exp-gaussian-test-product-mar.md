# Integrable exp gaussian Test product Marginal of mem distortion Feasible

Let $D > 0$, $\sigma^2 \ge 0$, and let $\mu$ be a probability measure on $\mathbb{R}^2$ that is distortion-feasible at level $D$ for the source variance $\sigma^2$. Then $(x, y) \mapsto \exp\left(t_{D, \sigma^2}(x, y)\right)$ is integrable with respect to $\mu_1 \otimes \mu_2$.

A probability measure $\mu$ on $\mathbb{R}^2$ is distortion-feasible at level $D$ for the source variance $\sigma^2$ if $D \ge 0$, the first marginal of $\mu$ is $\mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)$, and $\int (x - y)^2 \, d\mu(x, y) \le D$, the integral of a nonnegative function, possibly infinite. $\mu_1 \otimes \mu_2$ denotes the product of the marginals of $\mu$. Here $t_{D, \sigma^2}(x, y) = \ln \varphi_D(x - y) - \ln \varphi_{\sigma^2}(x)$ is the Gaussian test function, $\varphi_v$ being the Gaussian density for $v > 0$, extended by the zero function at $v = 0$, with $\ln 0 = 0$.
