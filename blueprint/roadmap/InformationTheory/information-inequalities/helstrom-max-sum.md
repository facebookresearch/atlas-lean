# Total variation: data processing and the classical Helstrom identity

Let $\alpha$ be a finite set and $p, q$ probability distributions on $\alpha$. Then

$$\sum_{a \in \alpha} \max\left(p(a), q(a)\right) = 1 + d_{\mathrm{TV}}(p, q), \qquad d_{\mathrm{TV}}(p, q) = \frac{1}{2} \sum_{a \in \alpha} \lvert p(a) - q(a) \rvert.$$

For two equally likely hypotheses $p$ and $q$, the optimal probability of guessing correctly from one observation, which is half this sum, is therefore $\frac{1}{2}\left(1 + d_{\mathrm{TV}}(p, q)\right)$.
