# Existence of the I-projection on a closed set

If $Q > 0$ on a finite alphabet and $\Gamma$ is a nonempty closed set of distributions, then
$D(\cdot\|Q)$ attains its minimum on $\Gamma$: $\Gamma$ is compact and $P \mapsto \sum_a P(a)\log(P(a)/Q(a))$
is continuous. Convexity is not needed for existence on a finite alphabet; Csiszár's 1975 Theorem 2.1
(arbitrary spaces, variation-closed convex sets) uses it, and convexity gives uniqueness through
strict convexity.

## Sources

- I. Csiszár, "I-divergence geometry of probability distributions and minimization problems", *Ann. Probab.* 3(1) (1975), 146–158, Theorem 2.1.
- I. Csiszár and P. C. Shields, *Information Theory and Statistics: A Tutorial*, Foundations and Trends in Communications and Information Theory 1(4) (2004), 417–528, Section 3 (existence and uniqueness for closed convex $\Pi$ and $Q > 0$).
