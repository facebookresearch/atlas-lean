# Pythagorean identity at an algebraic inner point

Under the hypotheses of the [Pythagorean inequality](pythagorean-inequality-for-i-projections.md),
suppose that $P^*$ is an algebraic inner point of $\Gamma$: for every $P \in \Gamma$ there is
$\varepsilon > 0$ with $P^* + \varepsilon(P^* - P) \in \Gamma$. Then

$$D(P\|Q) = D(P\|P^*) + D(P^*\|Q) \qquad \text{for every } P \in \Gamma.$$

Applying the first-order condition to $P$ and to $P^* + \varepsilon(P^* - P)$ gives both signs of the
first-order term. This is the last assertion of Csiszár's 1975 Theorem 2.2, restricted to finite
alphabets and $Q > 0$; the source also concludes that every $P \in \Gamma$ has finite divergence
from $Q$, which is automatic here.

## Sources

- I. Csiszár, "I-divergence geometry of probability distributions and minimization problems", *Ann. Probab.* 3(1) (1975), 146–158, Theorem 2.2 (last assertion) and the definition of algebraic inner point following it.
