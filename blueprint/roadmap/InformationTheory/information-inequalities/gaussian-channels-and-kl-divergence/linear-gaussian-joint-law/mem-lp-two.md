# Finite second moment from a power bound

Let $(\Omega, \mu)$ be a measure space, $E$ a normed abelian group, and $X : \Omega \to E$ an almost everywhere strongly measurable map satisfying the power constraint: $P \ge 0$ and $\int^{-} \lVert X \rVert^2 \, d\mu \le P$. Then $X \in L^2(\mu)$, that is, $X$ has a finite second moment. Equivalently, the instantaneous power $\omega \mapsto \lVert X(\omega) \rVert^2$ is integrable with respect to $\mu$.

For example, for $E = \mathbb{R}$ the power constraint is the scalar power bound $P \ge 0$ and $\int^{-} X^2 \, d\mu \le P$, and a real input satisfying it lies in $L^2(\mu)$; so does its embedding $\omega \mapsto (X(\omega))$ into one-dimensional Euclidean space $\mathbb{R}^1$, which has the same norm.
