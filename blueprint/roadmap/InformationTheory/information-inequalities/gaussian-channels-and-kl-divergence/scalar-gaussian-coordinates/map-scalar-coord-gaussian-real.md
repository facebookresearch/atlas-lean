# Gaussian law

Let $e : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}^1$, $e(x) = (x)$, be the scalar coordinate embedding into the one-dimensional Euclidean space, a linear isometry with inverse $e^{-1}(y) = y_0$. For $m \in \mathbb{R}$ and $s \ge 0$, the image of the real Gaussian $\mathcal{N}(m, s)$ under $e$ is the one-dimensional multivariate Gaussian with mean $(m)$ and covariance $[s]$:

$$e_\ast\,\mathcal{N}(m, s) = \mathcal{N}\big((m), [s]\big).$$

Equivalently, the inverse $e^{-1}$ is measurable and measure-preserving from $\mathcal{N}\big((m), [s]\big)$ to $\mathcal{N}(m, s)$: $(e^{-1})_\ast\,\mathcal{N}\big((m), [s]\big) = \mathcal{N}(m, s)$.
