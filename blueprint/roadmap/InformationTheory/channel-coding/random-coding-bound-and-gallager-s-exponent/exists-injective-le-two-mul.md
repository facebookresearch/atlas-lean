# Selecting the better half of a code

If $2M$ nonnegative numbers $x_1, \dots, x_{2M}$ have sum at most $2M A$ with $A \ge 0$, then at least
$M$ of them are at most $2A$: there is an injection $e$ of $\{1, \dots, M\}$ into
$\{1, \dots, 2M\}$ with $x_{e(m)} \le 2A$ for every $m$. Otherwise at least $M + 1$ of them would
exceed $2A$ and their sum alone would exceed $2MA$.

## Sources

- R. G. Gallager, "A simple derivation of the coding theorem and some applications", IEEE Trans. Inform. Theory IT-11(1):3–18, January 1965, proof of Corollary 2 ("it is impossible for over half the words in the code to have
  an error probability greater than twice the average").
