# Reed–Solomon codec

Let $\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_n$ be distinct points of a field $F$, let $k \le n$, and fix a key-equation solver for radius $t = \lfloor (n - k)/2 \rfloor$ that is sound (every pair it returns solves the key equations) and complete (it returns a pair whenever a solution exists). The codec has as messages the polynomials over $F$ of degree strictly less than $k$ and alphabet $F$: a message $p$ is encoded as $(p(\alpha_1), \dots, p(\alpha_n))$. A word $y$ of length $n$ is decoded with the [Berlekamp–Welch decoder](../reed-solomon-codes/decode.md): the solver is run on $y$, and if it returns a pair $(Q, E)$, the quotient $q$ of $Q$ by $E$ is returned only when **both** $q$ has degree less than $k$ and the Hamming distance from $y$ to $(q(\alpha_1), \dots, q(\alpha_n))$ is at most $t$. Otherwise decoding fails, as it does when the solver returns nothing and on a word of any other length. The distance test matters: over the field with five elements, with $n = 3$, $k = 1$, points $(0, 1, 2)$ and received word $(1, 3, 2)$, the pair $(1, X + 1)$ solves the key equations and has quotient $0$, but the zero word is at distance $3 > 1$, so the decoder rejects.
