# Erdős problem 106: $k^2 + 1$ squares in a unit square

Let $f(n)$ be the largest possible sum of the side lengths of $n$ squares, in any orientation, placed inside the closed unit square $[0,1]^2$ so that no two of them share an interior point. Erdős asked whether $f(k^2 + 1) = k$. Since $f(k^2 + 1) \ge k$ is elementary, the statement considered is the upper bound.

Conjecture: for every integer $k \ge 1$ and all closed squares $Q_1, \dots, Q_{k^2+1} \subseteq [0,1]^2$, where $Q_i$ is the image of $[0, s_i]^2$ with $s_i > 0$ under some rotation followed by some translation, if the interiors of $Q_i$ and $Q_j$ are disjoint whenever $i \ne j$, then

$$\sum_{i=1}^{k^2+1} s_i \le k.$$
