# Cover-decomposable planar sets

A set $P \subseteq \mathbb{R}^2$ is cover-decomposable when there is $k \in \mathbb{N}$ such that every $k$-fold covering of the plane by translates of $P$ splits into two coverings: for every index set $I$ and every family $(t_i)_{i \in I}$ in $\mathbb{R}^2$ such that every point of the plane lies in at least $k$ of the sets $P + t_i$ (counted by index), there is $s \subseteq I$ such that both $\{P + t_i : i \in s\}$ and $\{P + t_i : i \in I \setminus s\}$ cover the plane.
