# Three-coloring against convex homothets fails for disks

This result refutes the proposition stated as [Three-coloring points against homothets of a convex set](conjecture.md). The conjecture is false: it is not the case that for every convex set $D \subseteq \mathbb{R}^2$ there is an integer $m \ge 1$ such that every finite set $S \subset \mathbb{R}^2$ admits a coloring $c : S \to \{0,1,2\}$ for which every positive homothet $a + rD$ ($a \in \mathbb{R}^2$, $r > 0$) containing at least $m$ points of $S$ contains two points of different colors.

A counterexample is given by taking $D$ to be a disk: for every $m$ there is a finite planar point set such that every $3$-coloring leaves a disk containing exactly $m$ of its points, all of one color.

## Sources

- G. Damásdi and D. Pálvölgyi, Realizing an $m$-uniform four-chromatic hypergraph with disks, Combinatorica 42 (2022), 1027–1048. https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.12187
