# Wu's conjecture: no nondegenerate maps omitting $n+2$ hyperplanes

Conjecture (H. Wu, in Chern's Problem IX). Let $n \ge 1$ and let $H_0, \dots, H_{n+1}$ be linear forms on $\mathbb{C}^{n+1}$ in general position: every $n + 1$ of them are linearly independent. Their zero sets are $n + 2$ hyperplanes of $\mathbb{P}^n(\mathbb{C})$. Let $F : \mathbb{C}^n \to \mathbb{C}^{n+1}$ be an entire holomorphic map, viewed as a map $\mathbb{C}^n \to \mathbb{P}^n(\mathbb{C})$ in homogeneous coordinates, that omits all the hyperplanes:

$$H_i(F(z)) \ne 0 \quad\text{for all } z \in \mathbb{C}^n \text{ and } 0 \le i \le n+1.$$

Then the map is degenerate: for every $z \in \mathbb{C}^n$,

$$\det\bigl[\,F(z) \;\big\vert\; \partial_1 F(z) \;\big\vert\; \cdots \;\big\vert\; \partial_n F(z)\,\bigr] = 0,$$

that is, the Jacobian of the induced map to $\mathbb{P}^n(\mathbb{C})$ has rank less than $n$ everywhere.
