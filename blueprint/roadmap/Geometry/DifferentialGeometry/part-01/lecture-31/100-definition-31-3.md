# Definition 31.3

In the situation above, let $(X_{1}, \ldots, X_{n})$ be a basis of $TM_{y}$ such that $\operatorname{det}(X_{1}, \ldots, X_{n}, \nu(y)) > 0$, and $(Y_{1}, \ldots, Y_{n})$ a basis of $T\tilde{M}_{\phi(y)}$ such that $\operatorname{det}(Y_{1}, \ldots, Y_{n}, \tilde{\nu}(\phi(y))) > 0$. Take the matrix $A$ such that $D\phi_{y}(X_{i}) = \sum_{j} A_{ji} Y_{j}$, and define $\operatorname{det}(D\phi_{y}) = \operatorname{det}(A)$. This is independent of the choices of bases.
