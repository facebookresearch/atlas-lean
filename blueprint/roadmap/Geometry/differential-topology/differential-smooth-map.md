# Differential of a smooth map at a point, acting on derivations

For a smooth map $F \colon M \to N$ between smooth manifolds and a point $p \in M$, the differential $dF_p$ sends a tangent vector $v$ at $p$, viewed as a derivation acting on smooth functions, to the derivation at $F(p)$ that takes a smooth function $g$ on $N$ to $v(g \circ F)$; the map $dF_p$ is linear. Mathlib defines this map more generally, on point derivations of spaces modelled on normed spaces over any nontrivially normed field. Let $\mathbb{K}$ be a nontrivially normed field and let $M$ and $N$ be topological spaces with atlases of charts into models with corners over $\mathbb{K}$, possibly different for $M$ and $N$; smooth manifolds, with or without boundary or corners, are the main example. For $p \in M$, a *point derivation* at $p$ is a $\mathbb{K}$-linear map $v$ from the algebra $C^\infty(M, \mathbb{K})$ of smooth $\mathbb{K}$-valued functions on $M$ to $\mathbb{K}$ with $v(fg) = f(p)\,v(g) + g(p)\,v(f)$. For a smooth map $F \colon M \to N$ and $p \in M$, the differential of $F$ at $p$ is the $\mathbb{K}$-linear map from point derivations at $p$ to point derivations at $F(p)$ given by $dF_p(v)(g) = v(g \circ F)$ for every $g \in C^\infty(N, \mathbb{K})$. For finite-dimensional real smooth manifolds, with or without boundary, the point derivations at $p$ are the tangent vectors at $p$ in the description by derivations, and this is the familiar differential. Mathlib does not identify point derivations with tangent vectors in general.
