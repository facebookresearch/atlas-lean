# Quasi-isometry

An $(L, C)$-quasi-isometry $f : X \to Y$ is an $(L, C)$-quasi-isometric embedding whose image is $C$-dense: every $y \in Y$ is within distance $C$ of some $f x$. The second condition is what makes quasi-isometry symmetric: a quasi-isometry has a quasi-inverse.

Typical examples: the inclusion $\mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{R}$ is a $(1, 1)$-quasi-isometry, a nonempty bounded space is quasi-isometric to a point, and by the Švarc–Milnor lemma the orbit map of a geometric group action is a quasi-isometry.

## Sources

- M. Gromov, *Asymptotic invariants of infinite groups*, in *Geometric Group Theory*, vol. 2 (G. A. Niblo and M. A. Roller, eds.), London Math. Soc. Lecture Note Ser. 182, Cambridge University Press, 1993, §0.2.C (the $(L, C)$ form is the now-standard form of this definition).
- M. Gromov, *Infinite groups as geometric objects*, Proceedings of the International Congress of Mathematicians, Warszawa 1983, vol. 1, 385–392, §1.B (the "virtually Lipschitz" formulation, which agrees with this one for uniformly discrete spaces such as word metrics).
