# Quasi-isometric spaces

Two pseudometric spaces $X$ and $Y$ are quasi-isometric if there is an $(L, C)$-quasi-isometry $X \to Y$ for some constants $L$ and $C$. This is an equivalence relation (reflexive, symmetric by the quasi-inverse, transitive by composition), and it is the equivalence under which Gromov studies the large-scale geometry of finitely generated groups: the invariants of a group that matter are those of its quasi-isometry class.

## Sources

- M. Gromov, *Infinite groups as geometric objects*, Proceedings of the International Congress of Mathematicians, Warszawa 1983, vol. 1, 385–392, §1.B.
- M. Gromov, *Asymptotic invariants of infinite groups*, in *Geometric Group Theory*, vol. 2 (G. A. Niblo and M. A. Roller, eds.), London Math. Soc. Lecture Note Ser. 182, Cambridge University Press, 1993, §0.2.C and §0.3.
