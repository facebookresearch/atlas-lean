# Geodesic triangles in a hyperbolic space are thin

If $X$ is $\delta$-hyperbolic, then every geodesic triangle in $X$ is $4\delta$-thin.

For points $u = \gamma t$ and $v = \gamma' t$ on the sides from $x$ to $y$ and from $x$ to $z$ with $t \le (y \cdot z)_x$, one has $(u \cdot y)_x = t = (z \cdot v)_x$, and two applications of the $\delta$-inequality at $x$ give $(u \cdot v)_x \ge t - 2\delta$, that is $d(u, v) \le 4\delta$.

The constant is weaker than the source: Gromov's Lemma 6.3.A and Proposition 6.3.C give $2\delta$, through the approximation of finite subsets of a hyperbolic space by trees (§6.1).

## Sources

- M. Gromov, *Hyperbolic groups*, in *Essays in Group Theory* (S. M. Gersten, ed.), Math. Sci. Res. Inst. Publ. 8, Springer, 1987, 75–263, Lemma 6.3.A and Proposition 6.3.C (constant $2\delta$).
