# Sides admissible for comparison

For a curvature $\kappa$ and reals $a, b, c$, the sides $a, b, c$ are admissible for comparison when they satisfy the three weak triangle inequalities $a \le b + c$, $b \le a + c$, $c \le a + b$ and, if $\kappa > 0$, the perimeter is strictly below the cutoff, $a + b + c < 2\pi/\sqrt{\kappa}$. This is stronger than the existence of a triangle in the model plane: at $\kappa = 1$ three equally spaced points on a great circle realise sides $2\pi/3$ each, but their perimeter $2\pi$ fails the strict cutoff.
