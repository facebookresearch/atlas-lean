# The first Brillouin zone is a fundamental domain

Let $\Lambda$ be a Bravais lattice with basis matrix $B$ ($\det B \ne 0$), primitive vectors
$a_0, a_1, a_2$ (the columns of $B$) and lattice $L = \{Bn : n \in \mathbb{Z}^3\}$. Its first
Brillouin zone, the Wigner–Seitz cell of the reciprocal lattice with basis matrix
$2\pi(B^{-1})^{\mathsf T}$, is a fundamental domain for the translations by reciprocal lattice
vectors, for Lebesgue measure on $\mathbb{R}^3$: it is measurable up to a null set, almost every
wave vector has a translate by a reciprocal lattice vector in the zone, and translates of the zone
by distinct reciprocal lattice vectors overlap only in sets of measure zero. Every wave vector in
fact has such a translate, by the
[covering theorem](../unit-cells-and-bravais-types/wigner-seitz-cell-cover.md) for the reciprocal
lattice.
