# Conventional cell

A Bravais lattice $\Lambda$ has a real $3 \times 3$ basis matrix $B$ with $\det B \ne 0$, primitive
vectors the columns of $B$, and lattice $L(\Lambda) = \{Bn : n \in \mathbb{Z}^3\}$. The closed
conventional cell of $\Lambda_c$ is the parallelepiped spanned by its primitive vectors, the points
with all primitive coordinates in $[0, 1]$. Being closed, it contains the lattice points on its
faces, edges and corners, which neighbouring cells share; the
[number of lattice points per cell](points-per-cell.md) is a quotient count instead. For the cube of
side $a > 0$ and the face-centered cubic lattice, the count per cell is $4$
([count](points-per-cell-face-centered-cubic.md)), while the eight corners of the closed cube,
points of the simple cubic lattice, are already points of the face-centered cubic lattice
([inclusion](simple-cubic-lattice-le-fcc.md)).
