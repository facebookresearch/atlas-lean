# Joints of a family of lines in $\mathbb{R}^3$

Let $\mathcal{L}$ be a finite family of affine subspaces of $\mathbb{R}^3$ (in applications, lines: affine subspaces with one-dimensional direction). A point $x \in \mathbb{R}^3$ is a joint of $\mathcal{L}$ if there are $\ell_1, \ell_2, \ell_3 \in \mathcal{L}$ all passing through $x$ whose directions together span $\mathbb{R}^3$:

$$x \in \ell_1 \cap \ell_2 \cap \ell_3, \qquad \vec{\ell_1} + \vec{\ell_2} + \vec{\ell_3} = \mathbb{R}^3.$$

For lines this says that three non-coplanar lines of $\mathcal{L}$ meet at $x$.
