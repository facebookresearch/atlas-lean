# Erdős problem 210: the number of ordinary lines tends to infinity

Conjecture (Erdős and de Bruijn): the least number of ordinary lines of $n$ non-collinear points in the plane tends to infinity. Precisely, for every $M \in \mathbb{N}$ there is $N \in \mathbb{N}$ such that every finite set $s \subseteq \mathbb{R}^2$ with $|s| \ge N$, not contained in a line, satisfies $o(s) \ge M$.

An ordinary line of a finite set $s \subseteq \mathbb{R}^2$ is a line through two distinct points of $s$ that contains no other point of $s$, and $o(s)$ denotes the number of ordinary lines of $s$. It holds; the resolution is [Motzkin: the number of ordinary lines tends to infinity](ordinary-lines-tend-to-infinity-holds.md).
