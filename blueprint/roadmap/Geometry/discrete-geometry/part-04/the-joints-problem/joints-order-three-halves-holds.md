# The joints problem: order $N^{3/2}$

This result proves the proposition stated as [Statement of the joints problem: order $N^{3/2}$](joints-order-three-halves.md). Precisely, both of the following hold.

1. There is a constant $C$ such that every finite family $\mathcal{L}$ of lines in $\mathbb{R}^3$ (affine subspaces with one-dimensional direction) has at most $C\, \lvert \mathcal{L} \rvert^{3/2}$ joints, where a joint is a point lying on three lines of $\mathcal{L}$ whose directions span $\mathbb{R}^3$.
2. For every $k \in \mathbb{N}$ there is a family of exactly $3k^2$ lines in $\mathbb{R}^3$ with at least $k^3$ joints.

So the maximum number of joints of $N$ lines has order $N^{3/2}$.
