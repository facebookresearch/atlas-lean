# Erdős problem 105: a line through two points avoiding the blockers

Conjecture (Erdős and Purdy): let $A, B \subseteq \mathbb{R}^2$ be disjoint finite sets with $|A| = |B| + 3$ (so $|A| = n$ and $|B| = n - 3$ for some $n \ge 3$), and suppose $A$ is not contained in a line. Then there are distinct points $p, q \in A$ such that the line through $p$ and $q$ contains no point of $B$. It is false; the refutation is [Erdős problem 105 is false: every line through two points is blocked](line-avoiding-blockers-refuted.md).
