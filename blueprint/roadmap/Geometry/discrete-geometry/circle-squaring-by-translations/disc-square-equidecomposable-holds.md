# Circle squaring: disc and square are equidecomposable

This result proves the proposition stated as [Tarski's circle-squaring problem (Erdős problem 1124)](disc-square-equidecomposable.md). Precisely, the closed unit disc $D = \{x \in \mathbb{R}^2 : \lVert x \rVert \le 1\}$ and the closed square $Q = [0, \sqrt{\pi}]^2$, both of area $\pi$, are finitely equidecomposable by isometries. That is, for some $k \in \mathbb{N}$ there are sets $A_1, \dots, A_k$ and $B_1, \dots, B_k$ in $\mathbb{R}^2$ and isometries $g_1, \dots, g_k$ of the Euclidean plane onto itself such that the $A_i$ are pairwise disjoint with union $D$, the $B_i$ are pairwise disjoint with union $Q$, and $g_i(A_i) = B_i$ for every $i$. The pieces are arbitrary sets, not required to be measurable.

Laczkovich's decomposition, in which the pieces are matched by translations, gives such a decomposition.
