# Bourgain's incidence problem for $n^2$ lines in $\mathbb{R}^3$

Conjecture (Bourgain's incidence question, in the form answered by Guth and Katz): there is a constant $c > 0$ such that for every $n \in \mathbb{N}$, every set $L$ of exactly $n^2$ distinct lines in $\mathbb{R}^3$ and every finite set $S \subset \mathbb{R}^3$, if

- every plane of $\mathbb{R}^3$ contains at most $n$ lines of $L$, and
- every line of $L$ contains at least $n$ points of $S$,

then

$$\lvert S \rvert \ge c\, n^3.$$

Here lines and planes are affine subspaces of dimension $1$ and $2$. It holds; the resolution is [Guth–Katz bound for Bourgain's lines problem](bourgain-lines-incidence-cube-bound-hold.md).
