# Erdős problem 135: four points spanning five distances

Erdős Problem 135 asks for the following statement: there is $c > 0$ such that for all sufficiently large $n$, every set $A \subset \mathbb{R}^2$ of exactly $n$ points in which every $4$-element subset $S \subseteq A$ determines at least $5$ distinct distances (values $\lVert x - y \rVert$ with $x, y \in S$, $x \ne y$) determines at least $c\, n^2$ distinct distances. It is false; the refutation is [Erdős problem 135 fails: few distances with five per four points](four-points-five-distances-quadratic-ref.md).
