# Erdős problem 135 fails: few distances with five per four points

This result refutes the proposition stated as [Erdős problem 135: four points spanning five distances](four-points-five-distances-quadratic.md). The statement is false: it is not the case that there is $c > 0$ such that for all sufficiently large $n$, every set $A \subset \mathbb{R}^2$ of exactly $n$ points in which every $4$-element subset $S \subseteq A$ determines at least $5$ distinct distances (values $\lVert x - y \rVert$ with $x, y \in S$, $x \ne y$) determines at least $c\, n^2$ distinct distances. That is, for every $c > 0$ there are arbitrarily large $n$ and sets $A$ of $n$ points in the plane in which every four points determine at least five distinct distances, but $A$ determines fewer than $c\, n^2$ distinct distances.
