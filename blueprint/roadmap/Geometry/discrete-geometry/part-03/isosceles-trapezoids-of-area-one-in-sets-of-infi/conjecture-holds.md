# Erdős problem 353: isosceles trapezoids of area one

This result proves the proposition stated as [Statement of Erdős problem 353: isosceles trapezoids of area one](conjecture.md). Precisely, for every Lebesgue measurable set $A \subseteq \mathbb{R}^2$ of infinite Lebesgue measure, there are points $a, b, c, d \in A$ forming an isosceles trapezoid of area $1$: $a, b, c, d$ are listed counterclockwise in strictly convex position (every three of them, in list order, have negative oriented-angle sign), the line $ab$ is parallel to the line $cd$, the diagonals satisfy $\lVert a - c \rVert = \lVert b - d \rVert$, and the convex hull of $\{a, b, c, d\}$ has Lebesgue measure $1$.
