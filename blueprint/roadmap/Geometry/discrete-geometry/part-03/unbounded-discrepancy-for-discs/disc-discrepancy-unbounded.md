# Statement of Erdős problem 989: unbounded disc discrepancy

Erdős Problem 989 (first question) asks for the following statement: for every infinite set $A \subseteq \mathbb{R}^2$ such that every closed disc $\overline{B}(c, r)$ contains only finitely many points of $A$, and for every real $M$, there are a radius $r > 0$ and a center $c \in \mathbb{R}^2$ with

$$\left\lvert\, \#\bigl(A \cap \overline{B}(c, r)\bigr) - \pi r^2 \,\right\rvert > M.$$

Equivalently, $f(r) = \sup_{c} \bigl\lvert \#(A \cap \overline{B}(c, r)) - \pi r^2 \bigr\rvert$, the largest discrepancy of a closed disc of radius $r$, is unbounded as a function of $r > 0$.
