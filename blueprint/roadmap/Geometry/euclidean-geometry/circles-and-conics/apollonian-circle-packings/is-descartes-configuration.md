# Descartes configuration

A Descartes configuration is a quadruple of four distinct, pairwise tangent circles such that the three points at
which any one circle touches the other three are distinct, that is, no three of the circles have a common tangent at a
common point. Tangency is a one-point intersection. A configuration of concentric circles or a pencil of circles
tangent at one point is excluded. For example, the circles of radii $6, 3, 3, 2$ centred at $0, 3, -3, 4i$
(curvatures proportional to $(-1, 2, 2, 3)$) form a Descartes configuration whose first circle encloses the others.

## Sources

- J. C. Lagarias, C. L. Mallows and A. R. Wilks, *Beyond the Descartes circle theorem*, Amer. Math. Monthly 109 (2002) 338–361, arXiv:math/0101066v1, Section 1 (definition of a Descartes configuration).
