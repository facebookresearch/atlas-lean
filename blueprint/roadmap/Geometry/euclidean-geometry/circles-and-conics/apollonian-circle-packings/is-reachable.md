# Configurations reachable by Apollonian swaps

Let $C_0$ be any quadruple of plane circles. The configurations reachable from $C_0$ are $C_0$ itself (no swap) and
the configurations obtained from a reachable one by a swap: in a reachable configuration $C$, replace the circle $C_i$
by a different circle $D$ such that $D$ and the three circles $C_j$, $j \ne i$, form a Descartes configuration. When
$C$ is itself a Descartes configuration, for instance when $C_0$ is one, the theorem of Apollonius gives exactly two
generalised circles (circles or lines) tangent to the three mutually tangent circles $C_j$ with distinct tangency
points, so $D$ is the other one; when the other one is a line, no swap is possible at $i$, since lines are not plane
circles here.
