# Descartes quadratic form

For a quadruple $v = (v_1, v_2, v_3, v_4)$ over a commutative ring, the Descartes quadratic form is

$$Q(v) = 2(v_1^2 + v_2^2 + v_3^2 + v_4^2) - (v_1 + v_2 + v_3 + v_4)^2 .$$

A quadruple with $Q(v) = 0$ is a Descartes quadruple. Over $\mathbb{R}$ the form has signature $(3, 1)$, so the
Descartes quadruples form a light cone. By Descartes' circle theorem the signed curvatures of four mutually tangent
circles form a Descartes quadruple; Kontorovich and Oh use the form with an extra factor $\tfrac12$.
