# The two roots of the Descartes relation

Over an integral domain, let $v$ be a Descartes quadruple and suppose that replacing its $i$-th entry by $x$
again gives a Descartes quadruple. Then $x = v_i$ or $x = 2\sum_{j \ne i} v_j - v_i$.

Geometrically: given three mutually tangent circles of curvatures $a, b, c$, the curvatures $d, d'$ of the two
circles tangent to all three are the two roots of the Descartes relation in the fourth variable, so
$d + d' = 2(a + b + c)$. The proof is Vieta's formula: with the other three entries fixed, $Q$ is a monic quadratic
polynomial in the $i$-th entry whose linear coefficient is $-2\sum_{j \ne i} v_j$.
