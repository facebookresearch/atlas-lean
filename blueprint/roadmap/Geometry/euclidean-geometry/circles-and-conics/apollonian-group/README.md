# Apollonian group

By Descartes' circle theorem the signed curvatures of four mutually tangent circles form a zero of the Descartes
quadratic form $Q(v) = 2(v_1^2 + v_2^2 + v_3^2 + v_4^2) - (v_1 + v_2 + v_3 + v_4)^2$, a form of signature $(3, 1)$.
Replacing one circle of such a configuration by the other circle tangent to the remaining three replaces one curvature
$v_i$ by $2(\text{sum of the other three}) - v_i$. The four resulting linear maps $S_1, \dots, S_4$ are the orthogonal
reflections of $(\mathbb{R}^4, Q)$ in the hyperplanes $Q$-orthogonal to the standard basis vectors, and they generate
the Apollonian group, a subgroup of the integral orthogonal group $O_Q(\mathbb{Z})$. Its orbits stay on the cone
$Q = 0$, and integrality of a starting quadruple propagates along the orbit: this is why four integral curvatures give
an integral Apollonian packing.
