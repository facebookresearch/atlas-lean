# The Apollonian flips are orthogonal reflections

Let $B(u, w) = 2\sum_j u_j w_j - (\sum_j u_j)(\sum_j w_j)$ be the polar form of the Descartes form, so that
$B(v, v) = Q(v)$, and let $e_i$ be the $i$-th standard basis vector, which has $Q(e_i) = 1$. Then

$$S_i(v) = v - 2B(v, e_i)\, e_i ,$$

the orthogonal reflection of the quadratic space $(R^4, Q)$ in the hyperplane $Q$-orthogonal to $e_i$: it is the
reflection $v \mapsto v - f(v)\, e_i$ attached to the linear form $f = 2B(\cdot, e_i)$, which satisfies $f(e_i) = 2$.
Hence the Apollonian group $\langle S_1, S_2, S_3, S_4 \rangle$ is a reflection group inside the orthogonal group
$O_Q$. Geometrically (Oh, Section 2) the same group is generated by the inversions in the four circles dual to a
Descartes configuration, which act on hyperbolic 3-space as reflections in hyperbolic planes.
