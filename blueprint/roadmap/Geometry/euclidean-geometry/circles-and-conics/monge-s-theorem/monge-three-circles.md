# Monge's three-circle theorem

Let $c_1, c_2, c_3$ be points of the Euclidean plane
$\mathbb{R}^2$ and let $r_1, r_2, r_3$ be pairwise distinct positive real numbers such that the
closed disks of radius $r_i$ centered at $c_i$ are pairwise disjoint. Then the external homothety
centers $(r_1 - r_2)^{-1}(r_1 c_2 - r_2 c_1)$, $(r_2 - r_3)^{-1}(r_2 c_3 - r_3 c_2)$ and
$(r_1 - r_3)^{-1}(r_1 c_3 - r_3 c_1)$ of the three pairs of circles are collinear.

## Sources

- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monge%27s_theorem. The source's hypothesis is that no circle lies inside another; the pairwise disjointness of the closed disks is stronger. Omitted source case: the source also treats two circles of equal radius, whose external tangents are parallel and meet at a point at infinity. The hypothesis that the radii are pairwise distinct excludes it, and the affine formula for the centers cannot express it. The positivity and disjointness hypotheses are not needed for the conclusion.
