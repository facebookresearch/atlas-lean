# Projective rigidity of Finsler metrics on hyperbolic surfaces

Question 10.1.3 of the AIM Geodesics problem list, for closed surfaces carrying a hyperbolic metric, asks whether the following holds: for every compact connected Hausdorff smooth surface $M$ without boundary that carries a $C^\infty$ Riemannian metric of constant sectional curvature $-1$, and for all Finsler metrics $F_1, F_2$ on $M$ such that every geodesic of $F_1$ becomes a geodesic of $F_2$ after an orientation-preserving reparametrization, there are a constant $c > 0$ and a closed $1$-form $\beta$ on $M$ with

$$F_1(v) = c\, F_2(v) + \beta(v) \qquad \text{for every } v \in TM.$$

A Finsler metric on $M$ is a continuous function $F : TM \to \mathbb{R}$ that is $C^\infty$ off the zero section, positive on nonzero vectors, positively homogeneous of degree one on each fiber and strongly convex; it need not be reversible. A geodesic of $F$ is a curve $\gamma : \mathbb{R} \to M$ for which there is a speed $c \ge 0$ such that, locally around every time, $d_F(\gamma(t_1), \gamma(t_2)) = c\,(t_2 - t_1)$ for $t_1 \le t_2$, where $d_F$ is the forward distance of $F$. A closed $1$-form is a function on $TM$ that is locally the differential of a smooth function. It is false; the refutation is [Projective rigidity of Finsler metrics fails on hyperbolic surfaces](projective-rigidity-refuted.md).
