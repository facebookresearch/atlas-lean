# Projectively unique polytopes

Let $V \subseteq \mathbb{R}^d$ be finite. $V$ is projectively unique when for every vertex set $W \subseteq \mathbb{R}^d$ of a $d$-polytope and every combinatorial equivalence $f$ from $V$ to $W$ there is a $(d + 1) \times (d + 1)$ real matrix $M$ with $\det M \ne 0$ such that for every $v \in V$, writing $y = M (v, 1)$,

$$y_{d+1} > 0 \qquad \text{and} \qquad f(v) = \Bigl(\frac{y_1}{y_{d+1}}, \dots, \frac{y_d}{y_{d+1}}\Bigr).$$

Here a $d$-polytope vertex set is a finite set affinely spanning $\mathbb{R}^d$ in convex position, and a combinatorial equivalence is a bijection $V \to W$ carrying facet vertex sets exactly onto facet vertex sets.
