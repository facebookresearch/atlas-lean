# Real parts of roots of Steiner polynomials

A convex body in $\mathbb{R}^n$ is a compact convex set with nonempty interior. For a convex body $K$, let $r$ be its inradius (supremum of radii of closed balls contained in $K$), $R$ its circumradius (infimum of radii of closed balls containing $K$), and let $f$ be its Steiner polynomial, the real polynomial with $f(\lambda) = \operatorname{vol}(K + \lambda B^n)$ for all $\lambda \ge 0$, where $B^n$ is the closed unit ball.

Conjecture, posed after a problem of Teissier on intersection numbers of Cartier divisors (Henk and Hernández Cifre, arXiv:math/0703373v1, §1, give its origin in their references [10] and [11]; the Oberwolfach report credits its references [4] and [5]): for every $n \ge 1$ and every convex body $K \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$, if $a_1 \le \dots \le a_n$ are the real parts of the complex roots of $f$, then

$$a_1 \le -R \le -r \le a_n \le 0.$$

Precisely: some complex root of $f$ has real part at most $-R$, some complex root has real part at least $-r$, and every complex root has nonpositive real part. It is false; the refutation is [Real parts of roots of Steiner polynomials: the conjectured bounds fail](steiner-root-bounds-refuted.md).
