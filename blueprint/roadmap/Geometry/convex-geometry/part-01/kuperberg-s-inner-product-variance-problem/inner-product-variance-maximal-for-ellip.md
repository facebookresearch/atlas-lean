# Kuperberg's conjecture: inner product variance is maximal for ellipsoids

Conjecture (G. Kuperberg): for every $n \in \mathbb{N}$ and every origin-symmetric convex body $K \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$, that is, $K$ compact and convex with nonempty interior and $-x \in K$ for all $x \in K$,

$$\operatorname{Var}(K) \le \operatorname{Var}(B^n),$$

where $B^n = \{x : \lVert x \rVert \le 1\}$ is the closed Euclidean unit ball and $\operatorname{Var}(K)$ is the variance of $\langle X, Y\rangle$ for independent $X$ uniform on $K$ and $Y$ uniform on the polar $K^\circ = \{y : \langle x, y\rangle \le 1 \ \forall x \in K\}$. Every ellipsoid centred at the origin has the same variance as $B^n$, namely $n/(n+2)^2$, so the conjecture says that the variance is largest for ellipsoids. It is false; the refutation is [Kuperberg's inner product variance conjecture is false](inner-product-variance-maximal-for-ellip-2.md).
