# Representing an Int object up to isomorphism over a right-rigid source

Let $C$ be a symmetric monoidal category with symmetric trace $T$, let $\mathrm{Int}(T)$ be its [Int construction](README.md), whose objects are pairs $X = (X^+, X^-)$, and let $J : C \to \mathrm{Int}(T)$, $J(A) = (A, \mathbf{1})$, be the embedding; the negative component of $X \otimes Y$ is $Y^- \otimes X^-$. Suppose further that $C$ is right rigid, with a chosen right dual $U^\vee$ of each object $U$ of $C$. A traced category need not have such duals: in the discrete monoidal category $(\mathbb{N}, +)$ cancellation gives a trace, but $1$ has no dual. Then every object $X = (X^+, X^-)$ of $\mathrm{Int}(T)$ is isomorphic to the embedding of a specific object of $C$: $(X^+, X^-) \cong J(X^+ \otimes (X^-)^\vee)$, combining the [decomposition](decomposition.md), the isomorphism $(\mathbf{1}, X^-) \cong J((X^-)^\vee)$ and the tensor comparison of $J$. The bare existence of a representative, that $J$ is essentially surjective, is a separate consequence.
