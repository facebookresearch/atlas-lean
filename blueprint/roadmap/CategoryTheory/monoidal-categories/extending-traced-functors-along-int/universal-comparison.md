# A functor out of Int is the canonical extension of its restriction

Let $C$ be a symmetric monoidal category with a symmetric trace $T$, let $\mathrm{Int}(T)$ be its [Int construction](../the-int-construction/README.md), whose objects are pairs $X = (X^+, X^-)$ of objects of $C$, and let $J : C \to \mathrm{Int}(T)$, $J(A) = (A, \mathbf{1})$, be the embedding. Let $D$ be a symmetric monoidal category and $K, L : \mathrm{Int}(T) \to D$ braided strong monoidal functors. In $\mathrm{Int}(T)$, $(A, \mathbf{1})$ has the right dual $(\mathbf{1}, A)$ through the [positive pairing](../the-int-construction/positive-pairing.md). Suppose that $D$ is right rigid. Then $K \circ J$ preserves $T$, sending it to the [canonical trace](../symmetric-traces-and-dual-comparisons/symmetric-trace-of-rigid.md) of $D$, and $K$ is isomorphic to the [canonical extension](extend.md) $\operatorname{ext}(K \circ J)$: the universal comparison is the [extension comparison](extension-comparison.md) $K \cong \operatorname{ext}(K \circ J)$ for $F = K \circ J$ and $\sigma$ the identity. It is monoidal. The source $C$ need not have duals; the target $D$ must be right rigid.
