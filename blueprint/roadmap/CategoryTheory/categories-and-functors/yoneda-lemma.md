# Yoneda lemma, covariant form

Let $\mathsf{C}$ be a category whose hom-sets are sets in a fixed universe $\mathcal{U}$, let $F \colon \mathsf{C} \to \mathsf{Set}$ be a functor to the category of sets in $\mathcal{U}$, and let $c$ be an object of $\mathsf{C}$. The rule $\Phi(\alpha) = \alpha_c(1_c)$ is a bijection from the collection of natural transformations $\alpha \colon \mathsf{C}(c,-) \Rightarrow F$ to the set $Fc$. Its inverse sends $x \in Fc$ to the natural transformation $\Psi(x)$ with components $\Psi(x)_d(f) = (Ff)(x)$ for $f \colon c \to d$. The bijection is natural in both variables: it is a natural isomorphism between the functors $(c, F) \mapsto \operatorname{Nat}(\mathsf{C}(c,-), F)$ and $(c, F) \mapsto Fc$ on $\mathsf{C} \times \mathsf{Set}^{\mathsf{C}}$. For a morphism $h \colon c \to c'$ and a natural transformation $\alpha \colon \mathsf{C}(c,-) \Rightarrow F$, we have $\Phi(\alpha \circ \mathsf{C}(h,-)) = (Fh)(\Phi(\alpha))$. For a natural transformation $\eta \colon F \Rightarrow G$, we have $\Phi(\eta \circ \alpha) = \eta_c(\Phi(\alpha))$. Mathlib calls this covariant form the coyoneda lemma; it regards each set $Fc$ as a set in the possibly larger universe of the natural transformations, through a universe lift that keeps its elements.
