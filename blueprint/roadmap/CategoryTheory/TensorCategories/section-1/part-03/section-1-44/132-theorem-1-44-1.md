# Theorem 1.44.1

Let $B$ be a square matrix with nonnegative entries. (1) $B$ has a nonnegative real eigenvalue. The largest nonnegative real eigenvalue $\lambda(B)$ of $B$ dominates the absolute values of all other eigenvalues $\mu$ of $B$: $|\mu| \leq \lambda(B)$ (in other words, the spectral radius of $B$ is an eigenvalue). Moreover, there is an eigenvector of $B$ with nonnegative entries and eigenvalue $\lambda(B)$. (2) If $B$ has strictly positive entries then $\lambda(B)$ is a simple positive eigenvalue, and the corresponding eigenvector can be normalized to have strictly positive entries. Moreover, $|\mu|\lt \lambda(B)$ for any other eigenvalue $\mu$ of $B$. (3) If $B$ has an eigenvector $\mathbf{v}$ with strictly positive entries, then the corresponding eigenvalue is $\lambda(B)$.
