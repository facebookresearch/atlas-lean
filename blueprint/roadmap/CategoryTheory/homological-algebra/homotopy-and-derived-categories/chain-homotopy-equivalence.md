# Homotopy equivalence of homological complexes

A chain map $f\colon C \to D$ of chain complexes of modules over a ring is a *chain homotopy equivalence* when there is a chain map $g\colon D \to C$ with $g \circ f$ chain homotopic to the identity of $C$ and $f \circ g$ chain homotopic to the identity of $D$. Mathlib packages this as data, for homological complexes in any preadditive category: for complexes $C$ and $D$ of the same complex shape (for example chain complexes indexed by $\mathbb{Z}$ or by $\mathbb{N}$) in a preadditive category (for example modules over a ring), a *homotopy equivalence* between $C$ and $D$ consists of a chain map $\mathrm{hom}\colon C \to D$, a chain map $\mathrm{inv}\colon D \to C$, a homotopy from $\mathrm{inv} \circ \mathrm{hom}$ to the identity of $C$ and a homotopy from $\mathrm{hom} \circ \mathrm{inv}$ to the identity of $D$. A given chain map $f$ is a chain homotopy equivalence exactly when such data exist with $\mathrm{hom} = f$, and $C$ and $D$ are chain homotopy equivalent exactly when such data exist. Homotopies are chain homotopies: for chain complexes indexed by $\mathbb{Z}$, a homotopy from $\phi$ to $\psi$ is a family of morphisms $h_n\colon C_n \to D_{n+1}$ with $\phi_n = d_{n+1} h_n + h_{n-1} d_n + \psi_n$ for every $n$.
