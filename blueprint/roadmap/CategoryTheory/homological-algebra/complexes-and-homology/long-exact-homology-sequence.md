# Exactness of the six-term segments of the long exact homology sequence

A short exact sequence $0 \to A \to B \to C \to 0$ of chain complexes of modules over a ring gives a long exact sequence of homology modules $\cdots \to H_n(A) \to H_n(B) \to H_n(C) \to H_{n-1}(A) \to H_{n-1}(B) \to \cdots$, whose maps $H_n(C) \to H_{n-1}(A)$ are the connecting homomorphisms. Mathlib proves this, for homological complexes in any abelian category, as the exactness of each six-term segment: let $\mathcal{C}$ be an abelian category (for example modules over a ring), let $c$ be a complex shape on an index set $\iota$, let $0 \to X_1 \to X_2 \to X_3 \to 0$ be a short exact sequence of complexes of shape $c$, given by chain maps $f\colon X_1 \to X_2$ and $g\colon X_2 \to X_3$, and let $i, j \in \iota$ be related by $c$ (for chain complexes indexed by $\mathbb{Z}$, $j = i - 1$). Then the sequence $H_i(X_1) \to H_i(X_2) \to H_i(X_3) \to H_j(X_1) \to H_j(X_2) \to H_j(X_3)$, formed by the maps induced by $f$ and $g$ and by the connecting morphism $\delta\colon H_i(X_3) \to H_j(X_1)$, is exact at each of its four interior terms. Taking $j = i - 1$ for every $i \in \mathbb{Z}$, the overlapping segments give the exactness of the whole long sequence. The connecting morphism $\delta$ is defined from the snake lemma; for modules it is given on classes by the familiar recipe, a separate computation: lift a cycle $z \in X_3^i$ to $x \in X_2^i$, write $dx = f(y)$ with $y \in X_1^j$, and send the class of $z$ to the class of $y$.
