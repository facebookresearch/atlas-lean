# Tensor product of homological complexes

For chain complexes $C$ and $C^\prime$ of abelian groups, the tensor product $C \otimes C^\prime$ has $(C \otimes C^\prime)_n = \bigoplus_{i+j=n} C_i \otimes C^\prime_j$ and boundary $\partial(a \otimes b) = \partial a \otimes b + (-1)^i\, a \otimes \partial^\prime b$ for $a \in C_i$ and $b \in C^\prime_j$. Mathlib defines the tensor product more generally, for homological complexes in a monoidal category: let $\mathcal{C}$ be a preadditive monoidal category in which tensoring with a fixed object, on either side, is additive (for example abelian groups, or modules over a commutative ring, with the usual tensor product), let $c$ be a complex shape on an additive monoid $I$ with decidable equality, and let $K_1$ and $K_2$ be complexes of shape $c$ such that, for every $n \in I$, the coproduct of the objects $K_1^i \otimes K_2^j$ over $i + j = n$ exists. The shape carries tensor sign data, signs $\varepsilon(i) \in \{1, -1\}$ for $i \in I$ subject to compatibility conditions: $\varepsilon(0) = 1$ and $\varepsilon(i + j) = \varepsilon(i)\,\varepsilon(j)$ for all $i, j \in I$, and whenever $c$ relates $i$ to $j$, it also relates $i + k$ to $j + k$ and $k + i$ to $k + j$ for every $k \in I$, and $\varepsilon(j) = -\varepsilon(i)$. Then $K_1 \otimes K_2$ is the total complex of the bicomplex $(K_1^i \otimes K_2^j)$: in degree $n$ it is the coproduct of the objects $K_1^i \otimes K_2^j$ with $i + j = n$, and its differential is the sum of the horizontal differential, $d \otimes 1$ with sign $+1$, and the vertical differential, $1 \otimes d$ with sign $\varepsilon(i)$ on the summand of bidegree $(i, j)$. For chain complexes indexed by $\mathbb{N}$ the sign data are $\varepsilon(i) = (-1)^i$, which recovers the formula above in nonnegative degrees. The sign data that Mathlib provides on $\mathbb{Z}$ are for cochain complexes, whose differential raises the degree, so chain complexes indexed by $\mathbb{Z}$ are covered after reindexing $n \mapsto -n$, which does not change parities.
