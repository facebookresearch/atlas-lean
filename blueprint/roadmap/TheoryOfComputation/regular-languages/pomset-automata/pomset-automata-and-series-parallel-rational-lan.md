# Pomset automata and series-parallel rational languages

Pomsets generalize words to partially ordered occurrences of letters; pomset automata recognize sets of series-parallel pomsets
using a sequential transition function on letters and a parallel transition function that forks into two branches and joins
them when both accept.

**Pomsets.** A *labeled poset* $\langle C, \le, \lambda \rangle$ has a carrier $C$, a partial order $\le$ on $C$ and a labeling
$\lambda : C \to \Sigma$; a *pomset* is an isomorphism class of labeled posets, where an isomorphism is a bijection preserving
labels and order in both directions. Words are the totally ordered pomsets, $1$ is the empty pomset and $a \in \Sigma$ is the
pomset with one point labeled $a$. For pomsets $U, V$ with disjoint carriers, the *sequential composition* $U \cdot V$ orders
every point of $U$ before every point of $V$, and the *parallel composition* $U \parallel V$ adds no order between them. The
*series-parallel* pomsets $\mathsf{SP}$ form the smallest set containing $1$ and the primitive pomsets and closed under both
compositions.

**Series-parallel rational languages.** The operations lift to sets of pomsets, with sequential iteration
$\mathcal{U}^* = \bigcup_{n} \mathcal{U}^n$ and parallel iteration $\mathcal{U}^\dagger = \bigcup_n \mathcal{U}^{(n)}$, where
$\mathcal{U}^0 = \mathcal{U}^{(0)} = \{1\}$, $\mathcal{U}^{n+1} = \mathcal{U} \cdot \mathcal{U}^n$ and
$\mathcal{U}^{(n+1)} = \mathcal{U} \parallel \mathcal{U}^{(n)}$. *Series-parallel rational expressions* are generated by
$e ::= 0 \mid 1 \mid a \mid e + e \mid e \cdot e \mid e \parallel e \mid e^* \mid e^\dagger$ ($a \in \Sigma$) and denote sets of
series-parallel pomsets in the evident way; a set $\mathcal{U} \subseteq \mathsf{SP}$ denoted by such an expression is an
*spr-language*.

**Pomset automata.** A *pomset automaton* (PA) is a tuple $A = \langle Q, \delta, \gamma, F \rangle$ with a set $Q$ of states,
accepting states $F \subseteq Q$, a sequential transition function $\delta : Q \times \Sigma \to Q$ and a parallel transition
function $\gamma : Q \times Q \times Q \to Q$. Its trace relation $q \xrightarrow{U}_A q'$ is the smallest relation with
$q \xrightarrow{1}_A q$, $q \xrightarrow{a}_A \delta(q, a)$, closure under composition
($q \xrightarrow{U}_A q''$ and $q'' \xrightarrow{V}_A q'$ give $q \xrightarrow{U \cdot V}_A q'$), and the fork-join rule: if
$r \xrightarrow{U}_A r'$ and $s \xrightarrow{V}_A s'$ with $r', s' \in F$, then $q \xrightarrow{U \parallel V}_A \gamma(q, r, s)$.
The *language* of $A$ at a state $q$ is $L_A(q) = \{ U \in \mathsf{SP} : q \xrightarrow{U}_A q' \text{ for some } q' \in F \}$.
A PA does not fix an initial state; it is *finite* if $Q$ is finite.

Finite PA accept exactly the context-free pomset languages, those generated by context-free grammars whose right-hand sides
are terms built with sequential and parallel products. The Kleene theorem of the source matches the spr-languages with the
languages of pomset automata that satisfy structural restrictions, finite support and well-nestedness.
