# Nothing below the least pumping length is a pumping length

Words are finite lists over an alphabet, and a language is a set of words. A language is regular when it is recognized by a deterministic finite automaton. For words $x, y, z$ and $i \ge 0$, pumping gives the word $x y^i z$, in which $y$ is repeated $i$ times. A natural number $n$ is a pumping length for a language $L$ when every word $w \in L$ with $|w| \ge n$ can be written as $w = xyz$ with $|xy| \le n$ and $y$ nonempty, such that $x y^i z \in L$ for every $i \ge 0$. The least pumping length of a regular language is the smallest of its pumping lengths. For every regular language $L$ and every $n$ smaller than its least pumping length, $n$ is not a pumping length for $L$.
