# Injectivity of measure-once quantum finite automata is undecidable

No algorithm decides whether the acceptance probability function of a given measure-once quantum finite automaton is injective,
that is, whether distinct words always have distinct acceptance probabilities; this holds already for automata with $8$ states
over a $17$-letter alphabet.

A *measure-once quantum finite automaton* (MO-QFA) with $n$ states over a $k$-letter alphabet $\Sigma$, in the Moore–Crutchfield
model of [one-way quantum finite automata](one-way-quantum-finite-automata.md), is a triple $\mathcal{Q} = (P, \{X_a : a \in \Sigma\}, u)$
of a projection matrix $P \in \mathbb{C}^{n \times n}$, unitary matrices $X_a \in \mathbb{C}^{n \times n}$ and a unit vector
$u \in \mathbb{C}^n$. With $X_{a_1 \cdots a_t} = X_{a_1} \cdots X_{a_t}$, the acceptance probability of a word $w$ is

$$
\mathcal{Q}(w) = \lVert P X_{w^R} u \rVert^2 ,
$$

where $w^R$ is the reversal of $w$, so that the first letter acts first. $\mathcal{Q}$ is *injective* if $\mathcal{Q}(w_1) = \mathcal{Q}(w_2)$
implies $w_1 = w_2$ for all words $w_1, w_2 \in \Sigma^*$.

**Theorem.** The injectivity problem for measure-once quantum finite automata is undecidable for $8$ states over an alphabet of
size $17$ (Theorem 4 of the source), already for instances given as finite data: $P$ and the $X_a$ with rational entries, and $u$ with real algebraic entries, each given by its minimal polynomial over $\mathbb{Q}$ and an isolating interval with rational endpoints. These are the automata the proof constructs; the source states the problem for arbitrary complex automata, which are not finite inputs. The source also
shows that the *scalar ambiguity* problem, whether every value $\lVert P X u \rVert^2$ with $X$ in the semigroup generated by the
$X_a$ comes from a unique matrix $X$, is undecidable for $9$ states over $17$ letters, and that injectivity stays undecidable for
automata with only rational entries, at the price of a much larger number of states.

## Expansion

The proof reduces the mixed modification of the Post correspondence problem: given morphisms $h, g : \Sigma^* \to \Delta^*$ with
$|\Delta| = 2$, is there a word $x_1 \cdots x_k$ with $h_1(x_1) \cdots h_k(x_k) = g_1(x_1) \cdots g_k(x_k)$ for some choices
$h_j, g_j \in \{h, g\}$ not all equal? It is undecidable for $|\Sigma| \ge 9$ (Cassaigne, Karhumäki and Harju), also for Claus
instances (Halava, Harju and Hirvensalo). Words are embedded injectively into rational unitary $4 \times 4$ matrices through a free
semigroup of rational quaternions, and the pairs $(x, h(x))$, $(x, g(x))$ give $8 \times 8$ block-diagonal unitary generators, two
per letter of $\Sigma$; for a Claus instance with $9$ letters one generator can be absorbed into the initial vector, which leaves
$17$ letters. A product of generators is determined by the absolute values of a few of
its entries, and a projection and an initial vector with linearly independent square roots as coefficients turn these entries
into a single acceptance probability. Two distinct words get the same probability exactly when some matrix has two factorizations,
that is, when the instance has a solution.
