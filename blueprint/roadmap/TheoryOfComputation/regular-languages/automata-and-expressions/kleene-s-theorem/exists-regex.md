# Kleene's theorem, converse direction

Let $\alpha$ be a finite alphabet. Every regular language $L$ over $\alpha$ (accepted by a deterministic automaton with
finitely many states) is the language of some regular expression over $\alpha$. In particular, the language of a nondeterministic automaton with finitely many states is the language of a regular expression: the subset construction gives a deterministic automaton with the same language whose states, the sets of states, are finite in number.

- M. Sipser, *Introduction to the Theory of Computation*, 3rd ed., Cengage Learning, 2013, Theorem 1.54.
