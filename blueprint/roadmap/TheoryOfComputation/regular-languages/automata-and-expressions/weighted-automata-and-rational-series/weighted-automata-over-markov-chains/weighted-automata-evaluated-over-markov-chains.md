# Weighted automata evaluated over Markov chains

A nondeterministic weighted automaton assigns to each word the infimum of the values of its accepting runs; when words are
generated by a Markov chain this value becomes a random variable, with an expected value and a distribution function.

**Automata.** A *weighted automaton* $\mathcal{A} = (\Sigma, Q, Q_0, F, \delta, C)$ is a nondeterministic finite automaton with input
alphabet $\Sigma$, states $Q$, initial states $Q_0$, final states $F$ and transitions $\delta \subseteq Q \times \Sigma \times Q$,
together with rational weights $C : \delta \to \mathbb{Q}$. A run on a finite or infinite word gives the sequence of weights of
its transitions; a finite run is accepting if it ends in $F$, and every infinite run is accepting. A *value function* $f$ maps
weight sequences to reals: $\mathrm{Sum}$ adds the weights of a finite run, and $\mathrm{LimAvg}$ of an infinite run is
$\limsup_{k \to \infty} \frac{1}{k} \sum_{i=1}^{k} C(\pi)[i]$. The value $\mathcal{A}(w)$ of a word under an $f$-automaton is the
infimum of $f$ over the accepting runs on $w$, and $+\infty$ if there is none.

**Markov chains.** A *Markov chain* $\mathcal{M} = (\Sigma, S, s_0, E)$ has finite states $S$, an initial state $s_0$ and edge
probabilities $E : S \times \Sigma \times S \to [0, 1]$ with $\sum_{a, s'} E(s, a, s') = 1$ for every $s$; it defines a probability
measure $\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{M}}$ on infinite words through the probabilities of cylinders. A *terminating* Markov chain adds a set of
terminating states, reachable from every state, entered by $\varepsilon$-edges; it defines a probability distribution on finite
words. In both cases the probabilities are rational when the chain is an input.

**Questions.** For an automaton $\mathcal{A}$ and a (terminating) Markov chain $\mathcal{M}$, the function $w \mapsto \mathcal{A}(w)$ is
a random variable, with *expected value* $\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathcal{A})$ and *distribution*
$\mathbb{D}_{\mathcal{M}, \mathcal{A}}(\lambda) = \mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{M}}(\{w : \mathcal{A}(w) \le \lambda\})$. The *expected value question*
asks to compute $\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathcal{A})$ from $\mathcal{A}$ and $\mathcal{M}$; the *distribution question* asks to compute
$\mathbb{D}_{\mathcal{M}, \mathcal{A}}(\lambda)$ from $\mathcal{A}$, $\mathcal{M}$ and a rational $\lambda$. Sum-automata are evaluated over
terminating chains and LimAvg-automata over chains on infinite words.

The expected value may be $+\infty$: for a LimAvg-automaton exactly when the set of infinite words with no infinite run has positive probability (all infinite runs are accepting, but a word may have none), and for a Sum-automaton exactly when some finite word of positive probability has no accepting run; otherwise it lies between the minimal and the maximal weight, times the expected word length for Sum. These values can be irrational, even transcendental, already for integer weights and the uniform distribution.
