# Matrix reachability over the max-plus semiring is undecidable for two generators

No algorithm decides whether a given matrix, or a given row vector times a matrix, is reached by products of two given matrices over the
max-plus semiring, although the mortality problem there is decidable, as is reachability for a single generator.

Let $\mathbb{Z}_{\max} = (\mathbb{Z} \cup \{-\infty\}, \max, +)$ be the max-plus semiring, in which matrices multiply by $(AB)_{ij} = \max_k (A_{ik} + B_{kj})$. For $n \times n$ matrices $A_1, \dots, A_r$ over a semiring:

- the *matrix reachability problem* asks, for a given matrix $M$, whether $A_{i_1} \cdots A_{i_k} = M$ for some finite sequence $i_1, \dots, i_k$, that is, whether $M$ lies in the semigroup generated by the $A_i$ (the *mortality problem* is
  the case $M = 0$);
- the *vector reachability problem* asks, for given row vectors $\alpha, \eta$, whether $\alpha A_{i_1} \cdots A_{i_k} = \eta$ for some sequence;
- the *scalar reachability problem* asks, for a given row vector $\alpha$, column vector $\beta$ and scalar $\gamma$, whether $\alpha A_{i_1} \cdots A_{i_k} \beta = \gamma$ for some sequence.

**Theorem.** Over $\mathbb{Z}_{\max}$, the matrix reachability problem and the vector reachability problem for $r = 2$ generators are undecidable; so is the scalar reachability problem for $r = 2$, which is contained in Krob's proof
that equivalence of [max-plus weighted automata](../weighted-automata-over-a-semiring.md) is undecidable. The restrictions to matrices of some fixed, sufficiently large dimension remain undecidable.

The bound $r \ge 2$ is optimal: for $r = 1$ matrix reachability over $\mathbb{Z}_{\max}$ is decidable, and the mortality problem over $\mathbb{Z}_{\max}$ is decidable for every $r$. Over semirings that are effectively separated by morphisms of finite image, such as
the natural numbers, all three reachability problems are decidable.

## Expansion

In an arbitrary semiring, the source proves polynomial-time reductions: scalar reachability with $r$ generators of dimension $n$ reduces to scalar reachability with $2$ generators of dimension $rn$, and similarly for vectors and matrices; scalar reachability
with $r$ generators reduces to vector reachability with $r + 1$ generators, and vector reachability with $r$ generators to matrix reachability with $r + 1$, in slightly larger dimension. Combined with Krob's undecidability of scalar reachability over
$\mathbb{Z}_{\max}$ for two generators, which Krob derived from Hilbert's tenth problem, these reductions give the theorem. A complete expansion links Krob's scalar reachability theorem, which has no page of its own; Hilbert's tenth problem is the
[Davis–Putnam–Robinson–Matiyasevich theorem](../../../../undecidable-problems/diophantine-sets-and-hilbert-s-tenth-problem/dprm-theorem.md).
