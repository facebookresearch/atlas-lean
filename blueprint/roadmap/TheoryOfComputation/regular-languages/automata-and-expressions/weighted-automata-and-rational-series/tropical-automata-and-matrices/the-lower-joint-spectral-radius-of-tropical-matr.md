# The lower joint spectral radius of tropical matrices is uncomputable

No algorithm decides whether the joint spectral radius, in its infimum form, of a given finite set of tropical matrices is nonnegative, already
for three matrices with entries in $\{-\infty, -1, 0, 1\}$; whether the ultimate rank of the generated semigroup is
$1$ is undecidable as well.

For a set $\Gamma$ of $d \times d$ matrices over $\mathbb{Z} \cup \{-\infty\}$, with the max-plus product, the *joint spectral radius* considered here is

$$
\rho(\Gamma) = \inf \Bigl\{ \frac{1}{\ell} \lVert M_1 \cdots M_\ell \rVert_\infty : \ell > 0,\ M_1, \dots, M_\ell \in \Gamma \Bigr\},
$$

where $\lVert M \rVert_\infty$ is the largest entry of $M$; by Fekete's subadditive lemma this equals $\lim_{\ell \to \infty} \frac{1}{\ell} \min\{ \lVert M_1 \cdots M_\ell \rVert_\infty : M_1, \dots, M_\ell \in \Gamma \}$ (Lemma 2 of the source). With the maximum over products instead of the minimum, the usual joint spectral radius, the quantity would be easy to compute over $\mathbb{Z}_{\max}$ (footnote 2 of the source); the infimum form, also called the lower spectral radius or joint spectral subradius, is the one meant here. Instances are finite sets of matrices whose finite entries are integers written in binary. The problem $\mathrm{JSR}_k(S)$ asks, given $k$ matrices with entries in $S$, whether their joint spectral radius is at least $0$. The problem
$\mathrm{UR}_k(S)$ asks whether the ultimate rank of the semigroup generated by the matrices equals $1$, where the *ultimate rank* of a
tropical matrix is the sum of the cyclicities of the strongly connected components of its critical graph, and the ultimate rank of a set $\Gamma$ is the minimum of the ultimate ranks of the elements of the semigroup it generates.

**Theorem.**

1. $\mathrm{JSR}_3(\{-\infty, -1, 0, 1\})$ is undecidable.
2. $\mathrm{UR}_3(\{-\infty, -1, 0, 1\})$ is undecidable.

The published MFCS 2017 version states both already for two matrices (Theorems 8 and 9 there).

The source also states dimension-bounded versions, $\mathrm{JSR}^{554}_7$ and $\mathrm{UR}^{1109}_7$ over $\mathbb{Z} \cup \{-\infty\}$. They are not stated here, because their constants come from the state count of the comparison result with a bounded number of states, which the source's own Lemma 6 gives as $563$ rather than the printed $553$ (so $564$ and $1129$ here).

In contrast, the joint spectral radius is approximable: an algorithm computes it within any given precision $1/n$.

## Expansion

For a max-plus automaton whose states are all initial and final, its value on a word is the largest entry of the product of the matrices of its letters, so it is
nonnegative on every word exactly when the joint spectral radius of its transition matrices is nonnegative (by Fekete's subadditive lemma the infimum over products is a limit of normalized minima).
The comparison problem for such automata is undecidable, which gives item 1; item 2 follows from item 1 by a further reduction.
