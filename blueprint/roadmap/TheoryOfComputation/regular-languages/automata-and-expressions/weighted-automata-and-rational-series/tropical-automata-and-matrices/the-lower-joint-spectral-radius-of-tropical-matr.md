# The lower joint spectral radius of tropical matrices is uncomputable

No algorithm decides whether the joint spectral radius, in its infimum form, of a given finite set of tropical matrices is nonnegative, already
for three matrices with entries in $\{-\infty, -1, 0, 1\}$ or seven matrices of dimension 554; whether the ultimate rank of the generated semigroup is
$1$ is undecidable as well.

For a set $\Gamma$ of $d \times d$ matrices over $\mathbb{Z} \cup \{-\infty\}$, with the max-plus product, the *joint spectral radius* considered here is

$$
\rho(\Gamma) = \inf_{\ell > 0} \frac{1}{\ell} \max\bigl\{ \lVert M_1 \cdots M_\ell \rVert_\infty : M_1, \dots, M_\ell \in \Gamma \bigr\},
$$

where $\lVert M \rVert_\infty$ is the largest entry of $M$; with the supremum instead of the infimum it would be easy to compute. The problem $\mathrm{JSR}_k(S)$, respectively
$\mathrm{JSR}^d_k(S)$, asks, given $k$ matrices with entries in $S$, respectively also of dimension $d$, whether their joint spectral radius is at least $0$. The problem
$\mathrm{UR}_k(S)$, respectively $\mathrm{UR}^d_k(S)$, asks whether the ultimate rank of the semigroup generated by the matrices equals $1$, where the *ultimate rank* of a
tropical matrix is the sum of the cyclicities of the strongly connected components of its critical graph.

**Theorem.**

1. $\mathrm{JSR}_3(\{-\infty, -1, 0, 1\})$ and $\mathrm{JSR}^{554}_7(\mathbb{Z} \cup \{-\infty\})$ are undecidable.
2. $\mathrm{UR}_3(\{-\infty, -1, 0, 1\})$ and $\mathrm{UR}^{1109}_7(\mathbb{Z} \cup \{-\infty\})$ are undecidable.

In contrast, the joint spectral radius is approximable: an algorithm computes it within any given precision $1/n$.

## Expansion

For a max-plus automaton whose states are all initial and final, its value on a word is the largest entry of the product of the matrices of its letters, so it is
nonnegative on every word exactly when the joint spectral radius of its transition matrices is nonnegative (by Fekete's subadditive lemma the infimum is a limit).
The comparison problem for such automata is undecidable, which gives item 1; item 2 follows from item 1 by a further reduction.
