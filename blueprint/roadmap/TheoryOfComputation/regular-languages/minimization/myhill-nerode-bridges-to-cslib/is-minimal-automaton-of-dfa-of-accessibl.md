# Accessible state-separated automata satisfy the cardinality minimality predicate

Let $\Sigma$ be an alphabet and $L$ a language over it. Two words $x, y$ are Nerode equivalent for $L$ when $xz \in L \iff yz \in L$ for every word $z$; the Nerode classes of $L$ are the equivalence classes. Two constructions of these classes are used here: the quotient of words by the Nerode relation built directly, and the Nerode quotient of the general automata framework; the two have the same classes. The comparison takes the alphabet in the lowest universe of types, as the framework's quotient requires; the states may lie in a larger universe. A deterministic automaton given by a transition function, a start state and a set of accepting states can also be viewed as a deterministic automaton of the general framework, with the same states, start state, transitions and acceptance.

In that framework an automaton $M$ is minimal for $L$ when it recognizes $L$ and $|Q| = |N_L|$, where $Q$ is the set of states of $M$, $N_L$ is the set of Nerode classes of $L$, and $|S|$ denotes the number of elements of a set $S$ as a natural number, taken to be $0$ when $S$ is infinite. This cardinality-based predicate asks only for equal counts; it does not provide a bijection between states and Nerode classes. Because of the convention for infinite sets, it also holds for an automaton with infinitely many states that recognizes a language with infinitely many Nerode classes.

A deterministic automaton is accessible when every state is reached from the start state by some word, and state-separated when two states from which the same words are accepted are equal. If $M$ is accessible and state-separated, then $M$, viewed in the general framework, is minimal for the language $L(M)$ it accepts. No finiteness of the states is assumed.

The proof constructs a bijection and then compares counts: sending the state reached by a word $x$ to the residual $x^{-1}L(M) = \{z : xz \in L(M)\}$ is a bijection from the states onto the residuals of $L(M)$, and the residuals correspond bijectively to the Nerode classes. Sets in bijection have equal counts, so $|Q| = |N_{L(M)}|$; when the states are infinite this is the equality $0 = 0$. The predicate itself records only this equality, not the bijection.
