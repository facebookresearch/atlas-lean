# Accessible state-separated automata are minimal

Let $\Sigma$ be an alphabet and $L$ a language over it. Two words $x, y$ are Nerode equivalent for $L$ when $xz \in L \iff yz \in L$ for every word $z$; the Nerode classes of $L$ are the equivalence classes. Two constructions of these classes are compared here: the quotient of words by the Nerode relation built directly, and the Nerode quotient of the general automata framework; both have the same classes. A deterministic automaton given by a transition function, a start state and a set of accepting states can also be viewed as a deterministic automaton of the general framework, with the same states, start state, transitions and acceptance. In that framework an automaton is minimal for $L$ when it recognizes $L$ and its states are in bijection with the Nerode classes of $L$. A deterministic automaton is accessible when every state is reached from the start state by some word, and state-separated when two states accepting the same words from them are equal. If $M$ is accessible and state-separated, then $M$, viewed in the general framework, is minimal for the language it accepts: its states correspond bijectively to the Nerode classes. No finiteness of the states is assumed.
