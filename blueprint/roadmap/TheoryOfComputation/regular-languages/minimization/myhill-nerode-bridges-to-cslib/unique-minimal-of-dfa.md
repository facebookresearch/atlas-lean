# A finite minimal automaton has a unique state bijection with the Nerode classes

Let $\Sigma$ be an alphabet and $L$ a language over it. Two words $x, y$ are Nerode equivalent for $L$ when $xz \in L \iff yz \in L$ for every word $z$; the Nerode classes of $L$ are the equivalence classes. Two constructions of these classes are compared here: the quotient of words by the Nerode relation built directly, and the Nerode quotient of the general automata framework; both have the same classes. A deterministic automaton given by a transition function, a start state and a set of accepting states can also be viewed as a deterministic automaton of the general framework, with the same states, start state, transitions and acceptance. In that framework an automaton is minimal for $L$ when it recognizes $L$ and its states are in bijection with the Nerode classes of $L$. Let $M$ be a deterministic automaton with finitely many states that, viewed in the general framework, is minimal for the language it accepts. Then there is exactly one bijection $e$ from the states of $M$ to the Nerode classes of $L(M)$ with $e(\text{state reached by } x) = $ the Nerode class of $x$ for every word $x$.
