# Matching the two constructions of Nerode classes

Let $\Sigma$ be an alphabet and $L$ a language over it. Two words $x, y$ are Nerode equivalent for $L$ when $xz \in L \iff yz \in L$ for every word $z$; the Nerode classes of $L$ are the equivalence classes. Two constructions of these classes are used here: the quotient of words by the Nerode relation built directly, and the Nerode quotient of the general automata framework; the two have the same classes. The comparison takes the alphabet in the lowest universe of types, as the framework's quotient requires; the states may lie in a larger universe.

Both constructions are identified with the residuals of $L$, the languages $x^{-1}L = \{z : xz \in L\}$, and composing these identifications gives a canonical bijection from the direct construction to the general framework's Nerode quotient that sends the class of each word to the class of the same word. Its inverse sends the class of a word $x$ in the general framework's Nerode quotient to the Nerode class of $x$ in the direct construction.
