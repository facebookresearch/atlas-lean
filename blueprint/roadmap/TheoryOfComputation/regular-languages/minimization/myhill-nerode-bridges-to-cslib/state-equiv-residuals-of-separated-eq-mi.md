# An accessible state-separated automaton has a unique state bijection with the Nerode classes sending the state reached by each word to that word's class

Let $\Sigma$ be an alphabet and $L$ a language over it. Two words $x, y$ are Nerode equivalent for $L$ when $xz \in L \iff yz \in L$ for every word $z$; the Nerode classes of $L$ are the equivalence classes. Two constructions of these classes are used here: the quotient of words by the Nerode relation built directly, and the Nerode quotient of the general automata framework; the two have the same classes. The comparison takes the alphabet in the lowest universe of types, as the framework's quotient requires; the states may lie in a larger universe. A deterministic automaton given by a transition function, a start state and a set of accepting states can also be viewed as a deterministic automaton of the general framework, with the same states, start state, transitions and acceptance.

A deterministic automaton is accessible when every state is reached from the start state by some word, and state-separated when two states from which the same words are accepted are equal. The residual of $L$ by a word $x$ is $x^{-1}L = \{z : xz \in L\}$, and the residuals correspond bijectively to the Nerode classes.

Let $M$ be accessible and state-separated; no finiteness of the states is assumed. Let $e$ be any bijection from the states of $M$ to the classes of the general framework's Nerode quotient of $L(M)$ that sends the state reached by $x$ to the class of $x$, for every word $x$. Then $e$ equals the composite of the bijection from the states onto the residuals of $L(M)$, which sends the state reached by $x$ to $x^{-1}L(M)$, with the correspondence from residuals to Nerode classes. So there is at most one such bijection, and this explicit composite is where a state bijection is constructed. For finitely many states it is the bijection given by the uniqueness theorem for minimal automata, whose minimality hypothesis is the cardinality-based predicate $|Q| = |N_{L(M)}|$ (with $|S| = 0$ for an infinite set $S$) together with recognition of $L(M)$.
