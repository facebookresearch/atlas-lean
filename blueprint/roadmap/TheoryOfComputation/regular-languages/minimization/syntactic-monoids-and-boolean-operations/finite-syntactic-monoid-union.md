# Union preserves finiteness of the syntactic monoid

Words over an alphabet $\Sigma$ form the free monoid under concatenation, and a language is a set of words. Two words $u, v$ are syntactically equivalent for a language $L$ when $xuy \in L \iff xvy \in L$ for all words $x, y$; this is a congruence, the syntactic congruence of $L$, and the quotient is the syntactic monoid of $L$. If the syntactic monoids of languages $L$ and $K$ are finite, then the syntactic monoid of $L \cup K$ is finite.
