# The product of recognizers recognizes the union

Words over an alphabet $\Sigma$ form the free monoid under concatenation, and a language is a set of words. Two words $u, v$ are syntactically equivalent for a language $L$ when $xuy \in L \iff xvy \in L$ for all words $x, y$; this is a congruence, the syntactic congruence of $L$, and the quotient is the syntactic monoid of $L$. A monoid homomorphism $\varphi$ from the free monoid to a monoid $N$, together with a subset $A \subseteq N$, recognizes a language $L$ when $L = \{w : \varphi(w) \in A\}$. The product of two homomorphisms $\varphi_1, \varphi_2$ is the homomorphism $w \mapsto (\varphi_1(w), \varphi_2(w))$ into the product monoid. If $\varphi_1$ with $A_1$ recognizes $L$ and $\varphi_2$ with $A_2$ recognizes $K$, then the product $\varphi_1 \times \varphi_2$ with the set of pairs $(p_1, p_2)$ such that $p_1 \in A_1$ or $p_2 \in A_2$ recognizes $L \cup K$.
