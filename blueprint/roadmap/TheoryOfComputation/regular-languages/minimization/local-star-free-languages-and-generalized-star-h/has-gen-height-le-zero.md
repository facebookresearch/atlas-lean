# Locally star-free languages have generalized star height zero

Over an alphabet $\alpha$, the locally star-free languages are the smallest class containing the empty language and every one-letter language $\{a\}$, and closed under complement, union and concatenation. A generalized regular expression may use complement as well as union, concatenation and Kleene star; its generalized star height is the largest nesting depth of stars, and a language has generalized star height at most $n$ when some generalized regular expression of star height at most $n$ denotes it. Over any alphabet, every locally star-free language has generalized star height $0$. The generalized expressions also allow the language containing only the empty word as a base case; over an infinite alphabet that language is not locally star-free, so the converse fails there.
