# Aperiodic monoids

A monoid is aperiodic when the powers of every element eventually stabilize: for every $x$ there is a natural number $n$ with $x^{n+1} = x^n$. A finite group is aperiodic only when it is trivial, since $x^{n+1} = x^n$ forces $x = 1$ when $x$ is invertible.

## Sources

- J.-É. Pin, *Mathematical Foundations of Automata Theory*, version of 24 March 2025, Chapter VI, §2, Proposition 2.1 (powers of an element of a finite monoid). The definition here is stated for arbitrary monoids, a generalisation of the finite case.
