# Eilenberg's variety theorem

For a [variety of finite monoids](varieties-of-finite-monoids.md) $\mathbf{V}$, let $\mathcal{V}(A^*)$ be the set of regular languages of $A^*$ whose
[syntactic monoid](../syntactic-monoid/syntactic-monoid.md) belongs to $\mathbf{V}$, equivalently the languages [recognized](../syntactic-monoid/recognizes.md) by a monoid of $\mathbf{V}$; then $\mathcal{V}$ is a
[variety of languages](varieties-of-languages.md). Conversely, to a variety of languages $\mathcal{V}$ associate the variety of finite monoids generated by
the syntactic monoids of the languages in $\mathcal{V}$. These two correspondences are mutually inverse bijections between varieties of finite monoids
and varieties of languages (Eilenberg); in particular $\mathbf{V} \subseteq \mathbf{W}$ if and only if $\mathcal{V}(A^*) \subseteq \mathcal{W}(A^*)$ for every
finite alphabet $A$.
