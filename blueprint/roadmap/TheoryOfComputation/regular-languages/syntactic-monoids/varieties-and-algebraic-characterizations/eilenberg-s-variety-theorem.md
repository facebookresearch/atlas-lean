# Eilenberg's variety theorem

For a [variety of finite monoids](varieties-of-finite-monoids.md) $\mathbf{V}$, let $\mathcal{V}(A^*)$ be the set of recognizable languages of $A^*$ whose
[syntactic monoid](../syntactic-monoid/syntactic-monoid.md) belongs to $\mathbf{V}$; then $\mathcal{V}$ is a
[$*$-variety of languages](varieties-of-languages.md). Conversely, to a $*$-variety of languages associate the variety of finite monoids generated by
the syntactic monoids of its languages. These correspondences are mutually inverse bijections between varieties of finite monoids and
$*$-varieties of languages (Eilenberg). The same holds for varieties of finite semigroups and $+$-varieties, and, for ordered monoids and semigroups,
with positive varieties.
