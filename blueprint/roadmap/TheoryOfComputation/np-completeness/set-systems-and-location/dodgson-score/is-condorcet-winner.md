# Is Condorcet Winner

$c$ is a Condorcet winner for the ballots $V$ over $m$ candidates when, writing the rank of a candidate on a ballot for the position of its first occurrence, or the length of the ballot if it is absent, there is a strict total order for each voter whose strict comparisons are exactly the comparisons of those ranks, and, when $c < m$, $c$ beats every other candidate below $m$ by strict pairwise majority. On the ballot $[1]$ at $m = 2$, the present candidate $1$ ranks above the absent candidate $0$; two absent candidates would tie and could not define a strict total order. For $c \ge m$ the majority tests are vacuous and only the existence of the orders is required (for $m = 1$, ballot $[0]$ and $c = 1$ the predicate holds). The usual reading, with each ballot a permutation of the candidates, is the validated case.
