# Feasible KURODOKO (KUROMASU) instances

A KURODOKO instance $(w, h, \mathrm{clues})$ is feasible when some set $B$ of black squares of the $h \times w$ grid satisfies: (1) no clue whose coordinates name a square of the grid is on a black square; (2) no two black squares are horizontally or vertically adjacent; (3) every two white squares are joined by a path of white squares (vacuous when no square is white; for example, the all-black filling of a clue-free one-cell board qualifies); (4) for each clue whose coordinates name a square $v$ of the grid, with value $N$, exactly $N$ squares are visible from $v$, where a square is visible from $v$ when it lies in the same row or column and every square of the segment between them, both ends included, is white. The clue square itself counts as visible. A clue whose coordinates lie off the board imposes no condition; [well-formedness](well-formed.md) requires every clue to name a square of the grid, and with it this is the usual puzzle.
