# STRONG INEQUIVALENCE OF IANOV SCHEMES (loop-free, trivial $P_2$) is NP-complete

The STRONG INEQUIVALENCE OF IANOV SCHEMES (loop-free, trivial $P_2$) decision problem lies in NP, and every decision problem in NP has a polynomial-time many-one reduction to it.

Instance: finite sets $F$ and $P$ of function and predicate symbols, a single variable $x$, and two Ianov schemes over $F$, $P$ and $x$, each a sequence $I_1, I_2, \ldots, I_m$ of instructions of the form $x \leftarrow f(x)$, "if $p(x)$ then goto $I_j$ else goto $I_k$", and halt, where $f \in F$ and $p \in P$. Each scheme is a nonempty list, every jump target satisfies $1 \le j, k \le m$, and its last instruction is not an assignment; these are the [scheme conventions](is-scheme-2.md) that the decision problem checks. Neither program contains any loops, and $P_2$ is the single instruction halt, the trivial program that leaves the value of $x$ unchanged. Question: are the two given Ianov schemes not strongly equivalent, i.e., is there a domain set $D$, an interpretation of each $f \in F$ as a function $f : D \to D$, an interpretation of each $p \in P$ as a function $p : D \to \{T, F\}$, and an initial value $x_0 \in D$ for $x$, such that either both schemes halt with different final values for $x$ or one halts and the other doesn't?
