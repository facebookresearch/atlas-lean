# Parenthesized grammar

The parenthesized grammar of $(n, s, P)$ for a number $k$: every production $A \to w$ is replaced by $A \to (w)$, where $($ is terminal $k$ and $)$ is terminal $k + 1$, and the nonterminals and initial symbol are unchanged. Terminals already in a production are kept as they are. If the old terminal labels are below $k$, the inserted parentheses are fresh. The output terminal labels are below $k + 2$ exactly when the old terminal labels are below $k + 2$: for $k = 1$ the production $S \to 1$ becomes $S \to 1\,1\,2$, a production over $\{0, 1, 2\}$ although its terminal was not below $1$, while a terminal $7$ survives and stays out of that range.
