# REGULAR GRAMMAR INEQUIVALENCE FOR FINITE LANGUAGES is NP-complete

The decision problem REGULAR GRAMMAR INEQUIVALENCE FOR FINITE LANGUAGES belongs to NP, and every decision problem in NP has a polynomial-time many-one reduction to it.

## Sources

- M. R. Garey and D. S. Johnson, *Computers and Intractability: A Guide to the Theory of NP-Completeness*, W. H. Freeman (1979), problem [AL14] REGULAR GRAMMAR INEQUIVALENCE, p. 269, comment: the general problem is NP-complete "if both grammars generate finite languages (a property that can be checked in polynomial time)" (no separate citation; the related [AL9] comment, p. 267, records that REGULAR EXPRESSION INEQUIVALENCE is NP-complete for star-free expressions, which denote finite languages, citing L. J. Stockmeyer and A. R. Meyer, "Word problems requiring exponential time (preliminary report)", STOC 1973, 1-9, doi:10.1145/800125.804029).
