# Feasible INCOMPLETE PERFECT PHYLOGENY HAPLOTYPING instances

An INCOMPLETE PERFECT PHYLOGENY HAPLOTYPING instance is feasible when some completion $c$ of the genotypes has an expansion $H$, two haplotypes $H(j, \mathrm{false})$ and $H(j, \mathrm{true})$ explaining each completed genotype $c_j$, and an unrooted binary phylogenetic tree whose leaves are the **indexed** haplotypes $(j, b)$, two leaves per genotype, that is a [perfect phylogeny](../../../../Biology/phylogenetics/splits-compatibility-ultrametrics-and-upgma/is-perfect-phylogeny.md) for $H$: every nonconstant site changes state on exactly one edge, and a constant site on none.

Because the leaves are indexed, equal haplotypes can sit at distinct leaves and an edge need not carry a site: a single completed genotype `[0]` has two distinct leaves, both labelled `[0]`, joined by an edge with no mutation. Kimmel and Shamir's definition (Definition 1, p. 363) asks instead for one leaf per distinct row label, a unique edge for each column and a column on every edge. Contracting the edges without a site and merging leaves with equal labels passes from one form to the other, and for at least one genotype both are equivalent to the four-gamete condition on the haplotype matrix. Unrooted means that no root haplotype is specified; it does not forbid a rooted explanation.
