# Nearly complete samples

For natural numbers $p$ and $q$, the sample is nearly complete for $(p, q)$ when for some $n$ every string of $S$ and $T$ has length at most $n$ and $(|\Sigma^{(n)}| - |S \cup T|)^q \le |\Sigma^{(n)}|^p$, where $|S \cup T|$ counts distinct strings and the subtraction is truncated at $0$. When $S \cup T \subseteq \Sigma^{(n)}$, the base on the left is $|\Sigma^{(n)} \setminus (S \cup T)|$. For $q > 0$ the condition reads $|\Sigma^{(n)} \setminus (S \cup T)| \le |\Sigma^{(n)}|^{\varepsilon}$ with $\varepsilon = p/q$. For $q = 0$ the inequality reads $1 \le |\Sigma^{(n)}|^p$, which always holds since $|\Sigma^{(n)}| \ge 1$, so every sample is nearly complete.
