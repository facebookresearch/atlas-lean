# Feasible CONSECUTIVE BLOCK MINIMIZATION instances

An instance $(n, A, K)$ of CONSECUTIVE BLOCK MINIMIZATION is feasible when some permutation of the columns yields a matrix $B$ with at most $K$ blocks of consecutive $1$'s, that is, at most $K$ positions $(r, j)$, $j < n$, with $b_{rj} = 1$ and either $j = n - 1$ or $b_{r,j+1} = 0$.
