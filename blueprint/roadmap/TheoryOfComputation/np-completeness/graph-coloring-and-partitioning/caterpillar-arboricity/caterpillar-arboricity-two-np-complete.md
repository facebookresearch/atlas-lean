# CATERPILLAR ARBORICITY AT MOST 2 is NP-complete

The following decision problem is NP-complete: it belongs to NP, and every decision problem in NP has a polynomial-time many-one reduction to it. Given a simple graph $G$ on $\{0, \dots, n - 1\}$, given by a vertex count $n$ and an edge list without repeated edges (in either orientation) or loops, decide whether the edges of $G$ can be split into two caterpillar forests, forests in which every tree has a path that every vertex of the tree lies on or is adjacent to.

## Sources

- T. C. Shermer, "On rectangle visibility graphs III. External visibility and complexity", Proceedings of the 8th Canadian Conference on Computational Geometry (CCCG '96), Ottawa (1996), 234-239 (Section 1: definitions of caterpillars, caterpillar forests and caterpillar arboricity; Theorem 3.2: "Caterpillar Arboricity 2 is NP-complete").
- ISGCI (Information System on Graph Classes and their Inclusions), class "caterpillar arboricity <= 2" (gc_732), problem Recognition, NP-complete, citing Shermer.
