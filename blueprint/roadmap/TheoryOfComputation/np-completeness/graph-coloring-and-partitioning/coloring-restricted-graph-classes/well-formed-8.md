# Well-formed instances of PRECOLORING EXTENSION ON INTERVAL GRAPHS

An instance $(n, E, c, K)$ of PRECOLORING EXTENSION (where an entry $c_v = 0$ means that vertex $v$ is uncolored and $c_v = k + 1$ means that it is precolored with color $k$) is well-formed for PRECOLORING EXTENSION ON INTERVAL GRAPHS when it is well-formed for PRECOLORING EXTENSION (the list $E$ has no repeated entries, and every pair $(u, v)$ in $E$ satisfies $u < n$, $v < n$, $u \ne v$ and $(v, u) \notin E$, and $c$ has length $n$); the graph on $\{0, \dots, n-1\}$ with edge list $E$ is an interval graph (there are closed real intervals, one per vertex, such that two distinct vertices are adjacent exactly when their intervals intersect); $K > 0$; every entry of $c$ is at most $K$, so every precolor is below $K$; and no edge joins two vertices with the same nonzero entry of $c$.
