# k-DIR graphs

For a natural number $k$, a simple graph on the vertices $0, \dots, n-1$ is a $k$-DIR graph when there are real slopes $s_0, \dots, s_{k-1}$ (not necessarily distinct) and, for each vertex $v$, a closed segment from $a_v$ to $b_v$ in $\mathbb{R}^2$ with $a_v$ strictly to the left of $b_v$ and slope one of the $s_j$, such that two distinct vertices are adjacent exactly when their segments have a common point. The segments are non-vertical, which loses nothing: a suitable rotation of the plane makes all of finitely many directions non-vertical.
