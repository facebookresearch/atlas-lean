# Three-index vehicle formulation of capacitated vehicle routing

Let customers $i = 1, \dots, n$ have demands $q_i \ge 0$ (zero allowed), let $0$ be the depot, let vehicles $k = 1, \dots, m$ have capacity $Q \ge 0$, and let $c_{ij}$ be arc costs. Use binary variables $x_{kij}$ (vehicle $k$ traverses arc $(i, j)$, $i \ne j$, over all nodes $0, \dots, n$), $y_{ki}$ (vehicle $k$ serves customer $i$) and $u_k$ (vehicle $k$ is used), with constraints: $\sum_k y_{ki} = 1$ for every customer; $\sum_j x_{kij} = \sum_j x_{kji} = y_{ki}$ for every vehicle and customer; $\sum_j x_{k0j} = \sum_j x_{kj0} = u_k$; $y_{ki} \le u_k$; $\sum_i q_i y_{ki} \le Q u_k$; and, for every vehicle $k$, nonempty set $S$ of customers and $h \in S$, $\sum_{i, j \in S} x_{kij} \le \sum_{i \in S \setminus \{h\}} y_{ki}$. Then the integral solutions correspond one-to-one to fleet plans in which each vehicle $k$ either stays at the depot ($u_k = 0$, no arcs and no customers) or drives one directed cycle $0 \to i_1 \to \dots \to i_r \to 0$ through $r \ge 1$ distinct customers ($u_k = 1$), every customer lies on exactly one vehicle's cycle, and every cycle's total demand is at most $Q$; the plan's cost is $\sum_{k, i, j} c_{ij} x_{kij}$. A single-customer route uses the two arcs $0 \to i \to 0$.

Proof. Given an integral solution, fix $k$ and take the arcs with $x_{kij} = 1$. At every node the in-degree equals the out-degree, namely $y_{ki}$ at customer $i$ and $u_k$ at the depot, and there are no loops, so these arcs form vertex-disjoint directed cycles through exactly the depot (when $u_k = 1$) and the customers with $y_{ki} = 1$. If $u_k = 0$ then every $y_{ki} = 0$ and there are no arcs. A cycle avoiding the depot would have a customer set $S$ with $|S|$ arcs inside $S$, while $\sum_{i \in S \setminus \{h\}} y_{ki} = |S| - 1$, violating the subtour constraint; so for $u_k = 1$ there is exactly one cycle, it passes through the depot, and it visits a customer because the depot has out-degree $1$. The assignment constraints put each customer on exactly one cycle and the capacity constraints bound each load. Conversely, setting the variables from a fleet plan satisfies the degree, assignment, linking and capacity constraints directly. For a subtour constraint with set $S$ and $h \in S$, let $V_k$ be the customers of vehicle $k$: the arcs of its cycle inside $S$ form vertex-disjoint paths covering $S \cap V_k$, because the cycle also passes through the depot, so there are at most $|S \cap V_k| - 1$ of them when $S \cap V_k$ is nonempty and none otherwise, and this is at most $\sum_{i \in S \setminus \{h\}} y_{ki}$ in each case.

The subtour constraints are needed even when every demand is zero: without them a used vehicle could also carry a separate cycle $i \to j \to i$ of customers. Every fleet plan is feasible for the linear relaxation, so its optimal value is a lower bound on the optimal routing cost; a fractional optimum is not a routing plan.
