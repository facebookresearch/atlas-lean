# Schaefer's six alternatives

For a sequence $S$ of stored relations, each a natural arity $k$ and a list of Boolean lists, read through its rows of length $k$ as in [relations](relation.md), one of Schaefer's six alternatives holds when every relation of $S$ contains the all-true tuple; or every relation of $S$ contains the all-false tuple; or every relation of $S$ is logically equivalent to a CNF expression over its coordinates $0, \dots, k - 1$ with at most one negated literal per clause; or every one is equivalent to such a CNF expression with at most one unnegated literal per clause; or every one is equivalent to such a CNF expression with at most two literals per clause; or every relation of $S$ is the solution set of a system of linear equations over $\mathrm{GF}(2)$ in its coordinates.

The alternatives are tested on the relations as given. Schaefer's dichotomy applies them to nonempty relations; an empty relation contains neither constant tuple, so a sequence containing one can fail all six alternatives while every instance that uses it is unsatisfiable. This predicate alone is therefore not the tractability criterion.
