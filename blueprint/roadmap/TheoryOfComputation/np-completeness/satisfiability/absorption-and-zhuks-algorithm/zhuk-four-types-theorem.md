# Zhuk's four-types theorem: binary absorption, center, polynomially complete quotient or affine quotient

Let $\mathbf{A}=(A;w)$ be a finite algebra with $|A|\ge 2$ whose operation $w$ is a **special WNU** of arity $m$ (an idempotent WNU satisfying $x\circ(x\circ y)=x\circ y$, where $x\circ y := w(x,\dots,x,y)$). Then at least one of the following holds (Zhuk, Theorem 5.1):

1. $\mathbf{A}$ has a proper binary absorbing subuniverse $B\subsetneq A$;
2. $\mathbf{A}$ has a proper center $C\subsetneq A$;
3. there is a congruence $\sigma\ne A\times A$ such that $\mathbf{A}/\sigma$ is polynomially complete;
4. there is a congruence $\sigma\ne A\times A$ such that $\mathbf{A}/\sigma\cong(\mathbb{Z}_p;\ x_1+\dots+x_m)$ for a prime $p$.

## Proof sketch

Induct on $|A|$. If there is no binary absorption, let $M$ be the clone generated by $w$ and all constants; if $M$ is everything, $\mathbf{A}$ is PC. Otherwise, by Rosenberg's classification $M$ lies in a maximal clone: preserving a bounded partial order, an equivalence relation, a quasi-linear (affine) relation, a central relation, or an $h$-regular relation (autodual and permutation cases are excluded by the constants). An order or a central relation yields a center; an equivalence relation yields a proper congruence to whose quotient the induction hypothesis applies (and binary absorption or a center lifts back); the affine case gives a $\mathbb{Z}_p$ quotient.
