# INEQUIVALENCE OF SIMPLE FUNCTIONS is NP-complete

The decision problem INEQUIVALENCE OF SIMPLE FUNCTIONS belongs to NP, and every decision problem
in NP has a polynomial-time many-one reduction to it.

## Sources

- M. R. Garey and D. S. Johnson, *Computers and Intractability: A Guide to the Theory of NP-Completeness*, W. H. Freeman (1979), problem [PO15] INEQUIVALENCE OF SIMPLE FUNCTIONS, p. 276 (reference [Tsichritzis, 1970]; transformation from INEQUIVALENCE OF LOOP PROGRAMS WITHOUT NESTING).
- D. Tsichritzis, "The equivalence problem of simple programs", Journal of the ACM 17 (1970), 729-738, doi:10.1145/321607.321621 (the simple functions are the functions of the loop programs without nesting, `L₁`).
- R. L. Constable, H. B. Hunt III and S. Sahni, "On the computational complexity of program scheme equivalence", Technical Report TR 74-201, Department of Computer Science, Cornell University (1974), https://hdl.handle.net/1813/6041, §4, Definition 4.2 and Theorem 4.1, pp. 62–64: a satisfying assignment distinguishes the constructed loop program from the program computing zero, and an unsatisfiable formula does not, so deciding **inequivalence** of such programs is NP-hard.
- R. L. Constable, H. B. Hunt III and S. Sahni, "On the computational complexity of program scheme equivalence", SIAM Journal on Computing 9 (1980), 396-416, doi:10.1137/0209031 (journal version of the report).
