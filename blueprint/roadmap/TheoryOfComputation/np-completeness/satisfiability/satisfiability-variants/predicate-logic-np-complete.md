# PREDICATE LOGIC WITHOUT NEGATION is NP-complete

The decision problem PREDICATE LOGIC WITHOUT NEGATION belongs to NP, and every decision problem in NP has a polynomial-time many-one reduction to it.

## Sources

- M. R. Garey and D. S. Johnson, *Computers and Intractability: A Guide to the Theory of NP-Completeness*, W. H. Freeman (1979), problem [LO15] PREDICATE LOGIC WITHOUT NEGATION, p. 263, comment: remains NP-complete even if there are no universal quantifiers, no relation symbols, and only two functions, both with dimension 0 (and hence constants).
- D. Kozen, "First order predicate logic without negation is NP-complete", Report 77-307, Department of Computer Science, Cornell University (1977); journal version: D. Kozen, "Positive first-order logic is NP-complete", IBM Journal of Research and Development 25 (1981), 327-332, doi:10.1147/rd.254.0327.
