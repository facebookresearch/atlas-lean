# Bulatov–Jeavons–Krokhin: a core with no Taylor polymorphism has an NP-complete CSP

Let $\mathbb{C}$ be a finite core structure and $\mathbb{C}^c$ its expansion by all singleton unary relations. If $\mathbb{C}^c$ has no Taylor polymorphism (equivalently, the variety generated by the idempotent algebra $(C;\mathrm{Pol}(\mathbb{C}^c))$ contains a two-element algebra all of whose operations are projections), then $\mathrm{CSP}(\mathbb{C})$ is NP-complete. In the language of pp-constructions: if $\mathbb{C}^c$ has no Taylor polymorphism then $\mathbb{K}_3$ (and indeed every finite structure, e.g. a template for 3-SAT) is pp-constructible from $\mathbb{C}$.

## Proof sketch

By Taylor's theorem, an idempotent locally finite variety without a Taylor term contains a two-element algebra $\mathbf{2}$ of projections, obtained from $(C;\mathrm{Pol}(\mathbb{C}^c))$ by taking a subalgebra of a finite power and a quotient (HSP). Every relation on $\{0,1\}$ is preserved by projections, so on the relational side the relation encoding 1-in-3 SAT is pp-interpretable in $\mathbb{C}^c$ through the corresponding pp-definitions and quotient; pp-interpretations give polynomial reductions, and constants are removed by the core lemma.
