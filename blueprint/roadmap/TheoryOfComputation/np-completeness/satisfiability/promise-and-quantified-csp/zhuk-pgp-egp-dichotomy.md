# Zhuk: every finite algebra has either polynomially or exponentially generated powers

For every finite algebra $\mathbf{A}$, exactly one of the following holds: $\mathbf{A}$ has the PGP property, or $\mathbf{A}$ has the EGP property. There is no intermediate growth of $f_{\mathbf{A}}(n)$ (such as $2^{\sqrt n}$ or $n^{\log n}$). For idempotent algebras Zhuk also gives a simple combinatorial criterion characterizing EGP, in terms of subalgebras and a projective-type behaviour on a pair of subsets (a "switchability" failure), which makes the property decidable for a given finite idempotent algebra.

## Proof sketch

If the generating sets are not polynomial, one finds a subalgebra and two subsets $B_1,B_2$ whose union is closed in a "projective" way, so that products of copies of $B_1\cup B_2$ behave like the Boolean algebra of all subsets and require exponentially many generators; otherwise, an idempotent algebra is shown to be switchable and its powers are generated by polynomially many tuples constructed from a bounded number of "switches" per tuple.
