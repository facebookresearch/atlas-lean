# Chain precedence (reachability form)

For an instance with jobs $0, \dots, n-1$ and a list $A$ of arcs, job $j$ precedes job $k$ when $k$ is reachable from $j$ by one or more arcs of $A$ passing only through jobs (arcs with an endpoint outside $0, \dots, n-1$ are not used), and $j$ is an immediate predecessor of $k$ when $j$ precedes $k$ and no job $c$ has $j$ preceding $c$ and $c$ preceding $k$. The arcs form chains when every job has at most one immediate successor and at most one immediate predecessor. On raw inputs these two bounds do not imply acyclicity: for one job and the loop $(0, 0)$, the job precedes itself and is its own intermediate job, so it has no immediate predecessor and the bounds hold. For a well-formed instance, whose arcs are acyclic, they say the precedence order is a union of chains.
