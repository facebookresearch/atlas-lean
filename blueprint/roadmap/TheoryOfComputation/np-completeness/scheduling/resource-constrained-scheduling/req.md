# Resource requirement of a task in RESOURCE CONSTRAINED SCHEDULING

An instance of RESOURCE CONSTRAINED SCHEDULING is a tuple $(m, r, B, T, D)$ of a number $m$ of processors, a number $r$ of resources, a list $B$ of natural numbers (the resource bounds), a list $T$ of lists of natural numbers (one row of requirements per task), and a deadline $D$. Neither $B$ nor any row is required to have $r$ entries; in a well-formed instance $B$ has exactly $r$ entries and so does every row. For a task $t$ and a natural number $k$, the requirement $R_k(t)$ of task $t$ for resource $k$ is the entry at position $k$ of the row of $t$, and $0$ when the row has no entry at position $k$. That every requirement is at most the corresponding bound is likewise a condition of a well-formed instance. For example, with $r = 1$ and $T = ((), (1, 9))$ the requirement of task $1$ for resource $1$ is $9$, and that of task $0$ for resource $0$ is $0$.
