# TWO-PROCESSOR UNIT-TIME JOB SHOP WITH CHAINS as a decision problem

The decision problem TWO-PROCESSOR UNIT-TIME JOB SHOP WITH CHAINS accepts a bitstring $x$ exactly when $x$ decodes, under the canonical bitstring encoding of pairs of a job-shop instance $(m, J, D)$ and a list of pairs, to an instance that is well-formed (the job-shop instance is well-formed ($m > 0$, every job has at least one task, processors in $\{1, \dots, m\}$, consecutive tasks of a job on different processors, last component positive), $m = 2$, every task has length $1$, every arc names two jobs, no two distinct arcs share a tail or a head, and the arcs have no directed cycle) and feasible for TWO-PROCESSOR UNIT-TIME JOB SHOP WITH CHAINS. It rejects every bitstring that does not decode to such an instance.
