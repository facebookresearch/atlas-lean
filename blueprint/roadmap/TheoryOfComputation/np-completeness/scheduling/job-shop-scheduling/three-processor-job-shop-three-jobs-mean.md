# THREE-PROCESSOR JOB-SHOP MEAN FLOW TIME WITH THREE JOBS as a decision problem

The decision problem THREE-PROCESSOR JOB-SHOP MEAN FLOW TIME WITH THREE JOBS accepts a bitstring $x$ exactly when $x$ decodes, under the canonical bitstring encoding of triples $(m, J, D)$ (a natural number, a list of lists of pairs of natural numbers and a natural number), to an instance that is well-formed for THREE-PROCESSOR JOB-SHOP SCHEDULING WITH THREE JOBS ($m > 0$, every job has at least one task, every task's processor lies in $\{1, \dots, m\}$, consecutive tasks of a job use different processors, and the last component is positive, $m = 3$, exactly three jobs) and feasible for TWO-PROCESSOR JOB-SHOP MEAN FLOW TIME (a schedule respecting processors and job order whose job completion times sum to at most $K$, the last component). It rejects every bitstring that does not decode to such an instance.
