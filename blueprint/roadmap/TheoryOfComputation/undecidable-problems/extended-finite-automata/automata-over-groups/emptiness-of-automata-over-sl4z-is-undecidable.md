# Emptiness and universality of finite automata over SL(4, ℤ) are undecidable

For finite automata equipped with a register holding a 4×4 integer matrix of determinant 1, which accept when the register returns to the identity, no algorithm decides whether a given automaton accepts some word, nor whether it accepts every word.

**Automata over a group.** Let $G$ be a [group](../../../../GroupTheory/group-basics/group.md) with identity $e$. A *finite automaton over $G$* (a $G$-automaton, or extended finite automaton over $G$) is a tuple $\mathcal{E} = (Q, \Sigma, G, \delta, q_1, Q_a)$ with a finite set $Q$ of states, a finite input alphabet $\Sigma$, an initial state $q_1$, accepting states $Q_a \subseteq Q$ and a transition function $\delta : Q \times (\Sigma \cup \{\varepsilon\}) \to \mathcal{P}(Q \times G)$ with finitely many nonempty values, each finite. If $(q', g) \in \delta(q, \sigma)$, then in state $q$, reading $\sigma$ (or nothing), the automaton may move to $q'$ and replace the register content $x$ by $x g$. The register starts at $e$, and a word is accepted if some run reads it completely, ends in an accepting state, and leaves the register equal to $e$. The *emptiness problem* asks whether a given $G$-automaton accepts no word, and the *universe problem* whether it accepts every word over its alphabet. For matrix groups the group elements on the transitions are given by their integer entries.

**Theorem (Salehi).** For the group $G = \mathrm{SL}(4, \mathbb{Z})$ of $4 \times 4$ integer matrices of determinant $1$, the emptiness problem and the universe problem for $G$-automata are undecidable (Theorem 3.45 of the source).

## Expansion

If emptiness for $G$-automata were decidable, so would be the subsemigroup membership problem for $G$ (Theorem 3.38 of the source): an automaton that first multiplies the register by $g^{-1}$ and then by any product of the generators $h_1, \dots, h_n$ of the subsemigroup accepts some word exactly when $g$ lies in the subsemigroup. The universe problem is treated the same way with a one-letter alphabet (Theorem 3.44). Mikhailova (1958, as cited in the thesis) proved that the subgroup membership problem for the direct product $F_2 \times F_2$ of two free groups of rank $2$ is undecidable, and $F_2 \times F_2$ embeds in $\mathrm{SL}(4, \mathbb{Z})$; a subgroup is the subsemigroup generated by its generators and their inverses. Mikhailova's theorem and the embedding have no pages in this subject.
