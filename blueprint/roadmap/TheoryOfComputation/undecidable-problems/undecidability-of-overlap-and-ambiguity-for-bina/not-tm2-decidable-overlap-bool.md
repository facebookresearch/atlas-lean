# Overlap of binary context-free grammars is undecidable

An encoded context-free grammar over a terminal alphabet $T$ consists of a start nonterminal, a natural number, and a list of productions, each a nonterminal number with a right-hand side listing nonterminal numbers and terminals of $T$. Its language is the set of terminal words derivable from the start nonterminal. A grammar is ambiguous when some terminal word has two leftmost derivations from the start symbol using different lists of rules. The Post correspondence problem over the Booleans asks, for a finite list of pairs of binary words, whether some nonempty sequence of indices makes the concatenation of first components equal to the concatenation of second components. A predicate $P$ on encoded objects is decidable by a multi-stack Turing machine when some function $f$ on natural numbers, computed by such a machine with numbers written in binary, satisfies $f(\operatorname{code}(a)) = 1 \iff P(a)$ for every object $a$; nothing is required of $f$ on numbers that are not codes. Whether two encoded context-free grammars over the fixed two-letter alphabet of Booleans have a common word in their languages is not decidable by any multi-stack Turing machine.
