# The unary word map is injective

The index of the source alphabet, the disjoint union of the two Booleans and the natural numbers, is the numbering that sends false to $0$, true to $1$ and a natural number $n$ to $n + 2$. The unary code of a letter with index $i$ is the binary word $1^i 0$ (true repeated $i$ times, then false); the unary word map sends a word to the concatenation of the unary codes of its letters. This word map, from words over the infinite source alphabet to binary words, is injective.
