# The Post correspondence problem reduces to overlap of binary grammars

An encoded context-free grammar over a terminal alphabet $T$ consists of a start nonterminal, a natural number, and a list of productions, each a nonterminal number with a right-hand side listing nonterminal numbers and terminals of $T$. Its language is the set of terminal words derivable from the start nonterminal. A grammar is ambiguous when some terminal word has two leftmost derivations from the start symbol using different lists of rules. The Post correspondence problem over the Booleans asks, for a finite list of pairs of binary words, whether some nonempty sequence of indices makes the concatenation of first components equal to the concatenation of second components. The Post correspondence problem over the Booleans has a computable many-one reduction to the problem of whether two encoded context-free grammars over the Booleans have a common word in their languages.
