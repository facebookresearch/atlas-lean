# Satisfiability of probability logic with summation and free range variables is undecidable

For the logics comparing polynomials in probabilities, with summation over values and free value variables read universally, satisfiability of finite sequents is undecidable, both in purely probabilistic and in causal form and both over all models and over the models in which every variable has the same finite number $N$ of values, $N$ varying from model to model.

**Languages (Ibeling, Icard and Mossé).** Each random variable $V$ has constant symbols $c^V_1, c^V_2, \dots$ and range variables $v_1, v_2, \dots$ standing for its values. Base formulas are Boolean combinations of atoms $V = v$ and $V = c^V_i$. Probability terms are built from conditional probabilities $\mathbf{P}(\delta \mid \delta')$ of base formulas by summation $\sum_{v_i} t$ over a range variable, sum, product, negation and the constants and range variables themselves; formulas of $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{prob}}$ are Boolean combinations of comparisons $t \succsim t'$ of probability terms and of equalities between constants and range variables. The causal language $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{causal}}$ also allows interventional base formulas $[\mathbf{X} = \mathbf{x}]\,\delta$, evaluated in the model in which the equation of each intervened variable is replaced by the constant function with its value (Definitions 3 and 4 of the source; for finite models this is the [intervention on a set of nodes](../../../ComputerScience/machine-learning/probabilistic-graphical-models/structural-causal-models-and-counterfactuals/do-set-2.md)).

**Semantics.** Formulas are evaluated in *models*: recursive (acyclic), measurable structural causal models in which the range of every variable is a subset of $\mathbb{Z}^+ = \{1, 2, 3, \dots\}$ (*countable*), together with an interpretation of each constant symbol of $V$ as a value of $V$ that is onto the range of $V$ (*closed-world*). A range-variable assignment is a partial map sending $v_i$ to a value of $V$. Terms take values in $\mathbb{R} \cup \{\infty, -\infty, \bot\}$: constants and range variables denote their values in $\mathbb{Z}^+$; $\mathbf{P}(\delta \mid \delta') = P(\delta \wedge \delta')/P(\delta')$ if $P(\delta') \ne 0$ and $\bot$ otherwise; an operation with an operand $\bot$ gives $\bot$; $0 \cdot (\pm\infty) = 0$ and $\infty + (-\infty) = 0$; and a sum whose partial sums tend to $\infty$ or $-\infty$ takes that value. The source says that the syntax rules out other divergent sums, but partial sums can oscillate (for $\sum_{v} \big(v \cdot (v + 2) \cdot \mathbf{P}(V = v) - 2\big)$ with $P(V = 2k) = 1/(k(k+1))$ and odd values null, they alternate between $-2$ and $0$), and the source gives such sums no value. The Theorem holds whatever value they get, since the reduction in the Expansion uses no summation, and over $\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{fin}}$ every sum is finite. The comparison $t \succsim t'$ holds when neither side is $\bot$ and $t \ge t'$, and $\neg\varphi$ holds when $\varphi$ does not; so a comparison with a side conditioned on a null event fails, and its negation holds (Definitions 5–8). A formula is valid in a model if it holds under every assignment of values to its free range variables, so free range variables are read universally. A model satisfies a sequent $\Gamma \Rightarrow \varphi$ if some formula of $\Gamma$ is not valid in it or $\varphi$ is valid in it. $\mathsf{SAT}_{\mathcal{L}}$ asks, for a finite set $\Gamma$ and a formula $\varphi$ of $\mathcal{L}$, whether some model in a fixed class satisfies $\Gamma \Rightarrow \varphi$. The classes are $\mathcal{M}$, all models, and $\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{fin}} = \bigcup_{N \ge 1} \mathcal{M}_N$, where $\mathcal{M}_N$ consists of the models in which every variable has a range of exactly $N$ values; $N$ may vary between models but not between the variables of one model (Definition 10).

**Theorem (Ibeling, Icard and Mossé).** $\mathsf{SAT}_{\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{prob}}}$ and $\mathsf{SAT}_{\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{causal}}}$ are undecidable, both over $\mathcal{M}$ and over $\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{fin}}$ (Theorem 2 of the source).

Over $\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{fin}}$ both problems are recursively enumerable (Theorem 4). When every variable has the same fixed number $N$ of values, satisfiability for $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{causal}}(N)$ is complete for the class succ$\exists\mathbb{R}$ (Theorem 3), so undecidability comes from leaving the ranges unbounded together with the universal reading of free range variables.

## Expansion

The proof encodes the conditional independence implication problem. A [conditional independence](../../../Probability/random-fields/conditional-independence-on-finite-configuration/cond-indep.md) statement $\mathbf{X} \perp \mathbf{Y} \mid \mathbf{Z}$ becomes the formula $p$: $\mathbf{P}(\mathbf{x} \wedge \mathbf{y} \wedge \mathbf{z}) \cdot \mathbf{P}(\mathbf{z}) \approx \mathbf{P}(\mathbf{x} \wedge \mathbf{z}) \cdot \mathbf{P}(\mathbf{y} \wedge \mathbf{z})$ with the value variables left free. It conditions on nothing, so it holds at every value, null ones included, exactly when the conditional independence does; the source writes $\mathbf{P}(\mathbf{x} \wedge \mathbf{y} \mid \mathbf{z}) \approx \mathbf{P}(\mathbf{x} \mid \mathbf{z}) \cdot \mathbf{P}(\mathbf{y} \mid \mathbf{z})$, which under the semantics above fails at every value of $\mathbf{z}$ of probability $0$. An implication $\bigwedge_{i \ge 1} (\mathbf{X}_i \perp \mathbf{Y}_i \mid \mathbf{Z}_i) \Rightarrow (\mathbf{X}_0 \perp \mathbf{Y}_0 \mid \mathbf{Z}_0)$ then fails exactly when some model validates $p_1, \dots, p_\ell$ and some assignment falsifies $p_0$. Replacing the free range variables of $p_0$ by fresh constant symbols of the matching variables gives a closed formula $p_0^c$, and the implication fails exactly when the sequent $\Rightarrow p_1 \wedge \dots \wedge p_\ell \wedge \neg p_0^c$ is satisfiable. Li proved that this implication problem is undecidable for random variables with finite, unbounded supports, and also for discrete random variables (Theorem 1 of the source, quoting Li's Theorem 3 and its footnote). Over $\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{fin}}$, pad every range with values of probability $0$ to a common size $N$; each $p_i$ still holds there, since both sides vanish at such values. Over $\mathcal{M}$, whose ranges may be countably infinite, the reduction starts from the discrete form of Li's theorem. A good expansion adds and links a page for Li's theorem.
