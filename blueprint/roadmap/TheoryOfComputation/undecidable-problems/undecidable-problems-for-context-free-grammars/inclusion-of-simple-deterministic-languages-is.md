# Inclusion of simple languages is undecidable

Language inclusion is undecidable already for simple grammars, the context-free grammars in Greibach normal form with at most one production for each pair of a nonterminal and a terminal.

**Simple grammars.** A [context-free grammar](../../context-free-languages/grammars-and-pushdown-automata/context-free-languages.md) is in *strict Greibach normal form* if every production has the form $A \to a\alpha$ with $a$ a terminal and $\alpha$ a string of nonterminals, with no production of the empty word (unlike the linked general Greibach normal form, which allows the start symbol to produce the empty word). It is *simple* if it is in strict Greibach normal form and for every nonterminal $A$ and terminal $a$ there is at most one production $A \to a\alpha$. The languages generated by simple grammars are the *simple languages*.

**Theorem (Friedman).** There is no algorithm that decides, given two simple grammars $G_1$ and $G_2$, whether $L(G_1) \subseteq L(G_2)$.

This sharpens the [undecidability of inclusion for context-free languages](exists-not-tm2-decidable-language-le.md) to a deterministic subclass. Since trace preorder and simulation preorder coincide on deterministic processes, the theorem gives the undecidability of simulation preorder and equivalence for BPA processes.

## Expansion

The survey read states the theorem without proof; the results Friedman's original proof uses are those a good expansion links. Simple grammars give deterministic BPA processes, on which trace preorder and simulation preorder coincide; the survey derives the undecidability of these preorders for BPA from this theorem.
