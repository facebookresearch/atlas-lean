# Mortality for seven 3 × 3 matrices and freeness for fourteen nonnegative 3 × 3 matrices are undecidable

Claus instances of the Post correspondence problem give undecidability of mortality for semigroups generated by seven $3 \times 3$ integer matrices or by two $21 \times 21$ integer matrices, and of freeness for semigroups generated by fourteen $3 \times 3$ matrices with nonnegative integer entries.

For a finite set of $n \times n$ integer matrices generating a semigroup $S$, the *mortality problem* asks whether the zero matrix lies in $S$, and the *freeness problem* asks whether $S$ is free, that is, whether every element of $S$ has a unique factorization as a product of the generators.

**Theorem (Halava, Harju and Hirvensalo).**

1. There is a semigroup generated by six fixed $3 \times 3$ integer matrices such that it is undecidable, for a given $3 \times 3$ integer matrix $A$, whether $AM = 0$ for some $M$ in the semigroup; in particular, mortality is undecidable for semigroups generated by seven $3 \times 3$ integer matrices.
2. Mortality is undecidable for semigroups generated by two $21 \times 21$ integer matrices.
3. It is undecidable whether the semigroup generated by fourteen $3 \times 3$ matrices with nonnegative integer entries is free.

The same paper shows that it is undecidable whether the semigroup generated by seven $3 \times 3$ integer matrices contains a matrix with upper left entry $0$, and that vector reachability is undecidable for six $3 \times 3$ integer matrices. The mortality bounds were later improved to six $3 \times 3$ and two $18 \times 18$ matrices (Neary), using the undecidability of the Post correspondence problem for five pairs of words.

## Expansion

All three bounds use Paterson's injective coding of pairs of words by $3 \times 3$ integer matrices applied to Claus instances of size $7$, for which the Post correspondence problem is undecidable. For item 1, the six fixed generators encode the letters $b_2, \dots, b_7$ of a Claus instance and the input matrix encodes $b_1$, which carries the only variable word. Item 2 follows from item 1 by the general fact that undecidable mortality for $k$ matrices of dimension $n$ gives undecidable mortality for two matrices of dimension $nk$. Item 3 reduces the mixed Post correspondence problem, which the paper shows undecidable for Claus instances of size $7$, by the construction of Cassaigne, Harju and Karhumäki; none of these intermediate problems has a page here.
