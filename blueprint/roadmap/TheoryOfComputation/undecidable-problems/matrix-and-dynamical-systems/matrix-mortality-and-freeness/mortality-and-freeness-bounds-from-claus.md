# Mortality for seven 3 × 3 integer matrices and two 21 × 21 integer matrices is undecidable

Claus instances of the Post correspondence problem give undecidability of mortality for semigroups generated by seven $3 \times 3$ integer matrices, six of them fixed, and by two $21 \times 21$ integer matrices.

For a finite set of $n \times n$ integer matrices generating a semigroup $S$, the *mortality problem* asks whether the zero matrix lies in $S$.

**Theorem (Halava, Harju and Hirvensalo).**

1. There is a semigroup generated by six fixed $3 \times 3$ integer matrices such that it is undecidable, for a given $3 \times 3$ integer matrix $A$, whether $AM = 0$ for some $M$ in the semigroup; in particular, mortality is undecidable for semigroups generated by seven $3 \times 3$ integer matrices.
2. Mortality is undecidable for semigroups generated by two $21 \times 21$ integer matrices.

The same paper shows that it is undecidable whether the semigroup generated by seven $3 \times 3$ integer matrices contains a matrix with upper left entry $0$, that vector reachability is undecidable for six $3 \times 3$ integer matrices, and that it is undecidable whether the semigroup generated by fourteen $3 \times 3$ matrices with nonnegative integer entries is free, a bound later lowered to thirteen ([freeness of 13 natural 3×3 matrices is undecidable](freeness-of-3-3-natural-matrices-with-13.md)). The mortality bounds were later improved to six $3 \times 3$ and two $18 \times 18$ matrices ([mortality is undecidable for six 3 × 3 or two 18 × 18 integer matrices](mortality-is-undecidable-for-six-3-3-or-two-18-1.md)), using the undecidability of the Post correspondence problem for five pairs of words.

## Expansion

An instance of the Post correspondence problem is given by two morphisms $h, g \colon \Sigma^* \to \Gamma^*$ and asks for a nonempty word $w$ with $h(w) = g(w)$; its *size* is $|\Sigma|$. Let $\Gamma = \{a, b\}$, $d = aba$ and $A = ab^2 b^* a$. An instance with $\Sigma = \{b_1, \dots, b_n\}$ is a *Claus instance* if $h(b_i) \in (dA)^*$ for all $i$ with $h(b_n) = dd$, and $g(b_i) \in (Ad)^*$ for all $i$ with $g(b_1) = d$ and $g(b_n) \in (Ad)^+ d$; every nonempty solution of a Claus instance has the form $b_1 u b_n$ with $u$ over $\{b_2, \dots, b_{n-1}\}$. Claus showed that if some semi-Thue system with $n$ rules has an undecidable individual word problem, then the Post correspondence problem is undecidable for Claus instances of size $n + 4$; with the three-rule system this gives size $7$, and in fact a word $v_1$ and six fixed pairs $(u_2, v_2), \dots, (u_7, v_7)$ over $\{a, b\}$ such that it is undecidable, for a given word $u_1$, whether $u_1 u_{i_2} \cdots u_{i_m} u_7 = v_1 v_{i_2} \cdots v_{i_m} v_7$ for some $i_2, \dots, i_m \in \{2, \dots, 6\}$. For item 1, Paterson's injective coding of pairs of words by $3 \times 3$ integer matrices turns the six fixed pairs into the six fixed generators and the input pair into the matrix $A$. Item 2 follows from item 1 by the general fact that undecidable mortality for $k$ matrices of dimension $n$ gives undecidable mortality for two matrices of dimension $nk$. A good expansion adds and links pages for Claus's reduction, for the Post correspondence problem on Claus instances of size $7$, and for Paterson's coding.
