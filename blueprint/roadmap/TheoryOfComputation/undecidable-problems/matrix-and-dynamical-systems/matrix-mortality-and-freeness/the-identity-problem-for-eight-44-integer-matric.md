# The identity problem for eight 4×4 integer matrices of determinant one is undecidable

No algorithm decides whether the semigroup generated by eight given $4 \times 4$ integer matrices of determinant one contains the identity matrix,
while for $2 \times 2$ integer matrices and for the Heisenberg groups the identity problem is decidable.

For a finite set $G$ of square matrices, let $\langle G \rangle$ be the semigroup generated by $G$, the set of all nonempty finite products of elements of $G$. The *identity problem* asks whether the identity matrix
belongs to $\langle G \rangle$. $\mathrm{SL}(n, \mathbb{Z})$ denotes the group of $n \times n$ integer matrices with determinant $1$.

**Theorem.**

1. Given eight matrices in $\mathrm{SL}(4, \mathbb{Z})$, it is undecidable whether the semigroup they generate contains the identity matrix.
2. Given eight $4 \times 4$ integer matrices, it is undecidable whether the semigroup they generate contains a diagonal matrix.
3. Given eight $2 \times 2$ matrices over the rational quaternions, it is undecidable whether the semigroup they generate contains the identity matrix.

In contrast, the identity problem is NP-complete in $\mathrm{SL}(2, \mathbb{Z})$ (Bell, Hirvensalo and Potapov), and the source shows it is decidable in polynomial time for the Heisenberg group $\mathrm{H}(3, \mathbb{Q})$ and
decidable for the Heisenberg groups $\mathrm{H}(n, \mathbb{Q})$ in every dimension; it also shows that pairs of words cannot be embedded into $\mathrm{SL}(3, \mathbb{Z})$, so the classical technique does not reach dimension $3$.

## Expansion

The proof reduces the Post correspondence problem. Words over a group alphabet are embedded into $\mathrm{SL}(2, \mathbb{Z})$ through the free subgroup generated by $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ and $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 2 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$, and pairs of
words $(u, v)$ become block-diagonal matrices in $\mathrm{SL}(4, \mathbb{Z})$ with blocks for $u$ and $v$. Pairs with border letters build a word $w$ in both components and then cancel it by inverses of the images of an instance $(g, h)$, so a
designated pair is generated exactly when the instance has a solution; a new technique exploiting the anti-diagonal entries turns reaching that pair into reaching the identity, with eight generators in all, since the Post correspondence problem is undecidable already for five pairs (Neary) and the construction adds three matrices. The identity is then
the only diagonal matrix of the semigroup, which gives item 2, and replacing the matrix embedding by rational quaternions gives item 3.
