# Whether two nonnegative matrices generate a semigroup of constant spectral radius is undecidable

No algorithm decides, given two nonnegative rational square matrices, whether every matrix of the semigroup they generate has the same nonzero spectral radius; for irreducible families of rational matrices the property can be recognized in polynomial time.

For a square matrix $A$ let $\rho(A)$ be its spectral radius, the largest modulus of an eigenvalue. For a compact family $\mathcal{A}$ of real $d \times d$ matrices let $\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}}$ be the closure of the multiplicative semigroup generated by $\mathcal{A}$. A matrix semigroup $\mathcal{S}$ has *constant spectral radius* (is c.s.r.) if all its elements have the same spectral radius and this radius is nonzero, necessarily equal to $1$; a family $\mathcal{A}$ is c.s.r. if $\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}}$ is.

**Theorem (Protasov and Voynov).** The following problem is undecidable: given $d \ge 1$ and two $d \times d$ matrices $A_1, A_2$ with nonnegative rational entries, is the family $\{A_1, A_2\}$ c.s.r.? (Theorem 8 of the source.) The dimension $d$ is part of the input.

For irreducible families of rational matrices, those with no common nontrivial invariant subspace, and for positively irreducible families of nonnegative rational matrices, the same source decides the c.s.r. property in polynomial time (Theorem 7).

## Expansion

Given nonnegative $d \times d$ matrices $A_1, A_2$, form the $(d+1) \times (d+1)$ matrices $A_i'$ with diagonal blocks $(1)$ and $A_i$ and zeros elsewhere. A nonnegative semigroup is c.s.r. exactly when, in its block triangular factorization into positively irreducible parts, one part is c.s.r. and every other part has joint spectral radius at most $1$ (Theorem 4 of the source). So $\{A_1', A_2'\}$ is c.s.r. if and only if the joint spectral radius of $\{A_1, A_2\}$ is at most $1$. That inequality is undecidable for pairs of nonnegative rational matrices by results of Blondel and Tsitsiklis; the linked page states it for pairs of rational matrices, and a good expansion links pages for the nonnegative refinement and for the block characterization (Theorem 4 of the source).
