# Whether two nonnegative matrices generate a semigroup of constant spectral radius is undecidable

No algorithm decides, given two nonnegative rational square matrices, whether every matrix of the semigroup they generate has the same nonzero spectral radius; for irreducible families the property can be recognized in polynomial time.

For a square matrix $A$ let $\rho(A)$ be its spectral radius, the largest modulus of an eigenvalue. For a compact family $\mathcal{A}$ of real $d \times d$ matrices let $\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}}$ be the closure of the multiplicative semigroup generated by $\mathcal{A}$. A matrix semigroup $\mathcal{S}$ has *constant spectral radius* (is c.s.r.) if all its elements have the same spectral radius and this radius is nonzero, necessarily equal to $1$; a family $\mathcal{A}$ is c.s.r. if $\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}}$ is.

**Theorem (Protasov and Voynov).** The following problem is undecidable: given two $d \times d$ matrices $A_1, A_2$ with nonnegative rational entries, is the family $\{A_1, A_2\}$ c.s.r.? (Theorem 8 of the source.)

For irreducible families, those with no common nontrivial invariant subspace, the same source shows that the c.s.r. property can be decided in polynomial time.

## Expansion

Given nonnegative $d \times d$ matrices $A_1, A_2$, form the $(d+1) \times (d+1)$ matrices $A_i'$ with diagonal blocks $(1)$ and $A_i$ and zeros elsewhere. A nonnegative semigroup is c.s.r. exactly when, in its block triangular factorization into positively irreducible parts, one part is c.s.r. and every other part has joint spectral radius at most $1$ (Theorem 4 of the source). So $\{A_1', A_2'\}$ is c.s.r. if and only if the joint spectral radius of $\{A_1, A_2\}$ is at most $1$. That inequality is undecidable for pairs of nonnegative rational matrices by results of Blondel and Tsitsiklis; the linked page states it for pairs of rational matrices, and the nonnegative refinement and the block characterization (Theorem 4 of the source) have no pages.
