# Weyl matrices and the quantum Fourier lift

Let $\zeta$ be a primitive $d$-th root of unity, $D=\mathrm{diag}(1,\zeta,\dots,\zeta^{d-1})$ and $S$ the cyclic shift on $\mathbb{C}^d$. The Weyl matrices $D^aS^b$, $a,b\in\mathbb{Z}/d$, satisfy $SD=\zeta DS$, multiply like monomials up to scalars, and span all $d\times d$ matrices (finite Fourier inversion). Assigning the Weyl unit of $(a,b)$ to the exponent $(a,b)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}$ (lexicographically ordered) turns Hahn series with coefficients along monomial lines into a division ring that receives the Fourier lift of every matrix; this lifts matrix substitutions to a skew field in which every admissible nonzero rational formula becomes invertible.

## Proof sketch

The commutation relation and $D^d=S^d=I$ are direct computations; linear independence of the $d^2$ Weyl matrices follows from orthogonality of characters. Hahn series over a skew-commutative monomial system with a well-ordered support form a division ring by the usual Malcev–Neumann construction.
