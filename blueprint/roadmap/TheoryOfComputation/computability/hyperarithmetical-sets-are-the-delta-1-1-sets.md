# Kleene's theorem: the $\Delta^1_1$ sets are the hyperarithmetical sets

A set $A \subseteq \mathbb{N}$ is $\Delta^1_1$-definable in the language of second-order arithmetic if
and only if $A$ is hyperarithmetical (Kleene, 1955). Here, in the characterization of the cited source, a set is hyperarithmetical when it is
Turing reducible to an iterate $\emptyset^{(\alpha)}$ of the Turing jump along a notation for an
ordinal $\alpha$ in Kleene's system $\mathcal{O}$ of constructive ordinal notations, that is, computable
from a transfinite iteration of the jump below the first non-recursive ordinal $\omega_1^{CK}$.

## Expansion

A sound expansion fixes the language of second-order arithmetic and the analytical hierarchy, Kleene's
system $\mathcal{O}$ of ordinal notations, and the iterated jumps along notations in $\mathcal{O}$, with the
fact, from a source fixed for that page, that their Turing degree depends only on the ordinal denoted; it then has
separate pages for the two inclusions, and for Kleene's normal form theorem for $\Pi^1_1$ sets, which
the proof uses.
