# Back-and-forth systems

A *back-and-forth system* of depth $n$ between $\mathfrak{A}$ and $\mathfrak{B}$ is a sequence $I_n \subseteq I_{n-1} \subseteq \dots \subseteq I_0$ of nonempty sets of partial isomorphisms (given by finite tuples) such that for every $j < n$ and every $p \in I_{j+1}$: (forth) for every $a \in A$ there is $b \in B$ with $p \cup \{(a,b)\} \in I_j$, and (back) for every $b \in B$ there is $a \in A$ with $p \cup \{(a,b)\} \in I_j$. An *unbounded* back-and-forth system is a single nonempty set of partial isomorphisms with the back and forth properties at every member.
