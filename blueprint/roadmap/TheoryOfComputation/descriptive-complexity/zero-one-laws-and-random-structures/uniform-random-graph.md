# The uniform random graph $G(n, 1/2)$ and $G(n, p)$

For $n \in \mathbb{N}$, $G(n, 1/2)$ is the uniform distribution on the $2^{\binom n2}$ labelled simple graphs with vertex set $\{0,\dots,n-1\}$; for $0 < p < 1$, the binomial random graph $G(n,p)$ includes each of the $\binom n2$ possible edges independently with probability $p$. For a property $P$ of graphs, $\mu_n(P) = \Pr[G(n,1/2) \in P]$ is the fraction of labelled graphs on $n$ vertices with $P$; for a first-order sentence $\varphi$ in the language of graphs, $\mu_n(\varphi)$ is the probability that $G(n,1/2) \models \varphi$. A property exchanged with its negation by an involution on labelled graphs has $\mu_n = 1/2$.
