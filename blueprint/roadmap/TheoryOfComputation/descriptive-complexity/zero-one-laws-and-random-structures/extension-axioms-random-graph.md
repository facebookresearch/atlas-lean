# Extension axioms and the theory of the random graph

For $k \in \mathbb{N}$ and a pattern $s \in \{0,1\}^k$, the *extension axiom* $\mathrm{EA}_{k,s}$ is the first-order sentence in the language of graphs saying: for all pairwise distinct $x_1,\dots,x_k$ there is $z$ distinct from all $x_i$ such that $z$ is adjacent to $x_i$ exactly when $s_i = 1$. The *theory of the random graph* $T_{\mathrm{rg}}$ consists of the axioms of simple graphs (irreflexive symmetric adjacency) and all extension axioms. For a general finite relational vocabulary, extension axioms prescribe the full atomic type of a new element $z$ over $x_1,\dots,x_k$.
