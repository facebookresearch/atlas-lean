# The 0-1 law for $G(n,p)$ with constant edge probability

Fix $0 < p < 1$. For every first-order sentence $\varphi$ in the language of graphs, $\Pr[G(n,p) \models \varphi]$ converges to $0$ or $1$, and the limit is $1$ exactly when $\varphi$ holds in the Rado graph. In particular the almost-sure first-order theory of $G(n,p)$ does not depend on the constant $p$.

## Proof sketch

The extension axioms hold almost surely in $G(n,p)$ for every constant $p$; the rest of the argument is measure-independent.
