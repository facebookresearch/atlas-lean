# Trees, acyclicity and connectivity are not first-order: Hanf locality bounds

No first-order sentence defines the class of finite trees (connected acyclic graphs), nor the class of finite acyclic graphs, nor connectivity; more quantitatively, if a sentence of quantifier rank $n$ agrees with connectivity (respectively being a tree) on all graphs with fewer than $N$ vertices, then $N$ is bounded by a function of $n$ only. The witnesses are a long cycle versus a disjoint union of two long cycles, and a long path versus a path plus a disjoint long cycle, which are Hanf-equivalent at any fixed radius once the cycles are long enough.

## Proof sketch

All elements of long cycles and of the interiors of long paths have isomorphic neighborhoods of radius $d$, so the required bijections exist; apply Hanf locality.
