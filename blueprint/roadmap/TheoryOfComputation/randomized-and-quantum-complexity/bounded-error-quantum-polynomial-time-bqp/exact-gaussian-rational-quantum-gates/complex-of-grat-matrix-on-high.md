# Embedding on the second qubit is a Kronecker product

A rational encoding is a triple $(s, p, q)$ of a Boolean sign $s$ and natural numbers $p, q$, standing for $-p/q$ if $s$ is true and $p/q$ otherwise (with value $0$ when $q = 0$). A Gaussian-rational encoding is a pair of rational encodings, standing for the complex number whose real and imaginary parts they encode. The encoding of a rational $r$ is (true if $r < 0$, the absolute value of its reduced numerator, its reduced denominator); the encoding of $a + bi$ with $a, b$ rational is the pair of the encodings of $a$ and $b$. Two-qubit matrices have rows and columns indexed by pairs $(x, y)$ of bits, $x$ the first (low) qubit and $y$ the second (high) qubit. For every $2 \times 2$ matrix $U$ of Gaussian-rational encodings, the complex matrix of the two-qubit matrix that applies $U$ to the second qubit and the identity to the first is the Kronecker product $I_2 \otimes U$ of the $2 \times 2$ identity with the complex matrix of $U$.
