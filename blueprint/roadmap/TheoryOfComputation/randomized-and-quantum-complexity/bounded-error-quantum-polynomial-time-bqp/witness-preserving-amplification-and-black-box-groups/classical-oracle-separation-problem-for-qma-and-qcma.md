# The classical oracle separation problem for QMA and QCMA

The *QMA versus QCMA problem* asks whether quantum witnesses are more powerful than classical witnesses for efficient quantum verification. Relativized versions ask for an oracle $\mathcal{O}$ with $\mathrm{QMA}^{\mathcal{O}} \ne \mathrm{QCMA}^{\mathcal{O}}$: such a separation is known relative to quantum unitary oracles (Aaronson-Kuperberg), and the existence of a separating *classical* oracle (a Boolean function, accessed in superposition) is a central open problem; Group Non-Membership, once the leading candidate for a separation, is in $\mathrm{QCMA}$ by the Le Gall-Nishimura-Thakkar theorem.
