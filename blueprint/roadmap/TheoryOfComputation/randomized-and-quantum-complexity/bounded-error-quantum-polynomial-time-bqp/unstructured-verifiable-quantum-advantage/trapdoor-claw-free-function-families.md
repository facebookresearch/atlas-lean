# Trapdoor claw-free function families

A *trapdoor claw-free function family* consists of efficiently sampleable keys $k$, with trapdoors $t_k$, indexing pairs of injective functions $f_{k,0}, f_{k,1} : \mathcal{X} \to \mathcal{Y}$ with the same image, such that (i) given the key $k$ it is computationally hard for quantum polynomial-time adversaries to find a *claw* $(x_0, x_1)$ with $f_{k,0}(x_0) = f_{k,1}(x_1)$; (ii) given $t_k$, both preimages of any image point can be computed efficiently; and (iii) in the *adaptive hardcore bit* variant, with $\mathcal{X} = \{0,1\}^w$, no efficient adversary can output a bit $b$, a preimage $x_b$, a string $d \ne 0^w$ and a bit $c$ such that $c = d \cdot (x_0 \oplus x_1)$, where $x_{1-b}$ is the other preimage of $f_{k,b}(x_b)$, with probability non-negligibly larger than $1/2$; guessing $c$ already achieves $1/2$.

Brakerski, Christiano, Mahadev, Vazirani and Vidick note that no family with exactly these properties is known under standard cryptographic assumptions, and instead define a *noisy* trapdoor claw-free (NTCF) family and build one from LWE. There each $f_{k,b}$ maps $x$ to a probability distribution over $\mathcal{Y}$; the trapdoor inverts every point of the support of $f_{k,b}(x)$, and the pairs $(x_0, x_1)$ with $f_{k,0}(x_0) = f_{k,1}(x_1)$ form a perfect matching $R_k$ of $\mathcal{X}$. The superposition over the range is prepared efficiently for densities $f'_{k,b}(x)$ at negligible expected Hellinger distance from $f_{k,b}(x)$. The adaptive hardcore bit is required only through an efficient injection $J : \mathcal{X} \to \{0,1\}^w$ and only for strings $d$ in trapdoor-checkable sets $G_{k,b,x}$ that contain all but a negligible fraction of $\{0,1\}^w$: for every quantum polynomial-time $\mathcal{A}$, $\bigl|\Pr[\mathcal{A}(k) \in H_k] - \Pr[\mathcal{A}(k) \in \overline{H}_k]\bigr|$ is negligible, where $H_k$ is the set of tuples $(b, x_b, d, d \cdot (J(x_0) \oplus J(x_1)))$ with $(x_0, x_1) \in R_k$ and $d \in G_{k,0,x_0} \cap G_{k,1,x_1}$, and $\overline{H}_k$ is obtained from $H_k$ by flipping the last bit.
