# Cleve–Mittal: finite-dimensional operator solutions give perfect strategies

If a binary linear system has an operator solution $(X_v)$ in $M_d(\mathbb{C})$ for some finite $d \ge 1$, then its game has a perfect entangled strategy: on the maximally entangled state of $\mathbb{C}^d \otimes \mathbb{C}^d$, Alice measures the commuting observables $\{X_v : v \in S_j\}$ jointly and Bob measures $X_v^{\mathsf T}$, and they win with probability $1$ for every question distribution supported on incident pairs. Conversely (Cleve–Liu–Slofstra, Theorem 4 and Lemma 8), if every equation has at least one variable and the question distribution has full support, a commuting-operator strategy with state $\psi$ that wins with probability $1$ yields an operator solution, possibly on an infinite-dimensional space: for each variable $v$, restrict Alice's observable for $v$ in any equation containing $v$ to the closed subspace $\overline{\mathcal{A}\psi}$, where $\mathcal{A}$ is the unital algebra generated by Alice's operators (the restriction does not depend on the equation), and give variables in no equation the identity. Without full support the converse fails: for the system $x_1 = 0$, $x_1 = 1$ with all weight on the pair (first equation, $x_1$), answering $0$ always wins, but no operator solution exists.

## Proof sketch

On the maximally entangled state $(M \otimes 1)\psi = (1 \otimes M^{\mathsf T})\psi$, so Bob's outcome for $X_v^{\mathsf T}$ always equals Alice's outcome for $X_v$. Alice's joint outcomes satisfy the equation because the product of the commuting observables is $(-1)^{b_j}$.
