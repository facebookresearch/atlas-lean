# The low individual degree test

Let $q = 2^t$ with $t$ odd, let $\mathbb{F}_q$ be the field of $q$ elements, and let $m, d, k \ge 1$ be integers with $m$ dividing $q$. A polynomial $g : \mathbb{F}_q^m \to \mathbb{F}_q$ has *individual degree* at most $d$ if it has degree at most $d$ in each variable separately. The two-player *simultaneous low individual degree test* with parameters $(q, m, d, k)$ gives each player a question of one of three types, the pair of types being uniform over the nine possibilities: a uniformly random *point* $u \in \mathbb{F}_q^m$, answered by an element of $\mathbb{F}_q^k$; an *axis-parallel line* $\{u + t e_i : t \in \mathbb{F}_q\}$ through a uniform point $u$ in a uniform direction $i \in \{1, \dots, m\}$, answered by $k$ univariate polynomials of degree at most $d$; or a *diagonal line* $\{u + t v' : t \in \mathbb{F}_q\}$ through a uniform point $u$, where $v' = (0, \dots, 0, v_i, \dots, v_m)$ is a uniform $v \in \mathbb{F}_q^m$ with its first $i - 1$ coordinates set to zero for a uniform $i$, answered by $k$ univariate polynomials of degree at most $md$. When the two types are equal, both players receive the same question and the verifier accepts iff the answers are equal (consistency test). When one player receives a line and the other a point on it, the verifier accepts iff each of the $k$ line polynomials, evaluated at the parameter of the point, equals the corresponding coordinate of the point answer (axis-parallel and diagonal line-versus-point tests). In every other case it accepts. Players who answer with the values and line restrictions of a fixed $k$-tuple of polynomials of individual degree at most $d$ win with probability $1$.
