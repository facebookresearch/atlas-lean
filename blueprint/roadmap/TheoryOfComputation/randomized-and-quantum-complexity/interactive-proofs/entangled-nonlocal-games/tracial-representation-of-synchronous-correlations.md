# Synchronous correlations have tracial representations

A correlation $p(a, b \mid x, y)$ on $X = Y$, $A = B$ is *synchronous* if $p(a, b \mid x, x) = 0$ whenever $a \ne b$. If a commuting-operator strategy with state $\psi$ and projective measurements yields a synchronous correlation, then $P^x_a \psi = Q^x_a \psi$ for all $x, a$, the vector state restricts to a tracial state $\tau$ on the C*-algebra generated by Alice's operators, and

$$p(a, b \mid x, y) = \tau\big(P^x_a P^y_b\big).$$

Conversely, for every C*-algebra with a tracial state $\tau$ and projection-valued measures $\{E^x_a\}$, the formula $\tau(E^x_a E^y_b)$ defines a synchronous commuting-operator correlation (via commuting left and right actions on the GNS space).

## Proof sketch

Synchronicity gives $\sum_a \langle \psi, P^x_a Q^x_a \psi\rangle = 1$, which forces $\|(P^x_a - Q^x_a)\psi\| = 0$. Moving operators across with this identity and commutation shows $\langle\psi, ST\psi\rangle = \langle\psi, TS\psi\rangle$ on Alice's algebra. For the converse, the GNS space of a trace carries commuting left and right multiplication actions.
