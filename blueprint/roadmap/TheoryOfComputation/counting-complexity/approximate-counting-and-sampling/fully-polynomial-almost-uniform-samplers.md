# Fully polynomial almost uniform samplers and self-reducible witness families

For a relation $R$ with witness sets $R(x)=\{y:(x,y)\in R\}$, an **FPAUS** (fully polynomial almost uniform sampler) is a randomized algorithm that, on input $x$ and a tolerance $\delta>0$, runs in time polynomial in $|x|$ and $\log(1/\delta)$ and outputs a witness $W\in R(x)$ whose distribution is within total variation distance $\delta$ of uniform on $R(x)$; when $R(x)=\emptyset$ it may output a special symbol $\bot$. An **FPRAS** for $|R(x)|$ outputs, in time polynomial in $|x|$ and $1/\varepsilon$, a value within a factor $1\pm\varepsilon$ of $|R(x)|$ with probability at least $3/4$. Jerrum, Valiant and Vazirani's original *almost uniform generators* instead require every output probability to lie within a factor $1+\delta$ of a common value, outputs outside $R(x)$ having probability $0$, and allow rejection with probability at most $1/2$ when $R(x)\ne\emptyset$; such a generator is fully polynomial if it runs in time polynomial in $|x|$ and $\log(1/\delta)$. The equivalence with approximate counting holds for either notion (Jerrum, Valiant and Vazirani for theirs, Jerrum's monograph for the FPAUS). $R$ is **self-reducible** in the sense of Jerrum, Valiant and Vazirani if there are a polynomial-time computable length function $g$ with $(x,y)\in R\Rightarrow|y|=g(x)$, and polynomial-time computable functions $\psi$ and $\sigma$ with $\sigma(x)=O(\log|x|)$ and $g(x)>0\Rightarrow\sigma(x)>0$, such that $(x,y_1\cdots y_n)\in R\iff(\psi(x,y_1\cdots y_{\sigma(x)}),\,y_{\sigma(x)+1}\cdots y_n)\in R$. The **one-bit** case is $\sigma(x)=1$ whenever $g(x)>0$: writing $x_b=\psi(x,b)$, then $R(x)=\bigsqcup_{b\in\{0,1\}}b\cdot R(x_b)$ whenever $g(x)>0$, all witnesses of $x_b$ have length $g(x)-1$, and the recursion stops exactly at instances with $g(x)=0$, whose only possible witness is the empty word.
