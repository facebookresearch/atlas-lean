# The permanent of an integer matrix modulo 2 is its determinant modulo 2

For every square integer matrix $M$, the residue of $\operatorname{per} M$ modulo $2$ is the
determinant of the reduction of $M$ modulo $2$, computed over $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$. So the
permanent modulo $2$ is computable in polynomial time, by Gaussian elimination.

## Sources

- L. G. Valiant, *The complexity of computing the permanent*, Theoretical Computer Science 8 (1979), 189-201, Theorem 3, the case $k = 1$.
