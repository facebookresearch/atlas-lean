# Monotone circuits for bipartite perfect matching need superpolynomial size

Every monotone circuit that decides whether a bipartite graph with $m$ vertices on each side has a perfect matching has $m^{\Omega(\log m)}$ gates (Razborov, 1985), although the problem is solvable in polynomial time.

For $m\ge1$ let $f_m$ be the Boolean function of $m^2$ variables $x_{i,j}$ ($1\le i,j\le m$), one for each edge of the complete bipartite graph $K_{m,m}$, with $f_m(x)=1$ iff the edge set $E=\{(i,j): x_{i,j}=1\}$ contains a perfect matching, a set of $m$ vertex-disjoint edges:

$$
f_m=\bigvee_{\sigma\in S_m}\ \bigwedge_{i=1}^{m} x_{i,\sigma(i)} ,
$$

the logical permanent of the $m\times m$ adjacency matrix. $f_m$ is monotone.

A circuit here is a [well-formed](../../../TheoryOfComputation/complexity-classes/time-and-space-complexity-classes/well-formed-circuit.md) [monotone flat circuit](../../../TheoryOfComputation/complexity-classes/time-and-space-complexity-classes/is-monotone-circuit.md) on the $m^2$ inputs $x_{i,j}$ (taken in row-major order) that has [fan-in at most two](../../../TheoryOfComputation/complexity-classes/time-and-space-complexity-classes/is-fan-in-le-two.md); it computes $f_m$ when, for every input, its last gate has the value of $f_m$ under the [evaluation of flat circuits](../../../TheoryOfComputation/complexity-classes/time-and-space-complexity-classes/circuit-eval.md).

**Theorem.** There is a constant $c>0$ such that, for all sufficiently large $m$, every such circuit computing $f_m$ has at least $m^{c\log m}$ gates other than input gates.

## Expansion

Jukna proves the bound by Razborov's method of approximations. Theorem 9.30: for every legitimate lattice $\mathcal M$ of monotone families, a monotone circuit computing $f$ has at least $\rho(f,\mathcal M)$ gates, where $\rho$ measures how many approximation steps with small error families are needed to cover the difference between $f$ and a member of $\mathcal M$ (proved by induction on the number of gates). For $f_m$ the lattice consists of the monotone families generated by $r$-closed collections of matchings with at most $s$ edges, with $s=\lfloor(\log m)/8\rfloor$ and $r=\lfloor m^{1/4}(\log m)^8\rfloor$. Theorem 9.38 shows $\rho(f_m,\mathcal M_m)=m^{\Omega(\log m)}$ by two cases, $M=\emptyset$ and $M\ne\emptyset$, comparing the probability that a random perfect matching, respectively a random input rejected by $f_m$, falls into the error families with the bounds of the preceding lemmas. Jukna notes that the general monotone lower-bound criterion of the same chapter seems to fail for $f_m$, which is why the full approximation method is presented. He also contrasts the bound with non-monotone circuits for $f_m$ with $O(m^5)$ gates, obtained from Hopcroft and Karp's $O(m^{5/2})$-time algorithm.

*The linked circuit model.* Jukna's circuits have fan-in-two AND and OR gates over the variables, and their size is the number of AND and OR gates. A well-formed monotone flat circuit of fan-in at most two becomes such a circuit without more non-input gates: drop each gate with a single argument by redirecting its uses to that argument, and propagate the constants given by AND and OR gates with empty argument lists, which removes gates or leaves the output constant (impossible for $f_m$, which is not constant for $m\ge1$). So the bound counts the non-input gates of the theorem, and a fortiori all gates.

*Consequence for unbounded fan-in (derived, not stated by the source).* A well-formed monotone flat circuit with $g$ gates, input gates included and gates of any fan-in, has a well-formed equivalent of fan-in at most two with at most $g^2$ gates: copy input gates, remove repeated arguments of each AND or OR gate (which does not change its value), keep a gate with at most two distinct arguments, and replace a gate with $k\ge3$ distinct arguments, all earlier gates so $k\le g-1$, by a chain of $k-1$ binary gates of the same kind whose last gate takes its place; the last gate still computes the output, and each original gate contributes at most $g$ gates. Hence a well-formed monotone flat circuit of any fan-in computing $f_m$ has at least $m^{(c/2)\log m}$ gates, again $m^{\Omega(\log m)}$.
