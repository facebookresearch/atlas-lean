# Bounding a gate's arrival by a common bound on its inputs

A straight-line circuit program over a gate signature has a fixed number of input wires and a sequence of gates; each gate applies an operation of the signature to a list of wires (inputs or earlier gates), one for each argument position of the operation. Fix real arrival times for all wires, a real delay $d$ for a gate, and a real $t \ge 0$. If every wire read by the gate arrives at time at most $t$, then the gate's output arrives at time at most $d + t$. The gate's arrival is defined as $d$ plus the maximum of $0$ and its input arrivals; the bound above follows from that definition because $t \ge 0$. The upper bound and the lower bounds by each input plus $d$ do not by themselves characterize the arrival: with $d = 0$ and one input arriving at $-1$, the value $-1$ satisfies both, while the zero-seeded maximum gives $0$.
