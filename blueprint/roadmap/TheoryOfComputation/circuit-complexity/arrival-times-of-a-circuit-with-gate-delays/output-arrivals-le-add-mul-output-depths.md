# Output arrival times are bounded by depth under a nonnegative delay bound

A straight-line circuit program over a gate signature has a fixed number of input wires and a sequence of gates; each gate applies an operation of the signature to a list of wires (inputs or earlier gates), one for each argument position of the operation. A circuit is such a program with a list of designated output wires. The depth of an input wire is $0$, and the depth of a gate is one plus the maximum of $0$ and the depths of its input wires (so a gate with no inputs has depth $1$). Let $a_0 \ge 0$ and $D \ge 0$ be reals, suppose every input arrives at time at most $a_0$, and every gate delay is at most $D$; the delays may be negative. Then every designated output arrives at time at most $a_0 + D \cdot \delta$, where $\delta$ is the depth of that output.
