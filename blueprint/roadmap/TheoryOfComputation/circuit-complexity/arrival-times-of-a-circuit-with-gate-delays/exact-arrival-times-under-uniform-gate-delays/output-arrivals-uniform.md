# Exact output arrival times under a uniform delay, over a signature without constants

A straight-line circuit program has input wires and a sequence of gates, each applying an operation of a gate signature to wires that are inputs or earlier gates. The depth of an input wire is $0$, and the depth of a gate is one plus the maximum of $0$ and the depths of its input wires (so a gate with no inputs has depth $1$). A gate arrives at its delay plus the maximum of $0$ and the arrival times of the wires it reads. A circuit is such a program with a list of designated outputs. Suppose every operation of the signature takes at least one argument, let $a_0 \ge 0$ and $D \ge 0$ be reals, give every gate the delay $D$ and every input the arrival time $a_0$. Then every designated output of every circuit over the signature arrives at exactly $a_0 + D \cdot \delta$, where $\delta$ is the depth of that output. Over a signature that also has constants, the same holds for circuits whose gates are not constants.
