# The first-order theory of the real field is exponentially hard

The first-order theory of the real numbers with addition and multiplication (Tarski's elementary algebra) is exponentially complex in the sense of the [Fischer–Rabin lower bound for real addition](fischer-rabin-lower-bound-for-real-addition.md) and of [exponentially long proofs](real-addition-has-long-proofs.md): there is a constant $d > 0$ such that every deterministic or nondeterministic decision procedure needs more than $2^{dn}$ steps on some sentence of each sufficiently large length $n$, and every axiomatization with polynomial-time recognizable axioms has true sentences of each sufficiently large length $n$ without proofs of at most $2^{dn}$ symbols.
