# Domino named by a one-letter block

Let $\alpha$ be an alphabet. A domino over an alphabet $\alpha$ is a pair $d = (d^{\uparrow}, d^{\downarrow})$ of words over $\alpha$ (its top and bottom words). The block alphabet $\hat\alpha$ is the disjoint union of the set of all dominoes over $\alpha$ (whose elements, written $[d]$, are called domino letters) and $\alpha$ (plain letters). For a word $b$ over $\hat\alpha$, the domino named by $b$ is $d$ if
$b$ is the one-letter word $[d]$ consisting of a domino letter, and the domino $(\varepsilon,
\varepsilon)$ for every other word $b$ (a default value).
