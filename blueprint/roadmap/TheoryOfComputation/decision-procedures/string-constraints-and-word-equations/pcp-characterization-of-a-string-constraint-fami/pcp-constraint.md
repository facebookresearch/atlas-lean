# PCP encoding as a one-variable string constraint

Let $\alpha$ be an alphabet. A domino over an alphabet $\alpha$ is a pair $d = (d^{\uparrow}, d^{\downarrow})$ of words over $\alpha$ (its top and bottom words). The block alphabet $\hat\alpha$ is the disjoint union of the set of all dominoes over $\alpha$ (whose elements, written $[d]$, are called domino letters) and $\alpha$ (plain letters). The top morphism $\tau$ and the bottom morphism $\beta$ are the monoid morphisms $\hat\alpha^* \to \hat\alpha^*$ given on letters by $\tau([d]) = d^{\uparrow}$ and $\beta([d]) = d^{\downarrow}$ (as words of plain letters) and $\tau(a) = \beta(a) = \varepsilon$ for plain letters $a$. For a finite list $P$ of dominoes, $R_P$ is the regular expression $(\sum_{d \in P} [d])^*$ over $\hat\alpha$ (the sum being the regular expression for the empty language when $P$ is empty), whose language is the set of concatenations of domino letters of dominoes in $P$. For a finite list $P$ of dominoes, the PCP constraint of $P$
is the string-constraint formula over $\hat\alpha$ with the single variable $x$ given by the conjunction of
three atoms: $x$ lies in the language of $R_P$; $\tau(x) = \beta(x)$; and $|x| \ge 1$.
