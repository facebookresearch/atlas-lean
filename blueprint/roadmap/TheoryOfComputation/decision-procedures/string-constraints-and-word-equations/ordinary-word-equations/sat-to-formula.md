# Formula embedding of word equations preserves satisfaction

Let $\alpha$ be an alphabet of constants and $V$ a set of variables, disjoint from $\alpha$. A pattern is a finite word over $\alpha \cup V$, and for a substitution $\sigma : V \to \alpha^*$ the evaluation $\sigma(w)$ of a pattern $w$ is the word over $\alpha$ obtained by replacing each variable $x$ in $w$ by $\sigma(x)$. A word equation $L = R$ is a pair of patterns, and $\sigma$ solves it if $\sigma(L) = \sigma(R)$. The string-constraint formula of $L = R$ is the single equality atom between the term embeddings of
$L$ and $R$. For every substitution $\sigma$, $\sigma$ satisfies this formula if and only if $\sigma$ solves
$L = R$.
