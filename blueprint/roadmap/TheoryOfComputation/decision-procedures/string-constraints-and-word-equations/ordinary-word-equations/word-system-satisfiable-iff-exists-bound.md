# Solvability of word equation systems at a finite length bound

Let $\alpha$ be an alphabet, $n \in \mathbb{N}$, and let the variables be $x_0, \dots, x_{n-1}$; a pattern is a finite word over $\alpha \cup \{x_0, \dots, x_{n-1}\}$, and for a substitution $\sigma$ of words over $\alpha$ for the variables, $\sigma(w)$ is the word obtained from a pattern $w$ by replacing each $x_i$ by $\sigma(x_i)$. Let $E$ be a finite list of word equations between patterns. There is a substitution solving every
equation of $E$ if and only if there are $B \in \mathbb{N}$ and a substitution $\sigma$ with
$|\sigma(x_i)| \le B$ for all $i < n$ solving every equation of $E$.

The bound is obtained from a solution; this gives no terminating test for unsolvability.
