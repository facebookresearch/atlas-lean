# Lookup in a folded list below the starting length

String terms over an alphabet $\alpha$ with variables $x_0, x_1, \dots$ (indexed by $\mathbb{N}$) are built from constant words, variables, concatenation and letter-to-word morphisms; $\sigma(t)$ denotes the value of a term $t$ under an assignment $\sigma : \mathbb{N} \to \alpha^*$ of words to the variables, and string formulas are positive Boolean combinations (conjunction, disjunction, true, false) of atoms (term equations, regular memberships, length bounds). For a list $l$ of words over $\alpha$ and $j \in \mathbb{N}$, $l[j]$ denotes the $j$-th entry of $l$, or the empty word if $j \ge |l|$. The evaluation step sends a list $l$ of words and a term $t$ to the list $l$ followed by the word $\sigma_l(t)$, where $\sigma_l$ is the assignment $x_j \mapsto l[j]$. For a list of terms $t_0, \dots, t_{m-1}$ and a starting list $a$ of words, the folded list is obtained from $a$ by applying the evaluation step successively with $t_0, t_1, \dots, t_{m-1}$. For all terms $t_0, \dots, t_{m-1}$, every starting list $a$ of words and every $j <
|a|$, the folded list $F$ satisfies $F[j] = a[j]$.
