# Folded assignment values of a straight-line system

String terms over an alphabet $\alpha$ with variables $x_0, x_1, \dots$ (indexed by $\mathbb{N}$) are built from constant words, variables, concatenation and letter-to-word morphisms; $\sigma(t)$ denotes the value of a term $t$ under an assignment $\sigma : \mathbb{N} \to \alpha^*$ of words to the variables, and string formulas are positive Boolean combinations (conjunction, disjunction, true, false) of atoms (term equations, regular memberships, length bounds). A closed straight-line system $S$ consists of a finite list of defining terms $t_0, \dots, t_{m-1}$ ($t_k$ defines $x_k$) and a query formula $\varphi$. For a list $l$ of words over $\alpha$ and $j \in \mathbb{N}$, $l[j]$ denotes the $j$-th entry of $l$, or the empty word if $j \ge |l|$. The evaluation step sends a list $l$ of words and a term $t$ to the list $l$ followed by the word $\sigma_l(t)$, where $\sigma_l$ is the assignment $x_j \mapsto l[j]$. The values of $S$ form the list of words obtained from the empty list by applying the
evaluation step successively with $t_0, t_1, \dots, t_{m-1}$; its $k$-th entry is the value of $t_k$ under
the assignment given by the entries before it (variables beyond them reading as the empty word).
