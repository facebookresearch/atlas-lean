# Monotone circuits for bipartite perfect matching need superpolynomial size

Every monotone circuit that decides whether a bipartite graph with $m$ vertices on each side has a perfect matching has $m^{\Omega(\log m)}$ gates (Razborov, 1985), although the problem is solvable in polynomial time.

For $m\ge1$ let $f_m$ be the Boolean function of $m^2$ variables $x_{i,j}$ ($1\le i,j\le m$), one for each edge of the complete bipartite graph $K_{m,m}$, with $f_m(x)=1$ iff the edge set $E=\{(i,j): x_{i,j}=1\}$ contains a perfect matching, a set of $m$ vertex-disjoint edges:

$$
f_m=\bigvee_{\sigma\in S_m}\ \bigwedge_{i=1}^{m} x_{i,\sigma(i)} ,
$$

the logical permanent of the $m\times m$ adjacency matrix. $f_m$ is monotone.

**Theorem.** There is a constant $c>0$ such that, for all sufficiently large $m$, every [monotone circuit](../../complexity-classes/time-and-space-complexity-classes/is-monotone-circuit.md) (fan-in-2 AND and OR gates over the inputs $x_{i,j}$) computing $f_m$ has at least $m^{c\log m}$ gates. In contrast, $f_m$ is computed by non-monotone circuits with $O(m^5)$ gates, since Hopcroft and Karp decide it in time $O(m^{5/2})$.

## Expansion

Jukna proves the bound by Razborov's method of approximations. Theorem 9.30: for every legitimate lattice $\mathcal M$ of monotone families, a monotone circuit computing $f$ has at least $\rho(f,\mathcal M)$ gates, where $\rho$ measures how many approximation steps with small error families are needed to cover the difference between $f$ and a member of $\mathcal M$ (proved by induction on the number of gates). For $f_m$ the lattice consists of the monotone families generated by $r$-closed collections of matchings with at most $s$ edges, with $s=\lfloor(\log m)/8\rfloor$ and $r=\lfloor m^{1/4}(\log m)^8\rfloor$. Theorem 9.38 shows $\rho(f_m,\mathcal M_m)=m^{\Omega(\log m)}$ by two cases, $M=\emptyset$ and $M\ne\emptyset$, comparing the probability that a random perfect matching, respectively a random input rejected by $f_m$, falls into the error families with the bounds of the preceding lemmas. Jukna notes that the general monotone lower-bound criterion of the same chapter seems to fail for $f_m$, which is why the full approximation method is presented.
