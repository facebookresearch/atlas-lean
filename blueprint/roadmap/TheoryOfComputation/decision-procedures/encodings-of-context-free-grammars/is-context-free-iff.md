# A language is context-free exactly when an encoded grammar generates it

Let $T$ be an alphabet and $L$ a language over $T$. Then $L$ is context-free (generated by some
context-free grammar with terminals $T$) if and only if $L$ is the language of some encoded context-free
grammar over $T$, that is, of a grammar whose nonterminals are natural numbers, given by a start
nonterminal $n_0 \in \mathbb{N}$ and a finite list of productions $A \to \gamma$ with $A \in \mathbb{N}$
and $\gamma$ a finite word over the disjoint union of $\mathbb{N}$ and $T$.
