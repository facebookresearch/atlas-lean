# Nielsen transformations of quadratic word equations

Nielsen transformations rewrite a quadratic word equation by guessing which of the two leading symbols is a prefix of the other and cancelling it. They never lengthen the equation and keep it quadratic, so only finitely many equations are reachable; walks in the labelled graph of these steps describe all solutions.

Let $A$ be an alphabet of constants and $X$ a set of variables. For a quadratic equation $E$ given by $\alpha = \beta$ let $|E| = |\alpha\beta|$ and let $\mathrm{qv}(E)$ be the set of variables occurring exactly twice in $\alpha\beta$. For $z \in A \cup X$ and $y \in X$ let $\psi_{z<y}$ be the morphism of $(A \cup X)^*$ with $\psi_{z<y}(y) = zy$ that fixes every other symbol. For $Y \subseteq X$ let $\pi_{X \setminus Y}$ be the morphism that erases the variables in $Y$ and fixes every other symbol. If $E$ has the form $z\alpha' = y\beta'$ with $z, y \in A \cup X$, the following rewriting steps are defined:

1. if $z \in \mathrm{qv}(E)$ and $z \ne y$: $\; z\alpha' = y\beta' \;\Rightarrow_L\; z\,\psi_{y<z}(\alpha') = \psi_{y<z}(\beta')$;
2. if $y \in \mathrm{qv}(E)$ and $z \ne y$: $\; z\alpha' = y\beta' \;\Rightarrow_R\; \psi_{z<y}(\alpha') = y\,\psi_{z<y}(\beta')$;
3. if $z \in X \setminus \mathrm{qv}(E)$: $\; z\alpha' = y\beta' \;\Rightarrow_>\; z\alpha' = \beta'$;
4. if $y \in X \setminus \mathrm{qv}(E)$: $\; z\alpha' = y\beta' \;\Rightarrow_>\; \alpha' = y\beta'$;
5. if $z = y$: $\; z\alpha' = y\beta' \;\Rightarrow_>\; \alpha' = \beta'$.

In addition, for every quadratic equation $\alpha = \beta$ (with $\alpha$ or $\beta$ possibly empty) and every set $Y \subseteq \mathrm{var}(E)$ of variables occurring in it, $\alpha = \beta \Rightarrow_> \pi_{X \setminus Y}(\alpha) = \pi_{X \setminus Y}(\beta)$. For $Y = \varnothing$ this step leaves the equation unchanged; every other step of $\Rightarrow_>$ shortens the equation (the source's Remark 3.1 states the shortening for all of $\Rightarrow_>$, which taken literally fails for $Y = \varnothing$). The **Nielsen transformation relation** is $\Rightarrow_{NT} = \Rightarrow_L \cup \Rightarrow_R \cup \Rightarrow_>$, and $\Rightarrow\, = \Rightarrow_L \cup \Rightarrow_R$ is its length-preserving part. When two distinct constants lead the two sides no step of the forms 1–5 applies.

Each step carries a *label*: the morphism $\varphi$ of $(A \cup X)^*$ that transports solutions back, namely $\psi_{y<z}$ for a step of form 1 or 3, $\psi_{z<y}$ for form 2 or 4, the identity for form 5, and $\pi_{X \setminus Y}$ for an erasure. If $E \to E'$ is a step with label $\varphi$ and $h'$ solves $E'$, then $h' \circ \varphi$ solves $E$. The **Nielsen graph** $G_E$ of $E$ has as vertices the equations $E'$ with $E \Rightarrow_{NT}^* E'$ and one labelled edge for each step between them. A step may return to the same equation with a nontrivial label: for $xa = ax$ the step of form 1 with $\psi_{a<x}$ gives $axa = aax$ and, after cancelling, $xa = ax$ again, while transporting a solution $x \mapsto w$ back to $x \mapsto aw$. Such loops are kept. Only the erasure with $Y = \varnothing$, whose label is the identity, is omitted; omitting it changes neither the represented solutions nor reachability or distances. Following Day and Manea (Section 3.2, under their standing assumption that $A$ has at least two letters), every walk from $E$ to $\varepsilon = \varepsilon$ with labels $\varphi_1, \dots, \varphi_m$, together with an arbitrary assignment $g$ of the variables (all of which solve $\varepsilon = \varepsilon$), gives the solution $g \circ \varphi_m \circ \cdots \circ \varphi_1$ of $E$, and every solution of $E$ arises in this way. For $xa = ax$, taking the loop $k$ times and then erasing $x$ gives the solution $x \mapsto a^k$. Reachability and shortest directed distances are the same in the simple graph whose edges are the pairs $(E_1, E_2)$ of distinct equations with $E_1 \Rightarrow_{NT} E_2$, but that simple graph does not represent all solutions.

Every step maps quadratic equations to quadratic equations and regular equations to regular equations, introduces no new variables or constants, and satisfies $|E'| = |E|$ for $\Rightarrow$ and $|E'| < |E|$ for every non-identity step of $\Rightarrow_>$; in particular $G_E$ is finite.

## Expansion

This is a definition; the closing paragraph records the observations that make it useful. The transport property of the labels is checked directly from the rules. For form 1, applying $\psi_{y<z}$ to $z\alpha' = y\beta'$ gives $yz\,\psi_{y<z}(\alpha') = y\,\psi_{y<z}(\beta')$, which is $E'$ with the letter or variable $y$ prepended to both sides. For form 3, the variable $z$ occurs only once, so a solution $h'$ of $z\alpha' = \beta'$ gives $h'(y)h'(z)h'(\alpha') = h'(y)h'(\beta')$. Forms 2 and 4 are symmetric, form 5 cancels a common symbol, and an erasure sends the erased variables to the empty word. Conversely, every solution is represented by a walk. Given a solution $h_i$ of an equation $E_i \ne (\varepsilon = \varepsilon)$, choose a step $E_i \to E_{i+1}$ with label $\varphi$ and a solution $h_{i+1}$ of $E_{i+1}$ with $h_i = h_{i+1} \circ \varphi$ on every variable of $X$, as follows.

- If some variable occurring in $E_i$ has the empty image, erase the nonempty set $Y$ of all such variables, and keep $h_{i+1} = h_i$. Then $h_{i+1} \circ \pi_{X \setminus Y} = h_i$, because $h_i$ is empty on $Y$, and for the same reason $h_{i+1}$ solves the erased equation.
- Otherwise every occurring variable has a nonempty image. Then neither side is empty: a nonempty side would consist of constants and variables with nonempty images, and so could not evaluate to the empty word. Write $E_i$ as $z\alpha' = y\beta'$. If $z = y$, apply form 5 and keep $h_{i+1} = h_i$. Two distinct leading constants are impossible in a solution, so if $z \ne y$ at least one of them is a variable. By symmetry let $z$ be a variable with $|h_i(z)| \ge |h_i(y)|$, choosing the variable when both images have length one. By the length-directed Levi lemma applied to $h_i(z)h_i(\alpha') = h_i(y)h_i(\beta')$ there is a word $w$ with $h_i(z) = h_i(y)w$ and $h_i(\beta') = w\,h_i(\alpha')$. Apply form 1 if $z$ occurs twice and form 3 if it occurs once. In both cases the label is $\psi_{y<z}$; let $h_{i+1}$ agree with $h_i$ except $h_{i+1}(z) = w$. Then $h_{i+1} \circ \psi_{y<z} = h_i$, since $h_{i+1}(y)\,w = h_i(z)$. For form 1, cancelling the nonempty word $h_i(y)$ in $h_i(z\alpha') = h_i(y\beta')$ shows that $h_{i+1}$ solves $E_{i+1}$. For form 3, $h_{i+1}(z\alpha') = w\,h_i(\alpha') = h_i(\beta')$.

Variables that leave the equation keep their values, so $h_i = h_{i+1} \circ \varphi$ holds on all of $X$ at every step. Each erasure and each step of forms 3, 4 and 5 shortens the equation. Each step of forms 1 and 2 keeps its length (the equation is quadratic) and shortens the common solution word by the positive length of the cancelled prefix. So the pair (equation length, solution-word length) decreases lexicographically, and the process reaches $\varepsilon = \varepsilon$ after finitely many steps $m$. Composing the steps gives $h = h_m \circ \varphi_m \circ \cdots \circ \varphi_1$, with the final assignment $h_m$ (for $x = x$ it is $x \mapsto h(x)$, not the empty assignment). A step $\Rightarrow_L$ corresponds to the guess that, in a solution $h$, the image $h(y)$ of the other leading symbol is a prefix of $h(z)$, possibly equal to it: substituting $yz$ for $z$ everywhere and cancelling the common leading $y$ keeps $z$ occurring at most twice, and the new value of $z$ is the remaining suffix, which may be empty. Steps $\Rightarrow_>$ either let a once-occurring variable absorb the other leading symbol, cancel equal leading symbols, or erase a nonempty set of variables guessed to be empty; each strictly shortens the equation, the empty erasure being the identity. Since lengths never increase and the symbols are drawn from those of $E$, the reachable set is finite. Other textbook variants of the rules, such as Levi's method used by Lin and Majumdar, differ in the length-reducing steps and are separate relations; results stated for one variant are not transferred to another without proof.
