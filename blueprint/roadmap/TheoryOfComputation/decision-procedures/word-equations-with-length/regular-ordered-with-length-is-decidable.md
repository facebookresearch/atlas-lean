# Regular-ordered equations with length constraints are decidable

Satisfiability of a regular-ordered word equation with existential Presburger length constraints is decidable in nondeterministic exponential time, also with explicit NFA regular constraints. The proof needs Presburger arithmetic with divisibility as an oracle.

Let the input be a single regular-ordered word equation $L = R$ over an alphabet of constants, a length constraint $\theta$ over the length variables $|x|$ given as an **existential** Presburger formula with integer coefficients in binary, and, in the second statement, a set of regular constraints $x \in \mathcal L(\mathcal A)$ given by explicitly listed nondeterministic finite automata. Solutions map variables to possibly empty words. *Existential Presburger arithmetic with divisibility* (existential PAD) is the set of true existential sentences over $(\mathbb N, 0, 1, +, \le, \mid)$, in which divisibility $s \mid t$ may hold between terms with variables on both sides; a *PAD oracle* answers satisfiability of such formulas with binary coefficients.

1. Without regular constraints, satisfiability is decidable by a nondeterministic polynomial-time algorithm with a PAD oracle, and in NEXP.
2. With regular constraints, satisfiability is decidable by a polynomial-space algorithm with a PAD oracle, and in NEXP.

Lin and Majumdar call this class regular-oriented. Both statements are about a single equation; the usual reduction of a system of equations to one equation does not preserve regular-orderedness. These results do not decide general word equations with length constraints, whose decidability remains open.

## Expansion

The proof uses Lin and Majumdar's variant of Nielsen transformations (Levi's method), not the relation of Day and Manea.

*Counter system (Section 4).* For a quadratic equation $E$, build a counter system $\mathcal C(E)$: its control states are the equations reachable from $E$, its counters are the lengths of the variables, and each rewriting step carries a guard and an update: erasing a variable $y$ guesses $y = 0$; cancelling equal symbols changes nothing; removing a constant prefix from a variable $y$ is $\mathrm{DEC}_y$ ($y > 0$, $y' = y - 1$); replacing $y$ by $zy$ for variables $z \preceq y$ is $\mathrm{SUB}_{y,z}$ ($0 < z \le y$, $y' = y - z$); all other counters stay unchanged. The length abstraction of $E$ is the set of initial counter values from which $\varepsilon = \varepsilon$ is reachable (Theorem 4.1). With regular constraints, control states are pairs of an equation and an assignment $f$ of Boolean transition matrices; the length abstraction of $(E, S)$ is the union, over assignments $f$ consistent with the constraints, of the reachable initial values *intersected with* the realizable lengths $\{v : v(x) \in \mathrm{LEN}(\phi^{-1}(f(x))) \text{ for all } x\}$, the lengths of words whose transition matrix is $f(x)$ (Theorem 4.3). These semilinear sets of realizable lengths can be enumerated as linear sets in polynomial space (Lemma 4.4).

*Acceleration (Section 5).* A *1-variable-reducing* cycle uses only $\mathrm{DEC}_y$ and $\mathrm{SUB}_{y,z}$ for one counter $y$. Lemma 5.1 of the source expresses the reachability relation between two states of such a cycle as an existential PAD formula computable in polynomial time, built from the divisibility of $y - y'$ by the amount $M = a_0 + \sum_z a_z z$ by which one traversal decreases $y$. The source's displayed formula omits the guards of the $\mathrm{SUB}$ steps ($z > 0$) and of partial traversals. For example, the regular-ordered equation $xay = yz$ has the control cycle $xay = yz \to axy = yz \to xay = yz$ (subtract $x$ from $y$, then decrement $y$), and at $x = 0, y = 1$ the displayed formula would allow $y' = 0$ at the same state although the first step is disabled. The formula is therefore not reproduced here; a correct encoding must separate zero traversals (identity) from positive ones (every subtracted counter positive, $y' < y$, divisibility by $M$) and keep the guards on the entry and exit paths, which needs only polynomially many guard literals. For a flat counter system, one in which each control state lies on at most one simple cycle, all of them 1-variable-reducing, reachability together with an existential Presburger side condition reduces to guessing a path through the cycles and asking one existential PAD query (Theorem 5.2).

*Regular-ordered equations.* Rewriting preserves regular-orderedness (Lemma 5.5), and for a regular-ordered $E$ the counter system is flat with 1-variable-reducing simple cycles of length $O(|E|)$ and simple paths of length $O(|E|^2)$ (Lemma 5.6). Guessing a skeleton and querying the PAD oracle with the cycle formulas conjoined with $\theta$ gives statement 1 (Theorem 5.4). With regular constraints the counter system is again flat, with cycles of exponential length but skeletons of polynomial length (Lemma 5.9); cycle formulas with binary coefficients are computed in polynomial space (Lemma 5.10). The algorithm guesses a consistent $f$, enumerates one linear set $\eta$ for the realizable lengths of each fiber $\phi^{-1}(f(x))$ (their conjunction over the variables), and asks the polynomial-size query $\theta \wedge \eta \wedge \exists \bar x'\, \psi$ (Theorem 5.8). Existential PAD is decidable (Lipshitz), and in NEXP (Lechner, Ouaknine and Worrell). For the NEXP bounds, note that in both algorithms an accepting run asks a single query, of polynomial size, and accepts exactly when the query is satisfiable. A nondeterministic exponential-time machine therefore guesses the path (and in statement 2 the assignment $f$, the linear set $\eta$ and the cycle coefficients, all computable in polynomial space and so in exponential time), builds the polynomial-size query, and runs the nondeterministic exponential-time procedure for existential PAD on it. No negative oracle answer is ever needed. Decidability of existential PAD and the internal lemmas above have no pages here.

*Scope.* Section 2 of the source allows arbitrary Presburger length constraints, but Theorem 5.2 and the queries above require an existential side formula, so the statements are given for existential Presburger constraints. For arbitrarily quantified constraints the NEXP bound cannot hold: a constraint conjoined with the trivial equation $a = a$ can be any Presburger sentence, and deciding Presburger arithmetic has a double-exponential worst-case lower bound ([Fischer–Rabin](../presburger-arithmetic-and-real-addition/fischer-rabin-lower-bound-for-presburger-arithmetic.md)). This comparison is made here, not in the source. The source's example of a regular-oriented equation with regular constraints whose length abstraction needs divisibility (Proposition 5.11) states the equation $xy = yz$ but proves the claim for $xz = zy$; it is not used here. Whether divisibility is necessary for regular-ordered equations without regular constraints is left open by the source.
