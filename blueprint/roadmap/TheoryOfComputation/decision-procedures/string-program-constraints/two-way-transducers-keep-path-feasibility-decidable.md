# Two-way transducers keep path feasibility decidable

Loop-free string programs built from concatenation, replaceAll with regular patterns and variable replacements, string reversal and two-way transducers, with recognisable assertions, have decidable path feasibility, but the problem is non-elementary.

Let $\Sigma$ be a finite alphabet. A *two-way finite transducer* $T$ over $\Sigma$ has finitely many states, an initial state $q_0$, final states $F$, end-markers $\triangleright$ and $\triangleleft$ outside $\Sigma$, and transitions $(q, a, d, q', w)$ with $a \in \Sigma \cup \{\triangleright, \triangleleft\}$, a direction $d \in \{-1, 1\}$ and an output word $w \in \Sigma^*$, where $w = \varepsilon$ whenever $a$ is an end-marker and no transition moves left from $\triangleright$ or right from $\triangleleft$. On input $a_1 \cdots a_n$, with $a_0 = \triangleright$ and $a_{n+1} = \triangleleft$, a run starts in $q_0$ at position $0$ and each step applies a transition reading the letter at the current position and moves to the adjacent position; it is accepting if it ends at position $n + 1$, on the right end-marker, in a final state, and its output is the concatenation of the emitted words. $T$ defines the relation of pairs (input, output of an accepting run).

The language $\mathrm{SL}$ consists of symbolic executions, in the sense of [backward propagation decides path feasibility](backward-propagation-decides-path-feasibility.md), generated by

$$
S ::= z := x \circ y \mid z := \mathrm{replaceAll}_e(x, y) \mid y := \mathrm{reverse}(x) \mid y := T(x) \mid \mathrm{assert}(R(\vec x)) \mid S; S,
$$

where $\mathrm{replaceAll}_e(x, y)$ is the [leftmost-longest replaceAll](leftmost-longest-replaceall.md) with pattern $e$, a regular expression with $\varepsilon \notin L(e)$, subject $x$ and replacement $y$; $\mathrm{reverse}$ reverses a word; $T$ is a two-way finite transducer; and $R$ is a recognisable relation given by tuples of automata.

1. Path feasibility of $\mathrm{SL}$ is decidable.
2. It is non-elementary, that is, it lies in no class of $k$-fold exponential space for a fixed $k$; this already holds for the fragment with two-way transducers only, and for the fragment with one-way transducers and replaceAll.

## Expansion

This node still needs its proof broken down. Decidability follows from backward propagation once RegMonDec and RegInvRel hold (Proposition 4.6). Recognisable assertions satisfy RegMonDec. For a two-way transducer $T$ and an automaton $A$, the pre-image is the language of a two-way automaton whose states are pairs $(q, p)$ of a state of $T$ and a state of $A$, with a move from $(q_1, p_1)$ to $(q_2, p_2)$ on letter $a$ in direction $d$ when $T$ has a transition $(q_1, a, d, q_2, w)$ and $A$ can read $w$ from $p_1$ to $p_2$; a two-way automaton accepts a regular language (the linked result uses runs without end-markers, so the expansion must adapt it to the end-marker convention), and the source converts it into a one-way automaton with $2^{O(n \log n)}$ states (its Proposition 4.2, with the end-marker convention above). Reversal is the special two-way transduction that copies the input from right to left. For $\mathrm{replaceAll}_e$, the recognisable pre-image is the local pre-image construction of the 2018 replaceAll paper, not its satisfiability procedure.

Lower bound (Proposition 4.7, Appendix A): tiling problems over corridors of $n$-fold exponential width are reduced, each further two-way transducer checking the binary column addresses of the level below, so $n + 1$ transducers handle an $n$-fold exponential corridor. For one-way transducers with replaceAll, the two-way transducers of this reduction only make complete left-to-right passes separated by silent returns to the start; with $x_1 \in (a \backslash)^*$ and $x_2 := \mathrm{replaceAll}_a(x_1, x)$, the word $x_2$ is a sequence of copies of $x$ separated by $\backslash$, and a one-way transducer simulates the passes, treating $\backslash$ as a return. This does not simulate arbitrary two-way transducers.
