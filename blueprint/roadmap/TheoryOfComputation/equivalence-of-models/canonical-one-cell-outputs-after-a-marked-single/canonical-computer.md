# The canonical one-cell computer of a machine

For a finite, nonempty alphabet $\Gamma$ and a single-tape Turing machine $M$ over $\Gamma$, the canonical one-cell computer of $M$ is a single-tape machine over the marked alphabet that simulates $M$ while marking every cell it visits, then erases both halves of the tape and returns to the cell under the final head. It first normalizes the initial head cell; then each transition of $M$ is followed by one stationary step that normalizes the cell reached, also when $M$ halts; and it enters its cleanup phase only after the simulated machine enters its halting state. The cleanup records the answer under the head, erases the visited cells to the left, returns, erases the visited cells to the right, returns, and restores the answer as an ordinary cell.
