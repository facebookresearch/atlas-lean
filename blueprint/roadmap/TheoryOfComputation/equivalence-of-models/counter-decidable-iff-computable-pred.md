# Counter machines compute exactly the computable predicates

The following holds. For every predicate $P$ on $\mathbb{N}$, these are equivalent:

1. $P$ is counter-machine decidable: some counter-machine program, started with $n$ as its only input, halts with output $1$ if $P(n)$ holds and $0$ otherwise, for every $n \in \mathbb{N}$;
2. $P$ is a computable predicate.

## Sources

- M. Minsky, *Computation: Finite and Infinite Machines*, 1967, §11.
- R. I. Soare, *Recursively Enumerable Sets and Degrees*, Ch. I, Theorem 2.3.
