# The Dyck language $D_k$

For $k \in \mathbb{N}$, the Dyck language $D_k$ is the set of all Dyck words over $k$ bracket pairs: the words over the alphabet of opening brackets $(i, \mathrm{open})$ and closing brackets $(i, \mathrm{close})$, $i \in \{0, \dots, k-1\}$, generated from the empty word by concatenation of two Dyck words and by wrapping a Dyck word $w$ as $(i, \mathrm{open})\, w\, (i, \mathrm{close})$.

## Sources

- N. Chomsky and M. P. Schützenberger, "The algebraic theory of context-free languages", in *Computer Programming and Formal Systems*, North-Holland, 1963, 118-161.
