# Threshold edit distance to deterministic pushdown automata is undecidable

No algorithm decides, given a finite automaton, a deterministic pushdown automaton and an integer $k$, whether every word accepted by the first is within edit distance $k$ of a word accepted by the second.

**Theorem.** $\mathrm{TED}(\mathcal C,\mathrm{DPDA})$ is undecidable for every class $\mathcal C\in\{\mathrm{DFA},\mathrm{NFA},\mathrm{DPDA},\mathrm{PDA}\}$: already for a DFA $A_1$, there is no algorithm that, given $A_1$, a deterministic pushdown automaton $A_2$ and $k\ge0$, decides whether the [edit distance](edit-distance-between-languages.md) $\mathrm{ed}(L(A_1),L(A_2))$ is at most $k$. The automata, their runs and acceptance (one letter per transition, start with empty stack, accept in a final state with empty stack) are those of Section 2.1 of the source, as fixed on the definition page.

In the same section the source shows, by reductions from universality of PDA and from inclusion between DPDA, that $\mathrm{TED}(\mathcal C,\mathrm{PDA})$ and $\mathrm{FED}(\mathcal C,\mathrm{PDA})$ are undecidable for every such $\mathcal C$, and that $\mathrm{FED}(\mathcal C,\mathrm{DPDA})$ is undecidable for $\mathcal C\in\{\mathrm{DPDA},\mathrm{PDA}\}$. In contrast, $\mathrm{TED}(\mathcal C_1,\mathcal C_2)$ is EXPTIME-complete for $\mathcal C_1\in\{\mathrm{DPDA},\mathrm{PDA}\}$, $\mathcal C_2\in\{\mathrm{DFA},\mathrm{NFA}\}$; the status of $\mathrm{FED}(\mathrm{DFA},\mathrm{DPDA})$ is left open (conjectured coNP-complete).

## Expansion

Universality of DPDA is decidable, so a direct reduction from universality is not available. A PDA is *nearly deterministic* if it has one initial state and its transitions split into a function $\delta_1$ and a set $\delta_2$ such that every accepting run takes exactly one transition of $\delta_2$ (Definition 5.1 of the source). Such a PDA never accepts the empty word, since an accepting run on $\varepsilon$ takes no transition. The proof uses padded words, a modification of the proof of Lemma 3.8 described below: over $\Sigma$ with a letter $\diamond \in \Sigma$, let $R$ be the regular language of words of odd length with $\diamond$ at every even position and at no odd position, and consider nearly-deterministic PDA $A_N$ with $L(A_N) \subseteq R$. The missing prerequisite, which a good expansion states on its own page, is the undecidability of whether such an $A_N$ accepts every word of $R$. Lemma 5.2 of the source states universality over $\Sigma^*$, by inspecting the standard reduction from the halting problem of Turing machines to universality of PDA; the padded form over $R$ needs, in addition, the effective transformation that pads every word of a nearly-deterministic PDA with $\diamond$, which a good expansion supplies with that page.

The reduction (proof of Lemma 3.8, reused in the proof of Proposition 4, with the modification noted at the end of this paragraph) adds index letters $1, \dots, |\delta_2|$ outside $\Sigma$ and builds a DPDA $A_D$ that reads letters of $\Sigma$ with $\delta_1$, and reads the index letter $i$ followed by the letter of the $i$-th transition of $\delta_2$ by taking that transition. So $L(A_D)$ consists of the words of $L(A_N)$ with the index of the $\delta_2$ transition of an accepting run inserted just before the position where the run takes it, and every word of $L(A_D)$ has exactly one index letter. Hence $\mathrm{ed}(R, L(A_D)) \ge 1$, and $R \subseteq L(A_N)$ gives $\mathrm{ed}(R, L(A_D)) \le 1$. Conversely, if $w \in R$ is at distance $1$ from some $u \in L(A_D)$, the edit creates the index letter of $u$. If it is an insertion, deleting the index letter from $u$ gives both $w$ and a word of $L(A_N)$, so $w \in L(A_N)$. If it is a substitution, deleting the index letter from $u$ gives a word of $L(A_N) \subseteq R$ of even length, which is impossible. So $A_N$ accepts all of $R$ iff $\mathrm{ed}(R, L(A_D)) \le 1$, and $R$ is recognized by a DFA. The source substitutes the index letter for the letter it marks instead of inserting it; that loses the marked letter: with $\delta_2 = \{s \xrightarrow{b} f\}$, $\delta_1 = \{f \xrightarrow{\diamond} g,\ g \xrightarrow{a} f,\ g \xrightarrow{b} f\}$ and $\Sigma = \{a, b, \diamond\}$, the PDA rejects every word of $R$ that starts with $a$, yet each such word is one substitution away from a word of the substituted DPDA.
