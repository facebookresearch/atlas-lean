# Equivalence to a fixed language, finiteness and regularity of unary conjunctive grammars are undecidable

Even over a one-letter alphabet, it is co-r.e.-complete whether a conjunctive grammar generates a fixed conjunctive language such as the empty set, and undecidable whether it generates a finite or a regular language.

Let $G$ range over [conjunctive grammars](conjunctive-grammars.md) over the alphabet $\{a\}$.

**Theorem (Jeż and Okhotin).**

1. For every fixed unary conjunctive language $L_0 \subseteq a^*$, the problem of whether a given conjunctive grammar $G$ over $\{a\}$ satisfies $L(G) = L_0$ is co-r.e.-complete ($\Pi^0_1$-complete).
2. There is no algorithm that decides, given a conjunctive grammar $G$ over $\{a\}$, whether $L(G)$ is finite, and none that decides whether $L(G)$ is regular.

Taking $L_0 = \varnothing$ and $L_0 = a^*$ in clause 1, emptiness and universality of unary conjunctive grammars are co-r.e.-complete. For every fixed $L_0 \subseteq a^*$, the problem $L(G) = L_0$ is either co-r.e.-complete or trivial (Corollary 2 of the source).

## Expansion

The key step (Theorem 3 and Lemma 8 of the source) shows that for every Turing machine $T$ the set of numbers whose base-$k$ notations are the valid accepting computations of $T$, and the set for the invalid ones, are generated by effectively constructed unary conjunctive grammars; this rests on representing positional notation recognized by trellis automata through language equations. For clause 1, combining the grammar for $T$'s valid computations with a grammar for $L_0$ yields a grammar that generates $L_0$ exactly when $T$ accepts nothing, which reduces the co-r.e.-complete emptiness problem for Turing machines; membership in co-r.e. holds because equivalence of two decidable languages is co-r.e. For clause 2, a machine $T'$ that accepts only the empty string, and does so exactly when $T$ accepts something, has at most one valid computation; concatenating with $\{a^{k^n} : n \ge 0\}$ gives a grammar whose language is non-regular if $T$ accepts some input and finite otherwise.
