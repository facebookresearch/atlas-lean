# Inherent ambiguity of $\{a^i b^j c^k : i = j \text{ or } j = k\}$

Over the three-letter alphabet $\{a, b, c\}$, let

$$L = \{\, a^i b^j c^k : i, j, k \in \mathbb{N},\ i = j \text{ or } j = k \,\}.$$

The following holds: every context-free grammar $g$ over $\{a, b, c\}$ with $L(g) = L$ is ambiguous, that is, some word of $L$ has two different leftmost derivations in $g$.

## Sources

- S. Ginsburg, *The Mathematical Theory of Context-Free Languages*, McGraw-Hill, 1966.
- J. E. Hopcroft and J. D. Ullman, *Introduction to Automata Theory, Languages, and Computation*, Addison-Wesley, 1979, Chapter 7.
