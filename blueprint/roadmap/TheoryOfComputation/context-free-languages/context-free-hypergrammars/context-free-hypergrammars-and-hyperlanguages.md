# Context-free hypergrammars and hyperlanguages

A hyperlanguage is a set of languages. A context-free hypergrammar couples a context-free grammar over letters that are tuples indexed by word variables with a quantifier prefix over these variables; a language belongs to its hyperlanguage when the quantified words, suitably padded, are generated by the grammar.

**Definitions.** Let $\Sigma$ be an alphabet and $\#$ a padding symbol not in $\Sigma$. A *hyperlanguage* over $\Sigma$ is a set of languages over $\Sigma$. A *context-free hypergrammar* (CFHG) is a tuple $G = \langle \Sigma, X, V, V_0, P, \alpha \rangle$ where $X = \{x_1, \dots, x_k\}$ is a finite set of word variables, $\alpha = \mathbb{Q}_1 x_1 \cdots \mathbb{Q}_k x_k$ with $\mathbb{Q}_i \in \{\exists, \forall\}$ is a *quantification condition*, and $\hat G = \langle \hat\Sigma, V, V_0, P \rangle$ is a context-free grammar with nonterminals $V$, start symbol $V_0$ and productions $P$ over the alphabet $\hat\Sigma = (\Sigma \cup \{\#\})^X$ of assignments of a letter or $\#$ to each variable.

For words $w_1, \dots, w_k \in (\Sigma \cup \{\#\})^*$ of any lengths, the *word assignment* $[x_1 \mapsto w_1] \cdots [x_k \mapsto w_k]$ is the word over $\hat\Sigma$ of length $\max_j |w_j|$ whose $i$-th letter assigns to each $x_j$ the $i$-th letter of $w_j$ if $i \le |w_j|$ and $\#$ otherwise: shorter words are padded with $\#$ at the end (Definition 1 of the source). For $w \in \Sigma^*$, let $w{\uparrow_\#}$ be the set of words obtained from $w$ by inserting $\#$ symbols anywhere. Given a language $\mathcal{L} \subseteq \Sigma^*$ and a partial assignment $u$ of padded words to variables, define $\mathcal{L} \vdash_u (\alpha, \hat G)$ by

- $\mathcal{L} \vdash_u (\epsilon, \hat G)$ if the word assignment of $u$ belongs to $L(\hat G)$;
- $\mathcal{L} \vdash_u (\exists x.\, \alpha', \hat G)$ if there are $w \in \mathcal{L}$ and $w_\# \in w{\uparrow_\#}$ with $\mathcal{L} \vdash_{u[x \mapsto w_\#]} (\alpha', \hat G)$;
- $\mathcal{L} \vdash_u (\forall x.\, \alpha', \hat G)$ if for every $w \in \mathcal{L}$ there is $w_\# \in w{\uparrow_\#}$ with $\mathcal{L} \vdash_{u[x \mapsto w_\#]} (\alpha', \hat G)$.

When $\alpha$ quantifies all of $X$, $G$ *derives* $\mathcal{L}$ if $\mathcal{L} \vdash_\emptyset (\alpha, \hat G)$, and the *hyperlanguage* $\mathcal{H}(G)$ is the set of nonempty languages that $G$ derives (a condition beginning with $\forall$ is satisfied vacuously for the empty language, which is excluded). A CFHG with quantification condition $\mathbb{Q}_1 x_1 \cdots \mathbb{Q}_k x_k$ is called a $\mathbb{Q}_1 \cdots \mathbb{Q}_k$-CFHG, and $\forall^*$-CFHG, $\exists^*$-CFHG and so on denote the corresponding classes. The *nonemptiness problem* asks whether $\mathcal{H}(G) \ne \emptyset$, and the *emptiness problem* is its complement.

Unlike the hyperautomata from which they are generalized, in which $\#$ appears only at the end of a word, hypergrammars allow padding anywhere, which makes them asynchronous; *synchronous* hypergrammars, in which padding occurs only at the ends, form a better-behaved fragment. For $\exists^*$-CFHG nonemptiness is in polynomial time.
