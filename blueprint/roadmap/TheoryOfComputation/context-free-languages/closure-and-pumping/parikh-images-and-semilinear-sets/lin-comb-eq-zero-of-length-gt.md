# Linear combination with too many coefficients is zero

For an alphabet $T$, a list $P = (p_1, \dots, p_m)$ of vectors $p_i \in \mathbb{N}^T$ and a list $c$ of natural numbers, the linear combination of $P$ with coefficients $c$ is $\sum_i c_i p_i$ when $c = (c_1, \dots, c_m)$ has the same length as $P$, and the zero vector otherwise.
If the list $c$ of coefficients is longer than the list $P$ of vectors, the linear combination of $P$
with coefficients $c$ is the zero vector.

## Sources

- R. J. Parikh, "On context-free languages", *Journal of the ACM* 13 (1966), 570–581.
