# Injective coding preserves and reflects ambiguity

Let $\varphi$ send each terminal of an alphabet $T$ to a word over an alphabet $T'$, and suppose the induced word homomorphism $T^* \to T'^*$ is injective. Then the encoded context-free grammar obtained from $g$ by replacing every terminal $a$ by the word $\varphi(a)$ is ambiguous (some word has two distinct leftmost derivations) if and only if $g$ is ambiguous. This is a property of the grammars themselves, not only of their languages.
