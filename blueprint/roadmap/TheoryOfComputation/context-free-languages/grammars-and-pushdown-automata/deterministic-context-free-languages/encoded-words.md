# Language of end-marker encoded words

Let $T$ be an alphabet and let $\dashv$ be a new end-marker letter, not in $T$. The language of end-marker
encoded words is the set of words over $T \cup \{\dashv\}$ of the form $w\dashv$ with $w$ a word over $T$: some
letters of $T$ followed by exactly one end marker, which is the last letter.

## Sources

- J. E. Hopcroft, R. Motwani and J. D. Ullman, *Introduction to Automata Theory, Languages, and Computation*, 3rd ed., Addison-Wesley, 2006, Section 6.4 (deterministic pushdown automata, acceptance by empty stack with an end marker).
