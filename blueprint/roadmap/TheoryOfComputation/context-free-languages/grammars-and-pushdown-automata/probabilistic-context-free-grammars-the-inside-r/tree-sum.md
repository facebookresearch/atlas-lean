# Total weight of the bounded tree set

For a context-free grammar with nonterminals $N$ and terminals $\Sigma$, a derivation tree is either a leaf labelled by a nonterminal $A$ and a terminal $a$ (standing for the rule $A \to a$), or a node labelled by nonterminals $A, B, C$ with a left and a right subtree (standing for the rule $A \to B\,C$). The root of a tree is $A$ in both cases, and its yield is the word $a$ for a leaf and the concatenation of the yields of the left and right subtrees for a node. A tree is valid for the grammar when every leaf's rule $A \to a$ and every node's rule $A \to B\,C$ is a rule of the grammar and, at every node, the left subtree has root $B$ and the right subtree has root $C$. The depth of a leaf is $1$, and the depth of a node is one more than the larger depth of its subtrees. For a real weight $q(r)$ on each rule $r$, the weight of a leaf $A \to a$ is $q(A \to a)$, and the weight of a node is $q(A \to B\,C)$ times the weights of its two subtrees. For a depth bound $d$, a nonterminal $A$ and a word $w$, the bounded tree set is the finite set of valid trees of depth at most $d$ with root $A$ and yield $w$. The bounded tree sum for $d$, $A$ and $w$ is the sum of the weights of all trees in the bounded tree set: the valid trees of depth at most $d$ with root $A$ and yield $w$.
