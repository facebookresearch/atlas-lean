# One split position contributes the product of inside values

For a context-free grammar with nonterminals $N$ and terminals $\Sigma$, a derivation tree is either a leaf labelled by a nonterminal $A$ and a terminal $a$ (standing for the rule $A \to a$), or a node labelled by nonterminals $A, B, C$ with a left and a right subtree (standing for the rule $A \to B\,C$). The root of a tree is $A$ in both cases, and its yield is the word $a$ for a leaf and the concatenation of the yields of the left and right subtrees for a node. A tree is valid for the grammar when every leaf's rule $A \to a$ and every node's rule $A \to B\,C$ is a rule of the grammar and, at every node, the left subtree has root $B$ and the right subtree has root $C$. The depth of a leaf is $1$, and the depth of a node is one more than the larger depth of its subtrees. For a real weight $q(r)$ on each rule $r$, the weight of a leaf $A \to a$ is $q(A \to a)$, and the weight of a node is $q(A \to B\,C)$ times the weights of its two subtrees. For a depth bound $d$, a nonterminal $A$ and a word $w$, the bounded tree set is the finite set of valid trees of depth at most $d$ with root $A$ and yield $w$. Given real rule weights $q$, the inside value $I_d(A, w)$ is defined by recursion on $d$: $I_0(A, w) = 0$, and $I_{d+1}(A, w)$ is the leaf weight of $A$ on $w$ (which is $q(A \to a)$ if $w$ is a single terminal $a$ and $A \to a$ is a rule, and $0$ otherwise) plus the sum, over all rules $A \to B\,C$ of the grammar and all split points $0 \le k \le |w|$ for which the prefix $w_{<k}$ and the suffix $w_{\ge k}$ are both nonempty, of $q(A \to B\,C)\, I_d(B, w_{<k})\, I_d(C, w_{\ge k})$. Rules of other shapes contribute nothing. Suppose that at depth $d$ the inside value equals the total weight of the bounded tree set for every nonterminal and every word. Then, for nonterminals $A, B, C$, a word $w$ and a split point $k$, the term $q(A \to B\,C)\, I_d(B, w_{<k})\, I_d(C, w_{\ge k})$ (taken to be $0$ if $w_{<k}$ or $w_{\ge k}$ is empty) equals the total weight of the trees at split $k$, the nodes for $A \to B\,C$ with left subtree in the bounded tree set of $B$ on $w_{<k}$ and right subtree in that of $C$ on $w_{\ge k}$.
