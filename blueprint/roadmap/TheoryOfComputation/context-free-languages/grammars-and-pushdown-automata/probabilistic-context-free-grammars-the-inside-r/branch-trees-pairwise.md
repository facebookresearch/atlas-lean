# Branch trees for different rule bodies are disjoint

For a context-free grammar with nonterminals $N$ and terminals $\Sigma$, a derivation tree is either a leaf labelled by a nonterminal $A$ and a terminal $a$ (standing for the rule $A \to a$), or a node labelled by nonterminals $A, B, C$ with a left and a right subtree (standing for the rule $A \to B\,C$). The root of a tree is $A$ in both cases, and its yield is the word $a$ for a leaf and the concatenation of the yields of the left and right subtrees for a node. A tree is valid for the grammar when every leaf's rule $A \to a$ and every node's rule $A \to B\,C$ is a rule of the grammar and, at every node, the left subtree has root $B$ and the right subtree has root $C$. The depth of a leaf is $1$, and the depth of a node is one more than the larger depth of its subtrees. For a depth bound $d$, a nonterminal $A$ and a word $w$, the bounded tree set is the finite set of valid trees of depth at most $d$ with root $A$ and yield $w$. For a depth bound $d$, a nonterminal $A$, a word $w$ and a binary rule body $(B, C)$ of $A$, the split trees are the trees $A \to B\,C$ whose left subtree lies in the bounded tree set of $B$ on a nonempty prefix of $w$ and whose right subtree lies in the bounded tree set of $C$ on the complementary nonempty suffix. Split trees for different binary rule bodies of $A$ are pairwise disjoint.
