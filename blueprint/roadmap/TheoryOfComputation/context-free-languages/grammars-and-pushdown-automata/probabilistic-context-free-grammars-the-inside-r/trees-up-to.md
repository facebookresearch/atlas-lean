# Valid trees of bounded depth with given root and yield

For a context-free grammar with nonterminals $N$ and terminals $\Sigma$, a derivation tree is either a leaf labelled by a nonterminal $A$ and a terminal $a$ (standing for the rule $A \to a$), or a node labelled by nonterminals $A, B, C$ with a left and a right subtree (standing for the rule $A \to B\,C$). The root of a tree is $A$ in both cases, and its yield is the word $a$ for a leaf and the concatenation of the yields of the left and right subtrees for a node. A tree is valid for the grammar when every leaf's rule $A \to a$ and every node's rule $A \to B\,C$ is a rule of the grammar and, at every node, the left subtree has root $B$ and the right subtree has root $C$. The depth of a leaf is $1$, and the depth of a node is one more than the larger depth of its subtrees. The bounded tree set for a depth bound $d$, a nonterminal $A$ and a word $w$ is defined by recursion on $d$: it is empty for $d = 0$, and for $d + 1$ it is the union of the leaf for $A \to a$ (when $w$ is the single terminal $a$ and $A \to a$ is a rule) with all nodes for rules $A \to B\,C$ of the grammar, over all split points $k$ of $w$ into a nonempty prefix $w_{<k}$ and a nonempty suffix $w_{\ge k}$, whose left subtree lies in the set for $d$, $B$ and $w_{<k}$ and whose right subtree lies in the set for $d$, $C$ and $w_{\ge k}$. It consists exactly of the valid trees of depth at most $d$ with root $A$ and yield $w$.
