# The bounded tree set at depth d + 1

For a context-free grammar with nonterminals $N$ and terminals $\Sigma$, a derivation tree is either a leaf labelled by a nonterminal $A$ and a terminal $a$ (standing for the rule $A \to a$), or a node labelled by nonterminals $A, B, C$ with a left and a right subtree (standing for the rule $A \to B\,C$). The root of a tree is $A$ in both cases, and its yield is the word $a$ for a leaf and the concatenation of the yields of the left and right subtrees for a node. A tree is valid for the grammar when every leaf's rule $A \to a$ and every node's rule $A \to B\,C$ is a rule of the grammar and, at every node, the left subtree has root $B$ and the right subtree has root $C$. The depth of a leaf is $1$, and the depth of a node is one more than the larger depth of its subtrees. For a depth bound $d$, a nonterminal $A$ and a word $w$, the bounded tree set is the finite set of valid trees of depth at most $d$ with root $A$ and yield $w$. For every $d$, $A$ and $w$, the bounded tree set at depth $d + 1$ is the union of the leaf trees of $A$ on $w$ (the leaf for $A \to a$ when $w$ is the single terminal $a$ and $A \to a$ is a rule, and none otherwise) and the branch trees of $A$ on $w$ at depth $d$ (the nodes for rules $A \to B\,C$ over splits of $w$ into nonempty parts, with subtrees from the bounded tree sets at depth $d$).
