# Trees for one binary rule at one split position

For a context-free grammar with nonterminals $N$ and terminals $\Sigma$, a derivation tree is either a leaf labelled by a nonterminal $A$ and a terminal $a$ (standing for the rule $A \to a$), or a node labelled by nonterminals $A, B, C$ with a left and a right subtree (standing for the rule $A \to B\,C$). The root of a tree is $A$ in both cases, and its yield is the word $a$ for a leaf and the concatenation of the yields of the left and right subtrees for a node. For nonterminals $A, B, C$, a word $w$, a split point $k$ and finite sets $L$ and $R$ of trees, the trees at split $k$ form the empty set if the prefix $w_{<k}$ or the suffix $w_{\ge k}$ is empty, and otherwise the set of nodes for $A \to B\,C$ with left subtree in $L$ and right subtree in $R$.
