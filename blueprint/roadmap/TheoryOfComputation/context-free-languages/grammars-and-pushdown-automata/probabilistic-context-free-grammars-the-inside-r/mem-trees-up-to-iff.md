# Membership in the bounded tree set

For a context-free grammar with nonterminals $N$ and terminals $\Sigma$, a derivation tree is either a leaf labelled by a nonterminal $A$ and a terminal $a$ (standing for the rule $A \to a$), or a node labelled by nonterminals $A, B, C$ with a left and a right subtree (standing for the rule $A \to B\,C$). The root of a tree is $A$ in both cases, and its yield is the word $a$ for a leaf and the concatenation of the yields of the left and right subtrees for a node. A tree is valid for the grammar when every leaf's rule $A \to a$ and every node's rule $A \to B\,C$ is a rule of the grammar and, at every node, the left subtree has root $B$ and the right subtree has root $C$. The depth of a leaf is $1$, and the depth of a node is one more than the larger depth of its subtrees. A tree belongs to the bounded tree set for depth bound $d$, nonterminal $A$ and word $w$ if and only if it is valid, its depth is at most $d$, its yield is $w$ and its root is $A$.
