# The two parses of aaa are different

The ambiguous example grammar has one nonterminal $A$, which is its start symbol, one terminal $a$, and the two rules $A \to A\,A$ and $A \to a$. Its right-associated derivation tree for $a\,a\,a$, grouping it as $a\,(a\,a)$, and its left-associated derivation tree, grouping it as $(a\,a)\,a$, are different trees.
