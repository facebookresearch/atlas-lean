# Valid derivation trees

For a context-free grammar with nonterminals $N$ and terminals $\Sigma$, a derivation tree is either a leaf labelled by a nonterminal $A$ and a terminal $a$ (standing for the rule $A \to a$), or a node labelled by nonterminals $A, B, C$ with a left and a right subtree (standing for the rule $A \to B\,C$). The root of a tree is $A$ in both cases, and its yield is the word $a$ for a leaf and the concatenation of the yields of the left and right subtrees for a node. A leaf for $A \to a$ is valid when $A \to a$ is a rule of the grammar. A node for $A \to B\,C$ is valid when $A \to B\,C$ is a rule of the grammar, its left subtree has root $B$, its right subtree has root $C$, and both subtrees are valid.
