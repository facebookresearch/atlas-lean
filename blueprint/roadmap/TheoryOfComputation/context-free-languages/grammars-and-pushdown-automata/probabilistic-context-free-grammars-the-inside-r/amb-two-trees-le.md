# The inside value of an ambiguous word counts both parses

The ambiguous example grammar has one nonterminal $A$, which is its start symbol, one terminal $a$, and the two rules $A \to A\,A$ and $A \to a$. For a real weight $q(r)$ on each rule $r$, the weight of a leaf $A \to a$ is $q(A \to a)$, and the weight of a node is $q(A \to B\,C)$ times the weights of its two subtrees. Given real rule weights $q$, the inside value $I_d(A, w)$ is defined by recursion on $d$: $I_0(A, w) = 0$, and $I_{d+1}(A, w)$ is the leaf weight of $A$ on $w$ (which is $q(A \to a)$ if $w$ is a single terminal $a$ and $A \to a$ is a rule, and $0$ otherwise) plus the sum, over all rules $A \to B\,C$ of the grammar and all split points $0 \le k \le |w|$ for which the prefix $w_{<k}$ and the suffix $w_{\ge k}$ are both nonempty, of $q(A \to B\,C)\, I_d(B, w_{<k})\, I_d(C, w_{\ge k})$. Rules of other shapes contribute nothing. If $q$ is nonnegative on the rules of the grammar, then the weight of the right-associated tree for $a\,a\,a$ plus the weight of the left-associated tree is at most $I_3(A, a\,a\,a)$.
