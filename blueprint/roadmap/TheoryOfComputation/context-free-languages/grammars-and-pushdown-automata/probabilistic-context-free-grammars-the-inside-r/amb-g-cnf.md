# The ambiguous example grammar is in Chomsky normal form

The ambiguous example grammar has one nonterminal $A$, which is its start symbol, one terminal $a$, and the two rules $A \to A\,A$ and $A \to a$. It is in Chomsky normal form: every rule has the form $X \to Y\,Z$ for nonterminals $Y, Z$ or $X \to b$ for a terminal $b$ (no rule has an empty right-hand side).
