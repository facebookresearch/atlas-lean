# Step counts of the two depth-first passes of Kosaraju–Sharir

A directed graph is given by a duplicate-free list of all its vertices and, for each vertex, a list of its successors (its adjacency list); there is an edge from $s$ to $t$ when $t$ occurs in the adjacency list of $s$. The vertex type has a Boolean equality test that agrees with equality and a hash function, so finite sets of vertices can be stored in hash sets. The vertex type also has decidable equality. The Kosaraju–Sharir blocks of a graph are obtained by a first depth-first pass over all vertices, giving the vertices in decreasing finishing order, and a second depth-first pass on the transpose graph with roots taken in that order; the blocks are the trees of the second pass, each listed in preorder. Depth-first search is modelled as an abstract machine with a work list. For every such graph, the first pass reaches a final state, with empty work list and the whole-graph finish order, in exactly $2V + E$ machine steps, where $V$ is the number of vertices and $E$ the total length of the adjacency lists. The second pass, on the transpose graph in decreasing finishing order, reaches its final state in exactly $R + P + E_2$ steps, where $R$ is the length of that order, $P$ the length of the second pass's preorder and $E_2$ the total length of the transpose adjacency lists over that preorder. These are counts of abstract machine steps; they do not include the cost of evaluating the adjacency lists.
