# Each run costs at most the cost

Let $X$ and $Y$ be sets (the inputs of Alice and of Bob). A (deterministic) protocol is a finite binary tree whose leaves carry output bits and whose internal nodes are owned by Alice, labelled by a map $X \to \{0, 1\}$, or by Bob, labelled by a map $Y \to \{0, 1\}$; on inputs $(x, y)$ the run follows the announced bits from the root to a leaf, whose bit is the output, and the depth is the maximal number of bits announced along a root-to-leaf path. Let $R$ be a finite set. A public-coin protocol with a finite set $R$ of random strings is a family $(p_r)_{r \in R}$ of protocols; its cost is the maximum of the depths of the $p_r$ ($0$ if $R$ is empty). For every public-coin protocol $(p_r)_{r \in R}$ and every $r \in R$, the depth of
$p_r$ is at most the cost of the protocol.
