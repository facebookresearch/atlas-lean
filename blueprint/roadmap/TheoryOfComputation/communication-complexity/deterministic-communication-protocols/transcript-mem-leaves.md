# Transcripts are leaf paths

Let $X$ and $Y$ be sets (the inputs of Alice and of Bob). A protocol is a finite binary tree whose leaves are labelled by bits and whose internal nodes are each owned by Alice, labelled by a map $X \to \{0, 1\}$, or by Bob, labelled by a map $Y \to \{0, 1\}$, with a $0$-child and a $1$-child. On inputs $(x, y)$ the run starts at the root; at an Alice node with map $a$ the bit $a(x)$ is announced and the run moves to the child with that bit, at a Bob node with map $b$ the bit $b(y)$ is announced likewise; the output is the label of the leaf reached. The transcript of the run on $(x, y)$ is the list of bits announced. For every protocol $p$ and every $(x, y) \in X \times Y$, the transcript of the run of $p$ on $(x, y)$ is
the root-to-leaf path (written as its list of bits) of a leaf of $p$.
