# Kourovka Problem 6.15 (the number of pro-locally-finite varieties)

The proposition that the set of pro-locally-finite varieties of groups has the cardinality of the continuum. A variety is represented by its laws, a fully invariant subgroup $N$ of the free group $F$ on countably many generators; its groups are those satisfying every element of $N$, and inclusion of varieties reverses inclusion of these subgroups. A variety is locally finite when its finitely generated groups are finite, and pro-locally-finite when it is not locally finite while each proper subvariety (laws $N' > N$, $N'$ fully invariant) is locally finite. The set counted is the set of such law subgroups $N$.

Source: E. I. Khukhro and V. D. Mazurov (eds.), Unsolved Problems in Group Theory: The Kourovka Notebook, 21st issue, arXiv:1401.0300v49 (2026), Archive of Solved Problems, Problem 6.15 (A. V. Kuznetsov).
