# Kourovka Problem 1.2 (existence of algebraically closed groups)

The proposition that there is a nontrivial group $G$ that is algebraically closed: for every $n$, every element $v$ of the free product $G * F_n$ with the free group on $x_1, \dots, x_n$ that is not conjugate in $G * F_n$ to an element of $G$ is sent to $1$ by the homomorphism $G * F_n \to G$ that is the identity on $G$ and maps the $x_i$ to suitable elements of $G$.

Source: E. I. Khukhro and V. D. Mazurov (eds.), Unsolved Problems in Group Theory: The Kourovka Notebook, 21st issue, arXiv:1401.0300v49 (2026), Archive of Solved Problems, Problem 1.2 (L. A. Bokut'); resolution: S. D. Brodskiĭ, Equations over groups, and groups with one defining relation, Sib. Math. J. 25 (1984), 235-251.
