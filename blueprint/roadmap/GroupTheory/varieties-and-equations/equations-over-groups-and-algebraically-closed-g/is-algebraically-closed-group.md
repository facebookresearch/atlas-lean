# Algebraically closed groups in the sense of Bokut

A group $G$ is algebraically closed when, for every natural number $n$, every element $v$ of the free product $G * F_n$ of $G$ with the free group $F_n$ on $x_1, \dots, x_n$ that is not conjugate in $G * F_n$ to any element of $G$ has a solution in $G$: there are $g_1, \dots, g_n \in G$ such that the homomorphism $G * F_n \to G$ that is the identity on $G$ and sends $x_i$ to $g_i$ maps $v$ to $1$. For $n = 0$ every element of $G * F_0$ lies in $G$, so that case imposes nothing. The trivial group satisfies the definition.

Bokut'); resolution: S. D. Brodskiĭ, Equations over groups, and groups with one defining relation, Sib. Math. J. 25 (1984), 235-251.
