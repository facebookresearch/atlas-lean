# Periodic groups of unbounded exponent in a proper variety

There is a finitely generated periodic group of unbounded exponent that satisfies a nontrivial law:
some $w \ne 1$ in the free group on countably many generators takes the value $1$ under every
substitution of elements of the group, so the group generates a proper variety. Unbounded exponent
means that no positive integer $n$ has $g^n = 1$ for every element $g$.

This answers Kourovka Problem 17.79 (O. Macedońska): the Kourovka Archive answers "Yes, moreover,
there is a continuum of such groups" (Atabekyan, 1986), and records Osin's example: a free group of
the variety defined by $x^n y = y x^n$ is a central extension of a free Burnside group of exponent
$n$ with free abelian centre of countable rank (Ashmanov–Olshanskii), and adding the relations
$x_i^i = 1$ for a basis $(x_i)$ of the centre gives a periodic group of unbounded exponent
satisfying that law.
