# The Diophantine problem is undecidable in metabelian groups of full-rank deficiency at least two

In a metabelian group given by a full-rank presentation in the variety of metabelian groups with at least two more generators than relators, no algorithm decides whether a finite system of equations has a solution.

Let $\mathcal{M}$ be the variety of metabelian groups and let $G = \langle A \mid R \rangle_{\mathcal{M}} = \langle a_1, \dots, a_n \mid r_1, \dots, r_m \rangle_{\mathcal{M}}$ be the group given by a finite presentation in $\mathcal{M}$, that is, $G = F_{\mathcal{M}}(A)/\langle\langle R \rangle\rangle$, the quotient of the free group of $\mathcal{M}$ with basis $A$ by the normal subgroup generated by $R$. The relation matrix $M(A, R)$ of the presentation is the $m \times n$ integer matrix whose $(i, j)$ entry is the exponent sum of $a_j$ in $r_i$, and the presentation has *full rank* if $M(A, R)$ has rank $\min(m, n)$. The *Diophantine problem* $\mathcal{D}(G)$ asks, given a finite system of equations with constants from $G$, whether it has a solution in $G$.

**Theorem (Garreta, Legarreta, Miasnikov and Ovchinnikov).** Let $G = \langle A \mid R \rangle_{\mathcal{M}}$ be a metabelian group given by a full-rank presentation (Theorem 1.3 of arXiv:2006.06371v1).

1. If $|R| \le |A| - 2$, then the ring $\mathbb{Z}$ is interpretable in $G$ by finite systems of equations, and $\mathcal{D}(G)$ is undecidable.
2. If $|R| \ge |A|$, then $\mathcal{D}(G)$ is decidable; in fact the first-order theory of $G$ is decidable.

The case of deficiency one, $|R| = |A| - 1$, is left open in the source; there, some presentations define groups with decidable Diophantine problem, such as cyclic groups. The metabelian Baumslag–Solitar groups $BS(1, k) = \langle a, b \mid b^{-1} a b = a^k \rangle$ with $k \ge 2$ also have such presentations (the relator has exponent-sum row $(1 - k, 0)$, of full rank exactly when $k \ne 1$; $BS(1, 1) \cong \mathbb{Z}^2$ has none, since the abelianization of a full-rank presentation of deficiency one has torsion-free rank one). The source cites a proof by Kharlampovich, López and Miasnikov that their Diophantine problem is decidable (Remark 1.4), but the corrected version of that paper proves decidability only for quadratic equations (its Theorem 1) and says that the published proof for arbitrary systems has a gap; for arbitrary finite systems with constants the question is open for $k \ge 2$ (Kharlampovich and Miasnikov). The source also shows that random metabelian groups in the few-relators model with all relators of length $\ell$ satisfy clause 1 asymptotically almost surely as $\ell \to \infty$ when $m \le n - 2$ (Theorem 4.2). The nilpotent analogue is [the undecidability of the Diophantine problem in full-rank nilpotent groups](diophantine-problem-in-full-rank-nilpotent-groups.md).

## Expansion

For clause 1, the 2-nilpotent quotient $G/\gamma_3(G)$ has the presentation $\langle A \mid R \rangle_{\mathcal{N}_2}$, again of full rank and deficiency at least two, so $\mathbb{Z}$ is interpretable by equations in $G/\gamma_3(G)$ by the nilpotent result (Theorem 3.8 of arXiv:2006.06371v1, quoting Garreta, Miasnikov and Ovchinnikov). In a finitely generated metabelian group every verbal subgroup has finite width, so $\gamma_3(G)$ is definable by equations and $G/\gamma_3(G)$ is interpretable in $G$ (Proposition 3.9); by transitivity $\mathbb{Z}$ is interpretable in $G$, and the undecidability of $\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{Z})$ gives clause 1. For clause 2, $G$ is virtually abelian by the structure theorem for full-rank metabelian presentations (Theorem 1.1), and the Diophantine problem of a finitely generated virtually abelian group is decidable (Ershov). A good expansion links the finite width of verbal subgroups of finitely generated metabelian groups (Romankov) and Ershov's decidability theorem for virtually abelian groups.
