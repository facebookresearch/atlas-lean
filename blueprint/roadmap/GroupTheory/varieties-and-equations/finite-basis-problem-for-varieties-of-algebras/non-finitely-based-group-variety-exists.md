# Kourovka Problem 2.39(a) (non-finitely based varieties of groups)

This is the proposition that some variety of groups is not finitely based, stated for the variety generated by a single group: there is a group $G$ whose laws are not finitely based (no finite set of laws of $G$ implies all laws of $G$ in every group). Every variety is generated by its free group of countable rank, so nothing is lost. Equivalently, there is a set of laws not equivalent to any finite set of laws, the question of Kourovka Problem 1.82: a set of laws defines a variety, and the laws of a group define the variety it generates.

Source: E. I. Khukhro and V. D. Mazurov (eds.), Unsolved Problems in Group Theory: The Kourovka Notebook, 21st issue, arXiv:1401.0300v49 (2026), Archive of Solved Problems, Problem 2.39 (A. I. Mal'cev; part (a) H. Neumann), and Problem 1.82 (A. L. Shmel'kin).
