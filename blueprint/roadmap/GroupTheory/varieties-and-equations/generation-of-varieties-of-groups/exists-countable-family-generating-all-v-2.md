# No countable family of groups generates every variety

There is no sequence $(F_i)_{i \in \mathbb N}$ of groups such that every variety of groups is generated by some subfamily $(F_i)_{i \in S}$, that is, equals the class of groups satisfying every law common to the $F_i$ with $i \in S$. Kourovka Problem 4.47 has a negative answer. It is the negation of the proposition on [Kourovka Problem 4.47 (a countable family generating all varieties)](exists-countable-family-generating-all-v.md).

Source: E. I. Khukhro and V. D. Mazurov (eds.), Unsolved Problems in Group Theory: The Kourovka Notebook, 21st issue, arXiv:1401.0300v49 (2026), Archive of Solved Problems, Problem 4.47 (A. Yu. Olshanskii); resolution: Yu. G. Kleĭman, Some questions in the theory of varieties of groups, Math. USSR-Izv. 22 (1984), 33-65, https://doi.org/10.1070/IM1984v022n01ABEH001433.
