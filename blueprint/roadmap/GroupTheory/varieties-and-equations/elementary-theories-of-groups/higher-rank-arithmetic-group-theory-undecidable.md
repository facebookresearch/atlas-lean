# The first-order theory of a higher rank arithmetic group is undecidable

For a centerless congruence subgroup $\Gamma$ of $G(A)$, with $G$ absolutely simple and simply connected of $S$-rank at least $2$ over a ring of $S$-integers $A$ (under the source's isotropy or spin conditions), the first-order theory of $\Gamma$ is undecidable.

Let $K$ be a number field, $S$ a finite set of places containing the archimedean ones, $A$ the ring of $S$-integers, and $G$ a connected group scheme over $A$ with $G_K$ connected, simply connected and absolutely simple and $\mathrm{rank}_S G \ge 2$, such that either $G_K$ is isotropic, or $G = \mathrm{Spin}_q$ for a regular quadratic form $q$ on $A^n$ with $\mathrm{rank}_w G \ge 2$ at some place $w \in S$. Let $\Gamma$ be a centerless congruence subgroup of $G(A)$, and assume $n \ge 9$ in the spin case. Then the first-order theory of $\Gamma$, in the language of groups, is undecidable (Corollary 1.4 of the source).

By contrast, the first-order theory of non-abelian free groups is decidable (Kharlampovich and Myasnikov).

## Expansion

The main theorem of the source is that $\Gamma$ is bi-interpretable with the ring $\mathbb{Z}$ (Theorem 1.2). In particular $\Gamma$ interprets the ring of integers, and the first-order theory of every structure that interprets the ring of integers is undecidable, since the theory of $(\mathbb{Z}, +, \cdot)$ is undecidable (a classical result of Church and Gödel with no page here).
