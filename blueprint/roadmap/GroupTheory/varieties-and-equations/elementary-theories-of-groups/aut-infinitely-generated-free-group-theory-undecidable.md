# The first-order theory of the automorphism group of an infinitely generated free group is undecidable

For an infinitely generated free group $F$, the first-order theory of $\mathrm{Aut}(F)$ in the language of groups is undecidable, and unstable; indeed set theory of the rank of $F$ is interpretable in $\mathrm{Aut}(F)$.

Let $F$ be a free group of infinite rank $\varkappa$. The source proves that the second-order theory of the set $\varkappa$ and the first-order theory of the group $\mathrm{Aut}(F)$ are mutually syntactically interpretable, uniformly in $F$ (Theorem 7.1). As a consequence, the first-order theory of the automorphism group of an infinitely generated free group is undecidable and unstable (Proposition 7.3).

## Expansion

The source interprets, inside $\mathrm{Aut}(F)$ by first-order formulas, the second-order logic of the cardinal $\varkappa$, which in particular interprets arithmetic; a structure that interprets the ring of integers has undecidable first-order theory, because the first-order theory of $(\mathbb{Z}, +, \cdot)$ is undecidable (a classical result of Church and Gödel with no page here).
