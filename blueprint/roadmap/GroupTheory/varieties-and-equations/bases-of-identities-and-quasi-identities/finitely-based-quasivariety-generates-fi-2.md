# A finitely based quasivariety generating a non-finitely based variety

There is a finite set $Q$ of quasi-identities of groups such that the variety generated by the quasivariety of groups satisfying $Q$ is not finitely based. Anokhin's examples are soluble quasivarieties (of derived length at most $7$). Kourovka Problem 8.28 has a negative answer. A quasi-identity is a sentence $\forall x\,(w_1 = 1 \wedge \dots \wedge w_k = 1 \to w = 1)$ with finitely many premises, all words in finitely many unknowns. It is the negation of the proposition on [Kourovka Problem 8.28 (varieties generated by finitely based quasivarieties)](finitely-based-quasivariety-generates-fi.md).

Source: E. I. Khukhro and V. D. Mazurov (eds.), Unsolved Problems in Group Theory: The Kourovka Notebook, 21st issue, arXiv:1401.0300v49 (2026), Archive of Solved Problems, Problem 8.28 (A. V. Kuznetsov); resolution: M. I. Anokhin, A basis of identities of a variety generated by a finitely-based quasi-variety of groups, Sb. Math. 189 (1998), 1115-1124, Theorem 1, https://doi.org/10.1070/SM1998v189n08ABEH000330.
