# Kourovka Problem 3.52 (quasi-identities of the free group of rank two)

The proposition that the quasivariety generated by the free group $F_2$ of rank $2$ can be defined by quasi-identities in finitely many variables: there are $n \in \mathbb N$ and a (possibly infinite) set $S$ of quasi-identities, each in at most $n$ unknowns, such that a group satisfies every member of $S$ if and only if it satisfies every quasi-identity that holds in $F_2$. A quasi-identity is a sentence $\forall x\,(w_1 = 1 \wedge \dots \wedge w_k = 1 \to w = 1)$ with finitely many premises, all words in finitely many unknowns.

Source: E. I. Khukhro and V. D. Mazurov (eds.), Unsolved Problems in Group Theory: The Kourovka Notebook, 21st issue, arXiv:1401.0300v49 (2026), Archive of Solved Problems, Problem 3.52 (D. M. Smirnov).
