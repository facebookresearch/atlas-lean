# The quasi-identities of finite groups have no basis in finitely many variables

It is not the case that there are $m \in \mathbb{N}$ and a set $B$ of quasi-identities, each in at most $m$ unknowns and each holding in every finite group, such that every group satisfying all quasi-identities of $B$ satisfies every quasi-identity that holds in all finite groups; Kourovka Problem 6.43 has a negative answer. A quasi-identity in the unknowns $x_0, \dots, x_{n-1}$ is a formula $\forall x\,(w_1 = 1 \wedge \dots \wedge w_k = 1 \to w = 1)$ with $w_1, \dots, w_k, w$ in the free group on the $x_i$; it holds in a group when every assignment that makes all premises $1$ makes the conclusion $1$. It is the negation of the proposition on [Kourovka Problem 6.43 (quasi-identities of finite groups in finitely many variables)](finite-groups-quasi-identities-bounded-v.md).

Source: E. I. Khukhro and V. D. Mazurov (eds.), Unsolved Problems in Group Theory: The Kourovka Notebook, 21st issue, arXiv:1401.0300v49 (2026), Archive of Solved Problems, Problem 6.43 (D. M. Smirnov); resolution: A. K. Rumyantsev, Quasiidentities of finite groups, Algebra and Logic 19 (1980), 297-311, https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01674472.
