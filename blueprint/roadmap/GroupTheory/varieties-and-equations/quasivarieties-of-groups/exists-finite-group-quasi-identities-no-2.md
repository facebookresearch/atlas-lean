# A finite group whose quasi-identities have no independent basis

There is a finite group whose quasi-identities have no independent basis; Kourovka Problem 5.50 has a positive answer. A quasi-identity is a formula $\forall x\,(w_1 = 1 \wedge \dots \wedge w_k = 1 \to w = 1)$ with $w_1, \dots, w_k, w$ in a free group of finite rank; a set $B$ of quasi-identities is an independent basis for the quasi-identities of $G$ when the quasi-identities that hold in every group satisfying $B$ are exactly those that hold in $G$, and no member of $B$ follows in this sense from the others.

Source: E. I. Khukhro and V. D. Mazurov (eds.), Unsolved Problems in Group Theory: The Kourovka Notebook, 21st issue, arXiv:1401.0300v49 (2026), Archive of Solved Problems, Problem 5.50 (D. M. Smirnov); resolution: A. N. Fedorov, Subquasivarieties of nilpotent minimal non-Abelian group varieties, Siberian Math. J. 21 (1980), 840-850, https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00968471.
