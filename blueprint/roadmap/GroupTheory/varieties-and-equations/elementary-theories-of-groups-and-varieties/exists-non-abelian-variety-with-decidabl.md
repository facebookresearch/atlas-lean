# Kourovka Problem 4.63 (non-abelian varieties with decidable elementary theory)

The proposition that there is a set $W$ of laws defining a variety that contains a non-abelian group and whose elementary theory (the coded sentences true in all its groups) is decidable. Sentences are coded in prenex form: a list of quantifiers binding $x_0, x_1, \dots$ in order, followed by a matrix in conjunctive normal form over literals $w = 1$, $w \ne 1$; variables not bound by the prefix are read as $1$. Every first-order sentence of the language of groups is effectively equivalent to such a code.

Source: E. I. Khukhro and V. D. Mazurov (eds.), Unsolved Problems in Group Theory: The Kourovka Notebook, 21st issue, arXiv:1401.0300v49 (2026), Archive of Solved Problems, Problem 4.63 (A. Tarski).
