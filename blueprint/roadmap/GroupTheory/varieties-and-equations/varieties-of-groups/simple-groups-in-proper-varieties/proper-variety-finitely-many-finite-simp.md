# Kourovka Problem 3.10 (finite simple groups in a proper variety)

The proposition that for every $n \in \mathbb N$ and every non-trivial element $w$ of the free group $F_n$ of rank $n$ there is a bound $N$ such that every finite non-abelian simple group $G$ satisfying the law $w = 1$ (every homomorphism $F_n \to G$ sends $w$ to $1$) has order at most $N$. A proper variety satisfies some non-trivial law and lies in the variety of that law, so this says that a proper variety contains only finitely many finite non-abelian simple groups up to isomorphism.

Source: E. I. Khukhro and V. D. Mazurov (eds.), Unsolved Problems in Group Theory: The Kourovka Notebook, 21st issue, arXiv:1401.0300v49 (2026), Archive of Solved Problems, Problem 3.10 (Yu. M. Gorchakov).
