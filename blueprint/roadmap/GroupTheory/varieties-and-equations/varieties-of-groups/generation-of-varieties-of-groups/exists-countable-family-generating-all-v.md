# Kourovka Problem 4.47 (a countable family generating all varieties)

The proposition that there is a sequence $(F_i)_{i \in \mathbb N}$ of groups such that every variety of groups is generated by a subfamily: for every set $L$ of laws there is $S \subseteq \mathbb N$ such that a group satisfies all laws in $L$ if and only if it satisfies every law that holds in all $F_i$ with $i \in S$. Here a law is an element $w$ of the free group $F$ on countably many generators $x_0, x_1, x_2, \dots$, and a group $G$ satisfies $w$ when every homomorphism $F \to G$ (equivalently, every assignment of elements of $G$ to the generators) sends $w$ to $1$; the variety defined by a set $W$ of laws is the class of groups satisfying every law in $W$. A finite family is covered by repeating members.

Source: E. I. Khukhro and V. D. Mazurov (eds.), Unsolved Problems in Group Theory: The Kourovka Notebook, 21st issue, arXiv:1401.0300v49 (2026), Archive of Solved Problems, Problem 4.47 (A. Yu. Olshanskii).
