# Kourovka Problem 4.73(b) (non-abelian varieties whose periodic groups are abelian)

The proposition that there is a set $W$ of laws defining a variety that contains a non-abelian group but all of whose periodic groups (every element of finite order) are abelian. Here a law is an element $w$ of the free group $F$ on countably many generators $x_0, x_1, x_2, \dots$, and a group $G$ satisfies $w$ when every homomorphism $F \to G$ (equivalently, every assignment of elements of $G$ to the generators) sends $w$ to $1$; the variety defined by a set $W$ of laws is the class of groups satisfying every law in $W$. This implies part (a).

Source: E. I. Khukhro and V. D. Mazurov (eds.), Unsolved Problems in Group Theory: The Kourovka Notebook, 21st issue, arXiv:1401.0300v49 (2026), Archive of Solved Problems, Problem 4.73 (A. L. Shmel'kin).
