# Squares of products in free products can lie in the fourth-power subgroup

There are free groups $A$ and $B$ and elements $a \in A$, $b \in B$ such that, in the free product $G = A * B$ with $V$ is the subgroup of $G$ generated by all fourth powers $g^4$, the verbal subgroup for the law $x^4 = 1$, neither $a$ nor $b$ lies in $V$ but $(ab)^2 \in V$. For free groups with bases $a_1, a_2$ and $b_1, b_2$, the commutators $a = [[a_1, a_2, a_2], [a_1, a_2]]$ and $b = [[b_1, b_2, b_2], [b_1, b_2]]$ are such elements. This answers Kourovka Problem 3.8 negatively. It is the negation of the proposition on [Kourovka Problem 3.8 (squares of products modulo fourth powers)](square-of-product-avoids-fourth-powers.md).

Source: E. I. Khukhro and V. D. Mazurov (eds.), Unsolved Problems in Group Theory: The Kourovka Notebook, 21st issue, arXiv:1401.0300v49 (2026), Archive of Solved Problems, Problem 3.8 (V. G. Vilyatser); resolution: V. A. Roman'kov, talk at the seminar Algebra and Logic, 17 March 1970 (counterexample recorded in the Kourovka Archive); C. R. B. Wright, On the nilpotency class of a group of exponent four, Pacific J. Math. 11 (1961), 387-394.
