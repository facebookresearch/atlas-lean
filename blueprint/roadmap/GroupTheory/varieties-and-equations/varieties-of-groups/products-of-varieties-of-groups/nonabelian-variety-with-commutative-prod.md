# Kourovka Problem 2.44 (commutative relative multiplication of varieties)

The proposition that there is a variety $\mathfrak M$ of groups, given by a set of laws, such that: $\mathfrak M$ contains a non-abelian group; $\mathfrak M$ has, for every $n \in \mathbb N$, $n$ pairwise distinct subvarieties (an infinite lattice of subvarieties); and the $\mathfrak M$-product is commutative: for all subvarieties $\mathfrak A, \mathfrak B$ of $\mathfrak M$ and every group $G \in \mathfrak M$, $G \in \mathfrak A\mathfrak B$ if and only if $G \in \mathfrak B\mathfrak A$, where $G \in \mathfrak A\mathfrak B$ means that $G$ has a normal subgroup in $\mathfrak A$ with quotient in $\mathfrak B$.

Source: E. I. Khukhro and V. D. Mazurov (eds.), Unsolved Problems in Group Theory: The Kourovka Notebook, 21st issue, arXiv:1401.0300v49 (2026), Archive of Solved Problems, Problem 2.44 (A. I. Mal'cev).
