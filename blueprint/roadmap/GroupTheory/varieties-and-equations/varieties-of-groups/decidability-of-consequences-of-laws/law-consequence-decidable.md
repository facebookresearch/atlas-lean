# Kourovka Problem 3.4(b) (decidability of consequences of fixed laws)

The proposition that for every finite list of words $f_1, \dots, f_m$ there is an algorithm deciding, for any word $f$, whether the law $f = 1$ is a consequence of $f_1 = 1, \dots, f_m = 1$. Decidability means computability of the predicate on the natural coding of these finite objects (lists of letters), so it does not depend on the coding.

Source: E. I. Khukhro and V. D. Mazurov (eds.), Unsolved Problems in Group Theory: The Kourovka Notebook, 21st issue, arXiv:1401.0300v49 (2026), Archive of Solved Problems, Problem 3.4 (L. A. Bokut').
