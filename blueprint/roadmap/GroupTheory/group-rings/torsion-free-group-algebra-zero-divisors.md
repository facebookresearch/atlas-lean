# Zero divisors over a torsion-free group with a finite two-dimensional classifying space

There is a finitely presented group $G$ such that $g^n=1$ with $n>0$ implies $g=1$, and $G$ admits a finite two-dimensional classifying space. More precisely, a path-connected Hausdorff finite CW complex has at least one 2-cell and none above dimension two, has based fundamental group isomorphic to $G$, and admits a surjective covering map from a contractible space. For this same $G$ there are nonzero $a,b\in\mathbb F_2[G]$ with $ab=0$. The statement does not require uniqueness of roots or a failure of direct finiteness.

## Depends on

- Ordinary torsion-freeness, finite CW complexes with covering spaces, and group-ring multiplication.

## Proof depends on

- A concrete group presentation, torsion and asphericity arguments, and an explicit nonzero product-zero pair.

## Expansion

Port the source presentation and the cell attachment/covering argument, then identify the finite combinations and show separately that neither is zero. Conjoin all clauses on the same group.
