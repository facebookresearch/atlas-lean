# Kourovka Problem 3.50 (projective cover of the trivial module and Hall subgroups)

The proposition that whenever $p$ is a prime, $G$ is a finite group of order $p^\alpha m$ with $p \nmid m$, $k$ is an algebraically closed field of characteristic $p$, and the projective indecomposable $kG$-module of the trivial representation has dimension $p^\alpha$ over $k$; this module is $kG\,e$ for a primitive idempotent $e$ of the group algebra $kG$ (the only idempotents $f$ with $fe = f = ef$ are $0$ and $e$) whose augmentation $\varepsilon(e)$, the sum of its coefficients, is $1$, then $G$ has a Hall $p'$-subgroup, a subgroup of order $m$. The converse holds trivially.

Source: E. I. Khukhro and V. D. Mazurov (eds.), Unsolved Problems in Group Theory: The Kourovka Notebook, 21st issue, arXiv:1401.0300v49 (2026), Archive of Solved Problems, Problem 3.50 (A. I. Saksonov); resolution: W. Willems, Math. Z. 171 (1980), 163-174, Example 4.5.
