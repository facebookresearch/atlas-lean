# Grodal's theorem (the Carlson–Thévenaz conjecture)

Let $p$ be a prime, $G$ a finite group, $k$ an algebraically closed field of characteristic $p$, $S$ a nontrivial Sylow $p$-subgroup of $G$, and $\chi : N_G(S) \to k^\times$ a homomorphism with one-dimensional module $L_\chi$. Then there is an endotrivial $kG$-module $M$ whose restriction to $N_G(S)$ is isomorphic to $L_\chi \oplus P$ for some projective $kN_G(S)$-module $P$ if and only if $\rho^\infty(S) \le \ker \chi$. Here $\rho^\infty(S)$ is the union of the subgroups $\rho_i(S)$ defined by $\rho_1(Q) = A_{p'}(N_G(Q))$ and $\rho_{i+1}(Q) = \langle N_G(Q) \cap \rho_i(R) : R \le S,\ R \ne 1 \rangle$, with $A_{p'}(N)$ the [abelian $p'$-residual](abelian-pprime-residual.md), generated by the commutators $[a, b]$ with $a, b \in N$ and by the elements $g \in N$ with $g^{p^n} = 1$ for some natural number $n$ (for prime $p$, the elements of $p$-power order); a module $M$ is endotrivial when it is finite-dimensional and $\mathrm{End}_k(M)$ with the conjugation action is isomorphic to $k$ plus a projective module. Grodal proves this for every field of characteristic $p$.

Grodal, Endotrivial modules for finite groups via homotopy theory, J. Amer. Math. Soc. 36 (2023), no. 1, 177-250, Theorem F, arXiv:1608.00499.
