# Kourovka Problem 3.51 (automorphisms fixing only the identity class)

The proposition that every finite group $G$ having a group $\Phi \le \mathrm{Aut}(G)$ of automorphisms such that each $g \ne 1$ is mapped by some $\varphi \in \Phi$ outside its conjugacy class is soluble. Taking $\Phi = \mathrm{Aut}(G)$ gives the weakest hypothesis: $G$ has no characteristic conjugacy class of nontrivial elements.

Source: E. I. Khukhro and V. D. Mazurov (eds.), Unsolved Problems in Group Theory: The Kourovka Notebook, 21st issue, arXiv:1401.0300v49 (2026), Archive of Solved Problems, Problem 3.51 (A. I. Saksonov); resolution: Y. Fine, Characteristic classes of involutions in nonsolvable groups, J. Group Theory 22 (2019), no. 6, 1077-1087, Theorem 2, https://doi.org/10.1515/jgth-2019-0040.
