# Spread of a group

Let $G$ be a group. The spread $s(G)$ of $G$ is the supremum in
$\mathbb{N} \cup \{\infty\}$ of the natural numbers $k$ such that for any non-trivial elements
$x_1, \dots, x_k$ of $G$ (not necessarily distinct) there exists $y \in G$ such that the
subgroup $\langle x_i, y \rangle$ generated by $x_i$ and $y$ is all of $G$ for every $i$. In
particular $s(G) = \infty$ when there is no largest such $k$.
