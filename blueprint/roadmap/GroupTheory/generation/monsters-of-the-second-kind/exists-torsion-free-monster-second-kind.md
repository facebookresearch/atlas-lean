# Kourovka Problem 6.64(b) (torsion-free monsters of the second kind)

The proposition that there is a torsion-free monster of the second kind (every nonidentity element has infinite order). A group $G$ is a monster of the second kind when it has an element of order greater than $2$ (infinite order included) and, for every such element $a$ and every proper subgroup $H$, there are infinitely many conjugates $c = g^{-1}ag$ with $g \notin H$ and $\langle a, c \rangle = G$.

Source: E. I. Khukhro and V. D. Mazurov (eds.), Unsolved Problems in Group Theory: The Kourovka Notebook, 21st issue, arXiv:1401.0300v49 (2026), Archive of Solved Problems, Problem 6.64 (V. P. Shunkov); resolution: A. Yu. Olshanskii, The geometry of defining relations in groups, Kluwer, Dordrecht, 1991.
