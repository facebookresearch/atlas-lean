# Extensions of Dπ-groups by Dπ-groups are Dπ-groups

Let $\pi$ be a set of natural numbers, $G$ a finite group and $N$ a normal subgroup of $G$. If $N$ and $G/N$ are $D_\pi$-groups, then so is $G$. A group is a $D_\pi$-group when any two of its maximal $\pi$-subgroups are conjugate, where a $\pi$-subgroup is one whose order has all its prime divisors in $\pi$ (only the primes in $\pi$ matter). This is Revin and Vdovin's positive answer to Kourovka Problem 3.62; its proof uses the classification of finite simple groups.

Source: E. I. Khukhro and V. D. Mazurov (eds.), Unsolved Problems in Group Theory: The Kourovka Notebook, 21st issue, arXiv:1401.0300v49 (2026), Archive of Solved Problems, Problem 3.62 (L. A. Shemetkov); resolution: D. O. Revin and E. P. Vdovin, Hall subgroups of finite groups, in: Ischia Group Theory 2004, Contemp. Math. 402 (2006), 229-263, https://doi.org/10.1090/conm/402/07585.
