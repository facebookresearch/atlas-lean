# Finite groups have subgroups of every prime-power order dividing their order (Sylow existence)

For every prime $p$, a finite group $G$ has a subgroup whose order is the largest power $p^a$ of $p$ dividing $|G|$, a Sylow $p$-subgroup of $G$. Mathlib proves more: a subgroup of order $p^n$ exists for every power $p^n$ that divides $|G|$. Let $G$ be a finite group, let $p$ be a prime and let $n \ge 0$ be an integer such that $p^n$ divides $|G|$. Then $G$ has a subgroup $K$ with $|K| = p^n$. For $n = 0$ this is the trivial subgroup, also when $p$ does not divide $|G|$. A subgroup of order $p^n$ is a Sylow $p$-subgroup only when $p^n$ is the full power of $p$ in $|G|$: writing $|G| = p^a m$ with $p \nmid m$ and taking $n = a$ gives the familiar statement. Nothing is asserted about conjugacy or the number of Sylow subgroups.
