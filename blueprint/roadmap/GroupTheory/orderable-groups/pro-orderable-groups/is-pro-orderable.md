# Pro-orderable groups

A group $G$ is pro-orderable if for every positive cone $P$ of a bi-invariant partial order on $G$ there is a positive cone $Q \supseteq P$ of a bi-invariant partial order with $a \in Q$ or $a^{-1} \in Q$ for every $a \in G$; that is, every bi-invariant partial order extends to a bi-invariant linear order. A positive cone of a bi-invariant partial order on a group $G$ is a subset $P$ with $1 \in P$, $PP \subseteq P$, $a, a^{-1} \in P \Rightarrow a = 1$, and $gPg^{-1} \subseteq P$ for all $g$; it is linear if moreover $a \in P$ or $a^{-1} \in P$ for every $a$. Taking $P = \{1\}$ shows that a pro-orderable group is orderable.

Source: E. I. Khukhro and V. D. Mazurov (eds.), Unsolved Problems in Group Theory: The Kourovka Notebook, 21st issue, arXiv:1401.0300v49 (2026), Archive of Solved Problems, Problem 1.56 (A. I. Kokorin).
