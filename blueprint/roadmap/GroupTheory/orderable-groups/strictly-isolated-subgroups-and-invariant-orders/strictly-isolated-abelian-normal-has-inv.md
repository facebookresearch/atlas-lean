# Kourovka Problem 1.49 (invariant orders on strictly isolated abelian normal subgroups)

The proposition that whenever $G$ is an $S$-group and $A$ is an abelian strictly isolated normal subgroup of $G$, $A$ has a linear order compatible with multiplication and preserved by all inner automorphisms of $G$: there is a set $P \subseteq A$ (the positive cone, with $a \le b$ meaning $ba^{-1} \in P$) that is closed under multiplication, satisfies $P \cap P^{-1} \subseteq \{1\}$ and $A = P \cup P^{-1}$, and is mapped into itself by conjugation by every element of $G$. A subgroup $H$ is strictly isolated when whenever $x \cdot (g_1^{-1}xg_1) \cdots (g_n^{-1}xg_n) \in H$ for some $x, g_1, \dots, g_n \in G$ ($n \ge 0$), both $x$ and every $g_i^{-1}xg_i$ lie in $H$, and $G$ is an $S$-group when its trivial subgroup is strictly isolated.

Source: E. I. Khukhro and V. D. Mazurov (eds.), Unsolved Problems in Group Theory: The Kourovka Notebook, 21st issue, arXiv:1401.0300v49 (2026), Archive of Solved Problems, Problem 1.49 (A. I. Kokorin); resolution: V. V. Bludov, Algebra and Logic 11 (1972), 341-349.
