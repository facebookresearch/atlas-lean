# Conjugation-invariant orders on a subgroup

A subgroup $A$ of a group $G$ has a conjugation-invariant order when there is a set $P \subseteq A$ (the positive cone, with $a \le b$ meaning $ba^{-1} \in P$) that is closed under multiplication, satisfies $P \cap P^{-1} \subseteq \{1\}$ and $A = P \cup P^{-1}$, and is mapped into itself by conjugation by every element of $G$. For abelian $A$ this is a linear order on $A$ compatible with multiplication and preserved by all inner automorphisms of $G$.

Source: E. I. Khukhro and V. D. Mazurov (eds.), Unsolved Problems in Group Theory: The Kourovka Notebook, 21st issue, arXiv:1401.0300v49 (2026), Archive of Solved Problems, Problem 1.49 (A. I. Kokorin); resolution: V. V. Bludov, Algebra and Logic 11 (1972), 341-349.
