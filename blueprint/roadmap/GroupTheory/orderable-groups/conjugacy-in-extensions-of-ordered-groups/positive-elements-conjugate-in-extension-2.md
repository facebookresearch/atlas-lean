# Positive elements that are conjugate in no ordered extension

There are a linearly ordered group $G$ and elements $g, h > 1$ of $G$ such that for no linearly ordered group $H$ and strictly monotone homomorphism $f : G \to H$ are $f(g)$ and $f(h)$ conjugate; Kourovka Problem 5.4 has a negative answer. Orders are linear and invariant under multiplication on both sides. It is the negation of the proposition on [Kourovka Problem 5.4 (conjugating positive elements in ordered extensions)](positive-elements-conjugate-in-extension.md).

Source: E. I. Khukhro and V. D. Mazurov (eds.), Unsolved Problems in Group Theory: The Kourovka Notebook, 21st issue, arXiv:1401.0300v49 (2026), Archive of Solved Problems, Problem 5.4 (V. V. Bludov); resolution: V. V. Bludov, Completion of linearly ordered groups, Algebra and Logic 44 (2005), 370-380, https://doi.org/10.1007/s10469-005-0036-6.
