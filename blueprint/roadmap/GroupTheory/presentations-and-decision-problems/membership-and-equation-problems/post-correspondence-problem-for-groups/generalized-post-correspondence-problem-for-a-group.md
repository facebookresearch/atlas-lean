# Generalized Post correspondence problem for a group

The generalized (non-homogeneous) Post correspondence problem for a group $G$ takes pairs $(g_1, h_1), \dots, (g_n, h_n)$ of elements of $G$ and constants $a_1, b_1, a_2, b_2 \in G$, and asks whether some word $w$ in $n$ letters satisfies $a_1 w(g_1, \dots, g_n) b_1 = a_2 w(h_1, \dots, h_n) b_2$.

Let $G$ be a group generated by a finite set $A$, with elements given as words over $A \cup A^{-1}$, and let $F(x_1, \dots, x_n)$ be the free group on $x_1, \dots, x_n$. The generalized Post correspondence problem $\mathrm{GPCP}(G)$ is the decision problem

- input: $n \ge 1$, pairs $(g_1, h_1), \dots, (g_n, h_n)$ of elements of $G$, and constants $a_1, b_1, a_2, b_2 \in G$;
- question: is there a word $w(x_1, \dots, x_n) \in F(x_1, \dots, x_n)$ such that $a_1\, w(g_1, \dots, g_n)\, b_1 = a_2\, w(h_1, \dots, h_n)\, b_2$ in $G$?

$\mathrm{GPCP}_n(G)$ is the restriction to instances with at most $n$ pairs. The word $w$ may be empty, and the problem is linear-time equivalent to its restriction with $b_1 = a_2 = b_2 = 1$.

## Expansion

The homogeneous problem $\mathrm{PCP}(G)$ has no constants and asks for a word $w$ that is not an identity of $G$ with $w(g_1, \dots, g_n) = w(h_1, \dots, h_n)$; a kernel-based formulation of the homogeneous problem is on [Post correspondence problem for a group](post-correspondence-problem-for-a-group.md); for an instance in the normalized form $a\, w(g_1, \dots, g_n) = w(h_1, \dots, h_n)$, with $b_1 = a_2 = b_2 = 1$, all solutions are $w_0 u$ with $w_0$ one fixed solution and $u$ an arbitrary, possibly trivial, element of the equalizer, that is, a word with $u(g_1, \dots, g_n) = u(h_1, \dots, h_n)$ (Lemma 2.2); this description fails for general constants $a_1, b_1, a_2, b_2$, and also if $u$ must avoid identities of $G$. Decidability of $\mathrm{GPCP}$ passes to finitely generated subgroups. Ciobanu and Logan use a variant for free groups with constants $u_1, u_2, v_1, v_2$ and homomorphisms $g, h$ that requires $x \ne 1$; the two formulations differ only in this requirement and in how the input is presented.
