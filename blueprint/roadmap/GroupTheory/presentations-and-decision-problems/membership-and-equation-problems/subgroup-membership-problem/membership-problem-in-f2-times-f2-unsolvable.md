# The membership problem for finitely generated subgroups of F₂ × F₂ is unsolvable

No algorithm decides, given words $v, w_1, \dots, w_k$ in the direct product $F_2 \times F_2$ of two free groups of rank two, whether $v$ lies in the subgroup generated by $w_1, \dots, w_k$. This is Mikhailova's theorem.

Let $G$ be a group with a finite generating set $A$. The *membership problem* (also called the generalized word problem or occurrence problem) in $G$ asks, given words $v, w_1, \dots, w_k$ over $A^{\pm 1}$, whether the element represented by $v$ lies in the subgroup of $G$ generated by the elements represented by $w_1, \dots, w_k$. Let $F_2$ denote the free group of rank $2$.

**Theorem (Mikhailova).** The membership problem in $F_2 \times F_2$ is unsolvable.

Conventions: both the subgroup, given by finitely many generators, and the element are part of the input. In contrast, the membership problem is uniformly solvable in the class of fundamental groups of compact connected $3$-manifolds (the survey's convention for $3$-manifolds).

## Expansion

Let $Q = \langle a_1, \dots, a_k \mid r_1, \dots, r_m \rangle$ be a finitely presented group with unsolvable word problem, which exists by the Novikov–Boone theorem. Let $\pi : F_k \to Q$ send the free generators to $a_1, \dots, a_k$, and let $P = \{(u, v) \in F_k \times F_k : \pi(u) = \pi(v)\}$ be the associated fiber product. Then $P$ is generated by the $k + m$ elements $(a_i, a_i)$ and $(r_j, 1)$, and $(w, 1) \in P$ exactly when $w = 1$ in $Q$, so deciding membership in this finitely generated subgroup of $F_k \times F_k$ would solve the word problem of $Q$. Since $F_2$ contains a free subgroup of rank $k$, $F_k \times F_k$ embeds in $F_2 \times F_2$, and $P$ becomes a finitely generated subgroup of $F_2 \times F_2$ with unsolvable membership problem. A good expansion adds and links a page for the free subgroups of every finite rank in $F_2$.
