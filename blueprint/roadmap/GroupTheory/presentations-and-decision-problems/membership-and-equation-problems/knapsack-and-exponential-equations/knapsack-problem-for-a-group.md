# Knapsack problem for a finitely generated group

The knapsack problem for a finitely generated group $G$ asks, given elements $g_1, \dots, g_n, g$ of $G$ as words, whether there are exponents $x_1, \dots, x_n$ with $g_1^{x_1} \cdots g_n^{x_n} = g$; the exponents range over the natural numbers or, in the integer version, over $\mathbb{Z}$.

Let $G$ be a group with a finite generating set $X$. The knapsack problem $\mathrm{KP}(G)$ is the decision problem

- input: words $g_1, \dots, g_n, g$ over $X \cup X^{-1}$, with $n$ part of the input;
- question: are there natural numbers $x_1, \dots, x_n \ge 0$ such that $g_1^{x_1} g_2^{x_2} \cdots g_n^{x_n} = g$ in $G$?

The integer knapsack problem asks the same question with $x_1, \dots, x_n \in \mathbb{Z}$. Mishchenko and Treier state the integer version for groups with solvable word problem, the setting of their results; the definition itself needs no such hypothesis. More generally, the problem of solving exponent equations asks whether a finite system of equations $h_0 g_1^{x_1} h_1 \cdots g_n^{x_n} h_n = 1$, in which a variable may occur several times, has a solution in natural numbers.

## Expansion

Decidability of either version does not depend on the finite generating set. The integer version reduces to the natural-number version by replacing each $g_i^{x_i}$ with $g_i^{y_i} (g_i^{-1})^{z_i}$. Solving the knapsack problem includes deciding membership in products of cyclic subgroups; with $n = 1$ and integer exponents it is the power problem. The knapsack problem for groups was introduced by Miasnikov, Nikolaev and Ushakov.
