# Rational subset membership problem

The rational subset membership problem of a finitely generated monoid or group asks for an algorithm that decides, given a finite automaton and a word over the generators, whether the word represents an element of the image of the language the automaton accepts.

Let $M$ be a monoid with a surjective monoid homomorphism $\pi\colon A^* \to M$ from the free monoid on a finite set $A$; for a group generated as a group by a finite set $X$, take $A = X \cup X^{-1}$. A subset of $M$ is rational if it equals $\pi(L)$ for a regular language $L \subseteq A^*$; equivalently, the rational subsets form the smallest class of subsets of $M$ containing the finite subsets and closed under finite union, product $UV = \{uv : u \in U, v \in V\}$ and taking the submonoid generated by a subset. The rational subset membership problem for $M$ is the decision problem

- input: a finite automaton $\mathcal{A}$ over the alphabet $A$ and a word $w \in A^*$;
- question: is $\pi(w) \in \pi(L(\mathcal{A}))$, where $L(\mathcal{A})$ is the language accepted by $\mathcal{A}$?

## Expansion

Every finitely generated submonoid $\pi(W^*)$ is rational, and an automaton for $W^*$ is computed from $W$, so the [Submonoid membership problem](submonoid-membership-problem.md) reduces to the rational subset membership problem. Decidability does not depend on the finite generating set. A fixed rational subset $R$ in which membership is undecidable, that is, such that no algorithm decides for a word $w \in A^*$ whether $\pi(w) \in R$, makes the rational subset membership problem undecidable; for a group this fixed-subset problem is the one on [Membership problem for a subset of a finitely generated group](membership-problem-for-a-subset.md), and for a monoid it is defined in the same way over $A^*$.
