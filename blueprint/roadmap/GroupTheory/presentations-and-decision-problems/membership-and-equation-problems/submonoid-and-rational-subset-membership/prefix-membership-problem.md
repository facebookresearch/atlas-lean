# Prefix membership problem of a group presentation

The prefix monoid of a group presentation is the submonoid generated by the elements that the prefixes of its defining relators represent; the prefix membership problem asks for an algorithm that decides, given a word, whether it represents an element of that submonoid.

Let $G = \mathrm{Gp}\langle A \mid r_1 = 1, \dots, r_k = 1 \rangle$ be a group given by a finite alphabet $A$ and words $r_1, \dots, r_k$ over $A \cup A^{-1}$, with canonical homomorphism $\pi\colon (A \cup A^{-1})^* \to G$. The prefix monoid $P$ of this presentation is the submonoid of $G$ generated by the elements $\pi(p)$, where $p$ ranges over the prefixes of the words $r_1, \dots, r_k$. The prefix membership problem for the presentation is the decision problem

- input: a word $w \in (A \cup A^{-1})^*$;
- question: is $\pi(w) \in P$?

It is the membership problem for the fixed finitely generated submonoid $P$ within $G$. Decidability depends on the presentation, not only on the isomorphism type of $G$: two presentations of the same group can have different prefix monoids.

## Expansion

The problem arises from the word problem for one-relation monoids. By work of Ivanov, Margolis and Meakin, for a cyclically reduced word $r$ the word problem of the special inverse monoid $\mathrm{Inv}\langle A \mid r = 1 \rangle$ reduces to the prefix membership problem of $\mathrm{Gp}\langle A \mid r = 1 \rangle$; by work of Guba, the word problem for monoids $\mathrm{Mon}\langle a, b \mid bUa = a \rangle$ reduces to prefix (or suffix) membership in groups $\mathrm{Gp}\langle A \mid uv^{-1} = 1 \rangle$ with $u, v$ positive words.
