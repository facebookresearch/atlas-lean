# Positivity of elements is undecidable in some hyperbolic group

There is a residually finite hyperbolic group $G$ with a finite generating set $S$ such that no algorithm decides whether a given element of $G$ can be written as a positive word, a product of elements of $S$ without inverses. This answers a question of McCammond and Meakin.

Let $G$ be a group generated by a finite set $S$. An element of $G$ is positive (with respect to $S$) if it lies in the submonoid of $G$ generated by $S$, that is, if it is represented by a word over $S$ without inverse letters. Then (source):

1. there is a hyperbolic group $G$ generated by a finite set $S$ such that no algorithm takes an element of $G$, as a word over $S \cup S^{-1}$, and decides whether it is positive (Theorem 5.1);
2. the group $G$ can be chosen residually finite (Theorem 5.2).

## Expansion

If a finitely presented group $H$ contains a finitely generated submonoid with undecidable membership, then a finite presentation of $H * \mathbb{Z}$ can be chosen in which positivity is undecidable (Theorem 5.4): with $A = Y \cup \{t\} \cup \{t x t^{-1} : x \in X\}$, for $Y$ a monoid generating set of $H$ and $X$ generators of the submonoid, a lemma of Gray gives $t g t^{-1} \in \langle A \rangle_{\mathrm{mon}}$ exactly when $g$ lies in the submonoid. Rips' construction gives a finitely presented hyperbolic group with a finitely generated subgroup of undecidable membership (from a finitely presented group with undecidable word problem), and $H * \mathbb{Z}$ is again hyperbolic; Wise's residually finite version of Rips' construction gives clause 2.
