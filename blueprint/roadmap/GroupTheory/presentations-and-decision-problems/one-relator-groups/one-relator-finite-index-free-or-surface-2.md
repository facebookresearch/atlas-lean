# Groups all of whose finite-index subgroups are one-relator need not be free or surface groups

There is an infinite residually finite group all of whose subgroups of finite index are one-relator, which is neither free nor isomorphic to a closed surface group $\langle a_1, b_1, \dots, a_g, b_g \mid [a_1, b_1] \cdots [a_g, b_g] \rangle$ with $[a, b] = aba^{-1}b^{-1}$ or $\langle c_1, \dots, c_k \mid c_1^2 \cdots c_k^2 \rangle$ ($k \ge 1$). A group is one-relator if it is isomorphic to $\langle x_1, \dots, x_n \mid r \rangle$ for some $n$ and some word $r$ ($r = 1$ allowed). The groups $\langle x, y \mid y^{-1}xy = x^n \rangle$, $n \ge 2$, are examples: each of their finite-index subgroups is again of this form. This answers Kourovka Problem 7.36 negatively. It is the negation of the proposition on [Kourovka Problem 7.36, infinite-group case (groups all of whose finite-index subgroups are one-relator)](one-relator-finite-index-free-or-surface.md).

Mel'nikov); resolution: V. A. Churkin, Abstracts of the 8th All-Union Symposium on Group Theory, Kiev, 1982, 139-140 (Russian).
