# Some combable group has unsolvable conjugacy problem

There exist combable groups, finitely generated groups with a choice of words satisfying the fellow traveller property, whose conjugacy problem is unsolvable, although every combable group has solvable word problem.

A group $\Gamma$ with finite generating set $A$ is combable if there are words $\sigma_\gamma$ over $A^{\pm 1}$ representing each $\gamma \in \Gamma$ and a constant $k > 0$ such that for each $\gamma \in \Gamma$ and $a \in A$ the paths in the Cayley graph from the identity labeled $\sigma_\gamma$ and $\sigma_{\gamma a}$ remain uniformly $k$-close. There exist combable groups in which the conjugacy problem is unsolvable (Theorem A of the source). The source recalls that the conjugacy problem is solvable for hyperbolic, semihyperbolic and bicombable groups and fundamental groups of compact non-positively curved spaces, the classes it surveys other than automatic and combable groups.

## Expansion

For finitely generated groups $N$ and $F$ and a homomorphism $\phi\colon F \to N$ with graph $\hat F = \{(\phi(x), x)\} \subseteq N \times F$, if membership in $\mathrm{im}\,\phi$ is unsolvable and some $a_0 \in N$ has centralizer inside $\mathrm{im}\,\phi$, then the double $\Delta(N \times F; \hat F)$ has unsolvable conjugacy problem (Theorem 3.2 of the source). Such data exist with $N$ a product of free groups or a negatively curved group from Rips' construction (Lemmas 1.3 and 1.4), which rest on groups with unsolvable word problem, and the doubles are combable by the author's earlier work.
