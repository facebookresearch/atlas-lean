# Groups of reversible Turing machines

Turing machines with a finite alphabet and finitely many states that read, write and move within bounded distance of the head; the reversible ones form a group, with subgroups of machines that never write (finite-state automata), never move (local permutations), and the group these two generate (elementary machines).

Fix $d \ge 1$, an alphabet $\Sigma = \{0, \dots, n-1\}$ and a set $Q$ of $k$ states. A configuration is a triple $(x, q, v)$, written $x_q^v$, of a tape $x \in \Sigma^{\mathbb{Z}^d}$, a state $q \in Q$ and a head position $v \in \mathbb{Z}^d$. For finite sets $F_{\mathrm{in}}, F_{\mathrm{out}} \subseteq \mathbb{Z}^d$, a *local rule* is a map

$$
f \colon \Sigma^{F_{\mathrm{in}}} \times Q \to \Sigma^{F_{\mathrm{out}}} \times Q \times \mathbb{Z}^d .
$$

It defines $T_f(x_q^v) = \tilde x_{\tilde q}^{\,v + w}$, where $p$ is the pattern of $x$ on $v + F_{\mathrm{in}}$ read relative to $v$, $f(p, q) = (\tilde p, \tilde q, w)$, and $\tilde x$ agrees with $\tilde p$ on $v + F_{\mathrm{out}}$ (relative to $v$) and with $x$ elsewhere.

- A map of the form $T_f$ is a *moving head $(\mathbb{Z}^d, n, k)$-Turing machine*. It is *reversible* if some $(\mathbb{Z}^d, n, k)$-Turing machine $T^{-1}$ satisfies $T \circ T^{-1} = T^{-1} \circ T = \mathrm{id}$. Under composition the reversible machines form a group $\mathrm{RTM}(\mathbb{Z}^d, n, k)$.
- $\mathrm{LP}(\mathbb{Z}^d, n, k)$, the *local permutations*, are the reversible machines that never move the head.
- $\mathrm{RFA}(\mathbb{Z}^d, n, k)$, the *reversible finite-state automata*, are the reversible machines that never change the tape: their local rules have the form $f(p, q) = (p, q', v)$.
- $\mathrm{EL}(\mathbb{Z}^d, n, k)$, the group of *elementary Turing machines*, is the subgroup of $\mathrm{RTM}(\mathbb{Z}^d, n, k)$ generated by $\mathrm{RFA}(\mathbb{Z}^d, n, k)$ and $\mathrm{LP}(\mathbb{Z}^d, n, k)$.

A machine is *classical* when $d = 1$, $F_{\mathrm{in}} = F_{\mathrm{out}} = \{0\}$ and the head moves by at most one cell; reversible classical machines are elementary. Machines are given by their local rules: equality, composition, reversibility and inverses are computable from local rules, and $\mathrm{RTM}(\mathbb{Z}^d, n, k)$ has a recursive presentation with decidable word problem. $\mathrm{EL}(\mathbb{Z}, n, k)$ is finitely generated for all $n, k \ge 1$, while for $n \ge 2$ neither $\mathrm{RTM}(\mathbb{Z}^d, n, k)$ nor $\mathrm{RFA}(\mathbb{Z}, n, k)$ is.

## Expansion

Adding a configuration without a head turns the machines into endomorphisms of the subshift $\Sigma^{\mathbb{Z}^d} \times X_k$ that fix the headless configurations, which identifies $\mathrm{RTM}(\mathbb{Z}^d, n, k)$ with a group of cellular automata on that subshift. For one state, $\mathrm{RFA}$ is the topological full group of the full $\mathbb{Z}^d$-shift on $n$ symbols, and the machines on two symbols with one state form a subgroup of the Brin–Thompson group $2V$.
