# Power problem of a finitely generated group

The power problem of a finitely generated group asks for an algorithm that decides, given words $x$ and $y$ in the generators, whether the element represented by $x$ is a power of the element represented by $y$.

Let $G$ be a group with a finite generating set $X$ and canonical homomorphism $\pi\colon (X \cup X^{-1})^* \to G$. The power problem is solvable for $G$ if some algorithm decides, given words $x, y \in (X \cup X^{-1})^*$, whether $\pi(x)$ belongs to the cyclic subgroup $\langle \pi(y) \rangle = \{\pi(y)^k : k \in \mathbb{Z}\}$. Solvability does not depend on the finite generating set.

## Expansion

Taking $y$ to be the empty word, $\pi(x) \in \langle 1 \rangle$ exactly when $\pi(x) = 1$, so a solution of the power problem solves the word problem.
