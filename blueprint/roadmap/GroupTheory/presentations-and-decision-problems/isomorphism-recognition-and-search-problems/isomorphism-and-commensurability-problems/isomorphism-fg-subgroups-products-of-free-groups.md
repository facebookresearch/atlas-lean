# Isomorphism of finitely generated subgroups is undecidable in groups containing a product of free groups

If a finitely presented group $G$ contains a subgroup isomorphic to $F_2 \times F_2$, then no algorithm decides whether two finitely generated subgroups of $G$, given by finite generating sets, are isomorphic; this applies to mapping class groups of surfaces of sufficiently high complexity.

Let $G$ be a finitely presented group containing a subgroup isomorphic to $F_2 \times F_2$, the direct product of two free groups of rank two. Then there is no algorithm that, given two finite subsets of $G$ (as words in the generators), determines whether the subgroups they generate are isomorphic (Proposition 1.9 of the source). In particular there is no such algorithm for finitely generated subgroups of the mapping class group $\mathrm{Mod}_{g,p}$ of a surface of genus $g$ with $p$ punctures whenever it contains $F_2 \times F_2$ (Proposition 7.1); the source credits the mapping class group case to earlier work of Stillwell on the occurrence problem.

## Expansion

The isomorphism problem for finitely generated subgroups of $F_2 \times F_2$ is unsolvable (Lemma 7.3, a known result attributed to Miller): $F_6 \times F_6$ embeds in $F_2 \times F_2$, and the generation problem of $F_6 \times F_6$ (whether a finite subset generates the whole group; Lemma 7.2), unsolvable by work of Miller via Mihailova's construction, reduces to deciding whether a finitely generated subgroup inside $F_6 \times F_6$ is isomorphic to $F_6 \times F_6$, using that centralizers in free groups are cyclic. Restricting to subgroups of the embedded copy of $F_2 \times F_2$ gives the result for $G$.
