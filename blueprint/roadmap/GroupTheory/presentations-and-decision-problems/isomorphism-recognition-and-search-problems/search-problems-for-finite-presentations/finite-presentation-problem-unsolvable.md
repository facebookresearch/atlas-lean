# Finite presentations of finitely presentable subgroups cannot be computed in direct products of hyperbolic groups

There are recursive sequences of direct products $\Gamma_n \times \Gamma_n$ of residually finite hyperbolic groups with finitely presentable subgroups $H_n$ given by generators, such that no algorithm computes a finite presentation of $H_n$, or its first Betti number.

Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a class of finitely presentable groups. The *finite presentation problem* for a group $\Gamma$ is *solvable* if there is an algorithm that, given a finite subset $S \subseteq \Gamma$ generating a finitely presentable subgroup $\Lambda$, outputs a finite presentation of $\Lambda$; it is *uniformly solvable* in $\mathcal{C}$ if one algorithm does this given also a finite presentation of a group $\Gamma \in \mathcal{C}$. The algorithm need not behave in any particular way when $S$ generates a subgroup that is not finitely presentable. Write $b_1(\Lambda) = \dim_{\mathbb{Q}} H_1(\Lambda; \mathbb{Q})$.

**Theorem (Bridson–Wilton).**

1. There exist a finite set $Z$ and recursive sequences $(\Sigma_n)$, $(S_n)$ of finite sets of words in $Z^{\pm 1}$, of a fixed cardinality, such that each group $G_n = \langle Z \mid \Sigma_n \rangle$ is isomorphic to $\Gamma_n \times \Gamma_n$ with $\Gamma_n$ a residually finite word-hyperbolic group of cohomological dimension $2$, the subgroup $H_n \subseteq G_n$ generated by $S_n$ is finitely presentable, and the set $\{ n : b_1(H_n) = b_1(G_n) \}$ is recursively enumerable but not recursive. In particular, no algorithm computes a finite presentation of $H_n$ from $\langle Z \mid \Sigma_n \rangle$ and $S_n$.
2. The finite presentation problem is not uniformly solvable in any of the following classes: direct products of hyperbolic groups; CAT(0) groups; $\mathbb{Z}$-linear groups; right-angled Coxeter groups; right-angled Artin groups.
3. There is a finitely presented group with polynomial Dehn function in which no algorithm computes the first Betti number of finitely presentable subgroups; in particular its finite presentation problem is unsolvable.

Conventions: the word problem is uniformly solvable in each class of clause 2, so the obstruction is not the word problem. Classes with a uniform solution include free groups, abelian groups, locally quasiconvex hyperbolic groups and finitely presented residually free groups.

## Expansion

Start from a recursive sequence of finite presentations $Q_n$ of fixed size, each either trivial or aspherical, such that the set of $n$ with $Q_n$ trivial is recursively enumerable but not recursive (Collins and Miller). Wise's residually finite version of the Rips construction gives short exact sequences $1 \to K_n \to \Gamma_n \to Q_n \to 1$ with $\Gamma_n$ residually finite hyperbolic, and the 1-2-3 Theorem of Baumslag, Bridson, Miller and Short makes the fiber products $H_n \subseteq \Gamma_n \times \Gamma_n$ finitely presentable. A computation of the rational homology of fiber products shows that $b_1(H_n) = b_1(G_n)$ exactly when $Q_n$ is trivial. Clause 2 uses the virtually special version of the Rips construction due to Haglund and Wise and the embedding of special groups into right-angled Artin and Coxeter groups, and clause 3 embeds the resulting groups into a finitely presented group with polynomial Dehn function by a theorem of Birget, Olshanskii, Rips and Sapir. None of these results has a page here.
