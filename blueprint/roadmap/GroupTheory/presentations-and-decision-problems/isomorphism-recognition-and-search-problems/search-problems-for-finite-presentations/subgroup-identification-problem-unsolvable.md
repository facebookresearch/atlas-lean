# A finitely presented group with unsolvable subgroup identification problem

There is a finitely presented group $G$ for which no partial algorithm, given generators of a subgroup and a finite presentation of a group isomorphic to it, always outputs an explicit isomorphism from the presented group onto the subgroup.

Let $P = \langle X \mid R \rangle$ be a finite presentation of a group $\overline{P}$. The subgroup identification problem for $P$ is solvable if there is a partial algorithm that, on input of words $w_1, \dots, w_n$ over $X \cup X^{-1}$ and a finite presentation $Q = \langle Y \mid S \rangle$, outputs, whenever the subgroup $\langle w_1, \dots, w_n \rangle$ of $\overline{P}$ is isomorphic to $\overline{Q}$, a map $\phi\colon Y \to \{w_1, \dots, w_n\}^*$ that extends to an isomorphism from $\overline{Q}$ onto $\langle w_1, \dots, w_n \rangle$; on other inputs nothing is required. There is a finitely presented group with unsolvable subgroup identification problem (Theorem 4.6 of the source).

Such a group cannot be locally Hopfian: the source shows that the problem is uniformly solvable for finitely presented locally Hopfian groups (groups in which every finitely generated subgroup is Hopfian).

## Expansion

The proof combines two ingredients. First, for a finite presentation $P$ of the Baumslag–Solitar group $\mathrm{BS}(2, 3)$, which has solvable word problem, no partial algorithm takes a recursive presentation $Q$ of a group isomorphic to $\overline{P}$ and outputs a map on generators extending to an isomorphism $\overline{P} \to \overline{Q}$ (Lemma 4.2 of the source, derived from Theorem 4.1: such a map would turn the word problem of $P$ into one for $Q$, which no algorithm can produce). Second, by the uniform version of the Higman embedding theorem, the free product of all recursively presented groups $P_1, P_2, \dots$ embeds in a finitely presented group $\overline{P}$, and a uniform procedure gives, for each $i$, generators $S_i$ of a subgroup of $\overline{P}$ isomorphic to $\overline{P_i}$ with an explicit isomorphism. If $\overline{P}$ had solvable subgroup identification problem, then given a recursive presentation $Q = P_j$ and a finite presentation $H$ with $\overline{H} \cong \overline{Q}$, identifying $\langle S_j \rangle$ from $H$ and composing with the explicit isomorphism would produce an isomorphism $\overline{H} \to \overline{Q}$, contradicting Lemma 4.2. The problem was motivated by the distinction between strong and weak effective coherence: in a group with solvable subgroup identification problem the two notions agree.
