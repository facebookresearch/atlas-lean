# Almost finitely presented groups that are not finitely presented

There is a group that is almost finitely presented over $\mathbb{Z}$ but not finitely presented; Kourovka Problem 6.35 has a negative answer. Here $G$ is almost finitely presented over $\mathbb{Z}$ when for some $n$ there is a surjective homomorphism $\varphi$ from the free group $F_n$ of rank $n$ onto $G$ whose kernel $R$ satisfies $R = \langle\langle S \rangle\rangle [R, R]$ for some finite set $S \subseteq F_n$, where $\langle\langle S \rangle\rangle$ is the normal closure of $S$ in $F_n$; equivalently, the relation module $R/[R, R]$ is a finitely generated $\mathbb{Z}G$-module. Bestvina and Brady's groups of type $FP$ over $\mathbb{Z}$ that are not finitely presented are such groups. It is the negation of the proposition on [Kourovka Problem 6.35 (almost finitely presented groups)](almost-finitely-presented-is-finitely-pr.md).

Merzlyakov; question of R. Bieri and R. Strebel); resolution: M. Bestvina and N. Brady, Morse theory and finiteness properties of groups, Invent.
