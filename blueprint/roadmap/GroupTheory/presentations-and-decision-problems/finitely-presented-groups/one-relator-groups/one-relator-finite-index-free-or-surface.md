# Kourovka Problem 7.36, infinite-group case (groups all of whose finite-index subgroups are one-relator)

The proposition, the infinite-group case of Kourovka Problem 7.36 (which asks about all residually finite groups), that every infinite residually finite group $G$ in which every subgroup of finite index (including $G$) is one-relator is either free (on some set) or isomorphic to an orientable surface group $\langle a_1, b_1, \dots, a_g, b_g \mid [a_1, b_1] \cdots [a_g, b_g] \rangle$ with $[a, b] = aba^{-1}b^{-1}$ for some $g$, or to a non-orientable surface group $\langle c_1, \dots, c_k \mid c_1^2 \cdots c_k^2 \rangle$ for some $k \ge 1$. A group is one-relator if it is isomorphic to $\langle x_1, \dots, x_n \mid r \rangle$ for some $n$ and some word $r$ ($r = 1$ allowed).

Source: E. I. Khukhro and V. D. Mazurov (eds.), Unsolved Problems in Group Theory: The Kourovka Notebook, 21st issue, arXiv:1401.0300v49 (2026), Archive of Solved Problems, Problem 7.36 (O. V. Mel'nikov).
