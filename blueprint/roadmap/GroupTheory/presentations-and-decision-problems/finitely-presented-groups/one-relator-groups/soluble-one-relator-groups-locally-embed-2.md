# One-relator groups in soluble varieties need not be locally embeddable into finite groups

For some $k$, $n$ and $r \in F_n$, the group $F_n/(F_n^{(k)}\langle\langle r \rangle\rangle)$, defined by one relation in the variety of soluble groups of derived length at most $k$, is not locally embeddable into finite groups. A group $G$ is locally embeddable into finite groups ($U$-embeddable in the class of finite groups) if for every finite subset $M \subseteq G$ there are a finite group $A$ and a map $f : G \to A$ that is injective on $M$ and satisfies $f(xy) = f(x)f(y)$ whenever $x, y, xy \in M$. For $G = \langle a, b \mid (b^2)^a = b^3 \rangle$ and suitable $k$, the non-Hopfian quotient $G/G^{(k)}$ is an example. This answers Kourovka Problem 3.15(b) negatively. It is the negation of the proposition on [Kourovka Problem 3.15(b) (one-relator groups in soluble varieties)](soluble-one-relator-groups-locally-embed.md).

Kargapolov); resolution: A. I. Budkin and V. A. Gorbunov, Algebra and Logic 14 (1975), 73-84; example of Yu. I. Merzlyakov (1969) recorded in the Kourovka Archive.
