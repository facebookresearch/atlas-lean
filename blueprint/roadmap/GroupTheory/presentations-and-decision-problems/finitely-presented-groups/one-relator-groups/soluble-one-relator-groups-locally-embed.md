# Kourovka Problem 3.15(b) (one-relator groups in soluble varieties)

The proposition that for all natural numbers $k$ and $n$ and every $r$ in the free group $F_n$, the group $F_n/(F_n^{(k)}\langle\langle r \rangle\rangle)$, defined by the single relation $r$ in the variety of soluble groups of derived length at most $k$, is locally embeddable into finite groups. Here $F_n^{(k)}$ is the $k$-th derived subgroup and $\langle\langle r \rangle\rangle$ the normal closure of $r$. A group $G$ is locally embeddable into finite groups ($U$-embeddable in the class of finite groups) if for every finite subset $M \subseteq G$ there are a finite group $A$ and a map $f : G \to A$ that is injective on $M$ and satisfies $f(xy) = f(x)f(y)$ whenever $x, y, xy \in M$.

Source: E. I. Khukhro and V. D. Mazurov (eds.), Unsolved Problems in Group Theory: The Kourovka Notebook, 21st issue, arXiv:1401.0300v49 (2026), Archive of Solved Problems, Problem 3.15 (M. I. Kargapolov).
