# Equal permutation characters do not preserve primitivity

There are a finite group $G$ and actions such that a finite group $G$ acts on finite sets $X$ and $Y$ with the same permutation character, that is, every $g \in G$ has as many fixed points on $X$ as on $Y$, the action on $X$ is primitive and the action on $Y$ is not; Kourovka Problem 6.6 has a negative answer. Guralnick and Saxl give subgroups $H$, $K$ of a finite group with $1_H^G = 1_K^G$, $H$ maximal and $K$ not maximal. It is the negation of the proposition on [Kourovka Problem 6.6 (permutation characters and primitivity)](same-permutation-character-preserves-pri.md).

Source: E. I. Khukhro and V. D. Mazurov (eds.), Unsolved Problems in Group Theory: The Kourovka Notebook, 21st issue, arXiv:1401.0300v49 (2026), Archive of Solved Problems, Problem 6.6 (H. Wielandt); resolution: R. M. Guralnick and J. Saxl, Primitive permutation characters, in: Groups, Combinatorics and Geometry (Durham, 1990), London Math. Soc. Lecture Note Ser. 165, Cambridge Univ. Press, 1992, 364-367, https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511629259.032.
