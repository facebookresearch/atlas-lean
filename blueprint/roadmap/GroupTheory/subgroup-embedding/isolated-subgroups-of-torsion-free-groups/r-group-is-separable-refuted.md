# A nontrivial R-group that is not a union of two proper subsemigroups

There is a nontrivial $R$-group that is not the union of two proper subsemigroups. An $R$-group is a group in which $x^n = y^n$ with $n \ge 1$ implies $x = y$ (roots are unique); a group is separable if it is the union of two proper subsemigroups. This answers Kourovka Problem 1.64 negatively. It is the negation of the proposition on [Kourovka Problem 1.64 (separability of R-groups)](r-group-is-separable.md).

Source: E. I. Khukhro and V. D. Mazurov (eds.), Unsolved Problems in Group Theory: The Kourovka Notebook, 21st issue, arXiv:1401.0300v49 (2026), Archive of Solved Problems, Problem 1.64 (P. G. Kontorovich); resolution: S. J. Pride and J. Wiegold, Bull. London Math. Soc. 9 (1977), 36-37.
