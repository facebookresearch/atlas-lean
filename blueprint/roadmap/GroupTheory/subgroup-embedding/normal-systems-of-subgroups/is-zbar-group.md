# Z̄-groups

A group $G$ is a $\bar Z$-group when every chief factor $A/B$ of $G$ is central, $[A, G] \le B$; a chief factor is a pair of normal subgroups $B < A$ of $G$ with no normal subgroup of $G$ strictly between them. By a lemma of the resolving preprint this is equivalent to the Kurosh–Chernikov definition, that every normal system of $G$ is contained in a central one.

Source: E. I. Khukhro and V. D. Mazurov (eds.), Unsolved Problems in Group Theory: The Kourovka Notebook, 21st issue, arXiv:1401.0300v49 (2026), Problem 8.16 (Sh. S. Kemkhadze; marked solved in the main list); resolution: A. Jayadevan, Central chief factors of affine groups over local rings, preprint (2026), https://zenodo.org/records/22821641.
