# Kourovka Problem 8.73 (Thompson's question on separating cyclic subgroups)

The proposition that a finite group $G$ separates cyclic subgroups (for all $a, b \in G$ there is $g \in G$ with $\langle a \rangle \cap g^{-1}\langle b \rangle g = 1$) if and only if $G$ has no nontrivial cyclic normal subgroup. The forward direction is elementary: take $\langle a \rangle = \langle b \rangle$ a nontrivial cyclic normal subgroup.

Source: E. I. Khukhro and V. D. Mazurov (eds.), Unsolved Problems in Group Theory: The Kourovka Notebook, 21st issue, arXiv:1401.0300v49 (2026), Archive of Solved Problems, Problem 8.73 (J. G. Thompson); resolution: N. D. Podufalov, Abstracts of the 9th All-Union Group Theory Symposium, Moscow, 1984, 113-114 (Russian).
