# Kourovka Problem 8.74 (intersections of good subnormal subgroups)

The proposition that in every group $G$ the intersection of two good subnormal subgroups is good. A good subnormal subgroup of $G$ is a subnormal subgroup $H$ of $G$ such that the join $\langle H, J \rangle$ is subnormal in $G$ for every subnormal subgroup $J$ of $G$, where subnormal means reachable from $G$ by a finite chain of subgroups each normal in the next.

Source: E. I. Khukhro and V. D. Mazurov (eds.), Unsolved Problems in Group Theory: The Kourovka Notebook, 21st issue, arXiv:1401.0300v49 (2026), Problem 8.74 (J. G. Thompson; marked solved in the main list); resolution: K. Muliarchyk, Intersections of good subnormal subgroups: a counterexample to Kourovka Notebook Problem 8.74, preprint (2026), Theorem 1, https://kourovkanotebookorg.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/kourovka_8_74_solution.pdf.
