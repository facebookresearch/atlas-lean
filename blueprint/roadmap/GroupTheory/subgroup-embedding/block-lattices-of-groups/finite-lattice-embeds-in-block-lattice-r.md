# Not every finite lattice embeds in a block lattice of a finite group

Some finite lattice admits no embedding (injective, sending meets to intersections and joins to block joins) into the block lattice $C(G)$ of any finite group $G$. This follows from Smirnov's theorem that the variety generated by the block lattices of groups does not contain all finite lattices, since an embedding carries values of lattice terms in $L$ to their values in $C(G)$. The blocks of a group $G$ are the empty set and the cosets $gN$ of normal subgroups $N$; ordered by inclusion they form the block lattice $C(G)$, with meet the intersection and join of two blocks the intersection of all blocks containing their union. It is the negation of the proposition on [Kourovka Problem 9.62 (embedding finite lattices in block lattices)](finite-lattice-embeds-in-block-lattice.md).
