# The block lattices of groups do not generate all finite lattices

Some lattice identity holds in the block lattice $C(G)$ of every group $G$ but fails in some finite lattice; so the variety generated by the block lattices of groups does not contain all finite lattices. The blocks of a group $G$ are the empty set and the cosets $gN$ of normal subgroups $N$; ordered by inclusion they form the block lattice $C(G)$, with meet the intersection and join of two blocks the intersection of all blocks containing their union. It is the negation of the proposition on [Kourovka Problem 9.62 (the variety generated by block lattices)](block-lattice-variety-contains-finite-la.md).

Source: E. I. Khukhro and V. D. Mazurov (eds.), Unsolved Problems in Group Theory: The Kourovka Notebook, 21st issue, arXiv:1401.0300v49 (2026), Archive of Solved Problems, Problem 9.62 (D. M. Smirnov); resolution: D. M. Smirnov, On embedding lattices into block lattices of regular algebras and groups, Siberian Math. J. 33 (1992), no. 4, 663-668, https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00971131.
