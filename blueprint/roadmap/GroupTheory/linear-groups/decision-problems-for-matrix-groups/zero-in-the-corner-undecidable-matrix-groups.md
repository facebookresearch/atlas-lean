# The zero-in-the-corner problems for finitely generated rational matrix groups are undecidable

No algorithm decides, for a finite set of invertible rational $n \times n$ matrices with $n \ge 6$, whether the group they generate contains a matrix with $(1,1)$-entry zero; for $n \ge 9$, none decides whether it contains a non-identity matrix with $(1,n)$-entry zero.

Let $n \ge 1$ and let the input be a finite set $X$ of matrices in $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{Q})$, generating the subgroup $G = \langle X \rangle$. Then (source):

1. for $n \ge 6$ it is undecidable whether $G$ contains a matrix whose upper left $(1,1)$-entry is zero (Theorem 1);
2. for $n \ge 9$ it is undecidable whether $G$ contains a matrix other than the identity whose upper right $(1,n)$-entry is zero (Theorem 2).

The identity is excluded in the second problem because it always has zero off-diagonal entries. The source states that both results already hold with 17 generators. The analogous problems for semigroups of integer matrices are undecidable by reductions from the Post correspondence problem.

## Expansion

Theorem 1 reduces from the membership problem in $F_2 \times F_2$ (Theorem 4 of the source, Mihailova): for a group $Q$ given by a presentation with $k$ generators and $r$ relators, the subgroup $M(Q) = \{(x, y) \in F_k \times F_k : x = y \text{ in } Q\}$ is generated by the $k + r$ elements $(x_i, x_i)$ and $(1, r_j)$, and $(w, 1) \in M(Q)$ exactly when $w = 1$ in $Q$; taking $Q$ finitely presented with undecidable word problem and embedding $F_k \times F_k$ in $F_2 \times F_2$ gives a finitely generated subgroup with undecidable membership. The source embeds $F_2 \times F_2$ block-diagonally in $\mathrm{GL}_6(\mathbb{Q})$ through two copies of a free group $F_2 \le \mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$ given by Theorem 7, each with a vector $u_i \in \mathbb{Z}^3$ and a linear form $f_i$ such that $f_i(h u_i) \ge 0$ for every $h$ in that copy, with equality only for $h = 1$; let $u = (u_1, u_2)$ and let $H$ be the hyperplane $\ker(f_1(x) + f_2(y))$. For a finitely generated subgroup $\langle X \rangle$ and an element $g$, positivity gives that $g \in \langle X \rangle$ exactly when some $h \in \langle X \rangle$ maps $g^{-1} u$ into $H$ (the external hyperplane problem). A change of basis depending on $g$, sending $g^{-1} u$ to $e_1$ and $H$ to $\langle e_2, \dots, e_6 \rangle$, turns this into the question whether a conjugate of $\langle X \rangle$ contains a matrix with $(1,1)$-entry zero (Section 3); no subgroup is enlarged. Theorem 2 instead reuses the proof of the stabilizer problem: Borisov's torsion-free finitely presented group $G$ with undecidable word problem gives the finitely generated subgroup $M_1(G) = \{(x, y, z) \in F_2^3 : x = y,\ (y, z) \in M(G)\}$, the centralizer of $(a, b, w)$ meets $M_1(G)$ nontrivially exactly when $w$ has finite order in $G$ (Theorem 5), and three commuting copies of $F_2$ acting on $\mathbb{Q}^9$ turn this into the upper right corner problem. A good expansion adds and links pages for Theorems 5, 6 and 7 of the source and for Borisov's torsion-free group.
