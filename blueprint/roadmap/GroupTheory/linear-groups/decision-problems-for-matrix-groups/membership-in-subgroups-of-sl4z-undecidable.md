# Membership in finitely generated subgroups of SL(4, Z) is undecidable

No algorithm decides, given finitely many matrices in $\mathrm{SL}(4, \mathbb{Z})$ and one more matrix $T$, whether $T$ lies in the subgroup they generate; this is Mikhailova's theorem transferred from $F_2 \times F_2$.

Let $\mathrm{SL}(4, \mathbb{Z})$ be the group of $4 \times 4$ integer matrices of determinant $1$. The *group membership problem* in $\mathrm{SL}(4, \mathbb{Z})$ is the [membership problem](../../presentations-and-decision-problems/membership-and-equation-problems/submonoid-and-rational-subset-membership/membership-problem-for-a-subset.md) for finitely generated subgroups: given a finite set $\mathcal{G} \subseteq \mathrm{SL}(4, \mathbb{Z})$ and $T \in \mathrm{SL}(4, \mathbb{Z})$, decide whether $T$ belongs to the subgroup generated by $\mathcal{G}$.

**Theorem (Mikhailova).** Group membership is undecidable in $\mathrm{SL}(4, \mathbb{Z})$ (Theorem 3.9(i) of the survey read, which states it for $F_2 \times F_2$ and $\mathrm{SL}(4, \mathbb{Z})$).

Adding the inverses of the generators shows that membership in finitely generated subsemigroups of $\mathrm{SL}(4, \mathbb{Z})$ is undecidable as well. By contrast, these problems are decidable in $\mathrm{SL}(2, \mathbb{Z})$.

## Expansion

The group $F_2 \times F_2$ embeds in $\mathrm{SL}(4, \mathbb{Z})$: $\mathrm{SL}(2, \mathbb{Z})$ contains a free subgroup of rank $2$, and block-diagonal matrices realize the direct product. An undecidable problem of $F_2 \times F_2$ therefore stays undecidable in $\mathrm{SL}(4, \mathbb{Z})$, as the survey notes. Mikhailova's theorem for $F_2 \times F_2$ embeds the word problem of a finitely presented group with undecidable word problem. A good expansion would also link a page for the free subgroup of $\mathrm{SL}(2, \mathbb{Z})$.
