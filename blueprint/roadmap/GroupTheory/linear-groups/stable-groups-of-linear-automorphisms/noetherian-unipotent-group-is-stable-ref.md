# Noetherian unipotent groups that are not stable

There are $K$, $V$ and $\Gamma$ such that $K$ is a field, $V$ a vector space over $K$, and $\Gamma$ a group of $K$-linear automorphisms of $V$ that is Noetherian (every subgroup of $\Gamma$ is finitely generated) and all of whose elements are unipotent (for each $g \in \Gamma$ there is $m$ with $(g - 1)^m = 0$), but $\Gamma$ is not stable; Kourovka Problem 2.61 has a negative answer. A set $S$ of linear automorphisms is stable when there are subspaces $0 = W_0 \le W_1 \le \cdots \le W_r = V$ with $g(w) - w \in W_i$ for all $i < r$, $g \in S$ and $w \in W_{i+1}$. The Archive records counterexamples over fields of nonzero characteristic, and a positive result in characteristic $0$ when the unipotency indices are uniformly bounded. It is the negation of the proposition on [Kourovka Problem 2.61 (Noetherian unipotent groups are stable)](noetherian-unipotent-group-is-stable.md).

Plotkin); resolution: A. Yu. Ol'shanskii, The geometry of defining relations in groups, Kluwer, Dordrecht, 1991.
