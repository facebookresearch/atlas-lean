# Kourovka Problem 2.61 (Noetherian unipotent groups are stable)

The proposition that whenever $K$ is a field (of any characteristic), $V$ a vector space over $K$, and $\Gamma$ a group of $K$-linear automorphisms of $V$ that is Noetherian (every subgroup of $\Gamma$ is finitely generated) and all of whose elements are unipotent (for each $g \in \Gamma$ there is $m$ with $(g - 1)^m = 0$), the group $\Gamma$ is stable. A set $S$ of linear automorphisms is stable when there are subspaces $0 = W_0 \le W_1 \le \cdots \le W_r = V$ with $g(w) - w \in W_i$ for all $i < r$, $g \in S$ and $w \in W_{i+1}$.

Source: E. I. Khukhro and V. D. Mazurov (eds.), Unsolved Problems in Group Theory: The Kourovka Notebook, 21st issue, arXiv:1401.0300v49 (2026), Archive of Solved Problems, Problem 2.61 (B. I. Plotkin); resolution: A. Yu. Ol'shanskii, The geometry of defining relations in groups, Kluwer, Dordrecht, 1991.
