# Bounded unipotent groups in characteristic zero are locally finitely stable

Let $K$ be a field of characteristic $0$, $V$ a vector space over $K$ (of any dimension), and $G$ a group of $K$-linear automorphisms of $V$ whose elements are unipotent of uniformly bounded index: there is $m \in \mathbb{N}$ with $(g - 1)^m = 0$ for every $g \in G$. Then the subgroup generated by any finite subset of $G$ is stable. A set $S$ of linear automorphisms is stable when there are subspaces $0 = W_0 \le W_1 \le \cdots \le W_r = V$ with $g(w) - w \in W_i$ for all $i < r$, $g \in S$ and $w \in W_{i+1}$. This is the positive answer to Kourovka Problem 2.63, which the Archive derives from Heineken.
