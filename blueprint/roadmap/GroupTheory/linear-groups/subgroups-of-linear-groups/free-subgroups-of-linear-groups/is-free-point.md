# Free points in the complex plane

A complex number $z$ is a free point if the matrices $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ and $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ z & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ of $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{C})$ freely generate a free group of rank $2$: the homomorphism from $F_2$ sending its two basis elements to them is injective. For $z \ne 0$ the two matrices do not commute, so this is the same as generating a free group.

Source: E. I. Khukhro and V. D. Mazurov (eds.), Unsolved Problems in Group Theory: The Kourovka Notebook, 21st issue, arXiv:1401.0300v49 (2026), Archive of Solved Problems, Problem 4.41 (Yu. I. Merzlyakov).
