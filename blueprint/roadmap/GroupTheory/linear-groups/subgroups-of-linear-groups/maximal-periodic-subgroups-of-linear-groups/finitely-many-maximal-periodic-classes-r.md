# Infinitely many classes of maximal periodic subgroups in integral linear groups

For some $n$, some finitely generated subgroup $G$ of $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{Z})$ has maximal periodic subgroups in infinitely many conjugacy classes of $G$. A subgroup is periodic if all its elements have finite order, and maximal periodic if it is periodic and contained in no strictly larger periodic subgroup. Merzlyakov's example: with $H = F(a, b) \rtimes \langle\varphi\rangle$, where $\varphi$ inverts $a$ and fixes $b$, the subgroup of $H \times H'$ generated by $a$, $\varphi$, $a'\varphi'$, $bb'$ contains pairwise non-conjugate maximal periodic subgroups. This answers Kourovka Problem 1.73 negatively. It is the negation of the proposition on [Kourovka Problem 1.73 (conjugacy classes of maximal periodic subgroups)](finitely-many-maximal-periodic-classes.md).

Source: E. I. Khukhro and V. D. Mazurov (eds.), Unsolved Problems in Group Theory: The Kourovka Notebook, 21st issue, arXiv:1401.0300v49 (2026), Archive of Solved Problems, Problem 1.73 (V. P. Platonov); resolution: Yu. I. Merzlyakov (1973), as recorded in the Kourovka Archive.
