# Every square matrix is a product of transvections and a diagonal matrix

Let $\mathbb{k}$ be a field and $n$ a finite index set. For indices $i \ne j$ in $n$ and $c \in \mathbb{k}$, the
transvection $T_{ij}(c) = 1 + c\,E_{ij}$ is the identity matrix with $c$ added in position $(i, j)$. Every square
matrix $M$ over $\mathbb{k}$ indexed by $n$ can be written as

$$M = T_1 \cdots T_k \, D \, T'_1 \cdots T'_l$$

with transvections $T_1, \dots, T_k, T'_1, \dots, T'_l$ (either list may be empty) and a diagonal matrix $D$.
