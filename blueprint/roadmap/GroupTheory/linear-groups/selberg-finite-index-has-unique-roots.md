# Selberg's lemma, unique-roots form

Let $K$ be a field of characteristic zero and let $n$ be
a natural number. Every finitely generated subgroup $G$ of $\mathrm{GL}_n(K)$ has a subgroup $H$
of finite index in $G$ in which roots are unique: for all $a, b \in H$ and every natural number
$m \ne 0$, $a^m = b^m$ implies $a = b$.

## Sources

- A. Selberg, *On discontinuous groups in higher-dimensional symmetric spaces*, Contributions to Function Theory (1960): Selberg's lemma.
- R. C. Alperin, [*An elementary account of Selberg's lemma*](https://doi.org/10.5169/seals-87896), L'Enseignement Mathématique 33 (1987), 269–273, as background: it gives a torsion-free subgroup of finite index, which is weaker than unique roots.
- For unique roots: by Platonov's theorem, as stated in B. Nica, [*Linear groups — Malcev's theorem and Selberg's lemma*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1306.2385), §1, $G$ has finite-index subgroups that are residually $p$ and residually $q$ for two distinct primes $p, q$. Let $H$ be their intersection, of finite index in $G$. For a prime $\ell$, choose $r \in \{p, q\}$ with $r \ne \ell$; two distinct elements of $H$ have distinct images in some finite $r$-group, where $x \mapsto x^\ell$ is a bijection, so their $\ell$-th powers differ. Composing such maps gives injectivity of $x \mapsto x^m$ for every $m \ne 0$.
