# Infinitely many simple groups with proper subgroups of equal prime set

There are infinitely many finite simple groups $G$, none isomorphic to an alternating group, that
have a proper subgroup $H$ with $\pi(H) = \pi(G)$, where $\pi(X)$ is the set of prime divisors of
$|X|$: for every $N$ there is such a group of order greater than $N$.

This answers Kourovka Problem 11.64 (V. S. Monakhov) negatively: the Kourovka Archive records (V. I.
Zenkov) that the symplectic groups $G = S_{4k}(2^s)$ have proper subgroups $H \cong
\Omega^-_{4k}(2^s)$ with $\pi(H) = \pi(G)$, an infinite family of finite simple groups that are not
alternating.

## Sources

- E. I. Khukhro and V. D. Mazurov (eds.), Unsolved Problems in Group Theory: The Kourovka Notebook, 21st issue, arXiv:1401.0300v49 (2026), Archive of Solved Problems, Problem 11.64 (V. S. Monakhov); resolution: V. I. Zenkov, letter of 10 March 1994, Kourovka Archive, Problem 11.64: "No, there are infinitely many such groups. If `G = S_{4k}(2^s)` and `H ≅ Ω⁻_{4k}(2^s)`, then `π(G) = π(H)`", https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.0300v49.
