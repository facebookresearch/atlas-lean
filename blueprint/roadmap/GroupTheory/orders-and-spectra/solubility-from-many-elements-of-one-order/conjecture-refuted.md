# Many elements of one order do not force solubility

It is not the case that for every finite group $G$ and every positive integer $k$, if at least half of the elements of $G$ have order exactly $k$ (that is, $2\,|\{g \in G : \operatorname{ord}(g) = k\}| \ge |G|$), then $G$ is soluble. Kourovka Problem 21.43 has a negative answer: in $A_5^N$ the number of elements of order $30$ is $60^N - 45^N - 40^N - 36^N + 25^N + 21^N + 16^N - 1$, more than half of $60^N$ for $N \ge 5$, although $A_5^N$ is not soluble. It is the negation of the proposition on [Kourovka Problem 21.43 (many elements of one order force solubility)](conjecture.md).
