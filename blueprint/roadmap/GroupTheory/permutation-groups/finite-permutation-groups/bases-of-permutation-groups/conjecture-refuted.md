# Generalised Saxl graphs without common neighbours

It is not the case that for every finite set $\Omega$ with at least two points and every primitive group $G$ of permutations of $\Omega$ in which every base (finite set with trivial pointwise stabilizer) has at least two points, any two points $\alpha, \beta \in \Omega$ (not necessarily distinct) have a common neighbour in the generalised Saxl graph of $G$, the graph in which distinct points are adjacent when some minimum-size base contains both. Rizzoli and Thomas construct, for every $b \ge 2$, infinitely many primitive groups of minimum base size $b$ whose generalised Saxl graphs have two non-adjacent vertices with no common neighbour; Kourovka Problem 21.29 has a negative answer. It is the negation of the proposition on [The generalised Saxl common neighbour conjecture (statement)](conjecture.md).

Source: C. Freedman, H. Y. Huang, M. Lee and K. Rekvényi, arXiv:2410.22613v4 (2026), Definition 1.1 and Conjecture 1.2; E. I. Khukhro and V. D. Mazurov (eds.), Unsolved Problems in Group Theory: The Kourovka Notebook, 21st issue, arXiv:1401.0300v49 (2026), Problem 21.29; resolution: A. Rizzoli and A. R. Thomas, Common neighbour conjectures for Saxl graphs fail at every base size, arXiv:2609.01367 (2026).
