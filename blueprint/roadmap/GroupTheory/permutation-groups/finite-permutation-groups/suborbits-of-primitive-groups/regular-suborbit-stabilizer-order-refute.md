# Point stabilizers inducing a regular group on a suborbit but larger than it

There are $G$, $\Omega$, $\alpha$ and $\delta$ such that $G$ is a primitive group of permutations of a finite set $\Omega$, $\alpha \ne \delta$ are points of $\Omega$, and the stabilizer $G_\alpha$ induces a regular permutation group on the orbit $\Delta = G_\alpha \delta$, in the sense that any two elements of $G_\alpha$ that agree at $\delta$ agree on all of $\Delta$ (the elements acting trivially on $\Delta$ may form a nontrivial kernel), but $|G_\alpha| \ne |\Delta|$; Kourovka Problem 9.69 has a negative answer. Spiga's example is a primitive group of diagonal type with $G_{\alpha\beta}$ nontrivial and normal in $G_\alpha$. It is the negation of the proposition on [Kourovka Problem 9.69 (point stabilizers inducing a regular group on a suborbit)](regular-suborbit-stabilizer-order.md).

Cameron); marked solved in the main list); resolution: P. Spiga, A generalization of Sims' conjecture for finite primitive groups and two point stabilizers in primitive groups, J. Group Theory 25 (2022), no. 1, 113-126, https://doi.org/10.1515/jgth-2021-0032.
