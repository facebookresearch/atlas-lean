# Genus g systems of permutations

For natural numbers $g$, $n$ and $r$, a tuple $x_0, \dots, x_{r-1}$ of permutations of $\{0, \dots, n - 1\}$ is a genus $g$ system if every $x_i$ is different from the identity, the product $x_0 x_1 \cdots x_{r-1}$ is the identity, the subgroup generated by the $x_i$ is transitive, and $\sum_i \operatorname{ind} x_i = 2(n + g - 1)$ (the Riemann–Hurwitz relation), where the index of a permutation $\sigma$ of $\{0, \dots, n - 1\}$ is $\operatorname{ind}\sigma = \sum (\ell - 1)$ over the cycle lengths $\ell$ of $\sigma$ ($n$ minus the number of orbits of $\langle\sigma\rangle$). The subtraction is in $\mathbb{N}$ and truncates only when $n = g = 0$.

Source: R. M. Guralnick and J. G. Thompson, Finite groups of genus zero, J. Algebra 131 (1990), 303-341, as recalled in K. Magaard, S. Shpectorov and G. Wang, Generating sets of affine groups of low genus, arXiv:1108.4833, Section 3, Definition 11.
