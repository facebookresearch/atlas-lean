# The Guralnick–Thompson conjecture

The proposition that for every natural number $g$ there is a bound $N$ such that, for every genus $g$ system $x_0, \dots, x_{r-1}$ of any degree $n$, every composition factor $S$ of the subgroup of $S_n$ generated by the $x_i$ that is neither cyclic nor isomorphic to an alternating group $A_m$ (any $m$) has $|S| \le N$. A genus $g$ system of degree $n$ is a tuple $x_0, \dots, x_{r-1}$ of non-identity permutations of $\{0, \dots, n - 1\}$ with $x_0 \cdots x_{r-1} = 1$ that generate a transitive subgroup of $S_n$ and satisfy $\sum_i \operatorname{ind} x_i = 2(n + g - 1)$ (subtraction in $\mathbb{N}$, which truncates only when $n = g = 0$; see [genus $g$ systems](is-genus-system.md)), where the index of a permutation $\sigma$ of $\{0, \dots, n - 1\}$ is $\operatorname{ind}\sigma = \sum (\ell - 1)$ over the cycle lengths $\ell$ of $\sigma$ ($n$ minus the number of orbits of $\langle\sigma\rangle$). A composition factor of a group is a simple group that is a homomorphic image of a subnormal subgroup. By Riemann's existence theorem, for $n \ge 1$ these groups are the monodromy groups of connected degree-$n$ genus-$g$ branched covers of the sphere (the degree-$0$ systems generate the trivial group), so this says that only finitely many simple groups other than cyclic and alternating ones occur as their composition factors.

Source: R. M. Guralnick and J. G. Thompson, Finite groups of genus zero, J. Algebra 131 (1990), 303-341, as recalled in K. Magaard, S. Shpectorov and G. Wang, Generating sets of affine groups of low genus, arXiv:1108.4833, Section 3.
