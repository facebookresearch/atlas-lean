# Soluble subgroups of large symmetric and alternating groups have disjoint conjugates

There is $N$ such that for every $n \ge N$ and $G = S_n$ or $G = A_n$, any two soluble subgroups $H, K \le G$ admit $x \in G$ with $H \cap x^{-1}Kx = 1$. The Notebook marks part a) of Problem 21.3 solved; part b), the threshold $n \ge 21$, is not asserted.

Source: E. I. Khukhro and V. D. Mazurov (eds.), Unsolved Problems in Group Theory: The Kourovka Notebook, 21st issue, arXiv:1401.0300v49 (2026), Problem 21.3, part a); resolution: A. Rizzoli, The soluble-intersection limit in S_n, preprint, 2026, Corollary 4.1, https://kourovkanotebookorg.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/kourovka_21_3a.pdf.
