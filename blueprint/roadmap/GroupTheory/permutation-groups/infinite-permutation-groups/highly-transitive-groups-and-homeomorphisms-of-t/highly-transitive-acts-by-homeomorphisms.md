# Kourovka Problem 5.40 (highly transitive groups of homeomorphisms of the rationals)

The proposition that whenever a countable group $G$ acts faithfully on an infinite set $\Omega$, $k$-transitively for every $k \in \mathbb{N}$, and every nonidentity element of $G$ moves infinitely many points, there is a bijection $e : \Omega \to \mathbb{Q}$ under which every element of $G$ acts continuously, that is, $q \mapsto e(g \cdot e^{-1}(q))$ is continuous on $\mathbb{Q}$ with its usual topology for every $g \in G$ (each such map is then a homeomorphism, with inverse given by $g^{-1}$). $\Omega$ is required to be infinite.

Source: E. I. Khukhro and V. D. Mazurov (eds.), Unsolved Problems in Group Theory: The Kourovka Notebook, 21st issue, arXiv:1401.0300v49 (2026), Archive of Solved Problems, Problem 5.40 (P. M. Neumann); resolution: A. H. Mekler, Groups embeddable in the autohomeomorphisms of Q, J. London Math. Soc. (2) 33 (1986), 49-58, https://doi.org/10.1112/jlms/s2-33.1.49.
