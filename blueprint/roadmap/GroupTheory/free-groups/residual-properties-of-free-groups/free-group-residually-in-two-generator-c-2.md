# Free groups need not be residually in a generating class of 2-generator groups

There is a family $(K_i)$ of groups, each generated by two elements, that generates the variety of all groups (no nontrivial word of $F_{\mathbb{N}}$ is killed by every homomorphism into every $K_i$), such that some nontrivial $w \in F_2$ is sent to $1$ by every surjective homomorphism from $F_2$ onto every $K_i$. By Pride, the single group $\langle a, t \mid [a, t]^n \rangle$ with $n > 1$ is an example: a free group is residually in it, with surjections, exactly when its rank is at least $3$. This answers Kourovka Problem 6.18 negatively. It is the negation of the proposition on [Kourovka Problem 6.18 (free groups residually in a class of 2-generator groups)](free-group-residually-in-two-generator-c.md).

Source: E. I. Khukhro and V. D. Mazurov (eds.), Unsolved Problems in Group Theory: The Kourovka Notebook, 21st issue, arXiv:1401.0300v49 (2026), Archive of Solved Problems, Problem 6.18 (V. M. Levchuk); resolution: S. J. Pride, Residual properties of free groups. III, Math. Z. 132 (1973), 245-248; S. J. Pride, Residual properties of free groups (thesis abstract), Bull. Austral. Math. Soc. 10 (1974), 477-478.
