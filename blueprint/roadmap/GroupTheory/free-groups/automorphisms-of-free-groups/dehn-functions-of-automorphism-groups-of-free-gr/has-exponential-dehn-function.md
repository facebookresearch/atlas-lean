# Groups with exponential Dehn function

A group $G$ has exponential Dehn function if $G$ is finitely presented (some finite type $\alpha$ and finite set of relators $R \subseteq F(\alpha)$ give $\langle \alpha \mid R \rangle \cong G$), and for every such finite presentation the Dehn function $\delta$ satisfies $\delta \preceq 2^m$ and $2^m \preceq \delta$. Here the area of a relation $w$ is the least number of factors $u r^{\pm 1} u^{-1}$ ($r \in R$, $u$ in the free group) whose product is $w$, and $\delta(m)$ is the largest area of an element of the normal closure of $R$ of reduced length at most $m$, and $f \preceq g$ means that some integer $C > 0$ satisfies $f(m) \le C\,g(Cm + C) + Cm + C$ for all $m \in \mathbb{N}$.

Source: B. Farb (ed.), Problems on Mapping Class Groups and Related Topics (2006), Ch. 20 (M. Bridson and K. Vogtmann), Question 7.1.
