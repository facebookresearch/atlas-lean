# Triviality of a fixed subgroup's intersection with a subgroup of F_n × F_m is undecidable

No algorithm decides, given an automorphism $\Phi$ of a direct product $F_n \times F_m$ of free groups and a finitely generated subgroup $H$, whether the fixed subgroup of $\Phi$ meets $H$ only in the identity.

For a [group](../../../group-basics/group.md) $G$ and an endomorphism $\Phi$ of $G$, the *fixed subgroup* is $\mathrm{Fix}(\Phi) = \{g \in G : g\Phi = g\}$; for free groups this is the [fixed subgroup of a free group automorphism](fixed-subgroup.md). Here $F_n$ and $F_m$ are free groups of finite ranks $n$ and $m$.

**Theorem (Carvalho).** There is no algorithm that takes as input ranks $n, m$, an automorphism $\Phi \in \mathrm{Aut}(F_n \times F_m)$, given by the images of the standard generators as words, and a finitely generated subgroup $H \le F_n \times F_m$, given by a finite generating set of words, and decides whether $\mathrm{Fix}(\Phi) \cap H$ is trivial (Proposition 3.1 of the source).

By contrast, for ranks $m, n > 1$ (the source's convention is that free groups have rank at least $2$), the source gives an algorithm that decides, for two endomorphisms of $F_n \times F_m$ one of which is of the source's types VI or VII (which include all injective endomorphisms), whether their fixed subgroups intersect trivially (Theorem 3.3).

## Expansion

Let $G = \langle X \mid R\rangle$ be finitely presented and $w$ a word in $X$, written $w = z^k$ with $z$ not a proper power. On $F_X \times F_X$ take $\Phi(x, y) = (x\varphi, y\lambda_z)$, where $\varphi$ permutes the letters with no fixed letter and $\lambda_z$ is conjugation by $z$. The fibre subgroup $\{(u, v) : u =_G v\}$ is finitely generated, and its intersection with $\mathrm{Fix}(\Phi)$ equals its intersection with $\langle (1, z)\rangle$, which is trivial exactly when $z$, equivalently $w$, has infinite order in $G$. So the problem would decide the order problem of finitely presented groups, which is undecidable (Baumslag, Boone and Neumann). A good expansion also links the finite generation of the fibre subgroup $\{(u, v) : u =_G v\}$ for finitely presented $G$.
