# A product of two p-subgroups that is not a p-group

There are a prime $p$, a group $G$ and subgroups $A$, $B$ of $G$ that are $p$-groups (every element has order a power of $p$) with $G = AB$ (every element is a product $ab$), but $G$ is not a $p$-group; Kourovka Problem 1.36 has a negative answer. Such a group is necessarily infinite. It is the negation of the proposition on [Kourovka Problem 1.36 (products of two p-subgroups)](product-of-psubgroups-is-pgroup.md).

Source: E. I. Khukhro and V. D. Mazurov (eds.), Unsolved Problems in Group Theory: The Kourovka Notebook, 21st issue, arXiv:1401.0300v49 (2026), Archive of Solved Problems, Problem 1.36 (Sh. S. Kemkhadze); resolution: Ya. P. Sysak, Products of infinite groups, Math. Inst. Akad. Nauk Ukrain. SSR, Kiev, 1982 (Russian).
