# Kourovka Problem 1.36 (products of two p-subgroups)

The proposition that whenever $p$ is a prime and $G = AB$ is the product of subgroups $A$ and $B$ (every element is a product $ab$) that are $p$-groups (every element has order a power of $p$), then $G$ is a $p$-group. For finite $G$ this holds, since $|G|$ divides $|A||B|$.

Source: E. I. Khukhro and V. D. Mazurov (eds.), Unsolved Problems in Group Theory: The Kourovka Notebook, 21st issue, arXiv:1401.0300v49 (2026), Archive of Solved Problems, Problem 1.36 (Sh. S. Kemkhadze); resolution: Ya. P. Sysak, Products of infinite groups, Math. Inst. Akad. Nauk Ukrain. SSR, Kiev, 1982 (Russian).
