# A locally finite group with a full Sylow basis that is not locally soluble

There is a locally finite group with a full Sylow basis that is not locally soluble. Jayadevan's example is a countable residually finite, locally finite group generated by Sylow subgroups $P_2, P_3, P_5$ forming a full Sylow basis and containing $\mathrm{Sym}(5)$. The Notebook marks Problem 3.12 solved ("No, not always"). A full Sylow basis in Golberg's sense is a choice, for each prime $p$, of a Sylow $p$-subgroup $P_p$ (a maximal $p$-subgroup) such that for every finite set $J$ of primes the subgroup generated by the $P_p$ with $p \in J$ is a $J$-group (its elements have finite order with all prime divisors in $J$), and the $P_p$ together generate $G$. It is the negation of the proposition on [Kourovka Problem 3.12 (locally finite groups with a full Sylow basis)](full-sylow-basis-locally-soluble.md).

Source: E. I. Khukhro and V. D. Mazurov (eds.), Unsolved Problems in Group Theory: The Kourovka Notebook, 21st issue, arXiv:1401.0300v49 (2026), Problem 3.12 (Yu. M. Gorchakov); resolution: Achyuth Jayadevan, A nonsoluble residually finite, locally finite group with a full prime-support Sylow basis, preprint, Zenodo (12 September 2026), Theorem 1.1, https://zenodo.org/records/22721349.
