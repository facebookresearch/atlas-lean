# Periodic groups without a π × π' decomposition

There are $\pi$ and $G$ such that $\pi$ is a set of natural numbers, $\pi'$ its complement, $G$ is a periodic group (every element has finite order), and every $\pi$-element of $G$ commutes with every $\pi'$-element, where a $\pi$-element is an element of finite order all of whose prime divisors lie in $\pi$, but it is not the case that $G$ is the internal direct product of a maximal $\pi$-subgroup $P$ and a maximal $\pi'$-subgroup $Q$: $P \cap Q = 1$, $P$ and $Q$ generate $G$, and they commute elementwise; Kourovka Problem 2.13 has a negative answer. Merzlyakov's counterexample is a quotient of Adian's torsion-free central extension of a free Burnside group of odd exponent $n \ge 4381$, with $\pi = \{p\}$ for a prime $p$ coprime to $n$. It is the negation of the proposition on [Kourovka Problem 2.13 (π × π' decompositions of periodic groups)](pi-pi-prime-direct-decomposition.md).

Ivanov); resolution: Yu. I. Merzlyakov (1973), using S. I. Adian, On some torsion-free groups, Math. USSR-Izv. 5 (1971), 475-484.
