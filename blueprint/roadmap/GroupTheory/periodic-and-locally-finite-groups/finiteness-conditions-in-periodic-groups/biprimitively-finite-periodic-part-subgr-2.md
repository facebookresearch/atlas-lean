# In a biprimitively finite group the elements of finite order need not form a subgroup

There is a biprimitively finite group containing two elements of finite order whose product has infinite order. Since biprimitively finite groups are conjugacy biprimitively finite, this answers both variants of Kourovka Problem 6.57 negatively. It is the negation of the proposition on [Kourovka Problem 6.57 (elements of finite order in biprimitively finite groups)](biprimitively-finite-periodic-part-subgr.md).

Source: E. I. Khukhro and V. D. Mazurov (eds.), Unsolved Problems in Group Theory: The Kourovka Notebook, 21st issue, arXiv:1401.0300v49 (2026), Archive of Solved Problems, Problem 6.57 (V. P. Shunkov); resolution: A. A. Cherep, The set of elements of finite order in a biprimitively finite group, Algebra and Logic 26 (1987), 311-313, https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01980245.
