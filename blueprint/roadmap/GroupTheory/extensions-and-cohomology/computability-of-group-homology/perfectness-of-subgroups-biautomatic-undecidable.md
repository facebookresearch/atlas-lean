# Perfectness of finitely presentable subgroups of residually finite biautomatic groups is undecidable

No algorithm decides whether a finitely presentable subgroup of a residually finite biautomatic group, given by a finite generating set, is perfect.

A group $H$ is *perfect* if it equals its commutator subgroup, that is, if $H_1(H, \mathbb{Z}) = 0$. Biautomatic groups are those admitting a biautomatic structure in the sense of Epstein, Cannon, Holt, Levy, Paterson and Thurston, a strengthening of the structure of an [automatic group](../../free-groups/automorphisms-of-free-groups/automaticity-of-automorphism-groups-of-free-grou/is-automatic-group.md); [hyperbolic groups](../../geometric-group-theory/hyperbolic-groups/is-hyperbolic.md) and their finitely generated central extensions are examples (Neumann and Reeves). An instance consists of a finite presentation $\langle X \mid R \rangle$ of a group $G$ and a finite set $S$ of words in $X^{\pm 1}$, and the question is whether the subgroup $H < G$ generated by $S$ is perfect.

**Theorem (Bridson).** There is no algorithm that can determine whether or not a finitely presentable subgroup of a residually finite, biautomatic group is perfect (Theorem C of the source).

More precisely, the proof constructs a recursive sequence of pairs $(G_n, H_n)$, where $G_n = \langle X \mid R_n \rangle$ is a residually finite biautomatic group with a finite classifying space $K(G_n, 1)$ and $H_n$ is the subgroup generated by a finite set $S_n$ of words, such that $|X|$, $|R_n|$ and $|S_n|$ do not depend on $n$, every $H_n$ is finitely presentable, and no algorithm decides which $H_n$ are perfect. Consequently no algorithm can produce, from $(R_n, S_n)$, a finite presentation of $H_n$, although one exists.

## Expansion

The seed is a construction of Collins and Miller: a recursive sequence of finite presentations $Q_n = \langle X \mid R_n \rangle$ of perfect groups, with $|R_n| = k > |X|$, whose triviality is undecidable and which are aspherical when non-trivial. A modified Rips construction gives hyperbolic perfect groups $\Gamma_n$ with finitely generated normal subgroups $N_n$ and $\Gamma_n / N_n \cong Q_n$; passing to universal central extensions $\widetilde{\Gamma}_n$ gives biautomatic groups (Neumann and Reeves), and $G_n = \widetilde{\Gamma}_n \times \widetilde{\Gamma}_n$. The fiber product $H_n < G_n$ of $\widetilde{\Gamma}_n \to Q_n$ is finitely presentable by the 1-2-3 theorem, and a spectral sequence computation gives $H_1(H_n, \mathbb{Z}) \cong H_2(Q_n, \mathbb{Z})$. If $Q_n = 1$ then $H_n = G_n$ is perfect; if $Q_n \neq 1$ then $Q_n$ is aspherical with more relators than generators, so $H_2(Q_n, \mathbb{Z}) \neq 0$ and $H_n$ is not perfect. A complete expansion links pages for the Collins–Miller construction, the Rips construction, the 1-2-3 theorem, the biautomaticity of central extensions of hyperbolic groups and the residual finiteness of the groups involved, none of which has a page.
