# Zenkov's rank bound in elementary abelian extensions (Kourovka 21.150)

Call a subgroup
elementary abelian if it is abelian and there is a prime $p$ with $x^p = 1$ for all its elements
$x$. This is the proposition that for every finite group $G$, all elementary abelian subgroups $A$
and $B$ of $G$ (possibly for different primes) with $A$ normal, $A \cap B = 1$ and
$\langle A, B \rangle = G$ (so $B$ is a complement to $A$ and $B \cong G/A$), and every $a \in A$
whose centralizer $C_B(a)$ in $B$ is trivial, the following holds for $H = \langle a, B \rangle$,
with centre $Z(H)$ and derived subgroup $H' = [H, H]$: whenever $B$ is generated by a finite set of
$k$ elements, $Z(H) \cap H'$ (with $Z(H)$ viewed as a subgroup of $G$) is generated by a finite set
of at most $k$ elements. That is, the rank of $Z(H) \cap H'$ is at most the rank of $B$.

The question is due to V. I. Zenkov.
