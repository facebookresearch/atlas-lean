# Boundary maps induced by subgroup inclusions

For a subgroup $H$ of a group $G$, a generating family $P$ of $G$ and a generating family $Q$ of $H$, the inclusion $H \to G$ induces a boundary map when every sequence in $H$ converging at infinity for $Q$ converges at infinity in $G$ for $P$, and any two equivalent such sequences of $H$ remain equivalent in $G$. Here a sequence $(u_m)$ converges at infinity when $(u_m \cdot u_n)_1 \to \infty$ as $m, n \to \infty$, and two such sequences $(u_m), (v_n)$ are equivalent when $(u_m \cdot v_n)_1 \to \infty$, with Gromov products at the identity for the respective word metrics. This says that the Cannon–Thurston map $\partial H \to \partial G$ is well defined.

Source: M. Kapovich, Problems on boundaries of groups and Kleinian groups (collected at a 2005 AIM workshop), Problem 67 (M. Mitra).
