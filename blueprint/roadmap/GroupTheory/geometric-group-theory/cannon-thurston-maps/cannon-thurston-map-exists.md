# Mitra's question on Cannon–Thurston maps (Kapovich Problem 67)

The proposition that for every hyperbolic group $G$ and every subgroup $H$ of $G$ that is itself hyperbolic (not assumed quasiconvex), there are finite generating families $P$ of $G$ and $Q$ of $H$ for which the inclusion $H \to G$ induces a boundary map when every sequence in $H$ converging at infinity for $Q$ converges at infinity in $G$ for $P$, and any two equivalent such sequences of $H$ remain equivalent in $G$. Here a sequence $(u_m)$ converges at infinity when $(u_m \cdot u_n)_1 \to \infty$ as $m, n \to \infty$, and two such sequences $(u_m), (v_n)$ are equivalent when $(u_m \cdot v_n)_1 \to \infty$, with Gromov products at the identity for the word metrics. A group is hyperbolic if for some finite generating family its word metric is $\delta$-hyperbolic for some real $\delta$.

Source: M. Kapovich, Problems on boundaries of groups and Kleinian groups (collected at a 2005 AIM workshop), Problem 67 (M. Mitra).
