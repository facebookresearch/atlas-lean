# Cannon–Thurston maps do not always exist

There are a hyperbolic group $G$ and a hyperbolic subgroup $H$ such that for no finite generating families $P$ of $G$ and $Q$ of $H$ does the inclusion induce a boundary map, that is, it is not the case that the inclusion $H \to G$ induces a boundary map when every sequence in $H$ converging at infinity for $Q$ converges at infinity in $G$ for $P$, and any two equivalent such sequences of $H$ remain equivalent in $G$. Here a sequence $(u_m)$ converges at infinity when $(u_m \cdot u_n)_1 \to \infty$ as $m, n \to \infty$, and two such sequences $(u_m), (v_n)$ are equivalent when $(u_m \cdot v_n)_1 \to \infty$. A group is hyperbolic if for some finite generating family its word metric is $\delta$-hyperbolic for some real $\delta$. Baker and Riley give such a $G$ with $H$ free of rank $3$, answering Mitra's question negatively. It is the negation of the proposition on [Mitra's question on Cannon–Thurston maps (Kapovich Problem 67)](cannon-thurston-map-exists.md).

Source: M. Kapovich, Problems on boundaries of groups and Kleinian groups (collected at a 2005 AIM workshop), Problem 67 (M. Mitra); resolution: O. Baker and T. R. Riley, Cannon–Thurston maps do not always exist, Forum Math. Sigma 1 (2013), e3, DOI 10.1017/fms.2013.4, arXiv:1206.0505, Theorem 1.
