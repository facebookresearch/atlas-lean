# Polynomial growth

Let $P$ be a generating family of a group $G$, with growth function
$\gamma_P$. Then $P$ has polynomial growth if, for every natural number $n$, the ball of radius
$n$ (the set of elements of $G$ of word length at most $n$ with respect to $P$) is finite, and
there are natural numbers $C$ and $d$ such that $\gamma_P(n) \le C (n + 1)^d$ for every
natural number $n$.

Gromov's definition asks for $\#B(r) \le C r^d$ for all $r \ge 1$; with the exponent $(n + 1)^d$ the same inequality also covers the radius $0$, and the two definitions agree up to changing $C$. For a finite generating family the balls are always finite, and polynomial growth does not depend on which finite generating family is used, so it is a property of the finitely generated group.

## Sources

- M. Gromov, *Groups of polynomial growth and expanding maps*, Publ. Math. IHÉS 53 (1981), 53–73, p. 54.
