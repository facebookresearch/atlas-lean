# The Diophantine problem in Thompson's groups F and T is undecidable

No algorithm decides whether a finite system of equations with constants has a solution in Thompson's group $F$; the same holds in Thompson's group $T$, which contains $F$ as an equationally definable subgroup.

Let $G$ be a finitely generated group and $V$ a finite set of variables. An *equation* in $G$ is an element $w$ of the free product $G * F(V)$ of $G$ with the free group on $V$, written $w = 1$; a *solution* is a homomorphism $\phi \colon G * F(V) \to G$ that fixes $G$ pointwise and sends $w$ to $1$. A finite system of equations has a solution if one homomorphism solves all of them. The *Diophantine problem* in $G$ asks, given a finite system of equations in $G$ (with constants written as words in the generators), whether it has a solution.

**Theorem (Elliott and Levine).** The Diophantine problem in [Thompson's group $F$](thompson-f.md) is undecidable (Theorem 1.1 of arXiv:2502.14970v3). The Diophantine problem in Thompson's group $T$ is undecidable (Corollary 1.2 of arXiv:2502.14970v3).

By contrast, $F$ has decidable conjugacy problem, so $F$ is a group with decidable conjugacy problem and undecidable Diophantine problem. Bardakov and Tolstykh had shown earlier that the first-order theory of $F$ is undecidable, a weaker statement. The source expects the method not to apply to Thompson's group $V$, whose abelianization is trivial.

## Expansion

The natural numbers are interpreted in $F$ as the submonoid generated by $x_0$, which is definable by a system of equations; addition is then the product $x_0^k x_0^l = x_0^{k+l}$. Multiplication is defined through exponent sums: the abelianization of $F$ is $\mathbb{Z}^2$, with $x_0$ and $x_1$ mapping to a basis, and because every element of the commutator subgroup of $F$ is a product of at most two commutators, the sets of elements with prescribed exponent sums in $x_0$ and $x_1$ are equationally definable. A definable set of pairs $(x_1^p, (x_0 x_1^p)^q)$ then gives $kl = m$ exactly when suitable exponent sums match. So every system of polynomial equations over $\mathbb{N}$ becomes a system of equations in $F$ with the same solvability, and solvability of the former is undecidable (Theorem 2.12 of the source, Matiyasevich): by the DPRM theorem a recursively enumerable non-recursive set, which exists because halting is undecidable, is Diophantine. For $T$, the centralizer in $T$ of two elements $x_0', x_1'$ generating the elements of $F$ supported on $(0, \tfrac12)$ is the copy of $F$ consisting of the elements of $T$ that fix $[0, \tfrac12]$ pointwise; as a centralizer of a finite set it is equationally definable.
