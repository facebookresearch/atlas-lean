# Automaton group

An automaton group is the group of tree automorphisms generated by the states of a finite invertible Mealy automaton: each state acts on finite words over the alphabet, rewriting the first letter and passing to another state.

A Mealy automaton is a tuple $\mathcal{A} = (Q, \Sigma, \delta, \sigma)$ with finite sets $Q$ (states) and $\Sigma$ (alphabet), a transition map $\delta\colon Q \times \Sigma \to Q$ and an output map $\sigma\colon Q \times \Sigma \to \Sigma$. Each state $p$ defines a map $\sigma_p\colon \Sigma^* \to \Sigma^*$ by $\sigma_p(\text{empty word}) = \text{empty word}$ and

$$
\sigma_p(xw) = \sigma(p, x)\, \sigma_{\delta(p, x)}(w) \qquad (x \in \Sigma,\ w \in \Sigma^*).
$$

The automaton is invertible when $x \mapsto \sigma(p, x)$ is a permutation of $\Sigma$ for every state $p$; then every $\sigma_p$ is a bijection of $\Sigma^*$ preserving lengths and prefixes, that is, an automorphism of the rooted tree $\Sigma^*$, and it is finite-state since its sections are among the finitely many $\sigma_q$. The group generated by an invertible $\mathcal{A}$ is the subgroup of the automorphism group of $\Sigma^*$ generated by $\{\sigma_p : p \in Q\}$; an automaton group is a group of this form. It comes with the finite generating set $\{\sigma_p : p \in Q\}$.

## Expansion

Automaton groups include the Grigorchuk group, the Aleshin group and the Basilica group. Their word problem is decidable: words $u$ and $v$ in the generators and their inverses represent the same element exactly when $u v^{-1}$ acts trivially, and since the sections of $u v^{-1}$ lie in a finite set computable from the automaton, triviality is checked by a finite computation on the first letters and the sections. Conventions differ on composition order; the source composes as a right action, $\sigma_{pq} = \sigma_q \circ \sigma_p$, which does not affect the group up to isomorphism.
