# Functionally recursive groups and contracting automata groups

A functionally recursive group is a finitely generated group acting on finite words, given by a transducer that says how each generator rewrites the first letter and which word in the generators acts on the rest; automata and contracting automata groups are the cases where these words do not grow or eventually shrink.

**Functionally recursive groups.** Let $A$ be a finite alphabet, $S$ a finite set and $F_S$ the free group on $S$. An *asynchronous transducer* is a map $\Phi\colon A \times S \to F_S \times A$ such that for each $s \in S$ the map $a \mapsto a'$, where $\Phi(a, s) = (g', a')$, is a permutation of $A$. (This is a different object from the asynchronous transducers $(\Sigma, Q, q_0, \lambda)$ with transition function $\lambda\colon Q \times \Sigma \to Q \times \Sigma^*$ that define rational functions on infinite words, as in the [finiteness problem for groups generated by asynchronous transducers](../brin-thompson-groups/asynchronous-transducer-finiteness-undecidable.md).) Such a $\Phi$ defines an action of $F_S$ on the set $A^*$ of finite words over $A$: if $\Phi(a_1, s) = (g', a_1')$ then

$$
(a_1 a_2 \cdots a_n)^s = a_1' (a_2 \cdots a_n)^{g'},
$$

extended to all of $F_S$; by induction on word length each $s$ acts on $A^n$ by a bijection, so $F_S$ acts on $A^*$ by bijections that preserve lengths and prefixes. Without the permutation condition the formula need not define injective maps (for $A = \{0, 1\}$, $S = \{s\}$ and $\Phi(0, s) = \Phi(1, s) = (1, 0)$ both letters go to $0$), and no action of $F_S$ extends it; the source starts from a group acting faithfully on $A^*$ and takes $\Phi$ to be a lift of that action to the generators. The *group presented by $\Phi$*, written $\langle \Phi \rangle$, is the quotient of $F_S$ acting faithfully on $A^*$; a finitely generated [group](../../../group-basics/group.md) given in this way is *functionally recursive* (or self-similar).

**Automata groups.** When $\Phi$ takes the form $\Phi\colon A \times S \to S \times A$, again with each $s \in S$ permuting the first letter (a *finite state transducer*), $\langle \Phi \rangle$ is an [automaton group](automaton-group.md). Dropping the permutation condition gives automaton semigroups instead (Section 1.5 of the source).

**Contracting groups.** For $g \in G = \langle \Phi \rangle$ and $u \in A^*$, the state $g@u$ is the element with $(uv)^g = u^g v^{g@u}$ for all $v$. An automata group $G$ is *contracting* if there is a finite set $N \subseteq G$ such that for every $g \in G$ there is $n(g)$ with $g@u \in N$ whenever $|u| \ge n(g)$. The minimal such set $N$ is the *nucleus*. A finite state transducer is *nuclear* when $\overline{\Phi}(A \times S^2) \subseteq S \times A$, where $\overline{\Phi}\colon A \times G \to G \times A$ is the induced map: for every letter $a$ and states $s_1, s_2$, the product $s_1 s_2$ rewrites $a$ with a state of $S$ acting on the rest. Every contracting automata group has a nuclear transducer, obtained by adding the nucleus to $S$ and reading blocks of letters, and nuclear transducers generate contracting groups (Section 3.4).
