# One-ended hyperbolic groups that are not commensurably co-Hopfian

There is a one-ended hyperbolic group $\Gamma$ with a subgroup of finite index isomorphic to a subgroup of infinite index. A group is one-ended if for some finite generating set $S$ its Cayley graph (distinct $u, v$ adjacent when $us = v$ or $vs = u$ for some $s \in S$) has exactly one end. A group is hyperbolic if for some finite generating family its word metric is $\delta$-hyperbolic for some real $\delta$. Stark and Woodhouse give such groups, for example fundamental groups of simple surface amalgams. This answers Whyte's question (Bestvina Q 1.12) positively.

Source: M. Bestvina, Questions in Geometric Group Theory (updated July 2004), Q 1.12 (Whyte), https://www.math.utah.edu/~bestvina/eprints/questions-updated.pdf; resolution: E. Stark and D. J. Woodhouse, Hyperbolic groups that are not commensurably coHopfian, Int. Math. Res. Not. IMRN, arXiv:1812.07799, Theorem 1.1.
