# A separable 101-bounded representation of a countable nonamenable group

If a countable discrete group $G$ has no normalized positive left-invariant complex-linear mean on bounded complex functions on $G$, then there is a group representation $\pi$ on a separable complete complex Hilbert space $H$ by bounded linear operators, with $\|\pi(g)\|\leq101$ for every $g\in G$, that is not similar to a unitary representation. Left translation of $f$ by $g$ is $x\mapsto f(g^{-1}x)$; positivity means pointwise real nonnegative functions have real nonnegative mean. Similarity would require a bounded complex-linear isomorphism $S$ with $\|S\pi(g)S^{-1}x\|=\|x\|$ for all $g,x$. The group and Hilbert-space universes are independent in the source.

## Depends on
Positive means, bounded representations on complete Hilbert spaces, and separability of the witness.

## Proof depends on
A construction of a separable witness at a fixed absolute norm bound with a verified obstruction to every invariant equivalent inner product.

## Expansion
Separate the nonamenability input, countable witness construction, norm estimate and failure of unitarizability. The larger-universe all-group statement does not by itself establish the independent witness-universe formulation.
