# Near-isometric nonunitarizable representations of nonamenable groups

For every nonamenable discrete group $G$ and real $\varepsilon>0$, there exists a representation $\pi:G\to\mathcal B(H)$ on a complete complex Hilbert space $H$ with $\|\pi(g)\|\leq1+\varepsilon$ for all $g$, such that no bounded linear isomorphism makes all conjugated operators preserve the norm. If $G$ is countable, the same witness can be chosen separable. Amenability here means existence of a normalized positive complex-linear mean on bounded functions on $G$, invariant under left translations. In the comparator the witness space is in a universe dominating the group's universe.

## Depends on
Uniformly bounded representations and similarities on a complete Hilbert space, and the specified invariant mean on bounded functions.

## Proof depends on
The passage from nonamenability to a nonunitarizable representation, and lowering the uniform norm arbitrarily close to one without destroying the obstruction.

## Expansion
Verify the universal representation construction, control of the interpolated or perturbed operator norms, and transport of nonunitarizability. Prove separability only under the countability premise.
