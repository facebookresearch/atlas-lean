# Triangle groups are conjugacy separable

For all positive integers $l, m, n$, the group $\langle a, b \mid a^l = b^m = (ab)^n = 1 \rangle$ is conjugacy separable. A group $G$ is conjugacy separable if any two elements whose images are conjugate in every quotient $G/N$ by a normal subgroup $N$ of finite index are conjugate in $G$. For $1/l + 1/m + 1/n < 1$ these are Fuchsian groups, which Fine and Rosenberger prove conjugacy separable; the remaining triples give finite groups and the Euclidean triangle groups. This answers Kourovka Problem 9.2 positively.

Source: E. I. Khukhro and V. D. Mazurov (eds.), Unsolved Problems in Group Theory: The Kourovka Notebook, 21st issue, arXiv:1401.0300v49 (2026), Archive of Solved Problems, Problem 9.2 (R. B. J. T. Allenby); resolution: B. Fine and G. Rosenberger, Conjugacy separability of Fuchsian groups and related questions, Contemp. Math. 109 (1990), 11-18.
