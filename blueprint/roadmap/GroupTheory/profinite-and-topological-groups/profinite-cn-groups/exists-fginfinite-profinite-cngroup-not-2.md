# A finitely generated non-prosoluble profinite CN-group exists

There is an infinite profinite
group $G$, that is, a compact, Hausdorff, totally disconnected topological group, such that $G$ is
topologically finitely generated (the closure of the subgroup generated by some finite set is $G$),
the centralizer of every non-trivial element of $G$ is pronilpotent, and $G$ is not prosoluble: for
some open normal subgroup $N$ of $G$, the quotient $G/N$ is not soluble. This answers Kourovka
Problem 20.90.

Kinyon and Urban (Section 3.35) give an infinite two-generated closed subgroup of
$\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{F}_4[[t]])$ that maps onto $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{F}_4) \cong A_5$ and in
which every non-identity element has abelian, hence pronilpotent, centralizer.
