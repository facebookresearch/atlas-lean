# Isomorphism of profinitely isomorphic pairs of finitely presented groups is undecidable

No algorithm decides, given a finitely presented residually finite group $\Gamma$ and a finitely presentable subgroup $P \hookrightarrow \Gamma$ inducing an isomorphism of profinite completions, whether $P$ is isomorphic to $\Gamma$, or whether the inclusion is onto.

There does not exist an algorithm that, given a finitely presented, residually finite group $\Gamma$ and a finitely presentable subgroup $u\colon P \hookrightarrow \Gamma$ with $\hat u\colon \hat P \to \hat \Gamma$ an isomorphism, can determine whether or not $P$ is abstractly isomorphic to $\Gamma$; nor does there exist an algorithm that can determine whether or not $u$ is an isomorphism (Theorem B of the source).

More precisely (Theorem 4.1), there are a finitely generated free group $F(Y)$ and recursive sequences of finite subsets $T_n, U_n$ of fixed cardinality such that each $G_n = \langle Y \mid T_n \rangle$ is residually finite with a compact classifying space, the subgroup $P_n$ generated by the image of $U_n$ is finitely presentable and $P_n \hookrightarrow G_n$ induces an isomorphism of profinite completions, but no algorithm determines for which $n$ the inclusion is an isomorphism, or for which $n$ the groups $P_n$ and $G_n$ are isomorphic.

## Expansion

The proof feeds a recursive sequence of finitely presented groups $Q_n$ with no non-trivial finite quotients and undecidable triviality (Theorem 3.1 of the source, built from the Collins–Miller sequence) into the fibre product construction used for the conjugacy result: $P_n = G_n$ exactly when $Q_n$ is trivial, while $\hat P_n \cong \hat G_n$ for every $n$.
