# Profinite rank of residually finite hyperbolic groups is undecidable

For each $d_0 > 2$ there is no algorithm that decides whether the profinite completion of a torsion-free, residually finite hyperbolic group can be topologically generated by fewer than $d_0$ elements.

For a group $\Gamma$ let $\hat d(\Gamma)$ be the least cardinality of a subset of the profinite completion $\widehat{\Gamma}$ that generates a dense subgroup. Fix $d_0 > 2$. There is a recursive sequence of torsion-free, residually finite, hyperbolic groups $\Gamma_n$ such that (Theorem 8.3 of the source)

1. for every $n$: $\hat d(\Gamma_n) < d_0$ if and only if $\hat d(\Gamma_n) = 2$;
2. the set $\{ n \in \mathbb{N} : \hat d(\Gamma_n) \ge d_0 \}$ is recursively enumerable but not recursive.

In particular no algorithm decides whether the profinite completion of a torsion-free, residually finite hyperbolic group can be topologically generated by a set of cardinality less than $d_0$. Among residually finite hyperbolic groups the bound $d_0 > 2$ is optimal: such a group $\Gamma$ has $\hat d(\Gamma) < 2$ exactly when it is cyclic, which is decidable for hyperbolic groups. The finiteness hypothesis matters here: the additive group $\mathbb{Z}[1/2]$ is residually finite and not cyclic, yet its profinite completion is topologically generated by one element.

## Expansion

Take a recursive sequence of finitely presented groups $G_n$ such that $\{n : \widehat{G_n} \not\cong 1\}$ is recursively enumerable but not recursive, and let $G'_n$ be the free product of $M \ge \tfrac{60}{59} d_0$ copies of $G_n$; by Lemma 8.1 of the source (a free product of $M$ copies of a group with non-trivial profinite completion has profinite rank at least $\tfrac{59}{60} M$), either $\widehat{G'_n} \cong 1$ or $\hat d(G'_n) \ge d_0$. A version of the Rips construction (Theorem 8.2: Rips' $C'(1/6)$ construction, cubulated by Wise and virtually special by Agol) outputs residually finite torsion-free hyperbolic groups $\Gamma_n$ with $1 \to N_n \to \Gamma_n \to G'_n \to 1$ and $N_n$ two-generated. If $\hat d(\Gamma_n) < d_0$ then $\widehat{G'_n} \cong 1$, so $\widehat{N_n}$ maps onto $\widehat{\Gamma_n}$ and $\hat d(\Gamma_n) = 2$; and $\hat d(\Gamma_n) \ge d_0$ exactly when $\widehat{G_n} \not\cong 1$. This is item (5) of Theorem D in the introduction.
