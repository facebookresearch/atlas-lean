# Semigroup varieties, isoterms and Zimin words

A variety of semigroups is the class of all semigroups satisfying a set of identities; a semigroup is finitely presented inside a variety when it is defined by the variety's identities and finitely many relations, and Zimin words and isoterms measure how restrictive the identities are.

An *identity* $u = v$ is a pair of words over an alphabet of variables; a semigroup satisfies it if $u$ and $v$ take the same value under every substitution of elements for variables. The *variety* defined by a set $\Sigma$ of identities is the class of all semigroups satisfying all of $\Sigma$; the variety *generated* by a semigroup $S$ is the smallest variety containing $S$. A variety is *periodic* if it does not contain the additive semigroup $\mathbb N$ of natural numbers, equivalently if one of its defining identities is not balanced. A finitely generated semigroup is *finitely presented inside* a variety $\mathcal V$ if it is defined, among the semigroups of $\mathcal V$, by finitely many relations together with the identities of $\mathcal V$; for $\mathcal V_{\mathrm{fin}}$, the class of finite members of $\mathcal V$, the uniform word problem asks which quasi-identities hold in all of $\mathcal V_{\mathrm{fin}}$.

The *Zimin words* are $Z_1 = x_1$ and $Z_{n+1} = Z_n x_{n+1} Z_n$. A word $W$ is *not an isoterm* for an identity $u = v$ if for some substitution $\phi$ of words for variables we have $\phi(u) \ne \phi(v)$ but $W$ contains $\phi(u)$ or $\phi(v)$ as a subword; otherwise $W$ is an isoterm for $u = v$.

The small semigroups used below are built from the $2 \times 2$ matrix units $e_{ij}$: $\overrightarrow P = \{e_{11}, e_{12}, 0\}$, $\overleftarrow P = \{e_{11}, e_{21}, 0\}$ and $T = \{e_{11}, e_{12}, e_{22}, 0\}$, with $S^1$ denoting $S$ with an identity adjoined; $\mathcal M_1$ is the variety generated by $T \times \mathbb N$, $\overrightarrow{\mathcal M_2}$ the one generated by $\overleftarrow P \times \overrightarrow P^1 \times \mathbb N$ and $\overleftarrow{\mathcal M_2}$ the one generated by $\overleftarrow P^1 \times \overrightarrow P \times \mathbb N$.

## Expansion

For every two-counter machine $M$ the semigroups $S_1(M)$, $\overrightarrow{S_2}(M)$ and $\overleftarrow{S_2}(M)$ simulating $M$ generate the varieties $\mathcal M_1$, $\overrightarrow{\mathcal M_2}$ and $\overleftarrow{\mathcal M_2}$, which therefore contain finitely presented semigroups with undecidable word problem.
