# Word problems of semigroups finitely presented inside finitely based non-periodic varieties

For a finitely based non-periodic semigroup variety, every semigroup finitely presented inside it has decidable word problem exactly when its nil-semigroups are locally finite and it contains none of three small semigroups; in Zimin-word terms, $Z_{n+1}$ is not an isoterm for its identities.

**Theorem (Sapir; as stated by Margolis, Meakin and Sapir).** Let $\mathcal V$ be a non-periodic variety of semigroups defined by finitely many identities $u_i = v_i$ (see [semigroup varieties, isoterms and Zimin words](semigroup-varieties-isoterms-and-zimin-words.md)). The following are equivalent (Theorem 5.4 of the chapter):

1. every semigroup finitely presented inside $\mathcal V$ has decidable word problem;
2. every semigroup finitely presented inside $\mathcal V$ has decidable elementary theory;
3. every semigroup finitely presented inside $\mathcal V$ is faithfully representable by matrices over a field;
4. every nil-semigroup in $\mathcal V$ is locally finite, and $\mathcal V$ contains none of the semigroups $\overleftarrow P \times \overrightarrow P^1$, $\overleftarrow P^1 \times \overrightarrow P$ and $T$.

The chapter also lists residual finiteness and the Hopf property of every semigroup finitely presented inside $\mathcal V$ among the equivalent conditions.

**Zimin-word form.** Let $n$ bound the number of variables in the identities $u_i = v_i$. All nil-semigroups in $\mathcal V$ are locally finite iff some identity $u_i = v_i$ has a value $\phi(u_i) \ne \phi(v_i)$ with $\phi(u_i)$ a subword of $Z_{n+1}$ (Theorem 4.4 of the chapter), that is, iff $Z_{n+1}$ is not an isoterm for the set of identities. Since $\mathcal V$ contains $\mathbb N$, it contains one of the three semigroups iff it contains the corresponding variety $\mathcal M_1$, $\overrightarrow{\mathcal M_2}$ or $\overleftarrow{\mathcal M_2}$. So condition 4 reads: $\mathcal V$ contains none of $\mathcal M_1$, $\overrightarrow{\mathcal M_2}$, $\overleftarrow{\mathcal M_2}$, and $Z_{n+1}$ is not an isoterm for $\{u_i = v_i\}$. Sapir's survey states the theorem in this form (Theorem 4.3) for identities in at most $n$ variables but prints the last clause as "$Z_{n+1}$ is an isoterm"; the chapter's form shows that "is not an isoterm" is meant, which also matches the survey's proof of its Theorem 5.1 and the variety of commutative semigroups ($xy = yx$, $n = 2$: it contains none of the three semigroups, $Z_3$ is not an isoterm for $xy = yx$, and its finitely presented members have decidable word problem by a theorem of Mal'cev). When $\mathcal V$ is given by finitely many identities, condition 4 can be checked algorithmically. In particular, a finitely based non-periodic variety failing condition 4 contains a semigroup finitely presented inside it with undecidable word problem.

## Expansion

If $\mathcal V$ contains $\mathcal M_1$, $\overrightarrow{\mathcal M_2}$ or $\overleftarrow{\mathcal M_2}$, then the corresponding semigroup $S_1(M)$, $\overrightarrow{S_2}(M)$ or $\overleftarrow{S_2}(M)$ simulating a two-counter machine with undecidable halting problem lies in $\mathcal V$ (these varieties are the ones the three semigroups generate, Theorem 4.2 of the survey) and is finitely presented inside it, so it has undecidable word problem (Theorem 4.1 of the survey: two configuration words are equal in these semigroups iff the configurations are equivalent). The case where $Z_{n+1}$ is an isoterm uses a different simulation of Minsky machines, and the implications from 4 to 1, 2 and 3 use faithful matrix representations. The survey and the chapter refer to Sapir's 1988 paper for these proofs; a good expansion links them, Theorem 4.1 and the computation of the varieties generated by $S_1(M)$ and $S_2(M)$ (Theorem 4.2).
