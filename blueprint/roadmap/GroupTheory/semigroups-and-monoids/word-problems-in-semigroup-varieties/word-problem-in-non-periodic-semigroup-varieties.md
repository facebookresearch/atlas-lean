# Which non-periodic semigroup varieties contain finitely presented semigroups with undecidable word problem

For a non-periodic semigroup variety defined by identities in at most n variables, every semigroup finitely presented inside it has decidable word problem exactly when the variety contains none of three explicit small varieties and the Zimin word with n+1 letters is not an isoterm for its identities.

**Theorem (Sapir).** Let $\mathcal V$ be a non-periodic variety of semigroups defined by identities $u_i = v_i$ in at most $n$ variables (see [semigroup varieties, isoterms and Zimin words](semigroup-varieties-isoterms-and-zimin-words.md)). The following are equivalent:

1. every semigroup finitely presented inside $\mathcal V$ has decidable word problem;
2. every semigroup finitely presented inside $\mathcal V$ has decidable elementary theory;
3. every semigroup finitely presented inside $\mathcal V$ is faithfully representable by matrices over a field;
4. $\mathcal V$ contains none of the varieties $\mathcal M_1$, $\overrightarrow{\mathcal M_2}$, $\overleftarrow{\mathcal M_2}$, and the Zimin word $Z_{n+1}$ is not an isoterm for the set of identities $u_i = v_i$, that is, it is not an isoterm for at least one of them.

The survey prints condition 4 with "is an isoterm"; the reading "is not an isoterm" is the one consistent with its Theorem 5.1, whose condition is that $Z_{n+1}$ is not an isoterm and whose proof shows that an isoterm $Z_{n+1}$ allows a simulation of Minsky machines, and with the variety of commutative semigroups: it is defined by $xy = yx$ ($n = 2$), contains none of the three varieties, has $Z_3$ not an isoterm for $xy = yx$, and all its finitely presented members have decidable word problem (a classical theorem of Mal'cev). Condition 4 can be checked algorithmically from the finitely many defining identities. In particular, a finitely based non-periodic variety failing condition 4 contains a finitely presented semigroup with undecidable word problem.

## Expansion

If $\mathcal V$ contains $\mathcal M_1$ or one of the $\mathcal M_2$, then the semigroups $S_1(M)$, $\overrightarrow{S_2}(M)$, $\overleftarrow{S_2}(M)$ simulating a two-counter machine with undecidable halting problem lie in $\mathcal V$ and are finitely presented inside it, so they have undecidable word problem (Theorem 4.1 of the survey: two configuration words are equal in these semigroups iff the configurations are equivalent). The case where $Z_{n+1}$ is an isoterm uses a different simulation of Minsky machines, and the implications from 4 to 1, 2 and 3 use faithful matrix representations. The survey refers to Sapir's 1988 paper for these proofs; a good expansion links them, Theorem 4.1 and the computation of the varieties generated by $S_1(M)$ and $S_2(M)$ (Theorem 4.2).
