# Inverse monoid and inverse semigroup presentations

An inverse monoid presentation $\mathrm{Inv}\langle A \mid u_i = v_i \ (i \in I) \rangle$ defines the quotient of the free inverse monoid on $A$ by the congruence its relations generate; its word problem asks to decide, for two words over $A \cup A^{-1}$, whether they represent the same element.

An inverse monoid is a monoid $M$ in which every $m$ admits exactly one $m^{-1} \in M$ with $mm^{-1}m = m$ and $m^{-1}mm^{-1} = m^{-1}$; an inverse semigroup is defined in the same way without an identity. For a set $A$ let $A^{-1} = \{a^{-1} : a \in A\}$ be a set of formal inverses disjoint from $A$, and extend inversion to words by $(x_1 \cdots x_n)^{-1} = x_n^{-1} \cdots x_1^{-1}$ with $(a^{-1})^{-1} = a$. The Vagner congruence $\nu$ on the free monoid $(A \cup A^{-1})^*$ is the congruence generated by the pairs $(ww^{-1}w, w)$ and $(ww^{-1}uu^{-1}, uu^{-1}ww^{-1})$ for all words $u, w$; the free inverse monoid on $A$ is $(A \cup A^{-1})^* / \nu$. For words $u_i, v_i \in (A \cup A^{-1})^*$ $(i \in I)$, the inverse monoid

$$
\mathrm{Inv}\langle A \mid u_i = v_i \ (i \in I) \rangle = (A \cup A^{-1})^* / \sigma
$$

is the quotient by the congruence $\sigma$ generated by $\nu$ and the pairs $(u_i, v_i)$. The inverse semigroup presentation $\mathrm{Inv}\langle A \mid R \rangle$ is defined in the same way over the free semigroup $(A \cup A^{-1})^+$ of nonempty words. A presentation is finite when $A$ and $I$ are finite, special when every relation has the form $r_i = 1$, and positive when moreover every $r_i$ lies in the free semigroup $A^+$.

The word problem for a presentation with finite $A$ is the decision problem: given words $u, v$ over $A \cup A^{-1}$, are their images equal? The maximal group image of $\mathrm{Inv}\langle A \mid R \rangle$ is the group $\mathrm{Gp}\langle A \mid R \rangle$ with the same generators and relations.

## Expansion

The word problem of a free inverse monoid is decidable (Scheiblich, Munn: elements are represented by Munn trees, finite subtrees of the Cayley graph of the free group). A word $e$ represents an idempotent of the free inverse monoid exactly when it freely reduces to the empty word. In a special inverse monoid $\mathrm{Inv}\langle A \mid r = 1 \rangle$ the relator need not be freely reduced: $\mathrm{Inv}\langle a \mid \rangle$ is the free inverse monoid of rank one while $\mathrm{Inv}\langle a \mid aa^{-1} = 1 \rangle$ is the bicyclic monoid.
