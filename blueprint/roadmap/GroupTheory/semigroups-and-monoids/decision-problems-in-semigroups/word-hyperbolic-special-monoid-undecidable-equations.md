# Some word-hyperbolic special monoid has undecidable Diophantine problem

There is a finitely presented special monoid with trivial group of units and context-free word problem, hence word-hyperbolic, in which no algorithm decides whether a finite system of equations has a solution.

A monoid presentation $\mathrm{Mon}\langle A \mid u_1 = 1, \dots, u_k = 1 \rangle$, with all defining relations of the form $u_i = 1$, is *special*. The *group of units* $U(M)$ of a monoid $M$ is the group of its two-sided invertible elements. A special monoid generated by a finite set $A$ has *context-free word problem* if the set of words over $A$ representing the identity is a context-free language. A monoid $M$ is *word-hyperbolic* (in the sense of Duncan and Gilman) if some regular language over a finite generating set maps onto $M$ and the corresponding multiplication table is a context-free language. The *Diophantine problem* for $M$ asks, given a finite system of equations in variables with constants from $M$, whether it has a solution in $M$.

**Theorem (Nyberg-Brodda).** There exists a finitely presented special monoid $M$ such that (Theorem 2 of arXiv:2205.01056v1):

1. the group of units $U(M)$ is trivial;
2. $M$ has context-free word problem;
3. $M$ is word-hyperbolic;
4. the Diophantine problem for $M$ is undecidable.

The monoid is fixed and the system of equations is the input. Since the trivial group has decidable Diophantine problem, the theorem shows that decidability of the Diophantine problem does not pass from the group of units to a special monoid, and that the decidability of the Diophantine problem in [hyperbolic groups](../../geometric-group-theory/hyperbolic-groups/is-hyperbolic.md) (Dahmani and Guirardel) does not extend to word-hyperbolic monoids, answering two questions of Garreta and Gray. The source notes that an explicit example with $71$ defining relations can be constructed (Remark 1).

## Expansion

Otto constructed a finite special rewriting system $S$ without nontrivial critical pairs such that the Diophantine problem for the monoid $M_S$ it presents is undecidable, by encoding an instance of the Post correspondence problem (Theorem 1 of the source). A finite special system without nontrivial critical pairs presents a monoid with trivial group of units, because each defining word is then a single minimal invertible piece (Lemma 1). A special monoid with trivial group of units has context-free word problem by an earlier theorem of the author, and context-free monoids are word-hyperbolic. A good expansion links Otto's undecidability theorem for $M_S$ and the author's theorem that a finitely presented special monoid with trivial group of units has context-free word problem.
