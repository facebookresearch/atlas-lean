# Intersection, universality and recognizability of rational subsets of the queue monoid are undecidable

For rational subsets of the monoid of queue actions it is undecidable whether two of them intersect, whether one is the whole monoid, and whether one is recognizable, although membership in a rational subset is decidable in nondeterministic logarithmic space.

Let $\mathcal{Q}$ be the [monoid of queue actions](monoid-of-queue-actions.md) over an alphabet with at least two letters. Rational subsets of $\mathcal{Q}$ are the images of regular languages over $\Sigma$, as for the [rational subset membership problem](../../presentations-and-decision-problems/membership-and-equation-problems/submonoid-and-rational-subset-membership/rational-subset-membership-problem.md), and are given by finite automata over $\Sigma$. A subset is *recognizable* if it is the preimage of a subset of a finite monoid under a homomorphism.

**Theorem (Huschenbett, Kuske and Zetzsche).** For rational subsets of $\mathcal{Q}$ (Theorem 8.4):

1. the class of rational subsets is not closed under intersection;
2. it is undecidable whether two given rational subsets have empty intersection;
3. it is undecidable whether a given rational subset equals $\mathcal{Q}$, and consequently inclusion and equality of rational subsets are undecidable;
4. it is undecidable whether a given rational subset is recognizable.

By contrast, the uniform membership problem for rational subsets of $\mathcal{Q}$ is NL-complete (Theorem 8.2).

## Expansion

The submonoid $\mathcal{R}$ generated by $[a]$, $[ab]$, $[\bar b]$ and $[\overline{abb}]$ is isomorphic to $\{a, b\}^* \times \{c, d\}^*$, and recognizable subsets of $\mathcal{R}$ are recognizable in $\mathcal{Q}$ (Proposition 8.3). The isomorphism maps rational relations to rational subsets of $\mathcal{Q}$ and preserves intersections, so emptiness of intersection transfers directly. For universality, a rational relation $S$ is the whole product exactly when $\alpha(S) \cup (\mathcal{Q} \setminus \mathcal{R}) = \mathcal{Q}$, where $\mathcal{Q} \setminus \mathcal{R}$ is recognizable and hence rational; recognizability transfers through Proposition 8.3. The corresponding problems for rational relations are undecidable. Proposition 8.3 of the source, whose proof of the transfer of recognizability uses Mezei's theorem on recognizable subsets of direct products, has no page on main yet; a good expansion states it.
