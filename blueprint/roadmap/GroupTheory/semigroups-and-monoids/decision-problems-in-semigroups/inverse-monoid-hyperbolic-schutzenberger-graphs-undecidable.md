# An inverse monoid with uniformly hyperbolic Schützenberger graphs can have undecidable word problem

There is a finitely presented inverse monoid all of whose Schützenberger graphs are $\delta$-hyperbolic for one fixed $\delta$, but whose word problem is undecidable; it can be taken $E$-unitary and special.

For an inverse monoid $M$ generated by $A$, the Schützenberger graph of $m \in M$ is the strongly connected component of $m$ in the Cayley graph of $M$ with respect to $A \cup A^{-1}$. There are a finitely presented inverse monoid $M = \mathrm{Inv}\langle A \mid R \rangle$ and $\delta > 0$ such that (Theorem 5.1 of the source):

1. every Schützenberger graph of $M$ is $\delta$-hyperbolic;
2. $M$ has undecidable word problem;
3. moreover such examples exist in which all relations have the form $w = 1$ with $w \in A^*$, and $M$ is $E$-unitary.

By contrast, the source proves that inverse monoids whose Schützenberger graphs are quasi-isometric to trees, under suitable finiteness conditions, have decidable word problem and context-free Schützenberger automata.

## Expansion

Rips' construction gives a finitely presented hyperbolic group $H$ with a fixed finitely generated subgroup of undecidable membership (from a finitely presented group with undecidable word problem). Gray's theorem turns such a subgroup into an $E$-unitary special inverse monoid with maximal group image $H * \mathbb{Z}$ and undecidable word problem, by adjoining an idempotent word built from conjugates $t w_i t^{-1}$. The source shows its Schützenberger graphs form trees of uniformly hyperbolic graphs, hence are uniformly hyperbolic. The Rips construction and Gray's theorem have no pages here.
