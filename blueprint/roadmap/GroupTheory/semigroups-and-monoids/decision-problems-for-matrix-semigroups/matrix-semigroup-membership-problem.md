# Membership problem for matrix semigroups

The membership problem asks, given finitely many square matrices and one more matrix, whether the latter is a nonempty product of the given matrices; for the zero matrix it is the mortality problem.

**Membership** (also called matrix reachability). Input: $n \times n$ integer matrices $M_1, \dots, M_k$ and an $n \times n$ integer matrix $X$. Question: is $X \in \langle M_1, \dots, M_k \rangle$, that is, is $X = M_{i_1} \cdots M_{i_m}$ for some $m \geq 1$ and indices $i_1, \dots, i_m$? The *mortality problem* is the case in which $X$ is the zero matrix.

## Depends on

- [Semigroup generated by finitely many matrices](semigroup-generated-by-finitely-many-matrices.md)

## Proof depends on

None.

## Expansion

For matrices that generate a group, membership in the generated subgroup is the special case obtained by adding the inverses of the generators.
