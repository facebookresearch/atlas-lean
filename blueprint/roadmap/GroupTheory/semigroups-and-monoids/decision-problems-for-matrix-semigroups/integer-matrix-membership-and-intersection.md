# Membership for integer matrix semigroups of dimension six and intersection in dimension four are undecidable

No algorithm decides whether a given integer matrix of size at least six is a product of given matrices of that size, nor whether two finitely generated semigroups of integer matrices of size at least four meet; these are Markov's 1947 results.

Use the [decision problems for finitely generated matrix semigroups](decision-problems-for-matrix-semigroups.md) with integer entries.

**Theorem (Markov).**

1. For every $n \geq 6$, membership is undecidable for semigroups of $n \times n$ integer matrices: no algorithm decides, given $n \times n$ integer matrices $A_1, \dots, A_K$ and $T$, whether $T = A_{i_1} A_{i_2} \cdots A_{i_p}$ for some $p \geq 1$ and indices $i_1, \dots, i_p$.
2. For every $n \geq 4$, semigroup intersection is undecidable for $n \times n$ integer matrices: no algorithm decides, given two finite sets of $n \times n$ integer matrices, whether the semigroups they generate have a common element.

Clause 1 is described in the source as the first undecidability result obtained outside mathematical logic and the theory of computing. Membership in dimension $4$ is also undecidable: Mikhailova's undecidability of membership in finitely generated subgroups of $\mathrm{SL}(4, \mathbb{Z})$ gives it after adding the inverses of the generators.

## Expansion

For clause 2, the Post correspondence problem is a semigroup intersection problem in the direct product $\{a, b\}^* \times \{a, b\}^*$ of two free monoids: an instance $(u_1, v_1), \dots, (u_k, v_k)$ has a solution exactly when the semigroup generated by the pairs $(u_i, v_i)$ meets the diagonal semigroup generated by $(a, a)$ and $(b, b)$. For instance, embedding the free monoid $\{a, b\}^*$ into $2 \times 2$ integer matrices and taking block-diagonal $4 \times 4$ matrices turns this into semigroup intersection for $4 \times 4$ integer matrices. The copy read does not describe Markov's construction for clause 1; a good expansion would give it and link the undecidable problem it starts from.
