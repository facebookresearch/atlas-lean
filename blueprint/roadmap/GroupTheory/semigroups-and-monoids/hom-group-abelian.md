# Abelian group of additive homomorphisms into an abelian group

Let $M$ be a set with an addition and an element $0_M$ such that $0_M + a = a + 0_M = a$ for every $a \in M$; for example $M$ can be an abelian group $A$, written additively. Let $B$ be an abelian group, written additively. An *additive homomorphism* $\varphi\colon M \to B$ is a map with $\varphi(a + a') = \varphi(a) + \varphi(a')$ for all $a, a' \in M$ and $\varphi(0_M) = 0$; when $M$ is a group the second condition follows from the first. The set $\operatorname{Hom}(M, B)$ of additive homomorphisms from $M$ to $B$ is an abelian group under the *pointwise* operations: $(\varphi + \psi)(a) = \varphi(a) + \psi(a)$, the zero homomorphism $a \mapsto 0$, and $(-\varphi)(a) = -\varphi(a)$; subtraction and integer multiples are also computed pointwise. Because $B$ is abelian, $\varphi + \psi$ and $-\varphi$ are again additive homomorphisms; for instance $(\varphi + \psi)(a + a') = \varphi(a) + \varphi(a') + \psi(a) + \psi(a') = (\varphi + \psi)(a) + (\varphi + \psi)(a')$. For abelian groups $A$ and $B$ this is the *group of homomorphisms* $\operatorname{Hom}(A, B)$. Its group operation is the pointwise sum, not composition of maps.
