# Finiteness of automaton semigroups is undecidable

No algorithm decides whether the semigroup generated by a given complete deterministic synchronous Mealy automaton is finite, nor, for two given elements $f, g$ of such a semigroup, whether $f^n = g$ for some $n$.

Let $\mathcal{A} = (A, \Sigma, \delta, \sigma)$ range over Mealy automata, that is, complete [S-automata](automaton-semigroups.md), and let $\langle \mathcal{A} \rangle_+$ be the automaton semigroup generated by the maps $\sigma_a$, $a \in A$.

**Theorem (Gillibert).**

1. It is undecidable, given a Mealy automaton $\mathcal{A}$, whether $\langle \mathcal{A} \rangle_+$ is finite (Theorem 3.13 of arXiv:1304.2295v2).
2. It is undecidable, given a Mealy automaton $\mathcal{A}$ and two elements $f, g \in \langle \mathcal{A} \rangle_+$ (given as products of states), whether there is a positive integer $n$ with $f^n = g$ (Corollary 3.14 of arXiv:1304.2295v2).

The automaton, with its alphabet and number of states, is the input. The source recalls that finiteness is decidable for invertible reversible Mealy automata with two states or two letters (Klimann), and the finiteness problem for automaton groups is recorded as open in the sources cited here.

## Expansion

A finite tile set $T$ is NW-deterministic if each tile is determined by its north and west colors. From such a $T$ the source builds a Mealy automaton $\mathcal{A}_T$ whose states and letters are the tiles together with a new symbol $\bot$. It is a reset automaton: the transition goes to the letter just read, $\delta(x, y) = y$, so the new state does not depend on the old one; the output $\sigma(s, t)$ of state $s$ on letter $t$ is the tile $r$ whose north color is the south color of $t$ and whose west color is the east color of $s$, and $\bot$ when no such tile exists or $s$ or $t$ is $\bot$ (Definition 3.8). A valid tiling of $\mathbb{Z}^2$ yields arbitrarily long products of states acting differently, so $\langle \mathcal{A}_T \rangle_+$ is infinite (Lemma 3.11). Conversely, if $\mathbb{Z}^2$ has no valid tiling, then by compactness some $n \times n$ square cannot be tiled, and the source bounds the size of $\langle \mathcal{A}_T \rangle_+$ in terms of $n$, so it is finite (Lemma 3.12). Kari's theorem that the tiling problem is undecidable for NW-deterministic tile sets then gives clause 1. For clause 2 a state $c$ outputting only $\bot$ is added, and $\sigma_\bot^n = \sigma_c$ for some $n$ exactly when there is no valid tiling. A complete expansion also links the compactness theorem for Wang tilings (a valid tiling of $\mathbb{Z}^2$ exists if and only if every finite square can be tiled, Theorem 3.2 of the source), which has no page on main yet.
