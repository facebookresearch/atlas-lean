# Freeness of automaton semigroups and automaton monoids is undecidable

No algorithm decides whether the semigroup, or the monoid, generated by a given complete deterministic synchronous automaton is free.

Let $\mathcal{T} = (Q, \Sigma, \delta)$ range over complete [S-automata](automaton-semigroups.md), with generated semigroup $\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{T})$ and generated monoid $\mathscr{M}(\mathcal{T})$.

**Theorem (D'Angeli, Rodaro and Wächter).** It is undecidable, given a complete S-automaton $\mathcal{T}$, whether $\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{T})$ is a free semigroup, and it is undecidable, given a complete S-automaton $\mathcal{T}$, whether $\mathscr{M}(\mathcal{T})$ is a free monoid (Theorem 3.15 of the source).

The automaton, with its alphabet, states and transitions, is the input. This answers an open problem listed by Grigorchuk, Nekrashevych and Sushchanskii; an earlier claimed proof of the semigroup case was withdrawn by an erratum of the same authors. The same reduction shows that it is undecidable whether $\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{T})$ or $\mathscr{M}(\mathcal{T})$ is (left) cancellative or equidivisible (Theorem 3.16), whether a given map between the state sets of two complete S-automata extends to a homomorphism, or to an isomorphism, of the generated semigroups (Theorem 3.17), whether $\mathscr{M}(\mathcal{T}) \cong (Q \setminus \{e\})^*$ for a state $e$ acting as the identity (Theorem 3.18), and whether $\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{T}) \cong P^+$ for a given subset $P \subseteq Q$ of the states (Theorem 3.19). The freeness problem for automaton groups is not settled by this result.

## Expansion

The proof reduces the complement of the Post correspondence problem. From an instance the source computes a complete S-automaton $\mathcal{T}$ that combines an automaton generating a free semigroup on prescribed states (Proposition 3.1, with free products of automaton semigroups from Proposition 2.7) with states that check a candidate solution. If the instance has a solution, $\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{T})$ is not (left) cancellative, hence not free (Proposition 3.6); conversely every relation of $\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{T})$ is compatible with the construction, and a relation not visible after projection yields a solution (Lemmas 3.8, 3.10 and 3.12). Proposition 3.14 collects the equivalences, for the semigroup and the monoid alike: the instance has a solution exactly when $\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{T})$ is not free, and exactly when $\mathscr{M}(\mathcal{T})$ is not free, so the monoid case uses the same reduction.
