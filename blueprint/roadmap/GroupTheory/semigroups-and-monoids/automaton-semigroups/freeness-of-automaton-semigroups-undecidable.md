# Freeness of automaton semigroups and automaton monoids is undecidable

No algorithm decides whether the semigroup, or the monoid, generated by a given complete deterministic synchronous automaton is free, even over a fixed alphabet of 25 letters.

Let $\mathcal{T} = (Q, \Sigma, \delta)$ range over complete [S-automata](automaton-semigroups.md), with generated semigroup $\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{T})$ and generated monoid $\mathscr{M}(\mathcal{T})$.

**Theorem (D'Angeli, Rodaro and Wächter).** Fix an alphabet $\Sigma$ with $|\Sigma| = 25$. It is undecidable, given a complete S-automaton $\mathcal{T}$ with input and output alphabet $\Sigma$, whether $\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{T})$ is a free semigroup, and it is undecidable whether $\mathscr{M}(\mathcal{T})$ is a free monoid (Theorem 3.16 of arXiv:2402.01372v2). In particular, the freeness problems for automaton semigroups and for automaton monoids, with the automaton and its alphabet as input, are undecidable (Corollary 3.17).

The alphabet is fixed and the automaton, with its states and transitions, is the input. This answers an open problem listed by Grigorchuk, Nekrashevych and Sushchanskii; an earlier claimed proof of the semigroup case was withdrawn by an erratum of the same authors. The same reduction shows that it is undecidable whether $\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{T}) \cong P^+$ for a given subset $P \subseteq Q$ of the states (Theorem 3.18), and whether a given map between the state sets of two complete S-automata over alphabets of sizes $25$ and $2$ extends to a homomorphism, or to an isomorphism, of the generated semigroups (Theorem 3.20). The freeness problem for automaton groups is not settled by this result.

## Expansion

The proof reduces the complement of the Post correspondence problem. From an instance the source computes a complete S-automaton $\mathcal{T}$ over a fixed alphabet (of size $25$, Remark 3.3) that combines an automaton generating a free semigroup of given rank (Proposition 2.8, after Example 2.7) with gadgets that check a candidate solution (Propositions 2.9 and 3.1). If the instance has a solution, $\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{T})$ is not free, not even cancellative (Proposition 3.7); if it has none, $\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{T}) \cong P^+$ for a suitable set $P$ of states (Proposition 3.15). The monoid case uses an adaptation of the construction, reducing from a variant of the Post correspondence problem (Section 4, Corollary 4.8). A complete expansion links the undecidability of that variant, which has no page.
