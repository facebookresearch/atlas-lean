# Automaton semigroups, monoids and groups

An automaton semigroup is the semigroup of length-preserving maps on words generated by the states of a finite synchronous Mealy automaton; automaton monoids and automaton groups are defined in the same way.

**Automata.** An *automaton* is a triple $\mathcal{T} = (Q, \Sigma, \delta)$ with a finite nonempty set $Q$ of states, a finite alphabet $\Sigma$ and a set $\delta \subseteq Q \times \Sigma \times \Sigma \times Q$ of transitions; a transition $(q, a, b, p)$, written $q \xrightarrow{a/b} p$, reads $a$, outputs $b$ and moves from $q$ to $p$. The automaton is

- *deterministic* if for all $q \in Q$ and $a \in \Sigma$ there is at most one transition from $q$ with input $a$, and *complete* if there is at least one;
- *inverse-deterministic* (invertible) if its *inverse* automaton, obtained by exchanging input and output in every transition, is deterministic, and *bi-deterministic* if it is deterministic and inverse-deterministic;
- *reversible* if for all $a \in \Sigma$ and $p \in Q$ at most one transition with input $a$ ends in $p$, and *bi-reversible* if both it and its inverse are reversible.

A deterministic automaton is called an *S-automaton*; a complete deterministic automaton is the same as a Mealy automaton $(Q, \Sigma, \delta, \sigma)$ with transition map $Q \times \Sigma \to Q$ and output map $Q \times \Sigma \to \Sigma$.

**Generated semigroups.** For an S-automaton, each state $q$ acts on $\Sigma^*$ by $q \circ \varepsilon = \varepsilon$ and $q \circ a w = b\,(p \circ w)$ when $q \xrightarrow{a/b} p$ is a transition (the action is partial when the automaton is not complete). These maps are length-preserving and prefix-compatible. The *semigroup generated by* $\mathcal{T}$, written $\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{T})$, is the closure of $\{q \circ{} : q \in Q\}$ under composition; adding the identity map gives the generated monoid $\mathscr{M}(\mathcal{T})$. A semigroup (monoid) of this form for a complete S-automaton is a *complete automaton semigroup* (*monoid*). A complete and inverse-deterministic S-automaton is a *G-automaton*: its states act by bijections, and together with the states of its inverse they generate the *automaton group* $\mathscr{G}(\mathcal{T})$.

The word problem of automaton semigroups is decidable, as recalled in the introduction of Gillibert's paper. Automaton groups, the case of invertible automata, are also described on the page [Automaton group](../../geometric-group-theory/branch-and-thompson-groups/finite-state-automorphisms-of-rooted-trees/automaton-group.md).
