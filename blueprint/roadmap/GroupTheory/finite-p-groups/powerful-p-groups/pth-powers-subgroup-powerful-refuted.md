# Kourovka 21.137 fails for every prime

For every prime $p$ there is a finite $p$-group $G$
with a subgroup $P$ whose underlying set is exactly the set $\{x^p : x \in G\}$ of $p$-th powers in
$G$, such that $P$ is not powerful: the commutator subgroup $[P, P]$ is not contained in the
subgroup $P^m$ generated by the $m$-th powers of the elements of $P$, where $m = p$ for odd $p$
and $m = 4$ for $p = 2$.

For odd $p$ a counterexample is Muliarchyk's group of order $p^{3p+1}$ and exponent $p^2$, whose
$p$-th powers form a non-abelian subgroup of exponent $p$; for $p = 2$ it is the Sylow
$2$-subgroup of $S_8$, whose squares form $C_2 \times D_8$ (letter of A. Chang). The 21st issue of
the Kourovka Notebook marks Problem 21.137 solved ("No, it need not be").
