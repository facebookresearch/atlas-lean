# Kourovka Problem 20.76 has a negative answer

The statement of Kourovka Problem 20.76 is
false: there are a prime $p$, a natural number $k$, a finite $p$-group $G$ all of whose abelian
normal subgroups have order at most $p^k$, and an abelian subgroup of $G$ of order greater than
$p^{2k}$.

Muliarchyk's counterexample (Theorem 1.1) is a finite $2$-group of order $2^{144e+1}$, with
$e = 256363762$, whose abelian normal subgroups have order at most $2^{42e}$ while it has an
elementary abelian subgroup of order $2^{85e} > 2^{84e}$; the 21st issue of the Kourovka Notebook
marks 20.76 solved ("No, it is not").

Muliarchyk, Abelian subgroups of finite 2-groups: counterexamples to Problems 20.75 and 20.76, preprint (2026), Kourovka Notebook site, Theorem 1.1: for e = 256363762 an explicitly specified finite 2-group of order 2^(144e+1) whose abelian normal subgroups have order at most 2^(42e), with an elementary abelian subgroup of order 2^(85e) (p = 2, k = 42e); Kourovka Notebook (21st issue), Problem 20.76: No, it is not, https://kourovkanotebookorg.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/kourovka_20_75-20_76_solution.pdf.
