# Central elements of order 3 modulo the 3′-core

For a group $K$, the proposition that the centre of $K/O_{3'}(K)$ contains an element of order $3$, stated in $K$: some $h \in K$ lies outside $O_{3'}(K)$, satisfies $h^3 \in O_{3'}(K)$, and has $hkh^{-1}k^{-1} \in O_{3'}(K)$ for every $k \in K$, where $O_{3'}(K)$ is the subgroup generated by all normal subgroups of $K$ of order prime to $3$. For finite $K$, $O_{3'}(K)$ is the largest normal subgroup of order prime to $3$.

Source: E. I. Khukhro and V. D. Mazurov (eds.), Unsolved Problems in Group Theory: The Kourovka Notebook, 21st issue, arXiv:1401.0300v49 (2026), Archive of Solved Problems, Problem 6.42 (N. D. Podufalov); resolution: W. Xiao, Sci. China (A) 33 (1990), 1172-1181.
