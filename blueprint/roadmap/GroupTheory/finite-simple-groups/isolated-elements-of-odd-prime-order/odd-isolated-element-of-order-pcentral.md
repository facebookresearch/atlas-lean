# Kourovka Problem 4.21 for elements of order p (isolated elements of odd prime order)

The proposition that the following holds. Let $G$ be a finite group, $p$ an odd prime and $P$ a Sylow $p$-subgroup of $G$, and suppose every nontrivial normal subgroup of $G$ has order divisible by $p$. If $x \in P$ has order $p$ and is conjugate in $G$ to no element of $P$ other than itself (whenever $gxg^{-1} \in P$, then $gxg^{-1} = x$), then $x$ lies in the centre of $G$. The problem as posed concerns isolated elements of $P$ of any order; this is the case of elements of order $p$. For $p = 2$ the analogous statement is Glauberman's $Z^*$-theorem.

Source: E. I. Khukhro and V. D. Mazurov (eds.), Unsolved Problems in Group Theory: The Kourovka Notebook, 21st issue, arXiv:1401.0300v49 (2026), Archive of Solved Problems, Problem 4.21 (G. Glauberman); resolution: O. D. Artemovich, Isolated elements of prime order in finite groups, Ukrain. Math. J. 40 (1988), 343-345, https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01061317.
