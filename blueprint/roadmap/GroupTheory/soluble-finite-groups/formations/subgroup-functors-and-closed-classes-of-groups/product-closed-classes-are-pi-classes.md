# Kourovka Problem 4.16 (Baer's product-closed classes)

The proposition that whenever $\mathfrak{K}$ is a nonempty class of finite groups that contains every subgroup and every homomorphic image of its members, and contains every group $G = UV$ that is the product of two subgroups $U, V \in \mathfrak{K}$ (neither need be normal; every element of $G$ is a product $uv$), there is a set of primes $\pi$ such that $\mathfrak{K}$ is exactly the class of finite groups all of whose order's prime divisors lie in $\pi$. The class is required to consist of finite groups and to be nonempty.

Source: E. I. Khukhro and V. D. Mazurov (eds.), Unsolved Problems in Group Theory: The Kourovka Notebook, 21st issue, arXiv:1401.0300v49 (2026), Archive of Solved Problems, Problem 4.16 (R. Baer); resolution: S. A. Syskin, A question of R. Baer, Siberian Math. J. 20 (1979), 475-476, https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00969961.
