# Product-closed classes of finite groups that are not π-classes

There is a class $\mathfrak{K}$ such that $\mathfrak{K}$ is a nonempty class of finite groups that contains every subgroup and every homomorphic image of its members, and contains every group $G = UV$ that is the product of two subgroups $U, V \in \mathfrak{K}$ (neither need be normal; every element of $G$ is a product $uv$), but $\mathfrak{K}$ is not the class of all finite $\pi$-groups for any set of primes $\pi$; Kourovka Problem 4.16 has a negative answer. It is the negation of the proposition on [Kourovka Problem 4.16 (Baer's product-closed classes)](product-closed-classes-are-pi-classes.md).

Source: E. I. Khukhro and V. D. Mazurov (eds.), Unsolved Problems in Group Theory: The Kourovka Notebook, 21st issue, arXiv:1401.0300v49 (2026), Archive of Solved Problems, Problem 4.16 (R. Baer); resolution: S. A. Syskin, A question of R. Baer, Siberian Math. J. 20 (1979), 475-476, https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00969961.
