# Normal Fitting classes

A class of groups $\mathfrak{X}$ is a normal Fitting class (Blessenohl and Gaschütz) if it is a [Fitting class of finite soluble groups](is-soluble-fitting-class.md), it contains a non-trivial finite group, and for every finite soluble group $G$ the $\mathfrak{X}$-radical of $G$ is [$\mathfrak{X}$-maximal](../injectors-of-fitting-classes/is-xmaximal-in.md) in $G$. Here the $\mathfrak{X}$-radical is a normal subgroup $R$ of $G$ that lies in $\mathfrak{X}$ and contains every normal subgroup of $G$ lying in $\mathfrak{X}$, and $\mathfrak{X}$-maximal means that no subgroup of $G$ properly containing $R$ lies in $\mathfrak{X}$.

## Sources

- S. Reifferscheid, On the theory of Fitting classes of finite soluble groups, Dissertation, Universität Tübingen, 2002, Chapter 3, introduction, and Definition 3.1.5.
