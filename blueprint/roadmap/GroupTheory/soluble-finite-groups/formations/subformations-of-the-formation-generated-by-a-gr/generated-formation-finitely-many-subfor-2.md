# A finite group generating a formation with infinitely many subformations

There is a finite group $G$ such that infinitely many formations are contained in the formation generated by $G$; Burichenko's counterexamples are groups of type $2S_5$. Classes of finite groups are predicates on finite groups; a formation is closed under homomorphic images and finite subdirect products ($G/N_1, G/N_2 \in \mathfrak F \Rightarrow G/(N_1 \cap N_2) \in \mathfrak F$), the empty class included. It is the negation of the proposition on [Kourovka Problem 9.59 (subformations of the formation generated by a finite group)](generated-formation-finitely-many-subfor.md).

Source: E. I. Khukhro and V. D. Mazurov (eds.), Unsolved Problems in Group Theory: The Kourovka Notebook, 21st issue, arXiv:1401.0300v49 (2026), Archive of Solved Problems, Problem 9.59 (A. N. Skiba and L. A. Shemetkov (after W. Gaschütz)); resolution: V. P. Burichenko, On a problem of Gaschütz, J. Algebra 372 (2012), 428-458, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jalgebra.2012.10.006.
