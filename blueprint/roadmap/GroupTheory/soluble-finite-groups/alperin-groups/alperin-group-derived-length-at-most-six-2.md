# Finite Alperin groups have derived length at most six

Let $G$ be a finite group in which, for
all $x, y \in G$, the derived subgroup of the subgroup $\langle x, y \rangle$ generated by $x$ and
$y$ is cyclic (an Alperin group). Then the sixth derived subgroup $G^{(6)}$ of $G$ is trivial,
that is, $G$ has derived length at most $6$.

## Sources

- E. I. Khukhro and V. D. Mazurov (eds.), Unsolved Problems in Group Theory: The Kourovka Notebook, 21st issue, arXiv:1401.0300v49 (2026), Archive of Solved Problems, Problem 18.33 (B. M. Veretennikov); resolution: P. Longobardi, M. Maj and H. Smith, Rend. Sem. Mat. Univ. Padova 115 (2006), 29-40; B. Wilkens, J. Group Theory 17 (2014), no. 1, 151-174, Kourovka Archive, Problem 18.33: Yes, it is at most 6: finite Alperin groups of odd order are metabelian and finite Alperin groups are supersoluble (Longobardi-Maj-Smith), so the elements of odd order form a metabelian normal subgroup, and finite Alperin 2-groups have derived length at most 4 (Wilkens), https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.0300v49.
