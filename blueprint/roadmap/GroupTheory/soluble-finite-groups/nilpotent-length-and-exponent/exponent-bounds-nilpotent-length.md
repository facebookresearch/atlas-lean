# Kourovka Problem 3.26 (nilpotent length of groups of exponent p^α q^β)

The proposition that for all primes $p, q$ and all $\alpha, \beta \in \mathbb N$, every finite group whose exponent divides $p^\alpha q^\beta$ has nilpotent length at most $\alpha + \beta$. Exponent dividing (rather than equal to) $p^\alpha q^\beta$ gives the same claim; $p = q$ is allowed.

Source: E. I. Khukhro and V. D. Mazurov (eds.), Unsolved Problems in Group Theory: The Kourovka Notebook, 21st issue, arXiv:1401.0300v49 (2026), Archive of Solved Problems, Problem 3.26 (F. Gross; V. D. Mazurov).
