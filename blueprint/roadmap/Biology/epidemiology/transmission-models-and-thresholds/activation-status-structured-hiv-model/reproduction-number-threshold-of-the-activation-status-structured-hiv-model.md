# Reproduction number threshold of the activation-status structured HIV model

Let $H(t)$ be uninfected CD4+ T cells, $V(t)$ free virus, and $I(t, x)$ the density of infected cells with normalized activation status $x \in [0, 1]$ ($x = 0$ latent, $x = 1$ maximally productive). The model is

$$\begin{aligned}
\dot H &= \lambda - cH - H\int_0^1 (1 - \epsilon(y)) k_1 I(t, y)\, dy - (1 - \xi) k_2 H V,\\
\partial_t I(t, x) &= \beta(x)\left[H\int_0^1 (1 - \epsilon(y)) k_1 I(t, y)\, dy + (1 - \xi) k_2 H V\right] + \int_0^1 \gamma(y, x) I(t, y)\, dy - (\delta(x) + \alpha(x)) I(t, x),\\
\dot V &= \int_0^1 \eta(y) I(t, y)\, dy - dV,
\end{aligned}$$

with cell-to-cell infection rate $k_1$, cell-free infection rate $k_2$, drug efficacies $\xi$ (cell-free) and $\epsilon(y)$ (cell-to-cell, decreasing in $y$, with $\epsilon(0) = 1$), status distribution of new infections $\beta(x)$ with $\int_0^1 \beta = 1$, status-switching kernel $\gamma(y, x)$ with $\int_0^1 \gamma(x, y)\, dy = \delta(x)$ and $\gamma(x, x) > 0$, death rate $\alpha(x)$, and burst rate $\eta(y)$. All coefficients are nonnegative and continuous. The infection-free equilibrium is $E_0 = (H_0, 0, 0)$ with $H_0 = \lambda/c$.

Define the next-generation operator $L$ on $C([0, 1])$ by

$$(LI)(x) = \frac{\beta(x)}{\alpha(x) + \delta(x)}\left[\int_0^1 (1 - \epsilon(y)) k_1 H_0 I(y)\, dy + \frac{(1 - \xi) k_2 H_0}{d}\int_0^1 \eta(y) I(y)\, dy\right] + \frac{1}{\alpha(x) + \delta(x)}\int_0^1 \gamma(y, x) I(y)\, dy .$$

$L$ is compact and non-supporting, so (Marek's Krein–Rutman-type theorem) $\rho(L) > 0$ is an algebraically simple eigenvalue with a strictly positive eigenfunction, and it is the only eigenvalue with a nonnegative eigenfunction. Set $R_0 = \rho(L)$.

**Theorem.**

1. If $R_0 > 1$, an infected equilibrium $E^* = (H^*, I^*(\cdot), V^*)$ with $I^* \not\equiv 0$ exists.
2. Let $\bar L$ be the linearization at $E_0$. Then $s(\bar L) < 0$, $= 0$, $> 0$ according as $R_0 < 1$, $= 1$, $> 1$, where $s$ is the spectral bound. Hence $E_0$ is locally asymptotically stable for $R_0 < 1$ and unstable for $R_0 > 1$.
3. If $R_0 < 1$, $E_0$ is globally asymptotically stable in the nonnegative cone.

In particular, if treatment completely blocks cell-free infection ($\xi = 1$), the threshold is still governed by the cell-to-cell term. Infection can persist under therapy whenever cell-to-cell transmission from highly activated cells, which the drug blocks less efficiently, keeps $R_0 > 1$.

## Expansion

Existence of $E^*$ uses truncated nonlinearities $\min\{I, n\}$ and a perturbation $\varepsilon_n$, applies Schauder's fixed-point theorem on a ball of $C_+([0, 1])$, and passes to the limit using a uniform $L^1$ bound obtained by integrating the $I$ equation and using the balance condition. For the spectral claim, $s(\bar L) = s(\mathcal L)$, where $\mathcal L$ is the $(I, V)$ block. $\mathcal L$ splits into a stable diagonal part plus a positive, compact, irreducible part (irreducible because $\gamma(x, x) > 0$), so $s(\mathcal L)$ is a simple dominant eigenvalue with a strictly positive eigenvector. Eliminating $V$ from the eigen-equation shows that $s(\mathcal L) = 0$ exactly when $L\varphi = \varphi$, and that $s(\mathcal L) > 0$ exactly when $\rho(L) > 1$. Global extinction for $R_0 < 1$ follows by bounding $H \le H_0 + \epsilon_0$, comparing with the linear semigroup generated by $\mathcal L_{\epsilon_0}$ (whose $\rho < 1$ for small $\epsilon_0$), and using that the growth bound equals the spectral bound.
