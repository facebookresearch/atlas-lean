# Threshold dynamics of the reaction-diffusion SIS patch model

**Model.** Patches $j \in \Omega = \{1, \dots, n\}$, $n \ge 2$, are bounded smooth domains $\Omega_j$. Susceptible and infected densities $S_j(x,t), I_j(x,t)$ diffuse inside patch $j$ at rates $d_{S_j}, d_{I_j} > 0$ and move between patches with uniform rates $d_S, d_I > 0$ along a connectivity matrix $L = (L_{jk})$:

$$\partial_t I_j = d_{I_j} \Delta I_j + d_I \sum_{k \ne j}\Big(\frac{L_{jk} \bar I_k}{|\Omega_j|} - L_{kj} I_j\Big) + \frac{\beta_j S_j I_j}{S_j + I_j} - \gamma_j I_j, \qquad \bar I_k = \int_{\Omega_k} I_k,$$

with the analogous equation for $S_j$ (rates $d_{S_j}, d_S$, reaction term with opposite sign) and homogeneous Neumann conditions. Assume (A0) nonnegative continuous initial data with total mass $N > 0$; (A1) $\beta_j, \gamma_j \ge 0$ Hölder continuous, $\boldsymbol\beta, \boldsymbol\gamma \ne 0$; (A2) $L$ essentially nonnegative, irreducible, with zero column sums, $L_{jj} = -\sum_{k \ne j} L_{kj}$. Total population is conserved, and by Theorem 2.2 there is a unique global bounded classical solution. Let $\boldsymbol\alpha$ be the positive eigenvector of $L$ for the eigenvalue $0$ with $\sum_j \alpha_j = 1$ (Lemma 3.1).

**Disease-free equilibrium (Lemma 3.2).** The unique disease-free equilibrium is $E_0$ with $S_j \equiv \alpha_j N / |\Omega_j|$ and $I_j \equiv 0$.

**Basic reproduction number.** Let $T(t)$ be the semigroup on $\prod_j C(\bar\Omega_j)$ generated by the linearized infected equation without the infection term, $\partial_t I_j = d_{I_j}\Delta I_j + d_I \sum_{k\ne j}(L_{jk}\bar I_k/|\Omega_j| - L_{kj} I_j) - \gamma_j I_j$, and define $\mathcal{L}\boldsymbol\phi = \int_0^\infty T(t)(\beta_1\phi_1, \dots, \beta_n\phi_n)\,dt$ and $\mathcal{R}_0 = r(\mathcal{L})$, the spectral radius. By Lemma 3.3, $\mathcal{R}_0 > 0$, $\mathcal{R}_0 - 1$ has the sign of the principal eigenvalue $\lambda_1$ of

$$\lambda\varphi_j = d_{I_j}\Delta\varphi_j + d_I\sum_{k\ne j}\Big(\frac{L_{jk}\bar\varphi_k}{|\Omega_j|} - L_{kj}\varphi_j\Big) + (\beta_j - \gamma_j)\varphi_j,$$

with Neumann conditions, and $1/\mathcal{R}_0$ is the principal eigenvalue of the weighted problem $-\lambda\beta_j\varphi_j = d_{I_j}\Delta\varphi_j + d_I\sum_{k\ne j}(\cdots) - \gamma_j\varphi_j$.

**Theorem 4.1.** Under (A0)–(A2), if $\mathcal{R}_0 < 1$ then for every $j$

$$\lim_{t\to\infty}\big\|S_j(\cdot,t) - \alpha_j N/|\Omega_j|\big\|_{L^\infty(\Omega_j)} = \lim_{t\to\infty}\|I_j(\cdot,t)\|_{L^\infty(\Omega_j)} = 0 .$$

**Theorem 4.2.** Under (A0)–(A2), if $\mathcal{R}_0 > 1$ there is $\varepsilon_0 > 0$ such that every solution with $\boldsymbol I_0 \not\equiv 0$ satisfies $\liminf_{t\to\infty}\min_{\bar\Omega_j} S_j > \varepsilon_0$ and $\liminf_{t\to\infty}\min_{\bar\Omega_j} I_j > \varepsilon_0$ for all $j$, and the model has a positive (endemic) equilibrium.

## Expansion

The movement operator is a nonlocal coupling (patch $k$ feeds every point of patch $j$ in proportion to its total $\bar I_k$), so $\mathcal{R}_0$ is defined through the next-generation operator of the infinite-dimensional linearization and related to the spectral bound by the Thieme correspondence. If $\mathcal{R}_0 < 1$, standard incidence gives $\partial_t I_j \le (\text{linear operator}) I_j$ with spectral bound $\lambda_1 < 0$, so $I$ decays exponentially by comparison; integrating the $S$ equations then shows $\bar S_j \to \alpha_j N$, and the asymptotically autonomous limit system forces $S_j \to \alpha_j N/|\Omega_j|$. If $\mathcal{R}_0 > 1$, near $E_0$ the factor $S/(S+I)$ is close to $1$, so $I$ is a supersolution of a linear problem with positive principal eigenvalue and cannot converge to $E_0$; dissipativity, compactness and abstract uniform persistence theory then give persistence and an endemic equilibrium.
