# Well-posedness and optimal regional vaccination for a fractional-diffusion SIR model

**Model.** On a bounded domain $\Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^2$ with $Q_T = [0,T] \times \Omega$, susceptible and infected densities $S(t,x), I(t,x)$ spread by Neumann fractional diffusion $(-\Delta_N)^\sigma$ and interact through a general incidence $SI\psi(I)$:

$$
\partial_t S = -d_1 (-\Delta_N)^\sigma S - SI\,\psi(I) - a_1(x) S + b(x) - \chi_\omega(x) u_1 S, \qquad \partial_t I = -d_2 (-\Delta_N)^\sigma I + SI\,\psi(I) - a_2(x) I - u_2 I,
$$

with nonnegative initial data, $a_1, a_2, b \in L^\infty(\Omega)$ nonnegative (natural death, total removal, recruitment), $\psi \in C^1(\mathbb{R}_+)$, and $S + I$ bounded by the total population density. Vaccination $u_1$ acts only on a subregion $\omega \subset \Omega$ ($\chi_\omega$ its indicator) and treatment $u_2$ everywhere; admissible controls satisfy $0 \le u_1 \le U_1$ on $Q_\omega = [0,T] \times \omega$ and $0 \le u_2 \le U_2$ on $Q_T$. With $d_1 = d_2 = u_1 = u_2 = 0$, $a_1 = b = 0$, $a_2 = \gamma$ and $\psi \equiv \beta$, the system is the Kermack–McKendrick SIR model.

**Objective.**

$$
J(I, u) = \frac{\rho}{2}\|I\|^2_{L^2(Q_T)} + \frac{\sigma_1}{2}\|u_1\|^2_{L^2(Q_\omega)} + \frac{\sigma_2}{2}\|u_2\|^2_{L^2(Q_T)}.
$$

**Theorem.**

1. *(Well-posedness.)* For continuous nonnegative initial data there is a unique local mild solution $y = (S, I)$, $y(t) = \mathcal{S}(t) y_0 + \int_0^t \mathcal{S}(t-s) f(s, y(s))\, ds$, with $\mathcal{S}$ the contraction semigroup generated by the diffusion operator. For admissible $u$ and $y_0$ in the operator domain, the solution is a unique global strong solution in $W^{1,2}(0,T; D_A(\Omega))$, nonnegative and bounded, with a priori bounds on $\|\partial_t y_i\|_{L^2(Q_T)}$, $\|y_i\|_{L^2(0,T; D((-\Delta_N)^\sigma))}$ and $\|y_i\|_{H^\sigma(\Omega)}$.
2. *(Existence of an optimal control.)* $J$ attains its minimum over admissible controls.
3. *(Optimality system.)* The control-to-state map is Gateaux differentiable, its derivative in direction $u$ solving the linearized system $\partial_t z = Az + Bz + Lu$, $z(0) = 0$. With $p = (p_1, p_2)$ the solution of the corresponding adjoint system, an optimal control satisfies

$$
u_1^* = \min\Big(U_1, \max\Big(0, \frac{\chi_\omega S^* p_1}{\sigma_1}\Big)\Big), \qquad u_2^* = \min\Big(U_2, \max\Big(0, \frac{I^* p_2}{\sigma_2}\Big)\Big).
$$

Numerical simulations show that concentrating vaccination in a well-chosen subregion substantially reduces spread.

## Expansion

The diffusion part is self-adjoint and dissipative, so Lumer–Phillips gives a contraction semigroup, and Lipschitz continuity of the reaction terms on bounded sets gives local mild solutions; invariance of the nonnegative bounded set and energy estimates (using a Stroock–Varopoulos-type inequality for $(-\Delta_N)^\sigma$) extend them globally and upgrade them to strong solutions. Existence of an optimal control follows from a minimizing sequence, the a priori bounds and compactness, together with weak lower semicontinuity of $J$. The bang–bang-with-interior form of $u^*$ comes from the variational inequality obtained by differentiating $J$ along admissible directions and projecting onto $[0, U_i]$.
