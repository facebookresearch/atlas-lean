# Pathogen emergence on contact networks

Whether a newly introduced pathogen strain causes a major outbreak or dies out early is a branching-process question. When infectious periods are exponential and secondary infections occur at constant rates, the offspring law has a closed-form generating function; on a random contact network, every infected individual after the first has one fewer susceptible contact, which leads to an excess-degree correction. These articles derive the emergence probability in both settings.
