# Pareto frontier of vaccination strategies in the kernel SIS model

**Model.** Let $(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mu)$ be a probability space of individual features, $\gamma > 0$ a bounded measurable recovery rate and $k \ge 0$ a measurable transmission kernel with $\sup_x \big(\int_\Omega (k(x,y)/\gamma(y))^q\,\mu(dy)\big)^{1/q} < \infty$ for some $q > 1$. A vaccination strategy is $\eta \in \Delta = \{\eta \in L^\infty(\Omega) : 0 \le \eta \le 1\}$, where $\eta(x)$ is the fraction of feature-$x$ individuals left unvaccinated, with the weak-* topology (so $\Delta$ is compact). The infection probability $u_t(x)$ of unvaccinated feature-$x$ individuals follows

$$\partial_t u_t = (1 - u_t)\,T_{k\eta}(u_t) - \gamma u_t, \qquad T_{k\eta}(g)(x) = \int_\Omega k(x,y)\,\eta(y)\,g(y)\,\mu(dy).$$

The two losses are the **effective reproduction number** $R_e(\eta) = \rho(T_{k\eta/\gamma})$, the spectral radius of the effective next-generation operator, and the **endemic size** $\mathfrak{I}(\eta) = \int_\Omega \mathfrak{g}_\eta\,\eta\,d\mu$, where $\mathfrak{g}_\eta$ is the maximal equilibrium. The uniform cost is $C(\eta) = \int_\Omega (1 - \eta)\,d\mu$, the fraction vaccinated. A strategy is Pareto optimal if no other strategy has lower cost and lower loss with one strictly lower; let $L_\star(c) = \inf\{L(\eta) : C(\eta) \le c\}$ and $C_\star(\ell) = \inf\{C(\eta) : L(\eta) \le \ell\}$.

**Proposition (losses).** $R_e$ and $\mathfrak{I}$ are well defined, non-decreasing, sub-homogeneous ($L(\lambda\eta) \le \lambda L(\eta)$ for $\lambda \in [0,1]$) and weak-* continuous on $\Delta$; $R_e(\mathbf{1}) = R_0$, $R_e(\mathbf{0}) = 0$, $\mathfrak{I}(\mathbf{1}) = \mathfrak{I}_0$, and $\mathfrak{I}(\eta) > 0$ if and only if $R_e(\eta) > 1$. Every local minimum of either loss on $\Delta$ is global.

**Theorem (Pareto frontier).** For the SIS model with loss $L \in \{R_e, \mathfrak{I}\}$ (requiring $R_0 > 0$, resp. $R_0 > 1$) and uniform cost, with $\ell_{\max} = L(\mathbf{1})$ and $c_{\max} = 1$:

1. $C_\star$ is continuous and decreasing on $[0, \ell_{\max}]$;
2. $L_\star$ is continuous on $[0, c_{\max}]$, decreasing on $[0, C_\star(0)]$ and zero on $[C_\star(0), c_{\max}]$;
3. the Pareto frontier is compact, connected, and equals both graphs:

$$\mathcal{F} = \{(c, L_\star(c)) : c \in [0, C_\star(0)]\} = \{(C_\star(\ell), \ell) : \ell \in [0, \ell_{\max}]\};$$

4. the set of Pareto optimal strategies is compact in $\Delta$.

In particular, minimizing the loss at fixed cost and minimizing the cost at fixed loss give the same optimal strategies, and the critical Pareto optima coincide for the two losses: $\eta$ is Pareto optimal for $R_e$ with $R_e(\eta) = 1$ iff it is Pareto optimal for $\mathfrak{I}$ with $\mathfrak{I}(\eta) = 0$.

**Worst strategies.** Call $A\in\mathcal F$ *invariant* if $\int_{A^c\times A}k(z,y)\,\mu(dz)\mu(dy)=0$ (the sub-population $A$ does not infect $A^c$), let $\mathcal F_{\mathrm{inv}}$ be the $\sigma$-field generated by the invariant sets, and call an atom of $\mu$ in $\mathcal F_{\mathrm{inv}}$ *non-zero* if $k$ restricted to it is non-zero. The kernel $k$ is *monatomic* if it has a unique non-zero atom $\Omega_a$ (it may still transmit from $\Omega_a$ to the rest of the population, where infection cannot be sustained and which does not infect $\Omega_a$ back). If $k$ is monatomic with atom $\Omega_a$, the strategy $\mathbf{1}_{\Omega_a}$, vaccinating everyone outside the atom, is anti-Pareto optimal for $R_e$ (maximal loss at its cost); for $\mathfrak{I}$ with $R_0 > 1$ the anti-Pareto optimal strategy $\mathbf{1}_{\{\mathfrak{g} > 0\}}$ vaccinates exactly those who would never be infected at equilibrium. If $k$ is irreducible, the worst strategies at maximal loss cost nothing.

## Expansion

The proofs are carried out in an abstract bi-objective framework: a compact convex set of strategies in an ordered topological vector space with a continuous non-increasing cost and a continuous non-decreasing loss. Existence of optimizers follows from compactness. If, in addition, the cost has no local minimum except global ones on loss sublevel sets (true for convex or decreasing costs) and the loss is sub-homogeneous (so that scaling a strategy toward $\mathbf{0}$ strictly lowers a positive loss), then constraints bind, $L_\star$ and $C_\star$ are mutually inverse continuous decreasing bijections, and the frontier is the common graph. The SIS losses inherit sub-homogeneity from the monotonicity of the spectral radius and of the maximal equilibrium in the kernel. The feasible region of outcomes $(C(\eta), L(\eta))$ has no holes, and the frontier is stable under uniform perturbation of the loss.
