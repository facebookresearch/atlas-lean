# Barrier of the admissible and robust invariant sets for the SIR infection cap

**Setting.** Consider the normalized SIR subsystem with a time-varying contact rate $\beta(t)$ modelling social distancing, a known recovery rate $\gamma > 0$, and a hard infection cap $I_{\max} \in (0,1]$:

$$\dot S = -\beta(t) S I, \qquad \dot I = \beta(t) S I - \gamma I, \qquad I(t) \le I_{\max}, \qquad \beta(t) \in [\beta_{\min}, \beta_{\max}],$$

on the state space $G_\Pi = \{ (S,I) : S \ge 0,\ I \ge 0,\ S + I \le 1,\ I \le I_{\max} \}$, where $\beta_{\max} > 0$ is the nominal contact rate and $\beta_{\min} > 0$ the strongest feasible distancing. The **admissible set** $\mathcal{A}$ is the set of initial states from which some measurable input $\beta(\cdot)$ keeps $I(t) \le I_{\max}$ for all $t \ge t_0$; the **maximal robust positively invariant set** $\mathcal{M}$ is the set of initial states from which the cap holds for every admissible input. Their boundaries inside the constraint set are barriers made of trajectories that touch the cap tangentially.

**Proposition.**

1. The barrier of $\mathcal{A}$ is the trajectory ending tangentially on the cap at the single point $(S, I) = (\gamma/\beta_{\min}, I_{\max})$ (provided $\gamma/\beta_{\min} + I_{\max} \le 1$), generated by the bang-bang input $\bar\beta = \beta_{\max}$ if $\lambda_2 - \lambda_1 < 0$ and $\bar\beta = \beta_{\min}$ if $\lambda_2 - \lambda_1 > 0$, where the adjoint solves

$$\dot \lambda = \begin{pmatrix} \beta I & -\beta I \\ \beta S & -\beta S + \gamma \end{pmatrix} \lambda, \qquad \lambda(\bar t) = (0, 1)^{\mathsf T}$$

at the tangency time $\bar t$. The input equals $\beta_{\min}$ on an interval before $\bar t$, switches only at isolated times (no singular arcs), and the barrier lies strictly below the cap just before $\bar t$.
2. The barrier of $\mathcal{M}$ is obtained in the same way with minimization replaced by maximization: it ends tangentially at $(\gamma/\beta_{\max}, I_{\max})$ (if $\gamma/\beta_{\max} + I_{\max} \le 1$) and its input is $\bar\beta = \beta_{\max}$ if $\lambda_2 - \lambda_1 > 0$, $\bar\beta = \beta_{\min}$ if $\lambda_2 - \lambda_1 < 0$.

In both cases the axis $I = 0$ (equilibria) and the segment $S = 0$, $0 < I \le I_{\max}$ also belong to the barrier. Analogous characterizations hold for the SEIR model with distancing $\beta(t) \in [\beta_{\min}, \beta_{\max}]$ and isolation $\gamma(t) \in [\gamma_{\min}, \gamma_{\max}]$, where the tangency points form the segment $E = \gamma I_{\max}/\eta$, $I = I_{\max}$ with $\gamma = \gamma_{\max}$ for $\mathcal{A}$ and $\gamma = \gamma_{\min}$ for $\mathcal{M}$.

## Expansion

The theory of barriers gives necessary conditions for a trajectory on the boundary of $\mathcal{A}$ (or $\mathcal{M}$): it reaches the constraint boundary tangentially at a point of its "usable part", where $\min_\beta \dot I = 0$ (resp. $\max_\beta \dot I = 0$), and along it the Hamiltonian $\lambda^{\mathsf T} f(x, \beta)$ is minimized (resp. maximized) and equals zero, with the adjoint started from the constraint gradient. At $I = I_{\max}$, $\dot I = (\beta S - \gamma) I_{\max}$ vanishes with the extreme input only at $S = \gamma/\beta_{\min}$ (resp. $\gamma/\beta_{\max}$). A singular arc would force $\dot\lambda_2 - \dot\lambda_1 = \gamma \lambda_2 = 0$ on an interval and hence $\lambda \equiv 0$, contradicting the nontriviality of the adjoint. The second derivative of $I - I_{\max}$ along the barrier at the tangency is $-\gamma \beta_{\min} I_{\max}^2 < 0$, so the curve approaches the cap from below. Integrating the barrier backward in time then draws the set boundary.
