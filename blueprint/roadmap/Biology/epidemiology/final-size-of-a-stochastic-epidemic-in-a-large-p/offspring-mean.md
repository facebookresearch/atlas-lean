# Real-valued mean of an offspring law

For a measure $\nu$ on $\mathbb{N}$ let $m = \int x \, d\nu(x) \in \mathbb{R}$, a Bochner integral.
It is $\sum_{x \ge 1} x\, \nu(\{x\})$ when this sum is finite; when the sum is infinite, $m = 0$,
since the integral of a non-integrable function is $0$. For a probability measure $\nu$ with finite
mean, $m$ is the mean of $\nu$, the finite value of the
[mean offspring number](../../../Probability/branching-processes/galton-watson-process/mean.md).
