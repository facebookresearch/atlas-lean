# Partial identification of infection rates

When testing for an infection is selective rather than random, surveillance counts of tests and positive results do not point-identify the cumulative population infection rate. Combining the law of total probability with credible bounds on test accuracy, a monotonicity assumption on who gets tested, and the logical monotonicity of cumulative infection in time yields sharp intervals for the infection rate.
