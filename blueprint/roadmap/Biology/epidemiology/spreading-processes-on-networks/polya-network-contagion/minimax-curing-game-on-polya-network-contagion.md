# Minimax curing game on the Pólya network contagion process

**Pólya network contagion.** Let $G = (V, E)$ be a connected undirected graph with $V = \{1, \dots, N\}$, neighbourhoods $\mathcal{N}_i$ and closed neighbourhoods $\mathcal{N}_i' = \{i\} \cup \mathcal{N}_i$. Node $i$ holds an urn with $R_i \ge 0$ red balls (infection) and $B_i \ge 0$ black balls (health). Its **super urn** pools the urns of $\mathcal{N}_i'$ and is assumed nonempty. At each time $n$, every node draws independently (given the past) from its own super urn; let $Z_{i,n} = 1$ if the draw is red (node $i$ is infected at time $n$) and $0$ otherwise. After a red draw $\Delta_{r,i}(n) \ge 0$ red balls are added to urn $i$; after a black draw $\Delta_{b,i}(n) \ge 0$ black balls. With $X_{j,n}$ the number of balls in urn $j$ after $n$ draws, the red proportion of node $i$'s super urn is

$$
S_{i,n} = \frac{\sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}_i'} \big(R_j + \sum_{t=1}^{n} \Delta_{r,j}(t) Z_{j,t}\big)}{\sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}_i'} X_{j,n}},
$$

and $P(Z_{i,n} = 1 \mid \mathcal{F}_{n-1}) = S_{i,n-1}$, where $\mathcal{F}_{n-1}$ is generated by all draws up to time $n-1$. The **network exposure** is $\tilde S_n = \frac{1}{N}\sum_i S_{i,n}$ and the **average infection rate** is $\tilde I_n = \frac{1}{N}\sum_i P(Z_{i,n} = 1)$.

**Proposition (convexity–concavity).** For every $n$ and arbitrary initial conditions, the expected network exposure $E[\tilde S_n \mid \mathcal{F}_{n-1}]$ is convex in the curing parameters $x = (\Delta_{b,i}(n))_{i=1}^N \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^N$ and concave in the infection parameters $y = (\Delta_{r,i}(n))_{i=1}^N \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^N$.

**Theorem (Nash equilibrium for network exposure).** Fix a time $n$ and budgets $\mathcal{B}_b, \mathcal{B}_r > 0$. In the two-player zero-sum game where the curer minimizes and the infector maximizes $E[\tilde S_n \mid \mathcal{F}_{n-1}]$ over

$$
\mathcal{X} \times \mathcal{Y} = \Big\{x \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^N : \sum_i x_i \le \mathcal{B}_b\Big\} \times \Big\{y \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^N : \sum_i y_i \le \mathcal{B}_r\Big\},
$$

a Nash equilibrium (saddle point) exists, and some equilibrium spends both budgets in full: $\sum_i x_i = \mathcal{B}_b$ and $\sum_i y_i = \mathcal{B}_r$.

## Expansion

Conditionally on the past, write $S_{i,n} = (c_i + C_i y)/(c_i + d_i + C_i y + D_i x)$, where $c_i, d_i$ count accumulated red and black balls in the super urn and $C_i y = \sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}_i'} y_j Z_{j,n}$, $D_i x = \sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}_i'} x_j (1 - Z_{j,n})$ are linear in the controls. For each draw outcome this is convex in $x$; $1 - S_{i,n}$ has the same form in $y$, so $S_{i,n}$ is concave in $y$. The conditional law of $Z_n$ does not depend on the time-$n$ controls, so taking the expectation preserves both properties. The budget sets are convex and compact, so the classical minimax theorem gives a saddle point. The exposure is nonincreasing in each $x_j$ and nondecreasing in each $y_j$, so a player never gains by leaving budget unspent; not every equilibrium need spend in full (if every urn starts all red, every allocation is a saddle point). The exposure serves as a tractable proxy for $\tilde I_n$, which is hard to optimize directly.
