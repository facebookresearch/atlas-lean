# Stackelberg mean field game for optimal epidemic incentives

**Setting.** A continuum of agents moves between finitely many health states $E = \{1,\dots,m\}$ (e.g. $S, I, R$) as a continuous-time Markov chain. A representative agent picks an action $\alpha_t \in A$ (a **contact factor**), and its jump rates $q(t,i,j,\alpha,\rho)$ depend on its action and on the joint law $\rho_t$ of states and actions in the population; in the SIR case a susceptible is infected at rate $\beta\,\lambda^{(I)}_t\,\alpha_t\,p_t(I)$ and recovers at rate $\gamma$. A regulator (principal) announces a policy $(\boldsymbol{\lambda},\xi)$: a deterministic recommended contact level $\lambda_t$ per state and a terminal payment or penalty $\xi$, a random variable depending on the agent's path. Given the policy, agents play a **mean field Nash equilibrium** $(\hat{\boldsymbol\alpha},\hat{\boldsymbol\rho})$: $\hat{\boldsymbol\alpha}$ minimises the agent's cost $J^{\boldsymbol\lambda,\xi}(\boldsymbol\alpha,\hat{\boldsymbol\rho})$ (running cost $f$, including a deviation penalty $\frac{c_\lambda}{2}(\lambda^{(S)} - \alpha)^2$ for susceptibles, plus a terminal term $U(\xi)$), and $\hat\rho_t$ is the law of $(\hat\alpha_t, X_t)$. The regulator minimises its own cost (epidemic burden $c_0(t,p_t)$, policy cost $f_0(t,\lambda_t)$ and the expected payment) over policies that keep the agents' equilibrium cost at most $\kappa$.

**Theorem.**
1. (Equilibria as McKean–Vlasov BSDEs.) Under Lipschitz and nondegeneracy hypotheses on $q$ and $f$ and a unique, Lipschitz minimiser $\hat a_i(t,z,\rho)$ of the agent's Hamiltonian, $(\boldsymbol\alpha,\boldsymbol\rho)$ is a mean field Nash equilibrium if and only if it is part of a solution $(\boldsymbol Y,\boldsymbol Z,\boldsymbol\alpha,\boldsymbol\rho,\mathbb{Q})$ of a McKean–Vlasov backward SDE driven by the jump martingale of $X$, with $\alpha_t = \hat a_{X_{t-}}(t, Z_t, \rho_t)$ and terminal value $Y_T = U(\xi)$.
2. (Reduction of the Stackelberg problem.) If $U$ is invertible, the regulator's bilevel problem equals a standard optimal control problem of a McKean–Vlasov forward SDE in which the controls are the initial value $Y_0$ and the integrand $\boldsymbol Z$: every terminal payment is represented as $\xi = U^{-1}(Y_T)$ with $Y$ run forward from $Y_0$.
3. (SIR solution.) For the SIR model, let $\widetilde H$ be the regulator's reduced Hamiltonian, combining the transition terms $(y(I) - y(S))\beta\lambda^{(I)}\tilde\alpha\,\pi(I)\pi(S) + (y(R) - y(I))\gamma$ with the costs $c_0 + f_0 + \frac{c_\lambda}{2}(\lambda^{(S)} - \tilde\alpha)^2\pi(S) + c_I\pi(I)$. If the first-order conditions $(\nabla_{\tilde\alpha}, \nabla_\lambda)\widetilde H = 0$ have a unique admissible solution and $(\pi, y)$ solves the forward–backward ODE $\dot\pi = \nabla_y\widetilde H$, $\pi_0 = p_0$, $\dot y = -\nabla_\pi\widetilde H$, $y_T = 0$, then the induced recommendation $\hat{\boldsymbol\lambda}$ together with the payment $\hat\xi$ given by an explicit Stieltjes integral along the agent's path is an optimal contract, and the resulting equilibrium state distribution is $\hat\pi$.

## Expansion

The approach transplants Sannikov-style contract theory to mean field games. The agent's best response is encoded in its continuation value $Y_t$, whose martingale integrand $Z_t$ (one coordinate per possible jump) determines the optimal action through the Hamiltonian minimiser. Because the principal can implement any $Z$ by choosing $\xi$ as the terminal value of the corresponding BSDE, it optimises directly over $(Y_0, \boldsymbol Z)$, and the agents' participation constraint becomes the scalar constraint $\mathbb{E}[Y_0] \le \kappa$. In the SIR case the incentive $Z$ is nonzero only for susceptibles, since only their contact choice affects transmission, and it equals $c_\lambda(\hat\alpha - \hat\lambda^{(S)})/(\beta\hat\lambda^{(I)}\hat\pi(I))$: the reward for keeping contacts low is scaled by how much an extra contact raises the infection risk. The general case is solved numerically by Monte Carlo simulation with neural-network parametrisations of $Y_0$ and $Z$, and extends to SEIRD dynamics.
