# Nash equilibrium of individual vaccination in the SIR model

**Setting.** Use the SIR model with vaccination measure $dU$, infection cost $r_I$, vaccination cost $r_V$, and individual mixed strategies $\varphi_V$ from [optimal individual vaccination timing](optimal-individual-vaccination-timing-in-the-sir-model.md). Identical individuals aggregate consistently with the population when

$$
dU(t) = \frac{d\varphi_V(t)}{1 - \varphi_V(t)}\,S(t).
$$

An **equilibrium** is a pair $(U, \varphi_V)$ satisfying this compatibility relation in which $\varphi_V$ is an optimal individual response to the epidemic generated by $U$. For $(S,I) \in \Omega$ let $\zeta(S,I)$ be the number eventually infected without further vaccination, the solution of $1 - \zeta/S = e^{-(\beta/\gamma)(I + \zeta)}$. Individual vaccination pays exactly while $\zeta(S,I)/S > r_V/r_I$, that is, while

$$
F(S,I) := I + \frac{r_V}{r_I}\,S + \frac{\gamma}{\beta}\ln\Big(1 - \frac{r_V}{r_I}\Big) > 0.
$$

**Theorem (equilibrium, unbounded rate).** An equilibrium exists and is unique:

1. if $r_V \ge r_I$, or $r_V < r_I$ and $F(S_{0^-}, I_{0^-}) \le 0$: no vaccination, $dU = 0 = d\varphi_V$;
2. if $I_{0^-} + (\gamma/\beta)\ln(1 - r_V/r_I) > 0$: total instantaneous vaccination, $dU = S_{0^-}\delta_0$, $d\varphi_V = \delta_0$;
3. otherwise partial instantaneous vaccination, $dU = \alpha^*\delta_0$ and $d\varphi_V = (\alpha^*/S_{0^-})\delta_0$, where $\alpha^* \in (0, S_{0^-})$ is the unique solution of $F(S_{0^-} - \alpha, I_{0^-}) = 0$: vaccination stops exactly on the curve $F = 0$.

**Theorem (equilibrium, bounded rate $u_{\max} < \infty$).** If $r_V \ge r_I$, or $r_V < r_I$ and $F(S_{0^-}, I_{0^-}) \le 0$, the unique equilibrium is $U = 0 = \varphi_V$. Otherwise, run the dynamics with $dU = u_{\max}\,dt$ from $(S_{0^-}, I_{0^-})$ and let $\theta^{u_{\max}}$ be the first time it reaches $\{F = 0\} \cup \{S = 0\}$; the unique equilibrium is

$$
dU(t) = u_{\max}\,\mathbf{1}_{[0,\theta^{u_{\max}}]}(t)\,dt, \qquad \varphi_V'(t) = \frac{u_{\max}}{S(t)}\,(1 - \varphi_V(t))\,\mathbf{1}_{[0,\theta^{u_{\max}}]}(t).
$$

**Comparison with the societal optimum.** Let the societal cost be $C(U) = \int_{0^-}^{\infty} r_I S(t)\,\frac{d\varphi_I(t)}{1-\varphi_I(t)} + r_V\,dU(t)$, minimized by the optimal societal strategy (OS), and call the equilibrium (EIS). Under the compatibility relation, the individual cost is the per-capita societal cost, $C(U)/S_{0^-} = J(\varphi_V)$. Consequently the average cost per person under (OS) is at most that under (EIS) (a nonnegative price of anarchy), and the vaccination region of (OS), bounded by $\partial_S \zeta = r_V/r_I$, contains the vaccination region of (EIS), bounded by $\zeta/S = r_V/r_I$.

## Expansion

By the individual optimality result, any best response vaccinates (at maximal rate or instantly) from time $0$ until the residual risk falls to $r_V/r_I$, so compatibility forces $U$ to have the same bang-bang form; the equilibrium is a fixed point in the stopping time $\theta$. Along the maximal-vaccination trajectory the ratio $\zeta(S,I)/S$ is strictly decreasing (computed from the implicit equation for $\zeta$, using $S - \gamma/\beta < \zeta < S$), so the stopping equation $\zeta/S = r_V/r_I$, equivalent to $F = 0$, has at most one root; this gives uniqueness. The identity $(1 - \varphi_V)(1 - \varphi_I) = S/S_{0^-}$ turns the individual cost into the per-capita societal cost, so optimality of (OS) for $C$ yields the cost comparison. Since $\partial_S\zeta = (\zeta/S)/(1 + (\beta/\gamma)(\zeta - S)) \ge \zeta/S$, society keeps vaccinating in states where individuals already stop, the conflict region behind vaccine free-riding.
