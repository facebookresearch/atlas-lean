# Within-host coalescent density of a coloured genealogy

Consider an outbreak in which all $n$ infected hosts are observed and one pathogen genome is sampled from each host at its removal time. Let $T$ be the transmission tree (who infected whom and when, with all removal times) and let $G$ be the timed genealogy of the $n$ genomes. Assume:

- between hosts, $T$ is generated by the Markov SIR epidemic in a population of size $N$ with infection rate $\beta S I / N$ and removal rate $\gamma I$;
- within each host, lineages follow a neutral Kingman coalescent with constant timescale $N_e g$ (effective size times generation time), so that each pair of lineages coalesces at rate $1/(N_e g)$;
- the transmission bottleneck is complete: a single lineage, sampled at random from the infector, founds each new infection.

Colour each point of $G$ by the host in which that stretch of evolution occurred. For host $i$ let $G_i$ be the subtree of $G$ coloured $i$: its root is at the infection time $r_i$ of host $i$, and its $n_i$ leaves are at the removal time of $i$ and at the times of the transmissions from $i$. Then

$$p(G \mid T, N_e g) \;=\; \prod_{i=1}^{n} p\bigl(G_i \mid d_{i,1}, \dots, d_{i,n_i},\, r_i,\, N_e g\bigr),$$

where $p(G_i \mid \cdot)$ is the density of the within-host coalescent with dated leaves $d_{i,j}$ conditioned on all lineages having merged by time $r_i$. Computing it by adding leaves in order of increasing age: if, when leaf $j \ge 2$ is added, $A_j$ is the total branch length of the partial genealogy available to it between its date and the time it joins, and $B_j$ the total branch length available between its date and $r_i$, then

$$p\bigl(G_i \mid \cdot\bigr) \;=\; \prod_{j=2}^{n_i} \frac{\dfrac{1}{N_e g}\, e^{-A_j/(N_e g)}}{1 - e^{-B_j/(N_e g)}} .$$

The joint posterior is $p(T, \beta, \gamma, N_e g \mid G) \propto p(G \mid T, N_e g)\, p(T \mid \beta, \gamma)\, p(\beta)\,p(\gamma)\,p(N_e g)$, and transmission events coincide with coalescent nodes of $G$ only in the limit $N_e g \to 0$.

## Expansion

Conditional on $T$, the within-host genealogical processes are independent, and each $G_i$ is determined by the coalescent in host $i$ alone; pasting the leaf of $G_i$ at a transmission time to the root of the infectee's subtree reconstructs $G$, which gives the product. Within a host, a newly added lineage travelling back in time merges with the existing partial genealogy at rate $1/(N_e g)$ per unit of existing branch length present, so the time to merge is exponential in accumulated branch length, with density $(N_e g)^{-1} e^{-A_j/(N_e g)}$; truncating it to merge before the root time $r_i$ divides by $1 - e^{-B_j/(N_e g)}$. The epidemic term $p(T \mid \beta, \gamma)$ is the Markov-chain path density of the SIR process, with gamma priors conjugate for $\beta$ and $\gamma$. Inference proceeds by Markov chain Monte Carlo over infection times and infectors. Because the colouring places transmissions anywhere on branches, the posterior over who infected whom remains diffuse when within-host diversity is large, even with a perfectly known phylogeny.
