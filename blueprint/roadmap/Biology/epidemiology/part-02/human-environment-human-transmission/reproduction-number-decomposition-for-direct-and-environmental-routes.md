# Reproduction-number decomposition for direct and environmental routes

**Model.** Let $S, E, A, I, R$ be susceptible, exposed, asymptomatic, symptomatic and recovered hosts, and $V$ the concentration of pathogen on surfaces. With recruitment $\Lambda$, natural death $\omega$, disease death $\alpha$, direct transmission $\beta$ (asymptomatic relative infectiousness $\eta$), environmental transmission $\beta_1$, progression $E \to A$ at rate $k_2(1-\gamma)$ and $E \to I$ at rate $k_1\gamma$, $A \to I$ at rate $v_1\phi$, $A \to R$ at rate $v_2(1-\phi)$, $I \to R$ at rate $\epsilon$, waning $\rho$, shedding $m_1, m_2$ and environmental decay $\tau_1$ (all positive):

$$\begin{aligned}
\dot S &= \Lambda - \omega S - \beta(I + \eta A) S - \beta_1 V S + \rho R, &
\dot E &= \beta(I + \eta A) S + \beta_1 V S - T_1 E,\\
\dot A &= X_1 E - C_1 A, &
\dot I &= k_1\gamma E + v_1\phi A - Y_1 I,\\
\dot R &= v_2(1-\phi) A + \epsilon I - (\rho + \omega) R, &
\dot V &= m_1 A + m_2 I - \tau_1 V,
\end{aligned}$$

with $T_1 = k_1\gamma + k_2(1-\gamma) + \omega$, $X_1 = k_2(1-\gamma)$, $C_1 = \omega + v_1\phi + v_2(1-\phi)$, $Y_1 = \alpha + \epsilon + \omega$, $Q_1 = X_1 v_1 \phi$. The disease-free equilibrium has $S^0 = \Lambda/\omega$.

**Proposition.** The basic reproduction number $R_0 = \rho(FV^{-1})$ over the infected classes $(E, A, I, V)$ is

$$R_0 = \frac{\Lambda}{\omega T_1}\left[ \beta\Big( \frac{\eta X_1}{C_1} + \frac{k_1\gamma}{Y_1} + \frac{Q_1}{Y_1 C_1} \Big) + \beta_1\Big( \frac{m_1 X_1}{\tau_1 C_1} + \frac{m_2 k_1\gamma}{\tau_1 Y_1} + \frac{m_2 Q_1}{\tau_1 Y_1 C_1} \Big) \right] = R_A + R_I + R_{Ae} + R_{Ie},$$

the sum of infections seeded by asymptomatic and symptomatic hosts by direct contact ($R_A$, $R_I$) and through the environment ($R_{Ae}$, $R_{Ie}$). If $R_0 < 1$ the disease-free equilibrium is globally asymptotically stable in the biologically feasible region.

## Expansion

New infections enter only $E$, so $F$ has a single nonzero row $S^0(0, \eta\beta, \beta, \beta_1)$ and $FV^{-1}$ has rank one; its spectral radius is the $(E,E)$ entry, i.e. $S^0$ times the expected total infectiousness (direct plus environmental) generated by one exposed individual. Following an exposed individual: it reaches $A$ with probability $X_1/T_1$ and $I$ directly with probability $k_1\gamma/T_1$; an asymptomatic host reaches $I$ with probability $v_1\phi / C_1$; mean sojourn times are $1/C_1$ in $A$ and $1/Y_1$ in $I$, and each unit of time in $A$ or $I$ deposits $m_1/\tau_1$ or $m_2/\tau_1$ units of environmental pathogen-time. Multiplying by the corresponding transmission rates yields the four terms. Global stability uses the linear Lyapunov function

$$L = E + \frac{\Lambda}{\omega}\left[\Big(\frac{\beta\eta}{C_1} + \frac{\beta v_1\phi}{Y_1 C_1} + \frac{\beta_1 m_2 v_1\phi}{Y_1\tau_1 C_1} + \frac{\beta_1 m_1}{C_1\tau_1}\Big) A + \Big(\frac{\beta}{Y_1} + \frac{\beta_1 m_2}{Y_1\tau_1}\Big) I + \frac{\beta_1}{\tau_1} V\right],$$

whose coefficients are $S^0 = \Lambda/\omega$ times the expected future infectiousness, per susceptible, of an individual currently in each class; along solutions with $S \le S^0$ one gets $\dot L \le T_1 (R_0 - 1) E \le 0$, and LaSalle's invariance principle finishes the argument.
