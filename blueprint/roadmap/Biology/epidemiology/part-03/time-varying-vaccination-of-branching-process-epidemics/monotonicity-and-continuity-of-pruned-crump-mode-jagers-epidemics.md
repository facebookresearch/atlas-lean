# Monotonicity and continuity of pruned Crump–Mode–Jagers epidemics in the vaccination function

Approximate the early stage of an SIR-type epidemic in a large homogeneously mixing population by a Crump–Mode–Jagers (CMJ) branching process $Z = \{Z(t)\}_{t\ge0}$: each infective has an i.i.d. life history $(I,\xi)$, an infectious period $I$ and a point process $\xi$ of contact times, and every contact produces a new infective. Assume, as the source does throughout, that $Z$ is non-explosive. Model a vaccination programme by a function $\alpha : [0,\infty) \to [0,1]$, the immune fraction at time $t$; in the vaccinated process $Z_\alpha$ each birth occurring at time $t$ is deleted independently with probability $\alpha(t)$, together with all its descendants (**pruning**). Couple all $Z_\alpha$ on one probability space by deleting the $i$-th birth of $Z$, at time $b_i$, iff $U_i \le \alpha(b_i)$ for i.i.d. uniforms $U_i$. Write $\alpha \prec \alpha'$ if $\alpha(t) \le \alpha'(t)$ for all $t$, $\|\alpha - \alpha'\|_t = \sup_{s\le t}|\alpha(s) - \alpha'(s)|$.

A nonnegative functional $f$ of the process is **monotone with pruning**, $f \in \mathcal P$, if $f(Z^P) \le f(Z)$ whenever $Z^P$ is a pruning of $Z$; examples are the extinction time $T$, the maximum number infected $M = \sup_t Z(t)$, the number $N(t)$ of infections in $(0,t]$, the total outbreak size $N(\infty)$, and indicators such as $\mathbf 1\{T > t\}$. Write $f \in \mathcal P_t$ if in addition $f$ depends only on $\{Z(s) : s \le t\}$. Let $\mu^f_\alpha = \mathbb E[f(Z_\alpha)]$, $v^f_\alpha(x) = \mathbb P(f(Z_\alpha) \le x)$, $x^f_{\alpha,p} = \inf\{x : v^f_\alpha(x) \ge p\}$, and $v_\alpha$ the distribution function of the extinction time with quantiles $t_{\alpha,p}$.

**Theorem (monotonicity).** If $f \in \mathcal P$ and $\alpha \prec \alpha'$, then

$$\mu^f_\alpha \ge \mu^f_{\alpha'}, \qquad v^f_\alpha(x) \le v^f_{\alpha'}(x) \ \ \text{for all } x, \qquad x^f_{\alpha',p} \le x^f_{\alpha,p} \ \ \text{for all } p \in (0,1).$$

In particular more vaccination gives a stochastically shorter epidemic: $v_\alpha(t) \le v_{\alpha'}(t)$ for all $t$ and $t_{\alpha',p} \le t_{\alpha,p}$.

**Theorem (continuity).** Fix $t > 0$ and $f \in \mathcal P_t$. If $f(Z_\alpha) \le \hat f(Z)$ for all $\alpha$ with $\mathbb E[\hat f(Z)] < \infty$, then $\alpha \mapsto \mu^f_\alpha$ is uniformly continuous for $\|\cdot\|_t$; without the domination, $\sup_x |v^f_\alpha(x) - v^f_{\alpha'}(x)| \to 0$ as $\|\alpha - \alpha'\|_t \to 0$. For quantiles, let $\alpha_n \to \alpha$ uniformly on $[0,\infty)$, i.e. $\sup_{s\ge0}|\alpha_n(s)-\alpha(s)| \to 0$, and $p \in (0,1)$ with $x^f_{\alpha,p} < \infty$; if either $\alpha \prec \alpha_n$ for all $n$ (approximation from above), or $v^f_\alpha$ is continuous and strictly increasing at $x^f_{\alpha,p}$, then $x^f_{\alpha_n,p} \to x^f_{\alpha,p}$. (Without one of these conditions it can fail: for integer-valued $f$ approximated from below the quantile can stay one unit too high.) Likewise, for the extinction time, if $\alpha_n \to \alpha$ uniformly on $[0,\infty)$, $t_{\alpha,p} < \infty$ and $v_\alpha$ is continuous and strictly increasing at $t_{\alpha,p}$, then $t_{\alpha_n,p} \to t_{\alpha,p}$. (Convergence in $\|\cdot\|_t$ for a fixed $t$ does not suffice here: $\alpha \equiv 0$, $\alpha_n = \mathbf 1_{(t,\infty)}$.) If the infectious period $I$ is continuous, $v_\alpha$ is a continuous distribution function for every $\alpha$.

These properties justify searching for optimal vaccination policies (cheapest $\alpha$ keeping the mean or a quantile of the extinction time, or of the outbreak size, below a target) over finite-dimensional families of vaccination functions.

## Expansion

Under the coupling, $\alpha \prec \alpha'$ means every birth deleted in $Z_\alpha$ is also deleted in $Z_{\alpha'}$, so $Z_{\alpha'}$ is a pruning of $Z_\alpha$ pathwise and $f(Z_{\alpha'}) \le f(Z_\alpha)$ almost surely; expectations, distribution functions and quantiles inherit the order. For continuity, $Z_\alpha$ and $Z_{\alpha'}$ differ on $[0,t]$ only if some birth $b_i \le t$ has $U_i$ between $\alpha(b_i)$ and $\alpha'(b_i)$; the expected number of such births is at most $\|\alpha - \alpha'\|_t\,\mathbb E[N(t)]$, which is finite and small, and dominated convergence handles the expectations.
