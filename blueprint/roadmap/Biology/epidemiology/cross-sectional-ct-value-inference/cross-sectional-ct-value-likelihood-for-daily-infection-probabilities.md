# Cross-sectional Ct-value likelihood for daily infection probabilities

Fix a testing day $t$ and a maximal detectable infection age $A_{\max} \in \mathbb{N}$. For $a = 1, \dots, A_{\max}$ let $\pi_{t-a} \ge 0$ be the probability that a randomly chosen member of the population was infected on day $t - a$, with $\sum_{a} \pi_{t-a} \le 1$. Let $\phi_a \in [0,1]$ be the probability that an infection of age $a$ gives a detectable result (Ct below the limit of detection $C_{\mathrm{LOD}}$), and let $p_a(x)$ be the density of the observed Ct value $x$ at infection age $a$ conditional on detectability. Assume that $n$ individuals are sampled independently and uniformly at random, with results $X_1, \dots, X_n$, where $X_i \ge C_{\mathrm{LOD}}$ codes a negative test.

Then, for a detectable value $x < C_{\mathrm{LOD}}$,

$$P(X_i = x \mid \pi) \;=\; \sum_{a=1}^{A_{\max}} p_a(x)\, \phi_a\, \pi_{t-a}, \qquad P(X_i < C_{\mathrm{LOD}} \mid \pi) \;=\; \sum_{a=1}^{A_{\max}} \phi_a\, \pi_{t-a},$$

and the likelihood of the cross-section is

$$L(\pi) \;=\; \prod_{i=1}^{n} \Bigl[\sum_{a} p_a(X_i)\,\phi_a\,\pi_{t-a}\Bigr]^{\mathbf 1(X_i < C_{\mathrm{LOD}})} \Bigl[1 - \sum_{a}\phi_a\,\pi_{t-a}\Bigr]^{\mathbf 1(X_i \ge C_{\mathrm{LOD}})} .$$

If only the $n$ detectable values are recorded, the conditional likelihood is

$$L_{+}(\pi) \;=\; \frac{\prod_{i=1}^{n} \sum_{a} p_a(X_i)\,\phi_a\,\pi_{t-a}}{\bigl(\sum_{a}\phi_a\,\pi_{t-a}\bigr)^{n}},$$

which is invariant under rescaling $\pi \mapsto c\,\pi$ ($c > 0$): positives alone identify the shape of the recent incidence curve but not its level. For test days $t_1, \dots, t_T$ with independent samples, the likelihood is the product of the single-day likelihoods, each indexed by the infection probabilities $\{\pi_{t_j - a}\}$ preceding its own test day.

## Expansion

Condition on the infection day of a sampled individual. With probability $\pi_{t-a}$ the infection has age $a$; it is then detectable with probability $\phi_a$ and, if detectable, its Ct value has density $p_a$. Summing over $a$ (law of total probability) gives the two displayed probabilities, and independence across sampled individuals gives the product. Dividing each detectable term by the probability of detectability gives $L_{+}$; numerator and denominator are both homogeneous of degree $n$ in $\pi$, which proves the scale invariance. In applications the age-specific law $p_a$ is a Gumbel density with mode $C_{\mathrm{mode}}(a)$ following a piecewise-linear viral-kinetics curve (fast rise to a peak, then a slower decline), truncated to the observable range, and $\pi$ is either left free (nonparametric), constrained to exponential growth $\pi_{t-a} \propto e^{-ra}$, generated by an SEIR model, or given a Gaussian-process prior for multiple cross-sections.
