# Reproduction number of Zika virus with combined sexual and vector transmission

**Model.** Humans are susceptible ($S$), Zika-infected ($I_z$) or recovered ($R$); mosquitoes are susceptible ($S_m$) or infected ($I_m$):

$$\begin{aligned}
\dot S &= \Lambda - (\beta_m I_m + \beta_z I_z)S - \mu S, & \dot I_z &= (\beta_m I_m + \beta_z I_z)S - (\mu_z + \delta_z + \mu)I_z, & \dot R &= \delta_z I_z - \mu R,\\
\dot S_m &= \Lambda_m - \alpha_m I_z S_m - \mu_m S_m, & \dot I_m &= \alpha_m I_z S_m - \mu_m I_m,
\end{aligned}$$

with recruitment $\Lambda, \Lambda_m$, natural death rates $\mu, \mu_m$, disease death $\mu_z$, recovery $\delta_z$, sexual transmission $\beta_z$, mosquito-to-human transmission $\beta_m$ and human-to-mosquito transmission $\alpha_m$. The disease-free equilibrium is $(\Lambda/\mu, 0, 0, \Lambda_m/\mu_m, 0)$.

**Proposition.** Let $k = \mu_z + \delta_z + \mu$ and

$$\mathcal{R}_{z_1} = \frac{\beta_z\Lambda}{2\mu k}, \qquad \bar{\mathcal{R}}_{z_2} = \frac{\beta_m\alpha_m\Lambda_m\Lambda}{\mu\mu_m^2 k}.$$

1. The next-generation basic reproduction number is

$$\mathcal{R}_z = \mathcal{R}_{z_1} + \sqrt{\mathcal{R}_{z_1}^2 + \bar{\mathcal{R}}_{z_2}},$$

the positive root of $\lambda^2 - 2\mathcal{R}_{z_1}\lambda - \bar{\mathcal{R}}_{z_2} = 0$. Without sexual transmission it reduces to the geometric mean $\sqrt{\bar{\mathcal{R}}_{z_2}}$ of the two vector-borne legs; without vectors it is the sexual reproduction number $2\mathcal{R}_{z_1}$.
2. With $\mathcal{R}_z^* = 2\mathcal{R}_{z_1} + \bar{\mathcal{R}}_{z_2} = \mathcal{R}_z^2 + 2\mathcal{R}_{z_1}(1 - \mathcal{R}_z)$, one has $\mathcal{R}_z \gtrless 1 \iff \mathcal{R}_z^* \gtrless 1$, and $\mathcal{R}_z = 1 \iff \mathcal{R}_z^* = 1$.
3. If $\mathcal{R}_z^* < 1$ the disease-free equilibrium is locally asymptotically stable; if $\mathcal{R}_z^* > 1$ there is an endemic equilibrium

$$E_z^* = \Big(\frac{\Lambda}{\mu}\Big(1 - \frac{R^*}{R_{\max}}\Big),\ \frac{\mu}{\delta_z}R^*,\ R^*,\ \frac{\Lambda_m}{\mu_m}\cdot\frac{\mu_m\delta_z}{\mu_m\delta_z + \alpha_m\mu R^*},\ \frac{\alpha_m\Lambda_m\mu R^*}{\mu_m(\mu_m\delta_z + \alpha_m\mu R^*)}\Big), \qquad R_{\max} = \frac{\Lambda}{\mu}\frac{\delta_z}{k},$$

where $R^* \in (0, R_{\max})$ is the positive root of the quadratic obtained by substituting these expressions into the $I_z$ equation.

## Proof sketch

New infections in $(I_z, I_m)$ are generated by $F = \begin{pmatrix}\beta_z\Lambda/\mu & \beta_m\Lambda/\mu\\ \alpha_m\Lambda_m/\mu_m & 0\end{pmatrix}$ and removed by $V = \mathrm{diag}(k, \mu_m)$, so $FV^{-1}$ has characteristic polynomial $\lambda^2 - 2\mathcal{R}_{z_1}\lambda - \bar{\mathcal{R}}_{z_2}$ and $\mathcal{R}_z$ is its spectral radius. Since this polynomial is negative exactly between its roots and its value at $\lambda = 1$ is $1 - \mathcal{R}_z^*$, the sign of $\mathcal{R}_z - 1$ equals that of $\mathcal{R}_z^* - 1$, which avoids square roots in threshold conditions. At equilibrium, $S$, $I_z$, $S_m$, $I_m$ are explicit in $R$ (from $\dot R = 0$ and the mosquito equations; $\dot I_m = 0$ gives $I_m^* = \alpha_m I_z^* S_m^*/\mu_m$, consistent with $S_m^* + I_m^* = \Lambda_m/\mu_m$), reducing the endemic problem to one quadratic in $R$.
