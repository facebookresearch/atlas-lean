# Whittle's minor-outbreak probability for the stochastic SIR model

**Model.** In a closed population of size $N$, the SIR continuous-time Markov chain $(S(t), I(t))$ jumps from $(s,i)$ to $(s-1, i+1)$ at rate $\beta s i/N$ (infection) and to $(s, i-1)$ at rate $\gamma i$ (recovery), with $\beta, \gamma > 0$ and $R_0 = \beta/\gamma$. The state $I = 0$ is absorbing.

**Branching approximation.** When $S \approx N$, each infective independently either infects (rate $\beta$) or recovers (rate $\gamma$), so the next event produces two infectives or none. The offspring probability generating function is

$$f(u) = \frac{\gamma}{\beta+\gamma} + \frac{\beta}{\beta+\gamma}\,u^2, \qquad u \in [0,1] \quad \text{(Eq. 5)},$$

with $f'(1) = 2\beta/(\beta + \gamma) > 1 \iff R_0 > 1$.

**Proposition.** The fixed points of $f$ are $u = 1$ and $u = 1/R_0$; for $R_0 \ge 1$ both lie in $[0,1]$ (they coincide at $R_0 = 1$), and for $R_0 < 1$ the only fixed point in $[0,1]$ is $u = 1$. The extinction probability of the branching process started from $I(0) = i$ infectives is

$$\mathbb{P}(\text{minor outbreak}) = \begin{cases} (1/R_0)^i, & R_0 > 1,\\ 1, & R_0 \le 1,\end{cases} \qquad \mathbb{P}(\text{major outbreak}) = 1 - \mathbb{P}(\text{minor outbreak}).$$

For $R_0 > 1$ the fixed point $1/R_0$ is stable and $u = 1$ unstable; for $R_0 < 1$ the fixed point $u = 1$ is stable.

**Simulation.** Sample paths of the chain are generated by the Gillespie algorithm: with total rate $\lambda = \beta s i/N + \gamma i$, the inter-event time is $\tau = -\ln(u_1)/\lambda$ for $u_1 \sim U(0,1)$ (Eq. 6), and the event is infection with probability $(\beta s i/N)/\lambda$.

## Expansion

Solving $f(u) = u$ gives $\beta u^2 - (\beta + \gamma)u + \gamma = 0$, i.e. $(u - 1)(\beta u - \gamma) = 0$. The extinction probability of a Galton–Watson process is the smallest fixed point of its offspring generating function in $[0,1]$, which is $1/R_0$ when $R_0 > 1$ and $1$ otherwise. Separate lines of descent are independent in the branching approximation, so $i$ initial infectives all go extinct with probability $(1/R_0)^i$. The approximation is accurate for large $N$ and few initial infectives; with $\beta = 0.3$, $\gamma = 0.15$ and $I(0) = 2$ it predicts a minor outbreak in one of four runs.
