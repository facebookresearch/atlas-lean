# Superspreading raises the extinction probability

Let $R \ge 0$ and $0 < k_1 \le k_2$. Let $q_k$ be the extinction probability
$\sup_n \mathbb{P}(Z_n = 0)$ of the Galton–Watson process started from one individual with the
[negative-binomial offspring law](nb-offspring.md) of mean $R$ and dispersion $k$. Then
$q_{k_2} \le q_{k_1}$: at a fixed mean, the probability that the outbreak started by one case dies out
is nonincreasing in the dispersion (Lloyd-Smith et al. 2005).
