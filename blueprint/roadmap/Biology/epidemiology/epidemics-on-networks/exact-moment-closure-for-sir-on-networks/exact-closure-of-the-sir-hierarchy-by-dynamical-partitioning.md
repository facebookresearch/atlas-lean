# Exact closure of the SIR hierarchy by dynamical partitioning

**Model.** Let $D = (V, A)$ be a directed graph on $N$ nodes. An infectious node $j$ makes infectious contacts to node $i$ by a Poisson process of rate $T_{ij} \ge 0$, with $T_{ij} > 0$ iff $(j,i) \in A$ and $T_{ii} = 0$; a susceptible node receiving a contact becomes infectious, and node $i$ recovers after an exponential time with rate $\gamma_i$ (recovered is absorbing). Initial node states are statistically independent. $S_i, I_i$ denote the indicators of node $i$ being susceptible or infectious, and for disjoint node sets $X$ and a state assignment $A$ on $X$, $\psi^A_X$ is the indicator that the nodes of $X$ are in state $A$. Angle brackets denote expectation.

For $X \subseteq V$ let $\mathrm{IN}(X)$ be the set of nodes that can reach some member of $X$ by a directed path. Disjoint non-empty $X, Y, Z \subseteq V$ are such that $Z$ is *dynamically partitioning* for $X$ and $Y$ if $\mathrm{IN}(X) \cap \mathrm{IN}(Y) = \varnothing$ in the subgraph $D - Z$ (for undirected networks: $Z$ separates $X$ from $Y$).

**Theorem (exact closure).** If $Z$ is dynamically partitioning for $X$ and $Y$, every node of $Z$ is susceptible in the state $C$, and $\langle \psi^C_Z\rangle \ne 0$, then for all states $A$ on $X$ and $B$ on $Y$ and all times,

$$ \langle \psi^A_X \psi^B_Y \psi^C_Z \rangle = \frac{\langle \psi^A_X \psi^C_Z\rangle\,\langle \psi^B_Y \psi^C_Z\rangle}{\langle \psi^C_Z\rangle}. $$

**Theorem (trees).** If the underlying undirected graph of $D$ is a forest, the following pair-level system is exact:

$$ \begin{aligned} \frac{d}{dt}\langle S_i\rangle &= -\textstyle\sum_j T_{ij}\langle S_iI_j\rangle, \qquad \frac{d}{dt}\langle I_i\rangle = \textstyle\sum_j T_{ij}\langle S_iI_j\rangle - \gamma_i\langle I_i\rangle,\\ \frac{d}{dt}\langle S_iI_j\rangle &= \textstyle\sum_{k\ne i} T_{jk}\frac{\langle S_iS_j\rangle\langle S_jI_k\rangle}{\langle S_j\rangle} - \sum_{k \ne j} T_{ik}\frac{\langle S_iI_k\rangle\langle S_iI_j\rangle}{\langle S_i\rangle} - (T_{ij} + \gamma_j)\langle S_iI_j\rangle,\\ \frac{d}{dt}\langle S_iS_j\rangle &= -\textstyle\sum_{k\ne j} T_{ik}\frac{\langle S_iS_j\rangle\langle S_iI_k\rangle}{\langle S_i\rangle} - \sum_{k \ne i} T_{jk}\frac{\langle S_iS_j\rangle\langle S_jI_k\rangle}{\langle S_j\rangle}. \end{aligned} $$

**Theorem (system size).** Call $D[W]$ a *directed sub-block* if its underlying graph is biconnected and some node of $W$ is reachable from all others within $D[W]$, and a *transmission block* if it is a maximal directed sub-block. Applying the exact rate equations and Theorem 2.1 repeatedly to all singletons gives a finite closed set $M_E$ of subsystem states; the largest member of $M_E$ has as many nodes as the largest transmission block, or $2$ nodes if there is no transmission block. Consequently, cycle-partitioning approximations of order $x \ge n - 2$, where $n$ is the size of the largest transmission block, are exact.

## Expansion

The master equation gives, for every subsystem state, a rate equation involving states of the same size and states with one extra infectious neighbour from outside the subsystem; starting from $S$/$I$ singletons no recovered nodes ever enter the hierarchy. If all nodes of $Z$ are susceptible, infection has not passed through $Z$, and since no node outside $Z$ can reach both $X$ and $Y$, the states of $X$ and $Y$ are driven by disjoint sets of independent Poisson processes, recovery clocks and initial states; hence they are conditionally independent given $\psi^C_Z$, which is the exact closure theorem. On a tree every node $j$ separates its neighbours, so each triple term $\langle S_iS_jI_k\rangle$ and $\langle S_iI_jI_k\rangle$ arising at pair level factorizes with $Z = \{j\}$ or $Z = \{i\}$, giving the tree result. In general the closure fails only inside biconnected regions from which infection can reach a common node, which is what the transmission-block bound captures; for undirected graphs transmission blocks are the ordinary blocks, so the exact system needs subsystems no larger than the largest biconnected component.
