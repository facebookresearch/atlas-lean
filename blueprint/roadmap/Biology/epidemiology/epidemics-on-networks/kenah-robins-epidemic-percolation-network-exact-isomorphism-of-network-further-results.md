# Epidemic percolation networks: further results

Kenah and Robins (2007) state large-population consequences of the [epidemic percolation network](kenah-robins-epidemic-percolation-network-exact-isomorphism-of-network.md), a semi-directed random network on the population. (1) In the limit of a large population: the distribution of self-limited outbreak sizes equals the size distribution of small out-components; the epidemic threshold corresponds to the appearance of a giant strongly connected component; the probability of a large epidemic equals the probability that an initial infection lies in the giant in-component; and the relative final size of an epidemic equals the proportion of the network in the giant out-component. (2) For the SIR model studied by Newman, the epidemic percolation network gives the same mean outbreak size below threshold, the same threshold and the same final size as Newman's bond percolation model, but bond percolation gives the wrong outbreak size distribution and probability of an epidemic when the infectious period has a nondegenerate distribution. In the source these limit statements are argued informally, with self-limited outbreaks and epidemics defined by relative final size tending to zero or staying positive. This page records them as the source's informal claims; it states no limit theorem.

## Expansion

1. Giant components of random directed and semi-directed networks (in-, out- and strongly connected components) and the small-component size distribution, for an explicit network model.
2. Rigorous versions of the four large-population claims of clause (1) as limits along a sequence of populations.
3. Newman's bond percolation model and the computation showing equal mean outbreak size, threshold and final size.
4. An explicit nondegenerate infectious period for which the outbreak size distribution and the epidemic probability differ.
