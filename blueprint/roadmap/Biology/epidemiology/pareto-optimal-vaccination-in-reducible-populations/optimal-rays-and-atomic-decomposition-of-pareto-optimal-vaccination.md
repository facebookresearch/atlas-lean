# Optimal rays and atomic decomposition of Pareto-optimal vaccination

**Setting.** A population is a $\sigma$-finite measure space $(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mu)$ with a nonnegative next-generation kernel $\mathsf{k}$ of finite double norm. A vaccination strategy is a measurable $\eta : \Omega \to [0,1]$ giving the fraction of each type left **unvaccinated**; the loss is the effective reproduction number $R_e\mathsf{k}$, the spectral radius of the integral operator $f \mapsto \int \mathsf{k}(\cdot,y)\,\eta(y) f(y)\,\mu(dy)$, with $R_0 = R_e(\mathbf{1})$. The cost $C$ is continuous, decreasing and $C(\mathbf{1}) = 0$. A strategy is **Pareto optimal** if no other strategy has both lower cost and lower $R_e$, and **anti-Pareto optimal** if no other has both higher cost and higher $R_e$ (the worst way to spend a budget). A set $A$ is invariant if $\mathsf{k}(A^c,A) = 0$; the atoms $(\Omega_i)_{i\in I}$ of $\mu$ on the $\sigma$-field generated by invariant sets are the irreducible components, with restricted kernels $\mathsf{k}_i = \mathbf{1}_{\Omega_i}\mathsf{k}\,\mathbf{1}_{\Omega_i}$.

**Theorem.**
1. (Optimal ray.) Let $C(\eta) = \int_\Omega (1 - \eta)\,c\,d\mu$ for a positive $c \in L^1(\mu)$ and suppose $R_e[\mathsf{k}]$ is convex. If $\eta_\star$ is Pareto optimal with $\eta_\star < 1$ a.e., then $\lambda\eta_\star$ is Pareto optimal for every $\lambda \ge 0$ with $\lambda\eta_\star \le 1$; the Pareto frontier contains the segment from $(\int c\,d\mu, 0)$ to the point of $\eta_\star/\sup\eta_\star$, and eradication with a positive unvaccinated fraction everywhere is impossible below the full cost.
2. (Cordons sanitaires are never worst.) If $R_0 > 0$, a disconnecting strategy, one whose unvaccinated population splits the kernel into several non-communicating parts, is never anti-Pareto optimal.
3. (Reduction to atoms.) For every $\eta$,

$$R_e\mathsf{k} = \max_{i\in I} R_e\mathsf{k}_i.$$

An anti-Pareto optimal strategy at level $\ell$ vaccinates everything outside a single atom $\Omega_j$ (one maximising the atom-wise worst cost $C_j^\star(\ell)$) and is anti-Pareto optimal inside it. If $C$ is extensive (additive over disjoint vaccinated sets, e.g. any cost $\int \phi(\eta(x),x)\,\mu(dx)$), a strategy is Pareto optimal at level $\ell$ if and only if it leaves everyone outside the atoms unvaccinated and is Pareto optimal on each atom for the level $\min(\ell, R_0[\mathsf{k}_i])$; the minimal costs then add across atoms.

## Expansion

Convexity of $R_e$ together with homogeneity, $R_e(\lambda\eta) = \lambda R_e(\eta)$, gives the ray property: scaling a Pareto-optimal profile down scales the reproduction number linearly while the affine cost rises linearly, and a strictly better point below the ray would contradict convexity along the segment joining it to $0$. The decomposition follows because a reducible operator is block triangular along its invariant sets, so its spectral radius is the largest spectral radius of the diagonal blocks; vaccinating anyone outside the atoms (types that are infected but do not sustain transmission among themselves) is therefore wasted. For Pareto optimality each block must be brought to the target level independently, which makes costs add; for anti-Pareto optimality only the block that determines the maximum matters, so the worst strategies concentrate on one atom. Disconnecting strategies lower the maximum over blocks and can therefore never be the worst use of a given budget.
