# Under a finite measure the limit data are tight

Let $\mu$ be a finite measure on $\Omega$, $U_0 : \Omega \to \mathbb{R}^2$ measurable, and
$M(\omega) : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}^2$ paths continuous on $[0, T]$ whose values
$\omega \mapsto M(\omega)(t)$ are measurable. Then, with the sup norm $\|h\| = \max(|h_1|, |h_2|)$
on $\mathbb{R}^2$,

$$
\mu\bigl\{\omega : \|U_0(\omega)\| > k \text{ or }
\|M(\omega)(t)\| > k \text{ for some } t \in [0, T]\bigr\} \to 0
$$

as $k \to \infty$ through the natural numbers. The event is measurable: by continuity of the paths
it is a countable union over rational times.
