# H12 and H2/H1 haplotype homozygosity statistics for hard and soft sweeps

Fix a genomic window and a sample of $n$ haplotypes, group identical haplotypes, and let $p_1 \ge p_2 \ge \dots$ be the sample frequencies of the distinct haplotypes, $\sum_i p_i = 1$. Define

$$H_1 = \sum_i p_i^2, \qquad H_{12} = (p_1 + p_2)^2 + \sum_{i > 2} p_i^2 = H_1 + 2 p_1 p_2, \qquad H_2 = \sum_{i > 1} p_i^2 = H_1 - p_1^2 .$$

$H_1$ is haplotype homozygosity. $H_{12}$ pools the two most frequent haplotypes before computing homozygosity, so it stays high in a **soft sweep** (several adaptive haplotypes rise together) as well as in a **hard sweep** (one adaptive haplotype). The ratio $H_2/H_1$, measured where $H_{12}$ peaks, is low for hard sweeps and increases toward $1$ as sweeps become softer, so the pair $(H_{12}, H_2/H_1)$ separates hard from soft sweeps.

Elementary properties:

1. $0 < H_1 \le H_{12} \le 1$ and $0 \le H_2/H_1 < 1$; $H_2/H_1 = 0$ exactly when one haplotype is fixed in the sample.
2. If the sample consists of $m \ge 2$ equally frequent haplotypes, then $H_1 = 1/m$, $H_{12} = (m+2)/m^2$ and $H_2/H_1 = (m-1)/m$.
3. If one haplotype has frequency $p_1 = 1 - \varepsilon$ and the remaining $\varepsilon n$ haplotypes are singletons, with $\varepsilon n \ge 1$ (that is, $\varepsilon \ge 1/n$), then $H_1 = (1-\varepsilon)^2 + \varepsilon/n$, $H_{12} - H_1 = 2(1-\varepsilon)/n$ and $H_2/H_1 = (\varepsilon/n)/H_1$, so for a hard sweep pooling barely changes homozygosity and $H_2/H_1$ is near $0$ in large samples.

The window must be long enough that neutral demography rarely creates long shared haplotypes, yet shorter than the sweep footprint, which for a hard sweep extends over roughly $s / \big(\rho \log(N_e s)\big)$ base pairs for selection coefficient $s$, effective size $N_e$ and per-base recombination rate $\rho$.

## Remarks

Property 1: $H_{12} - H_1 = 2p_1p_2 \ge 0$ and $H_{12}$ is the homozygosity of a partition, hence at most $1$; $H_2 = H_1 - p_1^2 < H_1$ because $p_1 > 0$. Property 2 is direct substitution: $(2/m)^2 + (m-2)/m^2 = (m+2)/m^2$ and $H_2 = (m-1)/m^2$. Property 3: here $p_2 = 1/n$ and $H_2 = \varepsilon n \cdot n^{-2} = \varepsilon/n$. In simulations of Drosophila-like parameters with 145 haplotypes in 400-SNP windows, tests based on $H_{12}$ and iHS had similar power for hard sweeps, while $H_{12}$ was much more powerful for soft sweeps until they became extremely soft (adaptive mutation rate $\theta_A > 10$); $H_{123}$, which pools three haplotypes, added little. Hard and soft scenarios were compared by approximate-Bayesian Bayes factors on $(H_{12}, H_2/H_1)$, and all top 50 peaks in a North American Drosophila melanogaster panel were more easily generated by soft sweeps.
