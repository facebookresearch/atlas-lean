# Bounded Tajima statistics exclude nondegenerate Gaussian laws

Let `n ≥ 4`, let `μ` be a measure and let `π`, `S` be real functions with `S = 0` or `S ≥ 1` and
`|π - S / a₁| ≤ β S` `μ`-almost everywhere, for a real `β`. Then for every real `m` and every
`v > 0`, [Tajima's D](../tajima-s-d/tajima-d.md) does not have the law `N(m, v)` under `μ`: it is
not the case that `D` is almost everywhere measurable with image measure `N(m, v)`, whether or
not `μ` is a probability measure. Indeed `D` is almost everywhere [bounded](abs-tajima-d-le.md),
and `D = 0` where `S = 0` by the conventions `√0 = 0` and `x / 0 = 0`. This is a statement about
one fixed sample; it does not by itself rule out a Gaussian limit in distribution of a sequence of
such samples.
