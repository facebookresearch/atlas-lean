# Deleterious mutations cannot surf monostable waves

**Setting.** Let $u=(u_k)_{k\in\mathbb N_0}$ solve the spatial Muller's ratchet system

$$\partial_t u_k=\tfrac m2\Delta u_k+q_+(\|u\|_{\ell_1})\big(s_k(1-\mu)u_k+\mathbb 1_{k\ge1}s_{k-1}\mu u_{k-1}\big)-q_-(\|u\|_{\ell_1})u_k$$

with a monostable reaction term (strictly decreasing fitnesses $s_k$, $\mu\in(0,1)$, $q_+\ge q_-$ on $[0,1]$ with equality at $1$, $q_+>0$, $(1-\mu)q_+(0)>q_-(0)$). **Tracer dynamics** (Hallatschek-Nelson) label a sub-population at time 0, with density $f^*\le f$ componentwise, and follow its descendants: labelled individuals have the same per-capita birth and death rates as everyone at their location, but only labelled parents produce labelled offspring. The labelled densities $u^*=(u^*_k)$ solve the linear system

$$\partial_t u^*_k=\tfrac m2\Delta u^*_k+q_+(\|u\|_{\ell_1})\big(s_k(1-\mu)u^*_k+\mathbb 1_{k\ge1}s_{k-1}\mu u^*_{k-1}\big)-q_-(\|u\|_{\ell_1})u^*_k ,$$

driven by the total density $\|u\|_{\ell_1}$.

**Theorem.** There is a unique continuous mild solution $u^*$, locally bounded in $L_\infty(\mathbb R;\ell_1)$ with $C^{1,2}$ components. If the labelled population initially carries no mutation-free individuals, $f^*_0\equiv0$, then

$$\lim_{T\to\infty}\ \|u^*(T,\cdot)\|_{L_\infty(\mathbb R;\ell_1)}=0 .$$

So the descendants of any initial set of mutation carriers vanish everywhere, at the front included. Combined with the fact that, in the monostable regime, a positive fraction $\approx\alpha_k$ of the population carries $k$ mutations both in the bulk and at the front, the mutational load at the front is continually regenerated by new mutations in the mutation-free class that leads the wave; it is not the result of pre-existing deleterious mutations surfing.

## Expansion

By the Feynman-Kac formula, $u^*_k(T,x)=\mathbb E_x\big[f^*_k(W_T)e^{\int_0^T G_k}\big]+$ inflow from class $k-1$, with $W$ a Brownian motion of speed $m$ and per-capita growth $G_k=q_+(\|u\|)s_k(1-\mu)-q_-(\|u\|)$ evaluated along the path. With $f_0^*\equiv0$ the class $u^*_0$ stays identically zero, and for $k\ge1$ the growth rate is strictly below that of the mutation-free class, because $s_k<s_0=1$, so the labelled mass, which lacks the fastest-growing class, is discounted relative to the unlabelled mutation-free class that controls the total density. Induction on $k$ with uniform tail control in $\ell_1$ gives uniform decay.
