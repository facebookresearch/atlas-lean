# Isothermal theorem for every initial set of mutants

In the model of Lieberman, Hauert and Nowak (2005), $N$ individuals occupy the vertices of a graph with weights $w_{ij}$, $\sum_j w_{ij} = 1$; at each step an individual $i$ is chosen to reproduce with probability proportional to its fitness (mutant $r$, resident $1$) and its offspring replaces $j$ with probability $w_{ij}$. The temperature of vertex $i$ is $T_i = \sum_j w_{ji}$, and a vertex is hot if it is replaced often and cold if rarely. For $r > 0$, $r \ne 1$, write $\rho_k(r) = (1 - r^{-k})/(1 - r^{-N})$ for the Moran fixation probability from $k$ mutants, so that $\rho_1(r) = (1 - 1/r)/(1 - 1/r^N)$ is that of a single mutant. Assume throughout that the graph of positive weights is strongly connected, that is, $W$ is irreducible, as in the forward theorem linked below. Without this the equivalence fails: the $2 \times 2$ identity matrix is doubly stochastic, but a single mutant can never reach the other vertex, so its fixation probability is $0$, not $\rho_1(r)$. Following the Supplementary Notes of Lieberman, Hauert and Nowak (Definition 1), call $W$ $\rho$-equivalent to the Moran process for the fitness $r$ if, for every nonempty set $X$ of initially mutant vertices, the fixation probability equals $\rho_{|X|}(r)$. Their isothermal theorem (Supplementary Notes, Theorem 2) is the equivalence this page states: for irreducible $W$ and $r > 0$, $r \ne 1$, $W$ is $\rho$-equivalent to the Moran process if and only if all vertices have the same temperature, which is equivalent to $W$ being doubly stochastic. The Supplementary Notes derive it from a circulation theorem (their Theorem 1) for general nonnegative edge weights, which is not stated here. Taking $X = \{v\}$ for each vertex $v$ and averaging gives [the isothermal theorem, "if" direction](../../population-dynamics/isothermal-theorem-for-evolutionary-graph-dynami/average-fixation-eq-moran-fixation.md) for a single mutant at a uniformly random vertex. That single-mutant statement has no converse: Galanis, Göbel, Goldberg, Lapinskas and Richerby (2017, Proposition 12, proved in their Section 8) show that a strongly connected graph that is not isothermal can give a single mutant at a uniformly random vertex the fixation probability $\rho_1(r)$. For example, the irreducible $W$ with rows $(0, 1/4, 3/4)$, $(1/4, 0, 3/4)$ and $(1/2, 1/2, 0)$ has column sums $3/4$, $3/4$ and $3/2$, yet its absorption equations give the uniformly placed single mutant the fixation probability $r^2/(r^2 + r + 1) = \rho_1(r)$ for every $r \ne 1$; it is not $\rho$-equivalent, since for $r = 2$ a single mutant at the third vertex fixes with probability $10/21$, not $\rho_1(2) = 4/7$. The case $r = 1$ is separate: $\rho_1$ is not defined there, and on every finite graph on which fixation or extinction occurs almost surely the neutral fixation probability of a randomly placed mutant is $1/N$, isothermal or not, because the fixation probabilities of the $N$ initial lineages sum to $1$. Graphs that are not strongly connected are a separate case, outside the equivalence: call a vertex a root if no other vertex can replace it. With two or more roots a single mutant can never occupy every root, so its fixation probability is $0$; a single root is not enough for the value $1/N$. That claim needs the root to be a global source, a vertex from which every other vertex can be reached along positive-weight edges (Lieberman, Hauert and Nowak 2005, p. 314, where the type of the upstream root determines the fate of the entire graph). On such a graph the source is the only root, it is never replaced, and its type eventually spreads to every vertex, so fixation occurs exactly when the mutant starts there and its probability is $1/N$ for every $r$. Without reachability the claim fails: take four vertices with outgoing neighbours $a \to d$, $b \to c$, $c \to b, d$ (weights of $c$ split equally) and $d \to d$; the underlying graph is connected and $a$ is the only root, but a mutant starting at $a$ never enters $\{b, c\}$ and one starting elsewhere never replaces $a$, so fixation is impossible. The paper also states that the star amplifies selection; the asymptotic fixation probability of the star is a separate target in the Expansion. Later work examines the limits of the isothermal theorem: Kaveh, Komarova and Kohandel (2015) note that it is proved for birth–death updating with mutants differing in birth rate, show that for death–birth updating it holds only for mutants that differ in death rate, and show that for more general birth and death rates fixation probabilities on such graphs differ from those of the well-mixed population.

## Expansion

1. The model: the birth–death process on a weighted graph and the [Moran fixation probability](../../../Biology/population-genetics/moran-model/moran-fixation.md).
2. The neutral case $r = 1$, with value $1/N$ on every graph on which fixation or extinction is certain.
3. The isothermal theorem in $\rho$-equivalence form for irreducible $W$ and $r \ne 1$ (fixation probability $\rho_{|X|}(r)$ from every nonempty initial set $X$ of mutants if and only if $W$ is doubly stochastic), its single-mutant consequence, and the counterexample of Galanis et al. to the converse of that consequence.
4. Graphs that are not strongly connected: zero fixation probability with two or more roots, fixation probability $1/N$ for every $r$ when there is a global source from which every vertex is reachable along positive-weight edges; and separate targets for the star and super-star amplifiers: for the star with $N$ leaves and $r > 1$, the source's approximation $\rho_2 = (1 - 1/r^2)/(1 - 1/r^{2N})$ for large $N$, stated as the limit $1 - 1/r^2$ of the fixation probability as $N \to \infty$, against $1 - 1/r$ for $\rho_1$.
5. Death–birth updating and the limits of the isothermal theorem; relation to [the minimum fixation probability on graphs](../../../Biology/population-genetics/moran-model/minimum-fixation-probability-on-graphs.md).
