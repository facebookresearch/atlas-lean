# Law of a continuous modification on the space of continuous paths, measurable in the initial point

Let $E$ be a compact metric space and $(K_t)_{t \ge 0}$ a family of Markov kernels on $E$ satisfying the Chapman–Kolmogorov equations, with path measures $P_x$ on $E^{[0,\infty)}$ (product $\sigma$-field) whose finite-dimensional laws are those of the Markov chain with these transitions started at $x$. Suppose that for every $x \in E$ there is a process $Y^x$ with continuous paths on some probability space such that $Y^x_t$ has, jointly at every finite set of fixed times, the same law as the coordinates under $P_x$. Equip $C([0, \infty), E)$ with the topology of locally uniform convergence; its Borel $\sigma$-field equals the $\sigma$-field generated by the coordinate maps. Then:

1. the law $Q_x$ of $Y^x$ on $C([0, \infty), E)$ is a probability measure determined by the finite-dimensional laws of $P_x$, and so does not depend on the choice of the continuous modification;
2. $x \mapsto Q_x(A)$ is measurable for every Borel set $A \subseteq C([0, \infty), E)$, so $(Q_x)_{x \in E}$ is a Markov kernel from $E$ to $C([0, \infty), E)$.

## Expansion

A full proof page must still establish:

- the equality of the Borel and cylinder $\sigma$-fields on $C([0, \infty), E)$ for separable metric $E$;
- uniqueness of $Q_x$ from its finite-dimensional laws, and measurability in $x$ on cylinder sets, extended by a $\pi$–$\lambda$ argument;
- the neutral case: $Q_x$ is the law of the [continuous modified Wright–Fisher process](modified-process.md) started at $x$.
