# The infinite-level lookdown process on a finite type space

Let $E$ be a nonempty finite discrete space and let $\mu_0$ be a possibly random probability law on $E$. Conditional on $\mu_0$, take iid initial types $X_j(0)$, $j\ge1$. Independently of the initial data, take a countable independent family of rate-one reproduction clocks $L_{ij}$, $1\le i<j$, and mutation Poisson measures $M_j$ of intensity $\frac\theta2\,du\,dt$, with $\theta\ge0$ and measurable $h:E\times[0,1]\to E$. Every level uses the same mutation map. Give $E^{\mathbb N}$ the product metric

$$d(x,y)=\sum_{j\ge1}2^{-j}\mathbf1_{\{x_j\ne y_j\}}.$$

There is a unique pathwise solution of the shifting lookdown event equations: at an atom $(u,t)$ of $M_j$, set $X_j(t)=h(X_j(t-),u)$; at a reproduction arrow $i\to k$, copy the pre-event type at $i$ into level $k$ and shift every higher pre-event type up one level. All updates at an event use pre-event values. Its paths are càdlàg for this product metric, and every finite prefix is exactly the corresponding [finite-level shifting lookdown process](finite-level-lookdown-process-and-projective-con.md), on the same drivers. Each fixed-time infinite sequence is exchangeable.

## Construction and path proof

For the first $N$ levels only the clocks with target at most $N$ matter. Their combined rate is $\binom N2+N\theta/2$, so on each bounded time interval there are finitely many such events almost surely. Intersect these probability-one events over integer $N$ and integer horizons, also excluding ties between distinct driving clocks. This is one countable common event. On it the finite-prefix construction and restriction consistency hold simultaneously for all prefixes and all times.

Equivalently construct level $j$ after the lower levels. Its direct update clocks have total rate $\binom j2+\theta/2$; at an arrow targeted below $j$ it reads $X_{j-1}(t-)$, and at an arrow targeted at $j$ it reads the parent's pre-event type. Both constructions give the same finite prefixes. There is no claim that all clocks in the infinite system can be listed in a single locally finite chronological sequence.

For right continuity, choose a prefix so that the metric tail is smaller than a prescribed tolerance and use right continuity of its finitely many coordinates. For a left limit, take the coordinatewise left limits and use the same tail estimate. Thus the assembled path is càdlàg in the product topology, despite infinitely many clocks in the whole system. Agreement of all finite prefixes also gives pathwise uniqueness and determines the path law: the Borel sigma-algebra of the càdlàg space over this compact metric state space is generated by coordinate evaluations. On a fixed finite horizon, a dense countable evaluating set must include the terminal time.

Conditional-iid initial types are exchangeable. For any finite coordinate permutation, choose a prefix containing its support and apply the finite-level fixed-time exchangeability theorem. This proves infinite-sequence exchangeability at each fixed time. The construction itself also works for arbitrary initial types; the initial symmetry assumption is used only for exchangeability and subsequent sampling statements.

## Scope

The clock construction replaces an abstract Kolmogorov extension of finite path laws; it does not assume a pre-existing infinite solution. A proof page must still supply the countable product of noise sources, the measurable assembly of paths and the sigma-algebra on path space. The fixed-time exchangeability clause is shared with the finite-prefix theorem and the lookdown overview, not a new general de Finetti result. Directing-measure path regularity is a separate statement. No claim is made for an unspecified measurable or general infinite type space.
