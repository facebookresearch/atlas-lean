# Wright–Fisher construction of the two-parameter Poisson–Dirichlet diffusion

**Setting.** Fix $0 \le \alpha < 1$ and $\theta > -\alpha$. On the closed Kingman simplex $\overline\nabla_\infty = \{z \in [0,1]^\infty : z_1 \ge z_2 \ge \cdots \ge 0,\ \sum_i z_i \le 1\}$, the **two-parameter Poisson–Dirichlet diffusion** of Petrov is the Feller process generated by the closure of

$$\mathcal B\varphi(z) = \frac12\sum_{i,j\ge1} z_i(\delta_{ij} - z_j)\,\frac{\partial^2\varphi}{\partial z_i\,\partial z_j} - \frac12\sum_{i\ge1}(\theta z_i + \alpha)\,\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial z_i},$$

on the algebra generated by $\varphi_m(z) = \sum_i z_i^m$; for example $\mathcal B\varphi_2 = 1 - \alpha - (1+\theta)\varphi_2$. It is reversible with respect to $\mathrm{PD}(\theta,\alpha)$, and $\alpha = 0$ gives the infinitely-many-neutral-alleles diffusion.

**Model.** A Wright–Fisher population of $N$ haploids with $K$ allele types and frequencies $z \in \Delta_K$ undergoes, each generation, (i) **state-dependent migration** $z_i^* = z_i + p_i(z)m(z) - z_i m_i(z)$ with $m_i(z) = \alpha r(z_i)/(2N)$, $m(z) = \sum_j z_j m_j(z)$, where $r$ is decreasing and $(p_1,\ldots,p_K)$ is a symmetric map into $\Delta_K$ giving larger immigrant shares to rarer alleles ($p_i > p_j$ when $z_i < z_j$), both $C^4$; (ii) uniform mutation with $u_{ij} = (\theta+\alpha)/(2N(K-1))$; (iii) multinomial resampling of $N$ offspring. Let $\rho_K$ sort a vector in decreasing order.

**Theorem.** Under the paper's conditions on $p$ and $r$:
1. (Theorem 4.2) With time sped up by $N$, the chain converges in law in $D_{\Delta_K}[0,\infty)$ to a $K$-allele diffusion $Z_K$ (Proposition 4.1).
2. (Theorem 5.6) If the sorted initial laws converge to $\nu$, then $\rho_K(Z_K(\cdot)) \Rightarrow Z(\cdot)$ in $C_{\overline\nabla_\infty}[0,\infty)$ as $K \to \infty$, where $Z$ is the two-parameter Poisson–Dirichlet diffusion started from $\nu$.
3. (Theorems 6.1–6.2) The stationary laws also converge: $\mu^N_K \Rightarrow \mu_K$ as $N \to \infty$, and $\mu_K \circ \rho_K^{-1} \Rightarrow \mathrm{PD}(\theta,\alpha)$ as $K \to \infty$.

## Expansion

The drift $-\frac12(\theta z_i + \alpha)$ is split as $-\frac12(\theta+\alpha)z_i - \frac12\alpha(1 - z_i)$: the first part is uniform mutation at total rate $\theta + \alpha$, exactly as in the Ethier–Kurtz construction of the one-parameter model, and the second is the large-$K$ limit of the migration drift. Migration removes individuals at a rate decreasing in their allele's frequency and redistributes them toward rare alleles, so $\alpha$ balances reinforcement of common types against redistribution. The $K \to \infty$ limit is delicate because the generators do not converge on a core; the proof first shows, from the a priori estimate on $\mathcal B_K(\varphi_2 - \varphi_m)$ (Lemma 5.2), that limit points lose no mass, $\mathbb E\int_0^\infty (1 - \sum_i Z_i(t))\,dt = 0$ (Lemma 5.3), and then controls generator differences on $\varphi_m$ with $m > 2$ (Lemma 5.4). For $\alpha = 0$ the stationary law reduces to Kingman's $\mathrm{PD}(\theta)$ of [ranked Fleming–Viot frequencies](../../ranked-frequencies-are-poisson-dirichlet.md).
