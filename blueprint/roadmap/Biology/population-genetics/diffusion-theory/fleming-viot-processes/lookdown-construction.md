# The lookdown construction

In the lookdown (ordered) Moran model with $N$ levels, on the time scale of Birkner's Sec. 1.5, for each pair of levels $i < j$ at rate $1$ the individual at level $j$ is replaced by a copy of the type at level $i$, the types at levels $j, \dots, N - 1$ move up one level and the type at level $N$ is dropped, and each level mutates according to the mutation operator $B$ (Birkner, generator (1.27)). If the initial types are exchangeable, the empirical process of the ordered model has the same law as that of the unordered Moran model, and at each fixed time the types are exchangeable (Lemma 1.5.1). As $N \to \infty$ the empirical processes of the ordered systems converge almost surely on path space to a process with continuous paths, the Fleming–Viot process with mutation operator $B$ (Theorem 1.5.5), which solves the well-posed martingale problem of the Fleming–Viot generator (Theorem 1.5.8). In the $\Lambda$-case, at each point $(p_i, t_i)$ of a Poisson process with intensity $p^{-2}\Lambda(dp) \otimes dt$ a proportion $p_i$ of the levels is selected by independent coin tosses and all selected levels take the type of the lowest selected level (Berestycki 2009, Sec. 3.3.1). The target is the particle system, its consistency in $N$, the almost-sure limit of the empirical measures, and the identification of the genealogy of the first $n$ levels as a Kingman (or $\Lambda$-) coalescent.

## Expansion

1. The ordered particle system with $N$ levels, its Poisson construction, and its consistency in $N$.
2. Exchangeability of the types at each time, and equality in law of the ordered and unordered empirical processes.
3. The almost-sure limit of the empirical measures and continuity of its paths.
4. The martingale problem satisfied by the limit.
5. The genealogy of the first $n$ levels as a Kingman coalescent, and the $\Lambda$-lookdown with $\Lambda$-coalescent genealogies, as separate statements.
