# The lookdown construction: almost-sure path convergence to the Fleming–Viot process

The finite shifting lookdown system, its consistency in the number of levels and its fixed-time exchangeability are stated in [the finite-level lookdown process](finite-level-lookdown-process-and-projective-con.md); the Kingman genealogy of its first $n$ levels is [the finite-level lookdown chain](../../../coalescent-theory/coalescents/finite-level-lookdown-chain-and-its-embedded-lam.md); the infinite system and its continuous directing process on a finite type space are [the infinite-level lookdown process](infinite-level-lookdown-process-finite-type-spac.md) and [the common directing process](common-directing-process-of-the-infinite-lookdow.md); and the $\Lambda$-lookdown with its directing process is the programme [infinite lookdown, de Finetti limit and the Λ-Fleming–Viot process](../../../coalescent-theory/coalescents/infinite-lookdown-de-finetti-limit-and-lambda-fl.md). This page states the remaining result, the almost-sure convergence of the first levels of Donnelly and Kurtz (1999, Lemma 3.5, in the setting of Theorem 3.2, with the Fleming–Viot case of Sec. 6.2), whose assumptions differ from those pages in three ways.

1. Type space. $E$ is a complete separable metric space, possibly infinite or uncountable (Donnelly and Kurtz 1999, Sec. 1); for example $E = [0, 1]$.
2. Mutation. Every level mutates according to $B\phi(x) = \frac\theta2\int_0^1 (\phi(h(x, u)) - \phi(x))\,du$ with $\theta \ge 0$ and $h : E \times [0, 1] \to E$ measurable, so that mutation events occur at the bounded rate $\theta/2$ per level; the infinite-alleles model $h(x, u) = u$ on $[0, 1]$ is included.
3. Convergence. The finite systems are the first $N$ levels of one infinite ordered system, driven by rate-one reproduction clocks $L_{ij}$ for $i < j$ (the type at level $i$ is inserted at level $j$ and the higher types shift up one level) and by the mutation Poisson measures, with an exchangeable initial sequence $(\tilde\eta_0(i))_{i \ge 1}$ independent of the drivers (Donnelly and Kurtz 1999, Secs. 1.2 and 3). The almost-sure convergence is that of these coupled empirical processes (Donnelly and Kurtz 1999, Lemma 3.5, pp. 187–188). Theorem 3.2 of the source is a different statement: convergence in distribution, (3.21), of separately constructed finite ordered systems (their Model II) that share only their exchangeable initial levels.

Under these assumptions let $\tilde Z^{(N)}_t = N^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^N \delta_{\tilde\eta_t(i)}$, and at each fixed $t$ let $Z_t$ be the almost-sure limit $\lim_{m \to \infty} m^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^m \delta_{\tilde\eta_t(i)}$ given by de Finetti's theorem (Donnelly and Kurtz 1999, the definition in the display above Proposition 3.1; Proposition 3.1 gives the exchangeability of the levels at each fixed $t$). Then for each $f$ in the domain $\mathcal D(B)$ of $B$, almost surely $\langle f, \tilde Z^{(N)}_t\rangle \to \langle f, Z_t\rangle$ as $N \to \infty$, uniformly on compact time intervals (Donnelly and Kurtz 1999, Lemma 3.5 and (3.27)). Applied to a countable separating set $D \subset \mathcal D(B)$, this gives almost-sure convergence of $\tilde Z^{(N)}$ to $Z$, uniformly on compact time intervals, in the source's metric $\rho_B(\mu, \nu) = \sum_k 2^{-k}|\langle f_k, \mu\rangle - \langle f_k, \nu\rangle|$ on the space $\mathcal M_1(E)$ of probability measures, (1.1), with $D = \{f_k\}$. The limit $Z$ has paths continuous in $\rho_B$ (Theorem 3.2) and is the Fleming–Viot process with mutation operator $B$ (Sec. 6.2). The metric $\rho_B$ induces the weak topology when $D$ is convergence-determining, for example when $E$ is locally compact and $\mathcal D(B)$ is dense in $\hat C(E)$ (pp. 169–170; Remark 3.3(b)), as for $E = [0, 1]$; in general the conclusions hold in $\rho_B$.

## Expansion

1. The infinite ordered system on a complete separable metric type space $E$, as a system of Poisson-driven equations, and the identification of its first $N$ levels with [the finite-level lookdown process](finite-level-lookdown-process-and-projective-con.md); the finite-type case is [the infinite-level lookdown process](infinite-level-lookdown-process-finite-type-spac.md).
2. The fixed-time de Finetti limit $Z_t$ (Donnelly and Kurtz 1999, Proposition 3.1), from the fixed-time exchangeability of the levels.
3. The exponential concentration bound for averages of bounded exchangeable variables (Donnelly and Kurtz 1999, Lemma A.2).
4. Almost-sure convergence of $\langle f, \tilde Z^{(N)}_t\rangle$ for each $f \in \mathcal D(B)$, uniformly on compact time intervals (Donnelly and Kurtz 1999, Lemma 3.5, pp. 187–188), and continuity of the limit paths in $\rho_B$ (Theorem 3.2); the finite-type case is [the common directing process](common-directing-process-of-the-infinite-lookdow.md).
5. Separate targets: the convergence in distribution (3.21) of separately constructed finite ordered systems (Donnelly and Kurtz 1999, Theorem 3.2); weak convergence of the unordered Moran empirical processes when $Z^{(N)}_0 \to Z_0$ weakly; the martingale problem of the limit (Donnelly and Kurtz 1999, Sec. 4); the Kingman genealogy of the levels, already [the finite-level lookdown chain](../../../coalescent-theory/coalescents/finite-level-lookdown-chain-and-its-embedded-lam.md); and the $\Lambda$-lookdown of Berestycki, Sec. 3.3.1, already the programme [infinite lookdown, de Finetti limit and the Λ-Fleming–Viot process](../../../coalescent-theory/coalescents/infinite-lookdown-de-finetti-limit-and-lambda-fl.md).
