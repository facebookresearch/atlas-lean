# A common continuous directing process for the finite-type lookdown

Let $E$ be nonempty and finite, with $d$ types, and let $X$ be the [infinite shifting lookdown process](infinite-level-lookdown-process-finite-type-spac.md), started conditionally iid given a random law $\mu_0$. Use its common independent drivers, with mutation intensity $\theta/2$ per level. Define the empirical vector in the simplex by $Z_N^a(t)=N^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^N\mathbf1_{\{X_j(t)=a\}}$. There is a continuous simplex-valued process $Z$, with $Z(0)=\mu_0$ almost surely, such that on one probability-one event

$$\lim_{N\to\infty}\sup_{0\le t\le T}\|Z_N(t)-Z(t)\|_\infty=0\qquad\text{for every finite }T.$$

In particular convergence holds in probability uniformly on compact intervals and almost surely in the usual Skorokhod path topology. For each fixed time $t$, the level types are conditionally iid with directing law $Z(t)$; for every finite $m$ and $f:E^m\to\mathbb R$,

$$\mathbb E[f(X_1(t),\ldots,X_m(t))\mid Z(t)]=\langle f,Z(t)^{\otimes m}\rangle.$$

This fixed-time assertion does not, by itself, assert a conditional law given the entire past of $Z$.

## Fixed-time sampling and concentration

At a fixed time the level sequence is exchangeable. For each type $a$, its empirical indicators form a bounded reverse martingale for the decreasing sigma-algebras generated by the unordered first-$N$ sample and the levels above $N$. Indeed the empirical average is the conditional expectation of the first indicator given that sigma-algebra. The reverse-martingale convergence theorem gives almost-sure convergence; intersecting over the finitely many types gives a simplex-valued limit.

This also gives the finite-alphabet sampling statement without treating a binary theorem as a general-type theorem. The limit is invariant under finite coordinate permutations. Multiply $f(X_1,\ldots,X_m)$ by a bounded continuous function of that limit and average over ordered distinct $m$-tuples among the first $N\ge m$ levels. Exchangeability preserves the expectation. Replacing this without-replacement average by the with-replacement average $\langle f,Z_N^{\otimes m}\rangle$ changes it by at most $m(m-1)\|f\|_\infty/N$, by the collision union bound. Dominated convergence and a monotone-class argument prove the displayed conditional identity.

Conditional Hoeffding bounds for the resulting iid indicators imply, for $M\le N$ and $\varepsilon>0$,

$$\mathbb P\bigl(|Z_N^a(t)-Z_M^a(t)|>\varepsilon\bigr)\le4\exp(-M\varepsilon^2/2).$$

It suffices to apply the bound to each average's deviation from the directing mass, at threshold $\varepsilon/2$. No independence between the two averages is asserted.

## One null set and uniform convergence

Fix an integer horizon $T\ge1$. For dyadic $M=2^r$ use a grid of spacing $\delta_M=M^{-4}$ and put $\varepsilon_M=M^{-1/4}$. Union-bounding the fixed-time estimate over types, grid points and all $M\le N\le2M$ gives a grid-comparison failure probability at most

$$4d(M+1)(TM^4+1)\exp(-\sqrt M/2).$$

All these prefixes use only drivers below level $2M$, whose total rate is $\binom{2M}{2}+M\theta$. The number $J$ of their driving events in one grid cell is Poisson with mean at most $\lambda_M=2M^{-2}+\theta M^{-3}$. Each event changes each prefix's empirical coordinate by at most $1/M$: insertion changes its empirical measure by the inserted type minus the dropped last type, divided by its size. Mutation changes only one type. Thus the oscillation in a cell is at most $J/M$. Exponential Markov inequality at parameter one and a union bound give the additional failure bound

$$(TM^4+1)\exp\bigl((e-1)\lambda_M-M^{3/4}\bigr).$$

Both bounds are summable over dyadic $M$. On the complementary eventual event, every prefix $M\le N\le2M$ is within $3\varepsilon_M$ of $Z_M$ uniformly on $[0,T]$, by comparing through the preceding grid point. Since $\sum_r\varepsilon_{2^r}<\infty$, the dyadic empirical paths are uniformly Cauchy, and the same block bound brings in every integer $N$. Intersect over integer horizons. This constructs one common limit, rather than intersecting uncountably many fixed-time probability-one events.

The uniform limit is càdlàg. Each empirical coordinate has jumps of size at most $1/N$ on the common no-tie event, so the limit has no jumps and is continuous. Its fixed-time values agree with the reverse-martingale limits. Rational-time convergence alone would not establish this conclusion; the oscillation and block estimates are essential.

## Unordered Moran corollary

The empirical path law of the unordered model equals that of each ordered prefix. Hence the corresponding unordered empirical processes converge weakly to $Z$. More generally, if their initial empirical laws on the finite simplex converge weakly to the law of $\mu_0$, the same conclusion holds, with future clocks independent of the initial state.

For this extension, couple the initial empirical vectors to converge almost surely to $\mu_0$, and compare with an iid-$\mu_0$ initial population. Relabel to minimize the mismatched fraction $D_N(0)$, which is the total-variation distance of the initial empirical measures and tends to zero in mean. Couple both unordered populations with the same reproduction arrows and mutation marks. Their mismatched fraction is a bounded nonnegative supermartingale: neutral reproduction has zero total drift, and mutation cannot create a mismatch at a matched site. The maximal inequality gives

$$\mathbb P\bigl(\sup_{t\le T}D_N(t)>\varepsilon\bigr)\le\mathbb E[D_N(0)]/\varepsilon.$$

Empirical total variation is bounded by the mismatched fraction. This proves the extension by coupling and the already-established limit, without assuming martingale-problem uniqueness.

## Scope

This is a finite-type argument. A proof page must still supply the reverse-martingale step, the conditional sampling estimate, the concentration bound, the common null set and the path-space measurability. The statement does not use a de Finetti theorem for general type spaces, and a centered exchangeable martingale average is not assumed to concentrate around zero merely because it is centered. Existence and uniqueness for the martingale problem are separate questions. The fixed-time sampling clause is shared with the finite-level theorem; the lookdown overview covers the path-limit scope.
