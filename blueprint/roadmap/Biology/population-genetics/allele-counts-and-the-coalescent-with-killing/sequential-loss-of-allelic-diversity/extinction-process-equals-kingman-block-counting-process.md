# Extinction process of alleles equals the Kingman block-counting process

**Setting.** Consider a Moran population of constant size $N$ in which every individual carries a distinct allele at time $0$. Every unordered pair $\{a,b\}$ of individuals has an independent rate-$1$ Poisson clock; when it rings, one of the two, chosen uniformly, gives birth to an offspring of its allele and the other dies (equivalently, every ordered pair $(a,b)$ has a rate-$1/2$ clock at which $a$ reproduces and $b$ dies). Define the **extinction process** and its jump times

$$\hat A^N_t = \#\{\text{alleles present at time } t\}, \qquad \hat T^{N,k} = \sup\{t > 0 : \hat A^N_t \ge k\}, \quad k \in \{2,\dots,N\},$$

so $\hat T^{N,2}$ is the fixation time. For the $N$-Kingman coalescent let $A^N_t$ be the number of ancestral lineages (blocks) at time $t$ and $T^{N,k} = \sup\{t > 0 : A^N_t \ge k\}$; $A^N$ is the pure-death process started at $N$ with rate $k(k-1)/2$ from $k$ to $k-1$.

**Theorem.** For every $N \in \mathbb{N}$, the forward-time extinction process $(\hat A^N_t)_{t\ge 0}$ and the backward-time block-counting process $(A^N_t)_{t\ge 0}$ are identical in law. In particular the loss of allelic diversity is a pure-death process with rates $k(k-1)/2$, and $\hat T^{N,k}$ has the law of $T^{N,k}$, a sum of independent exponentials with rates $j(j-1)/2$, $j = k, \dots, N$.

**Correction to the source's rate convention.** The preprint's Section 2.1 prints the Moran dynamics as: "Every ordered pair $(a,b)$ of individuals is equipped with an independent Poisson clock of rate $1$. When a clock associated to a pair $(a,b)$ rings, $a$ gives birth to a new individual inheriting her type and $b$ dies simultaneously." Under that convention each unordered pair $\{a,b\}$ undergoes a birth–death event at total rate $2$, so the extinction process runs twice as fast as the block-counting process, which the preprint itself describes (after its (2.4)) as "a pure-death process starting from $N$ with transition rate $k(k-1)/2$ from level $k$ to $k-1$". For $N=2$ the printed dynamics give a fixation time $\hat T^{2,2}\sim\mathrm{Exp}(2)$, while $T^{2,2}\sim\mathrm{Exp}(1)$, so the preprint's Theorem 2.1 ("the extinction process $\hat A$ and the block counting process $A$ are identical in law") is false as printed and true only after the time change $t\mapsto 2t$. This page therefore uses rate $1/2$ per ordered pair, that is rate $1$ per unordered pair with the parent chosen uniformly. That is also what the preprint's own Section 3.1 gives for $\Lambda=\delta_0$: each $k$-tuple rings at rate $\lambda^N(k)=\int_0^1 x^{k-2}(1-x)^{N-k}\,\Lambda(dx)$, which is $1$ for $k=2$ and $0$ for $k\ge3$, the parent is "picked uniformly at random", and the preprint states that this case "corresponds to the classical Moran model presented above". With this rate Theorem 2.1 holds as stated above.

**Extension to $\Lambda$-Fleming–Viot populations.** For the $N$-particle $\Lambda$-Fleming–Viot process and its genealogy, the $\Lambda$-coalescent, the one-dimensional marginals agree: for every fixed $t > 0$, $A^N_t$ and $\hat A^N_t$ have the same law, and for every fixed $k$, $T^{N,k}$ and $\hat T^{N,k}$ have the same law. Equality of the whole processes fails in general (it does hold in degenerate cases such as $N=2$ or the star coalescent $\Lambda=\delta_1$, where both processes jump from $N$ to $1$ at rate $1$): if $\Lambda\neq\Lambda(\{0\})\delta_0$ and $\psi$ is regularly varying at $\infty$ with index in $(1,2]$, the limiting block-counting process $A^\infty$ has multiple jumps almost surely, while the limiting extinction process $\hat A^\infty$ has only unit jumps (Labbé), so the two differ in law.

## Expansion

In the Moran model, conditionally on the current allele count $k$ and the allele sizes, the next event that removes an allele is a birth–death pair in which the dying individual is the last carrier of its allele. A martingale or exchangeability argument shows that, whatever the allele sizes, the rate at which the number of alleles drops is the same function of $k$ as the merger rate $k(k-1)/2$ of $k$ Kingman lineages after averaging over the uniform (Dirichlet) allele-size configuration, and this configuration is preserved at jump times. The block-counting process is classical: each of the $\binom{k}{2}$ pairs of lineages merges at rate $1$. For multiple-merger $\Lambda$-coalescents the allele-size configuration at extinction times is no longer independent of the past in the same way, and in general only fixed-time and fixed-level marginals coincide.
