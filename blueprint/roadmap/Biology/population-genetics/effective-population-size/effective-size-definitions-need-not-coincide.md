# Different effective population sizes need not coincide

Besides the inbreeding, variance and eigenvalue effective sizes, which compare one-generation changes of identity, of allele-frequency variance and of the asymptotic decay of non-identity with a Wright–Fisher population and differ, for example, in a population whose size changes between generations, two further effective sizes are in use. The coalescent effective size is estimated from within-species diversity divided by the mutation rate and reflects the cumulative effects of genetic drift, background selection and genetic hitchhiking over long periods; with selection at many loci and abundant linkage disequilibrium it may not reflect the census size at all, or may reflect its logarithm (Wikipedia, *Effective population size*). The selection effective size $1/s_{critical}$ is defined through the critical selection coefficient at which selection becomes more important than drift (same source). These sizes need not agree with each other or with the census size. In Wright's island model with genic selection, Cherry and Wakeley (2003, abstract) relate the subdivided population to a panmictic Wright–Fisher population whose size is larger than the actual population and whose selection coefficient is smaller than the actual one, with the product unchanged, so that the fixation probability is unaffected by subdivision although the effective size increases; a single effective size therefore does not determine the fixation probability without the matching effective selection coefficient. Whitlock (2003, abstract) finds that in subdivided populations the fixation probability of a new beneficial allele is approximately $2hs(1 - F_{ST})N_e/N_{tot}$, which depends on a weighted $F_{ST}$ as well as on the effective size.

## Expansion

1. Definitions of the coalescent effective size through the pair coalescence time scale, and of a fixation effective size through Kimura's formula.
2. The island model with genic selection: effective size and effective selection coefficient with unchanged product.
3. Subdivided populations with dominance: the dependence of fixation on $F_{ST}$ beyond the effective size.
4. Offspring distributions with heavy tails, under which the genealogy does not converge to Kingman's coalescent and no coalescent effective size exists in the usual sense, as a separate statement.
