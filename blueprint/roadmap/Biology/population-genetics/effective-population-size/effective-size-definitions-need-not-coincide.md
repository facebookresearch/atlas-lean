# Effective sizes from diversity and selection need not match census size or determine fixation

Besides the [inbreeding, variance and eigenvalue effective sizes](effective-population-size-definitions.md), which [differ when population size changes](variance-versus-inbreeding-effective-size-can-di.md), effective sizes are also read off from diversity and from the efficacy of selection, and these need not track the census size. Under genetic draft, the stochastic force induced by substitutions at one locus on a closely linked locus, Gillespie (2001, abstract) finds that the substitution rates of weakly selected mutations depend only weakly on population size, in the opposite direction to drift. In a facultatively outcrossing population with rare outcrossing and recombination $r$, small compared with the scale $\sigma$ of fitness differences, Neher and Shraiman (2011, abstract) find that the fixation time of a neutral allele increases only slowly with the population size, and that alleles behave quasi-neutrally even for $Ns \gg 1$ provided $|s| < s_c$, where $s_c$ depends strongly on $r/\sigma$ but only weakly on $N$. These sizes need not agree with each other or with the census size. In Wright's island model with genic selection, Cherry and Wakeley (2003, abstract) relate the subdivided population to a panmictic Wright–Fisher population whose size is larger than the actual population and whose selection coefficient is smaller than the actual one, with the product unchanged, so that the fixation probability is unaffected by subdivision although the effective size increases; a single effective size therefore does not determine the fixation probability without the matching effective selection coefficient. Whitlock (2003, abstract) finds that in subdivided populations the fixation probability of a new beneficial allele is approximately $2hs(1 - F_{ST})N_e/N_{tot}$, which depends on a weighted $F_{ST}$ as well as on the effective size.

## Expansion

1. Definitions of the coalescent effective size through the pair coalescence time scale, and of a fixation effective size through Kimura's formula.
2. The island model with genic selection: effective size and effective selection coefficient with unchanged product.
3. Subdivided populations with dominance: the dependence of fixation on $F_{ST}$ beyond the effective size.
4. Offspring distributions with heavy tails, under which the genealogy does not converge to Kingman's coalescent and no coalescent effective size exists in the usual sense, as a separate statement.
