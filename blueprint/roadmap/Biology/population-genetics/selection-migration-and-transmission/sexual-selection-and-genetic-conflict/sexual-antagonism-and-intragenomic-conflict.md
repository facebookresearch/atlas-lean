# Sexual antagonism and intragenomic conflict

Sex-specific viability, parent-of-origin effects and biased transmission
are distinct mechanisms. The [two-sex boundary-invasion theorem](../../unequal-allele-frequencies-in-the-sexes-and-x-li/protected-polymorphism-under-sex-differential-vi.md)
specifies the first two in a deterministic autosomal model, including
ordered heterozygotes, Mendelian gametes and separately normalized fitnesses.
Its strict boundary protection test is not a theorem of unique interior
equilibrium or global stability.
[Meiotic drive without viability differences](../one-locus-selection-dynamics/meiotic-drive-without-viability-differences.md)
is a separate canonical model of transmission conflict, not viability selection.

## Expansion

The parent-of-origin linearization and both boundary tests are supplied by
the linked theorem. The residual is the number and stability of interior
equilibria with arbitrary dominance and the source-specific
mixed directional/overdominant criterion under its exact normalization.
Kidwell and coauthors describe the dominance pattern as an inference from
numerical calculations. Their abstract's average-heterozygote-fitness
threshold cannot be applied to unnormalized raw fitnesses.
Suppressor invasion and other competing-transmission mechanisms require
their own explicit models; the simple drive result does not establish them.
