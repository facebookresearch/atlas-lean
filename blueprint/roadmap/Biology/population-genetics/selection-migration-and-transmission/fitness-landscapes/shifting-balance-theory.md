# Shifting-balance theory

This page covers Wright's shifting-balance theory, a topic whose results depend on the model chosen. In a population subdivided into partly isolated demes on a fitness landscape, drift can carry a deme across a valley to a higher peak, selection then fixes that peak in the deme, and the deme spreads it to the others by migration. The theory as a whole is a historical hypothesis, not one proposition.

## Expansion

Define a structured finite-population model on a landscape, with its assumptions, and prove a particular transition, waiting-time or invasion result for it, each as a page of its own with a source. The broad historical theory is not to be stated as an unconditional proposition.

## Sources

- S. Wright, The roles of mutation, inbreeding, crossbreeding and selection in evolution, in *Proceedings of the Sixth International Congress of Genetics* 1 (1932), 356–366 (the shifting-balance theory).
- J. A. Coyne, N. H. Barton and M. Turelli, Perspective: a critique of Sewall Wright's shifting balance theory of evolution, *Evolution* 51 (1997), 643, <https://doi.org/10.2307/2411143> (the phases of the theory and their assumptions).
