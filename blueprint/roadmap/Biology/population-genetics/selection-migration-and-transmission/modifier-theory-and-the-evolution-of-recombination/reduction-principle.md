# Reduction principle for modifiers of transmission

This node covers the reduction principle for selectively neutral modifier genes. In randomly mating
populations at equilibrium, imperfect transmission of selected genes (through recombination, mutation,
migration and similar processes) allows a variance in their marginal fitnesses to be maintained, and
Altenberg and Feldman (1987) show that, within certain constraints, for modifier genes producing what
they call linear variation in the transmission processes, a new modifier allele can invade a population
at equilibrium if it reduces the level of imperfect transmission acting on the selected genes and is
expelled if it increases it; recombination and mutation modifiers are special cases.

## Expansion

A sound expansion transcribes from the paper the class of transmission operators, the equilibrium and
linear-variation hypotheses and the local stability notion used for invasion, and then states the
two-locus recombination-modifier case as a separate page.
