# Supergenes and inversions

An inversion can preserve a haplotype by suppressing recombination.
The [specified haploid island model](an-inversion-capturing-a-locally-favored-haploty.md)
compares a rare cost-free inversion with a resident equilibrium under
migration, selection and recombination. Its local multiplier depends on
recombination and post-selection linkage disequilibrium together; zero
recombination is neutral to first order in that model.

## Expansion

The existence of an equilibrium with positive post-selection linkage
disequilibrium is separate from the conditional invasion calculation.
Diploid heterokaryotype effects, more than two loci and subsequent
inversion dynamics require their own models. Kirkpatrick and Barton's
abstract reports spread without drift or epistasis, high frequency without
countervailing force, intermediate frequency with recessive load, cycling,
and establishment despite some underdominance. These are source-reported
results, not consequences all proved by the simplified multiplier identity.
A full expansion must retain their separate hypotheses and outcomes.
