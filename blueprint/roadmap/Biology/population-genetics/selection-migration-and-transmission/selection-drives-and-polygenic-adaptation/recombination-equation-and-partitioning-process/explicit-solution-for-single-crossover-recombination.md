# Explicit solution for single-crossover recombination

**Setting.** Consider the recombination equation on sites $S = \{1, \dots, n\}$ in linear order,

$$\dot\omega_t = \sum_{\mathcal{A}} \varrho(\mathcal{A}) \big(R_{\mathcal{A}}(\omega_t) - \omega_t\big),$$

with recombinators $R_{\mathcal{A}}(\omega) = \bigotimes_{A \in \mathcal{A}} \omega^A$, and its solution $\omega_t = \sum_{\mathcal{C}} a_t(\mathcal{C}) R_{\mathcal{C}}(\omega_0)$ with $a_t(\mathcal{C})$ the law of the partitioning process at time $t$ started from $\{S\}$. Under *single-crossover* recombination, $\varrho(\mathcal{A}) > 0$ only for the partitions

$$\mathcal{A}_k = \big\{\{1, \dots, k\}, \{k+1, \dots, n\}\big\}, \qquad 1 \le k \le n-1,$$

i.e. one crossover between sites $k$ and $k+1$. Interval partitions of $S$ correspond bijectively to sets $G \subseteq \{1, \dots, n-1\}$ of cut points; write $\varphi(\mathcal{C})$ for the cut set of an interval partition $\mathcal{C}$.

**Corollary.** Under single-crossover recombination, $a_t(\mathcal{C}) = 0$ unless $\mathcal{C}$ is an interval partition, and for an interval partition with cut set $G = \varphi(\mathcal{C})$,

$$a_t(\mathcal{C}) = \prod_{k \in G}\big(1 - e^{-t\varrho(\mathcal{A}_k)}\big) \prod_{\ell \in \{1,\dots,n-1\} \setminus G} e^{-t\varrho(\mathcal{A}_\ell)}.$$

Hence the cut points between neighbouring sites appear independently, the one between $k$ and $k+1$ at rate $\varrho(\mathcal{A}_k)$, and

$$\omega_t = \sum_{G \subseteq \{1,\dots,n-1\}} \prod_{k \in G}\big(1 - e^{-t\varrho(\mathcal{A}_k)}\big)\prod_{\ell \notin G} e^{-t\varrho(\mathcal{A}_\ell)}\; R_{\mathcal{S}(G)}(\omega_0),$$

where $\mathcal{S}(G)$ is the interval partition with cut set $G$.

## Expansion

A block $\{i, \dots, j\}$ of an interval partition can only be split by a crossover at a cut point $k$ with $i \le k < j$, and the marginal rate of that split is $\varrho(\mathcal{A}_k)$, independent of the block. So each potential cut point is an independent exponential clock with rate $\varrho(\mathcal{A}_k)$, and by time $t$ it has fired with probability $1 - e^{-t\varrho(\mathcal{A}_k)}$; the interval partition present at time $t$ is the one generated by the fired cut points. This recovers the classical closed-form solution of the single-crossover recombination dynamics.
