# Decay of linkage disequilibrium across a selective sweep

**Setting.** Consider the deterministic selection–recombination equation on three sites $S=\{i_\bullet,L,R\}=\{1,2,3\}$. Site $i_\bullet$ carries a beneficial allele with selective advantage $s>0$, and $L<R$ are linked neutral sites. Single crossovers occur at rates $\varrho_i$. Split the total crossover rate into

- $\varrho_{\rm s}$, the rate of crossovers that separate $L$ from $R$, and
- $\varrho_{\rm ns}$, the rate of crossovers that separate the intact block $\{L,R\}$ from $i_\bullet$.

If the selected site lies between the neutral ones ($i_\bullet=2$), then $\varrho_{\rm ns}=0$. The linkage disequilibrium between the neutral sites is

$$\mathrm{Cor}(\omega_t)=\omega_{\{L,R\},t}\bigl((1,1)\bigr)-\omega_{\{L\},t}(1)\,\omega_{\{R\},t}(1).$$

Let $\omega^{\rm ns}$ be the solution with $\varrho_{\rm s}$ set to $0$, and $\omega^{(0)}$ the solution under selection alone.

**Theorem.**

1. (Separating recombination acts multiplicatively.) For all $t\ge0$,

$$\mathrm{Cor}(\omega_t)=e^{-\varrho_{\rm s}t}\,\mathrm{Cor}(\omega^{\rm ns}_t).$$

2. (Limit without separating recombination.) If the beneficial type initially exists on a single background, so that $\omega^{(0)}_t$ converges to a point mass, then for $\varrho_{\rm ns}=0$ one has $\mathrm{Cor}(\omega^{\rm ns}_\infty)=0$. For $\varrho_{\rm ns}>0$,

$$\mathrm{Cor}(\omega^{\rm ns}_\infty)=\int_0^\infty\varrho_{\rm ns}e^{-\varrho_{\rm ns}\tau}\,\omega^{(0)}_{\{L,R\},\tau}\bigl((1,1)\bigr)\,d\tau-\Bigl(\int_0^\infty\varrho_{\rm ns}e^{-\varrho_{\rm ns}\tau}\,\omega^{(0)}_{\{L\},\tau}(1)\,d\tau\Bigr)\Bigl(\int_0^\infty\varrho_{\rm ns}e^{-\varrho_{\rm ns}\sigma}\,\omega^{(0)}_{\{R\},\sigma}(1)\,d\sigma\Bigr).$$

So the position of the selected site affects the disequilibrium only through $(\varrho_{\rm s},\varrho_{\rm ns})$. When $i_\bullet$ lies between the neutral sites, the disequilibrium generated by hitchhiking is eventually destroyed. When it lies outside, a positive limit can persist, and this limit depends non-monotonically on $\varrho_{\rm ns}$.

## Expansion

In the ancestral initiation graph the line ancestral to $\{L,R\}$ is hit at rate $\varrho_{\rm s}$ by splittings that separate $L$ from $R$. With probability $e^{-\varrho_{\rm s}t}$ no such splitting occurs, and the delivered pair has the law $\omega^{\rm ns}_t$. Otherwise the two alleles come from independent leaves and contribute no correlation. For the limit, the $\{L,R\}$ line hitchhikes with the selected site for an $\mathrm{Exp}(\varrho_{\rm ns})$ time and then detaches. Its type is therefore sampled from the pure-selection solution at that random time, which gives the integral formula. Because disequilibrium is a second-order quantity, the single-ancestry heuristic $\mathrm{Cor}(\omega^{(0)}_{1/\varrho_{\rm ns}})$ only approximates this limit.
