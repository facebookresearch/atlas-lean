# Diversity bounds from the most frequent allele

The frequency $M$ of the most common allele at a locus constrains every symmetric diversity or similarity statistic of the allele-frequency vector. Majorization makes this precise: among frequency vectors with largest entry $M$, the most concentrated one majorizes all others and the most even one is majorized by all others, so Schur-convex statistics (homozygosity and its $\alpha$-generalizations) and Schur-concave ones (Shannon and Rényi entropies) attain their extremes there.
