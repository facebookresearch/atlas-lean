# Clonal interference

This node covers competition between beneficial mutations that arise in different lineages of an asexual
population. In sexual populations such mutations can be recombined into one lineage; in asexual
populations the clones carrying them compete, which interferes with the progression of each mutation to
fixation. Gerrish and Lenski (1998) derive, for a model of this clonal interference, a fixation
probability for beneficial mutations, an expected substitution rate and an expected rate of fitness
increase; Desai and Fisher (2007) analyse the joint effect of multiple mutations and interference between
clones when beneficial mutations are common. Felsenstein (1974) argues that recombination has an
evolutionary advantage because it breaks down the random linkage disequilibrium that causes such
interference in finite populations.

## Expansion

A sound expansion separates exact finite statements about a fixed asexual Wright–Fisher model (for
example about which mutations can fix together without recombination) from the asymptotic results on
substitution rates, which need the precisely stated large-population regime of one source.
