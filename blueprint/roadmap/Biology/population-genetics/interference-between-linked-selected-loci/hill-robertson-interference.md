# Hill–Robertson interference

This node covers the interference between selection at linked loci in finite populations (the
Hill–Robertson effect). Hill and Robertson (1966) studied by computer simulation selection on two linked
loci with additive effects on a character in small populations and found that segregation at the second
locus can reduce the chance of fixation of the favoured allele at the first, the effect of linkage being
described by $Nc$ (effective size times crossover distance). Felsenstein (1974) interprets the effect as
random linkage disequilibrium generated by drift causing linked loci to interfere with each other's
response to selection, even without gene interaction, and McVean and Charlesworth (2000) study the
effect between weakly selected alleles.

## Expansion

A sound expansion defines a two-locus Wright–Fisher model with selection, recombination and random
sampling, proves the exact statements that hold without recombination, and states any dependence of
joint fixation probabilities on the recombination rate only as a conjecture unless a source proves it.
