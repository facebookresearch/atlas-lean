# Snyder's ratios for a recessive trait

Consider one autosomal locus with alleles $T$ and $t$ at frequencies $p$ and $q = 1 - p \in (0, 1)$, a recessive phenotype carried by $tt$, Hardy–Weinberg proportions $p^2 + 2pq$ dominant and $q^2$ recessive in the parents, and random mating with respect to the locus. Snyder (1932) observes that a dominant-phenotype parent is heterozygous with probability $2pq/(p^2 + 2pq)$, that the only recessive children of dominant $\times$ dominant matings are one quarter of the offspring of heterozygote $\times$ heterozygote matings, and that the only recessive children of dominant $\times$ recessive matings are one half of the offspring of heterozygote $\times$ recessive matings. Since $2pq/(p^2 + 2pq) = 2q/(1 + q)$ and the two parents' genotypes are independent under random mating, the expected recessive fraction is

$$S = \frac{2q}{1+q} \cdot \frac12 = \frac{q}{1+q}$$

among children of dominant $\times$ recessive matings and

$$R = \Bigl(\frac{2q}{1+q}\Bigr)^2 \cdot \frac14 = \Bigl(\frac{q}{1+q}\Bigr)^2$$

among children of dominant $\times$ dominant matings. Snyder used these ratios to test recessive inheritance of taste deficiency for phenylthiocarbamide in 800 families.

## Expansion

1. The model: one biallelic autosomal locus, Hardy–Weinberg proportions and random mating, Mendelian transmission, complete recessivity.
2. The conditional heterozygote probability of a dominant-phenotype parent.
3. The two segregation ratios, using independence of the parents' genotypes under random mating.
4. The comparison of observed and expected proportions as a test of recessive inheritance, with the probable errors of the source, as a separate statistical statement.
