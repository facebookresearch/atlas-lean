# Two waves of adaptation from dormancy in a seasonal environment

**Model.** A discrete-time population has $N$ active and $K_N$ dormant (seed-bank) individuals. The environment $R_N(m) \in \{1, -1\}$ is $1$ (summer) for $V_N$ generations and $-1$ (winter) for $U_N - V_N$ generations, periodically. Each individual has a type $k \in \mathbb{Z}$ with fitness $(1 + s_N)^{k R_N(m)}$ in generation $m$; initially all types are $0$. Each generation, $c_N$ dormant individuals become active and $c_N$ active ones become dormant, both chosen at random; the other $N - c_N$ active individuals choose parents from the previous active generation with probability proportional to fitness and inherit the parent's type, except that with probability $\mu_N$ each the type moves up or down by one. Assume $s_N \to s \in (0, \infty]$, $V_N / U_N \to \beta \in (0,1)$, $U_N \gg \log N$, $c_N \ll N$ and $N^b \mu_N \to \infty$ for some $b > 0$. Let $\omega(u)$ be the survival probability of a Galton–Watson process with Poisson$(1+u)$ offspring and $\rho_N = N\mu_N\,\omega(s_N)$, the per-generation probability of a mutation that will sweep.

**Regimes.** Regime 1: $c_N/(K_N\rho_N) \to \alpha \in (0,\infty)$ and $U_N \rho_N \to \theta \in (0, \infty)$, with limiting environment $R(t) = 1$ on $[j\theta, j\theta + \beta\theta)$ and $-1$ otherwise. Regime 2: $U_N \ll K_N / c_N \ll \rho_N^{-1}$ and $c_N^2 U_N^2 / (K_N \log N) \to \infty$. Let $\mathcal{N}^\pm$ be independent Poisson processes with intensities $\mathbf{1}\{R(t) = \pm 1\}$ (Regime 1), respectively $\beta$ and $1 - \beta$ (Regime 2); let $T_k$ ($k \ge 1$) be the jump times of $\mathcal{N}^+$ and $T_{-k}$ those of $\mathcal{N}^-$. Write $X_{k,N}(m) = A_{k,N}(m)/N$ and $Y_{k,N}(m) = D_{k,N}(m)/K_N$ for the fractions of type $k$ among the $N$ active and the $K_N$ dormant individuals, where $A_{k,N}(m)$ and $D_{k,N}(m)$ count the active and dormant individuals of type $k$ (the source's §1.4 prints $D_{k,N}/N$, a misprint: it calls $Y_{k,N}$ the proportion in the dormant population, takes $\mathbf{Y}_N$ simplex-valued with limit started at $\delta_0$, and uses $D_{k,N}/K_N$ in its proofs).

**Theorem.**
1. *(Sweep times.)* In both regimes, for $a \in (0,1)$, the first times $S_{k,N}(a)$ at which type $k$ exceeds fraction $a$ of the active population satisfy $(\rho_N S_{k,N}(a))_{|k| \le K} \Rightarrow (T_k)_{|k| \le K}$.
2. *(Active population, Regime 1.)* With $J(t) = \mathcal{N}^+(t)\mathbf{1}\{R(t) = 1\} - \mathcal{N}^-(t)\mathbf{1}\{R(t) = -1\}$ and $\mathbf{J}(t)$ the indicator vector of $J(t)$, the type composition $\mathbf{X}_N(\lfloor \rho_N^{-1} t \rfloor)$ converges to $\mathbf{J}(t)$ in the Meyer–Zheng topology: the summer-dominant type is the number of positive sweeps so far, and each winter the winter-dominant type returns from the seed bank.
3. *(Seed bank, Regime 1.)* $\mathbf{Y}_N(\lfloor \rho_N^{-1}t \rfloor)$ converges in Skorokhod $J_1$ to the solution of $\frac{d}{dt}\mathbf{Y}(t) = \alpha(\mathbf{J}(t) - \mathbf{Y}(t))$ with $\mathbf{Y}(0) = \delta_0$.
4. *(Seed bank, Regime 2.)* $\mathbf{Y}_N(\lfloor \rho_N^{-1}t \rfloor)$ converges in the Meyer–Zheng topology to the vector with $Y_k(t) = \beta\,\mathbf{1}\{T_k \le t < T_{k+1}\}$ for $k \ge 1$, $Y_k(t) = (1-\beta)\mathbf{1}\{T_k \le t < T_{k-1}\}$ for $k \le -1$, and $Y_0(t) = \beta\,\mathbf{1}\{t < T_1\} + (1-\beta)\mathbf{1}\{t < T_{-1}\}$.

## Expansion

Strong selection makes each beneficial mutation sweep in $O(\log N)$ generations, much shorter than a season, so sweeps arrive as a Poisson process run only in the season in which they are beneficial. Without dormancy, winter sweeps would push the summer type back down. With a seed bank, the type that won the last summer is stored and re-emerges at the start of the next summer, so the positive and negative lineages each accumulate their own sweeps: two separately adapting subpopulations, a mechanism for speciation. In Regime 2 the seed bank mixes faster than sweeps arrive but slower than seasons turn, so it holds the two current dominant types in proportions $\beta$ and $1 - \beta$.
