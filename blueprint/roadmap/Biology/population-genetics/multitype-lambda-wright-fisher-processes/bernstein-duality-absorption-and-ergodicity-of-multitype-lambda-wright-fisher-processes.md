# Bernstein duality, absorption and ergodicity of multitype Lambda-Wright-Fisher processes with polynomial drift

**Process.** Fix $d \ge 1$ and let $\Delta_d$ be the simplex of frequency vectors $\mathbf{x} = (x(1),\dots,x(d+1))$. For a finite measure $\Lambda$ on $[0,1]$, the $(d+1)$-type $\Lambda$-Wright–Fisher process with polynomial drift has generator $\mathcal{A} = \mathcal{A}_{\Lambda(0)} + \mathcal{A}_{\Lambda_>} + \mathcal{A}_{\mathbf{d}}$:
$$\begin{aligned} \mathcal{A}_{\Lambda(0)}f(\mathbf{x}) &= \frac{\Lambda(\{0\})}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^d x(i)\big(\mathbf{1}_{i=j} - x(j)\big)\partial_{ij}f(\mathbf{x}),\\ \mathcal{A}_{\Lambda_>}f(\mathbf{x}) &= \int_{(0,1]}\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}x(i)\big[f((1-r)\mathbf{x} + r\mathbf{e}_i) - f(\mathbf{x})\big]\frac{\Lambda(dr)}{r^2},\\ \mathcal{A}_{\mathbf{d}}f(\mathbf{x}) &= \sum_{i=1}^d \mathbf{d}(\mathbf{x};i)\,\partial_i f(\mathbf{x}), \qquad \mathbf{d}(\mathbf{x};i) = \sum_{m=1}^\kappa b(m)\sum_{|\boldsymbol\beta| = m}\Big(p(\boldsymbol\beta;i) - \frac{\beta(i)}{m}\Big)\binom{m}{\boldsymbol\beta}\mathbf{x}^{\boldsymbol\beta}, \end{aligned}$$
with $b(m) \ge 0$, probability vectors $p(\boldsymbol\beta;\cdot)$ on the $d+1$ types, and $p(\boldsymbol\beta;i) = 0$ when $\beta(i) = 0$ and $|\boldsymbol\beta| > 1$. The $m = 1$ term is mutation with matrix $P = (p(\mathbf{e}_i;j))$; write $\theta(j) = \min_i P_{ij}$ for its parent-independent part. Higher $m$ encode frequency-dependent selection. Let $\lambda_{n,k} = \int_{[0,1]} z^{k-2}(1-z)^{n-k}\Lambda(dz)$ be the $\Lambda$-coalescent merger rates.

**Theorem (Bernstein duality).** For every $\mathbf{x} \in \Delta_d$ the martingale problem for $\mathcal{A}$ on $C^2(\Delta_d)$ has a solution, and every solution satisfies
$$\mathbb{E}\Big[\sum_{|\boldsymbol\alpha| = \dim\mathbf{v}}v(\boldsymbol\alpha)\binom{\dim\mathbf{v}}{\boldsymbol\alpha}\mathbf{X}_t^{\boldsymbol\alpha}\Big] = \mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{v}}\Big[\sum_{|\boldsymbol\alpha| = L_t}V_t(\boldsymbol\alpha)\binom{L_t}{\boldsymbol\alpha}\mathbf{x}^{\boldsymbol\alpha}\Big],$$
where $(L, \mathbf{V})$ is an urn-type dual: $L$ is the number of balls, which jumps $n \to n-k+1$ at rate $\binom nk\lambda_{n,k}$ ($k \ge 3$), $n \to n-1$ at rate $n|\theta|b(1) + \binom n2\lambda_{n,2}$, and $n \to n+m-1$ at rate $nb(m)(1 - \mathbf{1}_{m=1}|\theta|)$, and $\mathbf{V}$ is the vector of Bernstein coefficients. Hence the martingale problem is well posed and $\mathbf{X}$ is Feller.

**Theorem (long-term behaviour).** Assume
$$\sum_{m=2}^\kappa(m-1)b(m) < \int_{[0,1]}|\log(1-z)|\frac{\Lambda(dz)}{z^2} + |\theta|\,b(1)$$
(e.g. whenever $\Lambda(\{0\}) > 0$), which makes $L$ positive recurrent.
1. (No mutation, $b(1) = 0$.) From any interior $\mathbf{x}$, $\mathbf{X}_t$ converges a.s. to a random vertex $\mathbf{e}_i$, each vertex having positive probability $\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{X}_\infty = \mathbf{e}_i) = \mathbb{E}[H(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{V}^{(i)}_\infty)] \in (0,1)$, where $H(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{v}) = \sum_{\boldsymbol\alpha}v(\boldsymbol\alpha)\binom{\dim\mathbf{v}}{\boldsymbol\alpha}\mathbf{x}^{\boldsymbol\alpha}$ and $\mathbf{V}^{(i)}_\infty$ is the limit of the dual coefficient process.
2. (Mutation.) If either $|\theta|b(1) > 0$ and $P$ is irreducible, or $b(1) > 0$, $|\theta| = 0$, $\kappa = 1$ and $P$ is irreducible and aperiodic, then no vertex is hit asymptotically and $\mathbf{X}$ has a unique stationary distribution $\mathcal{L}$, characterized by its mixed moments $\int \mathbf{x}^{\boldsymbol\alpha}\,\mathcal{L}(d\mathbf{x}) = \mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol\alpha}[U_\infty]$, where $U_\infty$ is the common limit of the dual Bernstein coefficients.

## Expansion

Polynomials in Bernstein form $\sum_{\boldsymbol\alpha} v(\boldsymbol\alpha)\binom{n}{\boldsymbol\alpha}\mathbf{x}^{\boldsymbol\alpha}$ are mapped by $\mathcal{A}$ into polynomials of the same form whose degree changes like the moves of $L$: $\Lambda$-mergers reduce the degree (coalescence of sampled lineages), parent-independent mutation removes a lineage, and the drift terms of order $m$ add $m-1$ lineages (branching of the ancestral selection graph). The coefficient vector evolves by averaging and recolouring moves, which is the urn interpretation. The recurrence condition balances lineage creation by selection against the logarithmic rate of loss by mergers. Without mutation the coefficients freeze along each vertex direction and the dual identifies fixation probabilities; with mutation the coefficients merge into a single random value, which gives the stationary moments.
