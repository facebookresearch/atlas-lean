# Truncation selection on a standard normal trait

For the truncation fitness $W = \mathbf{1}_{(0, \infty)}$ ($W(z) = 1$ for $z > 0$ and $W(z) = 0$
for $z \le 0$) and a standard normal trait $z \sim N(0, 1)$,

$$\frac{\int z \, W(z) \, dN(0, 1)(z)}{\int W(z) \, dN(0, 1)(z)} = \sqrt{2 / \pi}.$$

The left side is the [selection differential](selection-differential.md) of $W$ for $\mu = 0$ and
$v = 1$: the numerator is $1 / \sqrt{2 \pi}$ and the denominator, the
[mean fitness](mean-fitness.md), is $1/2$.
