# With no carriers the law of the SKAT statistic is the point mass at 0

If no individual carries a minor allele at any variant, `G = 0`, then for every `μ ∈ ℝⁿ`, every real
`n × n` matrix `V'`, and whatever `X`, `V` and `w`, the law of the [SKAT statistic](skat-q.md) under
`y ~ N(μ, V')` is the point mass at `0`. Here `N(μ, V')` is the Gaussian law with mean `μ` and
covariance `V'` when `V'` is positive semidefinite, and the point mass at `μ` otherwise. A test
based on `Q` then cannot tell any two such laws apart.
