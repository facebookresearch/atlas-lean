# Han–Eskin random-effects meta-analysis

In the setting of [fixed-effects meta-analysis as a likelihood-ratio test](fixed-effects-meta-analysis-as-a-likelihood-ratio-test.md), let the study estimates follow $X_i\sim\mathcal N(\mu,V_i+\tau^2)$, $\tau^2\ge0$, and test the null hypothesis of no association **and** no heterogeneity, $H_0:\mu=0,\ \tau^2=0$, against $(\mu,\tau^2)$ unrestricted with $\tau^2\ge0$. The likelihood-ratio statistic is

$$S_{\mathrm{New}}=-2\log\lambda=\sum_i\log\frac{V_i}{V_i+\hat\tau^2}+\sum_i\frac{X_i^2}{V_i}-\sum_i\frac{(X_i-\hat\mu)^2}{V_i+\hat\tau^2},$$

where $(\hat\mu,\hat\tau^2)$ maximize the likelihood of $X_i\sim\mathcal N(\mu,V_i+\tau^2)$ over $\mu\in\mathbb R$ and $\tau^2\ge0$, the maximizer $\hat\tau^2=0$ on the boundary included. They can be computed by the Hardy–Thompson iteration with its update truncated at zero,

$$\hat\mu^{(t+1)}=\frac{\sum_iX_i/(V_i+\hat\tau^{2(t)})}{\sum_i1/(V_i+\hat\tau^{2(t)})},\qquad \hat\tau^{2(t+1)}=\max\!\left(0,\ \frac{\sum_i\big[(X_i-\hat\mu^{(t+1)})^2-V_i\big]/(V_i+\hat\tau^{2(t)})^2}{\sum_i1/(V_i+\hat\tau^{2(t)})^2}\right),$$

started from a moment estimate such as the DerSimonian–Laird value; without the truncation the update can leave the parameter space (if all $X_i$ are equal and all $V_i=1$ it returns $-1$), whereas the constrained maximizer is then $\hat\tau^2=0$. When $\hat\tau^2=0$, $\hat\mu$ is the inverse-variance-weighted mean and $S_{\mathrm{New}}=Z_{FE}^2$.

Because $\mu$ is unrestricted and $\tau^2$ lies on the boundary $\tau^2=0$ under $H_0$, $S_{\mathrm{New}}$ is asymptotically, as the number of studies grows, an equal mixture of $\chi^2_1$ and $\chi^2_2$ (Self–Liang). For few studies this asymptotic $p$-value is overly conservative in the genome-wide tail, and $p$-values are taken from null tables generated by simulating $X_i\sim\mathcal N(0,V_i)$.

## Proof sketch

Under the null the likelihood has variances $V_i$; under the alternative it has $V_i+\tau^2$ and mean $\mu$. The log-ratio of the normal densities at the maximisers gives the three sums. Unlike the conventional random-effects test, the new statistic does not assume heterogeneity under the null, and in the source's simulations it gains power when effects are heterogeneous across studies (for example from LD differences between populations, which change the effect observed at a tag SNP), while remaining comparable to the fixed-effects test when they are homogeneous. In a reparametrisation in which the estimates of $\mu$ and $\tau^2$ are asymptotically orthogonal, $\mu$ contributes a $\chi^2_1$ and the boundary parameter $\tau^2$ a half-normal component, which gives the mixture limit.
