# FDR control of knockoff statistics reweighted by external data

**Setting.** In a target population (environment $e=0$) with genotypes $X^0\in\mathbb R^{n\times p}$ and phenotype $Y^0$, test the conditional hypotheses $H^0_j:\ Y^0\perp X^0_j\mid X^0_{-j}$, $j=1,\dots,p$. Model-X knockoffs $\tilde X^0$ are generated from $X^0$ without looking at $Y^0$, so that $\tilde X^0\perp Y^0\mid X^0$ and swapping any null column with its knockoff leaves the joint law invariant. Knockoff statistics $W_j=w_j(X,\tilde X,Y)$ have the *flip-sign property*: swapping $X_j$ and $\tilde X_j$ only changes the sign of $W_j$ (e.g. $W_j=|\hat b_j|-|\hat b_{j+p}|$ from a lasso fit on $[X,\tilde X]$; when the lasso minimizer is not unique, $\hat b$ must be chosen by a swap-equivariant rule, one that exchanges $\hat b_j$ and $\hat b_{j+p}$ when $X_j$ and $\tilde X_j$ are swapped). Signs use the convention $\operatorname{sign}0=0$. The knockoff filter at level $q$ rejects $\{j:W_j\ge T\}$ with

$$T=\min\Big\{|W_j|:\ W_j\ne0,\ \frac{1+\#\{i:W_i\le -|W_j|\}}{\#\{i:W_i\ge |W_j|\}\vee 1}\le q\Big\},\qquad T=+\infty\ \text{if no such } j,$$

the minimum being over the finitely many observed nonzero magnitudes (as in the linked knockoff threshold; a minimum over all real $t>0$ need not exist, e.g. for $W=(1,1)$ and $q=1/2$ every $t\in(0,1]$ qualifies). Write $\mathcal H_0$ for the set of target nulls and let $\varepsilon=(\varepsilon_1,\dots,\varepsilon_p)$ be independent of the data, with $\varepsilon_j=\pm1$ with probability $1/2$ each for $j\in\mathcal H_0$ and $\varepsilon_j=1$ otherwise. The filter controls the false discovery rate at $q$ whenever $W$ has the **null sign-flip property** $W\overset d=\varepsilon\odot W$ (coordinatewise product); equivalently, conditional on $(|W_1|,\dots,|W_p|)$ and on the non-null statistics, the signs of the **nonzero** null $W_j$ are i.i.d. fair coin flips. Null statistics equal to $0$ have sign $0$; they are never rejected and never counted as negatives. External data from environments $e=1,\dots,E$ (other ancestries or related outcomes) are independent of the target data.

**Proposition 2.1 (linear re-ordering).** Let $W^0$ be computed on the target data and $W^{\mathrm{ext}}$ on the pooled external data, and for a fixed $\theta\in[0,1]$ set

$$W^{\mathrm{lro}}_j=\operatorname{sign}\big(W^0_j\big)\,\Big((1-\theta)|W^0_j|+\theta|W^{\mathrm{ext}}_j|\Big),$$

so $\operatorname{sign}W^{\mathrm{lro}}_j=\operatorname{sign}W^0_j$ and $|W^{\mathrm{lro}}_j|=(1-\theta)|W^0_j|+\theta|W^{\mathrm{ext}}_j|$ whenever $W^0_j\ne0$, while **a zero target statistic stays zero**: $W^0_j=0$ gives $W^{\mathrm{lro}}_j=0$ whatever $W^{\mathrm{ext}}_j$ is. Then $W^{\mathrm{lro}}$ has the null sign-flip property, so the knockoff filter applied to $W^{\mathrm{lro}}$ controls the FDR in the target population.

**Proposition 2.2 (weighted lasso).** Let prior weights $\phi_1,\dots,\phi_{2p}>0$ depend only on external data or prior knowledge, with $\phi_j=\phi_{j+p}$. Fit on the target data the weighted lasso $\min_b\ \tfrac12\|Y^0-[X^0,\tilde X^0]b\|_2^2+\lambda\sum_{k=1}^{2p}\phi_k|b_k|$ and set $W^{\mathrm{wl}}_j=|\hat b_j|-|\hat b_{j+p}|$. Choose $\hat b$ by a swap-equivariant rule when the minimizer is not unique. Then $W^{\mathrm{wl}}$ has the null sign-flip property, and FDR is controlled. **Proposition 2.3:** the same holds when $\phi=\phi(Y^0,X^0,\tilde X^0,Y^{\mathrm{ext}},X^{\mathrm{ext}},\tilde X^{\mathrm{ext}})$ is computed on the pooled data from all environments including the target, with model-X knockoffs $\tilde X^e$ generated in every environment, provided

1. every variable null in the target is null in every environment, and
2. $\phi$ is invariant under the simultaneous knockoff swap: for every set $S$ of target-null variables, swapping $X^e_j$ with $\tilde X^e_j$ for all $j\in S$ in **every** environment $e=0,\dots,E$ leaves $\phi$ unchanged. This holds, for example, for $\phi_j=\phi_{j+p}=1/(0.05+|\hat b^{\mathrm{pool}}_j|+|\hat b^{\mathrm{pool}}_{j+p}|)$ when the pooled sparse fit on $[X,\tilde X]$ exchanges the coefficients of swapped columns.

**Departures from the source.** The source states each conclusion as "conditional on $|W|$, the signs of $W_j$ for all null $j$ corresponding to a true $H^0_j$ are i.i.d. coin flips", and defines the re-ordered statistic only through $\operatorname{sign}(W^{\mathrm{lro}}_j)=\operatorname{sign}(W^0_j)$ and $|W^{\mathrm{lro}}_j|=(1-\theta)|W^0_j|+\theta|W^{\mathrm{ext}}_j|$. Those two equations define no real number when $W^0_j=0$, $|W^{\mathrm{ext}}_j|>0$ and $0<\theta<1$, and a null statistic equal to $0$ (e.g. when a penalized fit sets every coefficient to $0$) has sign $0$, not a fair sign. Its threshold (3) is printed as $\min\{t:\ \ldots\le q\}$ over real $t$, with $\min\emptyset=\infty$; that minimum need not exist (the $W=(1,1)$ example above), so this page minimizes over the observed nonzero $|W_j|$, as in Barber and Candès's definition. This page also fixes the zero rule above and states the guarantees as the joint property $W\overset d=\varepsilon\odot W$, which is what the source's proofs of Propositions 2.2 and 2.3 (its Appendix A.2–A.3) establish and what the knockoff filter needs. The source's statement of Proposition 2.3 also lists only condition 1; its proof uses condition 2 ("$\phi$ is symmetric in $(X^0,X^{\mathrm{ext}})$ and $(\tilde X^0,\tilde X^{\mathrm{ext}})$") to get the flip-sign identity after the swap, so it is stated here as a hypothesis.

## Expansion

Proposition 2.1: by the flip-sign property and exchangeability, $W^0\overset d=\varepsilon\odot W^0$; $W^{\mathrm{ext}}$ is independent of the target data and of $\varepsilon$, so $(W^0,W^{\mathrm{ext}})\overset d=(\varepsilon\odot W^0,W^{\mathrm{ext}})$, and since $W^{\mathrm{lro}}=g(W^0,W^{\mathrm{ext}})$ with $g(\varepsilon\odot w,w')=\varepsilon\odot g(w,w')$ (this is where the zero rule matters), $W^{\mathrm{lro}}\overset d=\varepsilon\odot W^{\mathrm{lro}}$. External evidence thus only reorders hypotheses, moving likely non-nulls to the end of the sequence the filter scans, which raises power without affecting validity. Proposition 2.2: the symmetric penalty $\phi_j=\phi_{j+p}$, with a swap-equivariant choice of minimizer, makes the weighted lasso equivariant under swapping a variable with its knockoff, preserving the flip-sign property. Proposition 2.3 swaps null variables with their knockoffs simultaneously in all environments: condition 2 keeps $\phi$ fixed, so the swapped statistics are the sign-flipped ones, and condition 1, with independence across environments, makes the swap leave the pooled joint law invariant.
