# At a monomorphic locus the fitted statistic is almost surely zero

Let $P$ be a finite measure on $\Omega$, let the genotype classes
$X_0, X_1, \dots : \Omega \to \{0, 1, 2\}$ be identically distributed under $P$, and let
$P(X_0 = i) = \pi_i(p)$ for every class $i$, where $\pi(p)$ are the
[Hardy–Weinberg proportions](hwe-prob.md) at $p = 0$ or $p = 1$, that is $(1, 0, 0)$ or $(0, 0, 1)$.
No independence is needed. Then for $P$-almost every $\omega$, every individual has the same
homozygous genotype and the [fitted Hardy–Weinberg statistic](hwe-stat.md) of the first $N$
individuals is $0$ for every sample size $N$. The sampling equalities force $P$ to have total mass
$1$.
