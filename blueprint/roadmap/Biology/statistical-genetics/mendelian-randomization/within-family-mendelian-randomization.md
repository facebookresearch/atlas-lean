# Within-family Mendelian randomization

For individual $i$ in family $k$, let $x_{k,i}$ be an exposure, $y_{k,i}$ an outcome, $g_{k,i}$ genotypes at instruments, $C_{k,i}$ an exposure–outcome confounder and $f_k$ a family-level effect (dynastic effects, assortative mating, residual population structure). The model is

$$x_{k,i} = \gamma_0 + \gamma_1 g_{k,i} + \gamma_2 C_{k,i} + f_k + v_{k,i}, \qquad y_{k,i} = \beta_0 + \beta_1 x_{k,i} + \beta_2 C_{k,i} + f_k + u_{k,i},$$

with causal effect $\beta_1$. Because $g_{k,i}$ and $f_k$ both depend on the family's genetic background, $\operatorname{Cov}(g, f) \ne 0$ in general and population-level MR violates the instrument independence assumption.

**Sibling-difference estimator.** For a sibling pair $(k,1), (k,2)$ and variant $j$, let $\delta_{k,j} = g_{k,1,j} - g_{k,2,j}$, and estimate by least squares

$$x_{k,1} - x_{k,2} = \gamma_j \delta_{k,j} + \dot v_{k,j}, \qquad y_{k,1} - y_{k,2} = \Gamma_j \delta_{k,j} + \dot u_{k,j} .$$

Differencing removes $f_k$, and $\delta_{k,j}$ is generated by independent meioses given the parents, so it is independent of family-level confounding. The pairs $(\hat\gamma_j, \hat\Gamma_j)$ then feed any summary-data MR estimator, for example the [IVW estimator](ivw.md).

**Family fixed effects.** Equivalently, regress $x$ and $y$ on $g_{k,i,j}$ with a separate intercept $f_k$ for each family; this is the within transformation, which subtracts family means. With exactly two siblings per family, the fixed-effect and difference estimators of $\gamma_j$ and $\Gamma_j$ coincide; with more siblings they differ but stay close.

**Trios.** With parental genotypes $g^m_{i,j}, g^f_{i,j}$, regress the child's $x_i$ and $y_i$ on $(g_{i,j}, g^m_{i,j}, g^f_{i,j})$ and use the coefficients on $g_{i,j}$. Conditional on both parents' genotypes, the child's genotype is a Mendelian lottery.

## Expansion

With two siblings, $x_{k,i} - \bar x_k = \pm\tfrac12(x_{k,1} - x_{k,2})$ and $g_{k,i} - \bar g_k = \pm\tfrac12\delta_k$ with the same sign. So the demeaned regression has the same normal equations as the difference regression, up to a factor of $2$ that cancels in the slope. Given parental genotypes, sibling genotype differences are mean-zero and independent of anything fixed at the family level. Hence the within-family SNP–outcome association equals $\beta_1$ times the SNP–exposure association, which is the Wald identity with a valid instrument.
