# Convergence of IBSS to a stationary point of the ELBO

In the [SuSiE model](sum-of-single-effects-model.md), suppose $0 < \sigma < \infty$,
$0 < \sigma_{0l} < \infty$ for all $l = 1, \dots, L$, and $\pi_j > 0$ for all $j = 1, \dots, p$.
Parameterize each factor $q_l$ by its posterior inclusion probabilities $\alpha_l \in \Delta_{p-1}$
and conditional Gaussian means and variances $(\mu_{1l}, \sigma_{1l}^2)$.
Then the sequence of iterates generated by IBSS converges to a stationary point of the ELBO $F$
(Wang et al., Proposition 2).

## Expansion

The whole-sequence convergence is the statement of Wang et al., Proposition 2; this page records it
with that attribution. The ingredients below give only the weaker limit-point conclusion. Under the
finiteness and positivity assumptions on $\sigma, \sigma_{0l}, \pi_j$, the ELBO is bounded above by
$\log p(y \mid X, \sigma^2, \sigma_0^2)$ and nondecreasing along IBSS, each block subproblem has a unique
maximizer (the SER posterior), and the iterates remain in a compact parameter set. The block
coordinate-ascent result invoked in Appendix B.5.2 of the source (Bertsekas, *Nonlinear Programming*,
Proposition 2.7.1 as corrected in the published errata) then shows that the iterates have a limit point and
that every limit point is a stationary point of $F$. Uniqueness of each block maximizer does not by itself
make that limit point unique, so the step from limit points to convergence of the whole sequence is not
derived here; a formalization must supply an IBSS-specific argument for it (for example, that the
stationary points of $F$ in the compact set are isolated and consecutive iterates become arbitrarily close)
or take the weaker limit-point statement as its target.
