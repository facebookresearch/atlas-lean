# Covariance of the single-effect regression model

Let $I$ be a finite index set. For nonnegative reals $\sigma^2$ and $\sigma_0^2$ and a vector $x \in \mathbb{R}^I$, the covariance of the single-effect regression model is the $I \times I$ matrix

$$\Sigma(\sigma^2, \sigma_0^2, x) = \sigma_0^2\, x x^\top + \sigma^2 \mathrm{I}.$$

It is the covariance of $y = x \beta + e$ when $\beta \sim N(0, \sigma_0^2)$ is independent of $e \sim N(0, \sigma^2 \mathrm{I})$.
