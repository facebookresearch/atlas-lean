# One-sided degenerate Cahn–Hilliard model with anisotropic traction

Let $\Omega\subset\mathbb R^d$ ($d\in\{2,3\}$) be a bounded domain with outer normal $n$, and let $\rho(x,t)\ge0$ be the density of motile (distal) cells in a digit-forming organoid. With interface length $\varepsilon>0$ and a double-well potential $W$ favouring a low- and a high-density phase, the Ginzburg–Landau energy is

$$E(\rho)=\int_\Omega\Bigl(\varepsilon^{-1}W(\rho)+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}|\nabla\rho|^2\Bigr)dx .$$

Mechanically biased cell–cell traction along a prescribed orientation field $\nu(x,t)$ is encoded by a symmetric positive semidefinite tensor $D(\nu)$ (prototype $D(\nu)=\nu\otimes\nu$) and a traction strength $\eta\ge0$. With mobility $m(\rho)=\rho$ the reduced model is

$$\partial_t\rho=\operatorname{div}(\rho\nabla q)+\varepsilon^{-1}\eta^2\operatorname{div}\bigl(\rho D(\nu)\nabla\rho\bigr),\qquad q=\varepsilon^{-1}W'(\rho)-\varepsilon\Delta\rho,$$

that is, $\partial_t\rho=\operatorname{div}J$ with total flux $J=\rho\nabla q+\varepsilon^{-1}\eta^2\rho D(\nu)\nabla\rho$, subject to $\nabla\rho\cdot n=0$ and $J\cdot n=0$ on $\partial\Omega$ and $\rho(\cdot,0)=\rho_0$.

Structural properties:

- **Mass conservation.** The no-flux conditions give $\frac{d}{dt}\int_\Omega\rho\,dx=0$ (fixed number of motile cells).
- **One-sided degeneracy.** The mobility vanishes only at vacuum $\rho=0$, which is what keeps densities non-negative.
- **Not a gradient flow.** The Cahn–Hilliard part $\operatorname{div}(\rho\nabla\,\delta E/\delta\rho)$ is a generalized gradient flow of $E$ for the mobility-induced metric, but the traction flux $\kappa\operatorname{div}(\rho D(\nu)\nabla\rho)$, $\kappa=\varepsilon^{-1}\eta^2$, is a prescribed conservative anisotropic transport term that is not generated by the variational derivative of $E$; it contributes sign-indefinite terms to the energy balance.

## Expansion

The equation is a mean-field reduction of an agent-based model in which distal and proximal cells interact by short-range repulsion and type-dependent tractions that are biased relative to morphogen gradients; in the limit of many cells with interaction radius of order $\varepsilon$ the distal density obeys the equation above when the two populations are effectively segregated and the orientation field is treated as given. The double well drives segregation into cell-rich and cell-free regions, the gradient term penalizes sharp interfaces, and the anisotropic traction flux produces the elongated, protrusive digit-like morphologies.
