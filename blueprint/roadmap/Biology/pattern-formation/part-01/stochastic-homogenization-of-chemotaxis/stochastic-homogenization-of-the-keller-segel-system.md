# Stochastic homogenization of the Keller–Segel system

**Setting.** Cells with density $u^\varepsilon$ move by diffusion and chemotaxis towards a chemoattractant $v^\varepsilon$ in a bounded domain $Q\subset\mathbb R^d$ ($d\le3$) whose microstructure is random on scale $\varepsilon$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\partial_tu^\varepsilon&=\nabla\cdot\big(D_u^\varepsilon(x)\nabla u^\varepsilon-\chi^\varepsilon(x)\,u^\varepsilon\nabla v^\varepsilon\big),\\
\partial_tv^\varepsilon&=\nabla\cdot\big(D_v(x)\nabla v^\varepsilon\big)-\gamma v^\varepsilon+\alpha u^\varepsilon,
\end{aligned}
$$

with no-flux boundary conditions and initial data $u_0\in H^1(Q)$, $v_0\in H^2(Q)$ with $u_0,v_0\ge0$ a.e. in $Q$. Here $D_u^\varepsilon(x)=\tilde D_u(\mathcal T(x/\varepsilon)\omega)$ and $\chi^\varepsilon(x)=\tilde\chi(\mathcal T(x/\varepsilon)\omega)$ are stationary random fields generated by an ergodic measure-preserving dynamical system $\mathcal T(x)$ on a probability space $(\Omega,P)$. Assume $0<d_u^0\le\tilde D_u\le d_u^1$ and $0\le\tilde\chi\le\chi^1$ (no smoothness), $D_v\in W^{2,\infty}$ uniformly elliptic, and $\alpha,\gamma>0$. For $d=2,3$ assume moreover the smallness condition (2) of the source's Assumption 1(iii):

$$
\Big(1+|Q|^{\frac{2-r}{2}}\|u_0\|_{L^1(Q)}^{r/2}\Big)\Big[\max\Big\{\|u_0\|_{L^r(Q)},\ C_g\big(\|u_0\|_{L^1(Q)}+\|u_0\|_{L^1(Q)}^{\frac{2}{d+2}}\big)\Big\}+\|\nabla v_0\|_{L^q(Q)}\Big]<\frac{2d_u^0}{r}\,\frac{1}{\chi^1C_bC_v},
$$

where $q=\max\{2+\zeta,d\}$, $1+\frac{\zeta}{4+\zeta}<r\le2$ for some $\zeta>0$ if $d=2$, $\frac d2<r\le2$ if $d=3$, and $C_v,C_b,C_g$ are the constants of the source's estimates (20) and (22)–(25), which depend only on $Q$, $d$ and $D_v$. These are the hypotheses (Assumption 1) under which the source's Theorems 4 and 15 are stated.

**Theorem.**

1. *(Uniform estimates.)* For each $\varepsilon$ there is a unique weak solution, and $\|u^\varepsilon\|_{L^\infty(0,\tau;H^1)}+\|\partial_tu^\varepsilon\|_{L^2(Q_\tau)}$ and $\|v^\varepsilon\|_{L^\infty(0,\tau;H^1)}+\|\partial_tv^\varepsilon\|_{L^2(0,\tau;H^1)}+\|v^\varepsilon\|_{L^2(0,\tau;H^2)}$ are bounded independently of $\varepsilon$, $P$-almost surely.
2. *(Homogenized limit.)* $P$-a.s., $(u^\varepsilon,v^\varepsilon)$ converges strongly in $L^2(Q_\tau)$ and weakly in $L^2(0,\tau;H^1(Q))$ to the solution of

$$
\partial_tu=\nabla\cdot\big(D^*\nabla u-\chi^*\,u\,\nabla v\big),\qquad
\partial_tv=\nabla\cdot(D_v\nabla v)-\gamma v+\alpha u,
$$

   with no-flux conditions, where for $\xi\in\mathbb R^d$

$$
D^*\xi=\int_\Omega\tilde D_u(\omega)\big(\bar u_{1,\xi}+\xi\big)\,dP,\qquad
\chi^*\xi=-\int_\Omega\big(\tilde D_u(\omega)\hat u_{1,\xi}-\tilde\chi(\omega)\xi\big)\,dP,
$$

   and the correctors $\bar u_{1,\xi},\hat u_{1,\xi}$ are the potential random fields ($L^2_{\rm pot}(\Omega)$) for which $\tilde D_u(\bar u_{1,\xi}+\xi)$ and $\tilde D_u\hat u_{1,\xi}-\tilde\chi\xi$ are solenoidal ($L^2_{\rm sol}(\Omega)$).
3. *(Periodization.)* The effective coefficients computed by solving the cell problems on periodized samples of size $\rho$ converge to $D^*$ and $\chi^*$, $P$-a.s., as $\rho\to\infty$.

## Expansion

The estimates come from energy identities in which the chemotactic term is controlled through $H^2$ (or, in 1D, $W^{1,\infty}$) bounds on $v^\varepsilon$, which needs only boundedness of $\tilde D_u,\tilde\chi$; this is what allows rough, non-differentiable random media. Stochastic two-scale convergence (with the ergodic theorem replacing periodicity) gives $u^\varepsilon\rightharpoonup u$ and $\nabla u^\varepsilon\rightharpoonup\nabla u+u_1(x,\omega)$ with a potential corrector, and passing to the limit in the flux identifies the effective tensors through the two auxiliary problems. Strong convergence of $u^\varepsilon$ (from the time-derivative bound) handles the nonlinear term $u^\varepsilon\nabla v^\varepsilon$. The effective chemotactic sensitivity $\chi^*$ is generally a matrix even for scalar microscopic $\tilde\chi$, and it couples to diffusion heterogeneity through the corrector $\hat u_{1,\xi}$: tissue heterogeneity changes the balance between aggregation and spreading at the macroscopic scale.
