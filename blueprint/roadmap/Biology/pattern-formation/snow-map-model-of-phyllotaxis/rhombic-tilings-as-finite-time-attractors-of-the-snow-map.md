# Rhombic tilings as finite-time attractors of the Snow map

**Setting.** Primordia (organ initials) form at the growing tip of a plant; unrolling the meristem gives a cylinder of fixed circumference, here normalized to $1$. The *Snow map* $\mathcal S$ (after Douady and Couder, with roots in Hofmeister, van Iterson and Williams) models primordium formation as stacking disks of diameter $D$ on the cylinder: given a configuration of the most recent $K$ primordia, the next one is placed at the lowest height above the existing ones at which it does not overlap them (the map acts on the configuration of the $K$ most recent primordia). A *primordia front* (van Iterson's zig-zag line) is a closed chain of pairwise tangent primordia encircling the cylinder; its numbers of up- and down-steps are the front parastichy numbers $(M,N)$. A *rhombic tiling* is a configuration in which neighbouring primordia centres form rhombi; cylindrical $(M,N)$-lattices (the classical phyllotactic lattices) are special rhombic tilings, and general rhombic tilings are their deformations, allowing undulating parastichies and multijugate patterns.

**Theorem.** Let $M,N$ be coprime and $K\ge M+N$. The set of *dynamical tilings* of length $K$ (segments of rhombic tilings with parastichy numbers $(M,N)$ generated by $\mathcal S$ in its domain of differentiability) is an open submanifold of the space of $(M,N)$ rhombic tilings of length $K$. Moreover, there is an open neighbourhood $\mathcal V$ of this set in the configuration space $\mathrm{Cyl}^K$ such that every configuration in $\mathcal V$ is mapped into it by $\mathcal S^j$ for all $j>K$: the attraction occurs in finite time. The manifold is non-empty whenever, for the given $D$, the bifurcation diagram of fixed points contains a non-critical $(M,N)$-lattice. Its elements are periodic orbits of period $MN$ of $\mathcal S$ (in shape space), it has dimension $M+N$, and it contains the corresponding cylindrical lattice.

## Expansion

The top front of a configuration determines its future: each new disk rests on two tangent disks of the front, and the number of sides of the polygonal tile between the new primordium and the front tells whether the parastichy numbers stay the same or change. Heuristically, on a rhombic tiling each new primordium sits in a rhombic gap and reproduces the front pattern shifted, so tilings are mapped to tilings, and since the map only remembers the last $K$ primordia, a nearby configuration is fully renewed after $K$ steps, which is why capture happens in finite time. Rhombic tilings thus form a large, robust attracting family in which Fibonacci, decussate-to-spiral and multijugate patterns, as well as transients usually discarded, appear as steady states. Since the number of primordia in a front is the sum of its parastichy numbers, a front with Fibonacci parastichy numbers has a Fibonacci number of primordia, suggesting a geometric explanation for Fibonacci counts of ray florets.
