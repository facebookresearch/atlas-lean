# For diagonal diffusion, the dispersion polynomial is the Turing one

For a real $2 \times 2$ matrix $J = \begin{pmatrix} f_u & f_v \\ g_u & g_v \end{pmatrix}$ and real
$d_u$, $d_v$ and $q$, the cross-diffusion [dispersion polynomial](cross-dispersion-det.md) of $J$
and $\operatorname{diag}(d_u, d_v)$ is the
[dispersion polynomial](../turing-patterns-and-diffusion-driven-instability/dispersion-det.md) of
the diagonal theory: $h(q) = d_u d_v q^2 - (d_v f_u + d_u g_v) q + \det J$.
