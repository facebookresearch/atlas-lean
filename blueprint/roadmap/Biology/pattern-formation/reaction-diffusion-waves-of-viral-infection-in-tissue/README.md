# Reaction-diffusion waves of viral infection in tissue

Virus spreading through a tissue such as a lymph node or spleen can be modelled by a scalar reaction-diffusion equation balancing diffusion, logistic replication and elimination by an immune response whose intensity depends on viral load in a bell-shaped way. The resulting nonlinearity is monostable near zero and can be bistable at high load, so infection spreads as a low-level pulled front, a high-level pushed front, or two consecutive fronts, depending on the initial dose and on the two front speeds.
