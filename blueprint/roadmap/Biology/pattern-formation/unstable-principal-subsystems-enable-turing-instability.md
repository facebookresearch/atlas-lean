# Unstable principal subsystems enable diffusion-driven instability

Let $n \ge 2$ and let $A = (a_{ij}) \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$ be the Jacobian of the kinetics $u_t = f(u)$ at a spatially uniform steady state $u_s$ of the reaction-diffusion system

$$\partial_t u_j = D_j \,\partial_x^2 u_j + f_j(u), \qquad j = 1, \dots, n,$$

with diffusion coefficients $D_1, \dots, D_n \ge 0$. A Fourier mode of wavenumber $k \in \mathbb{R}$ evolves under the matrix $C(k) = A - k^2 D$, $D = \operatorname{diag}(D_1, \dots, D_n)$. Assume $A$ is stable (every eigenvalue has negative real part). For a nonempty proper index set $J \subsetneq \{1, \dots, n\}$, write $A_J$ for the principal submatrix of $A$ on the rows and columns in $J$; the linear system $\dot v = A_J v$ is the **subsystem** on the species in $J$.

**Theorem (an unstable subsystem of order $n - 1$ with a wrong-sign determinant gives a steady Turing instability).** Suppose that for some index $j$ the principal submatrix on $J = \{1, \dots, n\} \setminus \{j\}$ has a determinant of the wrong sign, $\operatorname{sgn} \det A_J = (-1)^n$ (for $n = 2$: $a_{ii} > 0$ for the index $i \ne j$, a self-activating species). Then $A_J$ has a positive real eigenvalue, and for every wavenumber $k \neq 0$ there is $\varepsilon_0 > 0$ such that for each $0 < \varepsilon < \varepsilon_0$, with $D_i = \varepsilon$ for all $i \ne j$, there is $\delta_c > 0$ for which $C(k)$ has the eigenvalue $0$ at $D_j = \delta_c$ and a positive real eigenvalue for every $D_j > \delta_c$. So the uniform state is stable for the kinetics but unstable for the reaction-diffusion system, through a steady (zero-eigenvalue) Turing bifurcation.

Conversely, if every principal minor of $A$ satisfies $\operatorname{sgn} \det A_J = (-1)^{|J|}$ (all subsystems sign-stable), then $\det C(k) \ne 0$ for every $D \ge 0$ and every $k$, so no choice of $D \ge 0$ produces a steady Turing bifurcation (a real eigenvalue crossing zero). This sign condition does not exclude an oscillatory instability: for $n \ge 4$ there are stable matrices satisfying it for which $C(k)$ acquires a complex pair of eigenvalues with positive real part (see below). This corrects Theorem 1 of Satnoianu, Menzinger and Maini as printed, "If the kinetic system (2.8) of the problem (2.1–2) is s-stable then no Turing bifurcation is possible from the uniform steady state solution $u_s$ for any $n \ge 1$", where Section 2 counts as Turing unstable any mode with an eigenvalue of positive real part, and with it their Theorem 2, "A is strongly stable if and only if A is s-stable": every term of the expansion of $\det C(k)$ in the Expansion has the sign of $\det A$ or vanishes, so the sign condition rules out only a real eigenvalue crossing $0$, and the Satnoianu–van den Driessche example below has a complex pair crossing instead. Satnoianu, Menzinger and Maini (Remark 5) call a Turing bifurcation **of type $p$**, $1 \le p \le n - 1$, when some principal minor of order $p$ has the wrong sign, $\operatorname{sgn} \det A_J = (-1)^{p+1}$ with $|J| = p$, and no minor of smaller order does: type I means some $a_{ii} > 0$ (a single self-activating species, the classical activator), type II means no $a_{ii} > 0$ but some $2 \times 2$ principal minor $\det A_{\{i,j\}} < 0$, and so on. For $n = 2$ only type I exists; for $n \ge 3$ the "activator" can be a network of several species none of which is self-activating.

## Expansion

For $n = 2$ the theorem recovers the classical two-species condition of [diffusion-driven instability](turing-patterns-and-diffusion-driven-instability/turing-instability.md), where the unstable subsystem is a self-activating species. *Proof of the theorem* (Satnoianu, Menzinger and Maini, Theorem 3 and its proof, Eqs. (2.23)–(2.25)). For the nonnegative diagonal matrix $\Lambda = k^2 D$, the constant term of the characteristic polynomial $\det(\omega I - C(k))$ is $c_n = \det(\Lambda - A) = \sum_{I} \det\big((-A)_I\big) \prod_{i \notin I} \Lambda_{ii}$, summed over all $I \subseteq \{1, \dots, n\}$ with the empty minor equal to $1$; it is affine in each $D_i$. With $D_i = \varepsilon$ for $i \ne j$ this reads $c_n = \alpha(\varepsilon) + \beta(\varepsilon) D_j$ with $\alpha(\varepsilon) = (-1)^n \det A + O(\varepsilon)$ and $\beta(\varepsilon) = k^2 (-1)^{n-1} \det A_J + O(\varepsilon)$. Stability of $A$ gives $(-1)^n \det A > 0$, and the hypothesis gives $(-1)^{n-1} \det A_J < 0$, so for small $\varepsilon$ we have $\alpha > 0 > \beta$: $c_n$ vanishes at $\delta_c = -\alpha/\beta > 0$, where $0$ is an eigenvalue, and is negative for $D_j > \delta_c$, where the monic real polynomial $\det(\omega I - C(k))$, negative at $\omega = 0$, has a positive real root. The source's proof says that $\delta_c$ exists "independent of the sign of" $\det A_J$; it does not, since for $(-1)^{n-1} \det A_J > 0$ and small $\varepsilon$ both $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are positive and $c_n > 0$ for every $D_j \ge 0$, so the theorem keeps the wrong-sign hypothesis that the proof uses. The same sign count shows that $A_J$ is unstable: $\det A_J$ is the product of its eigenvalues, a complex pair contributes a positive factor, and if all its real eigenvalues were negative, $\det A_J$ would have sign $(-1)^{n-1}$; since $\det A_J \ne 0$, some real eigenvalue is positive.

*Remark (the page's own extension; no cited source states it).* Diffusion-driven instability, an eigenvalue of $C(k)$ with positive real part for some $D_1, \dots, D_n > 0$ and $k \ne 0$, also occurs whenever some principal submatrix $A_J$ has an eigenvalue with positive real part, whatever the sign of $\det A_J$; the instability may then be oscillatory rather than steady. Anma, Sakamoto and Yoneda (2012) point out that Theorem 3 of Satnoianu, Menzinger and Maini does not account for such wave instabilities, and treat three-component systems. Argument: order the species so that $J = \{1, \dots, p\}$ and write $A = \begin{pmatrix} A_J & B \\ E & A_K \end{pmatrix}$. Fix $k \ne 0$, set $D_i = 0$ for $i \in J$ and $D_i = L$ for $i \notin J$. For $\lambda$ in a fixed bounded set and $L$ large, $A_K - k^2 L I - \lambda I$ is invertible with inverse of norm $O(1/L)$, so by the Schur complement the eigenvalues of $C(k)$ in that set are the roots of $\det\big(A_J - \lambda I - B(A_K - k^2 L I - \lambda I)^{-1} E\big)$, which converge to the eigenvalues of $A_J$ as $L \to \infty$; the remaining $n - p$ eigenvalues tend to $-\infty$. Hence for $L$ large $C(k)$ inherits an eigenvalue of positive real part from $A_J$, and by continuity of eigenvalues the same holds after replacing the zero coefficients on $J$ by small positive ones. Because $A$ itself is stable, the $k = 0$ mode is stable, so the instability is diffusion driven.

For the converse, expanding $\det(A - \Lambda)$ for a nonnegative diagonal $\Lambda = k^2D$ gives $\sum_J \det A_J \prod_{j \notin J}(-\Lambda_{jj})$, a sum of terms of sign $(-1)^n$ or zero whose term $J = \{1, \dots, n\}$ is $\det A \ne 0$; so $\det C(k)$ keeps the sign of $\det A$ and $0$ is never an eigenvalue. Stability of $A - \Lambda$ for every nonnegative diagonal $\Lambda$ ("strong stability") requires the weak sign condition ($(-1)^{|J|}\det A_J \ge 0$ for all $J$, with some minor of each order nonzero); the strict sign condition together with stability of $A$ implies strong stability for $n \le 3$, but not for $n = 4$: Satnoianu and van den Driessche (Example 3.1) give a stable $4\times4$ matrix with all signed principal minors positive for which $A - \operatorname{diag}(0,0,0,1)$ has an unstable complex pair of eigenvalues. The same example is a counterexample to Theorem 2 of Satnoianu, Menzinger and Maini as stated there; what the strict sign condition rules out is the steady (zero-eigenvalue) kind of diffusion-driven instability.
