# Unstable principal subsystems enable diffusion-driven instability

Let $n \ge 2$ and let $A = (a_{ij}) \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$ be the Jacobian of the kinetics $u_t = f(u)$ at a spatially uniform steady state $u_s$ of the reaction-diffusion system

$$\partial_t u_j = D_j \,\partial_x^2 u_j + f_j(u), \qquad j = 1, \dots, n,$$

with diffusion coefficients $D_1, \dots, D_n \ge 0$. A Fourier mode of wavenumber $k \in \mathbb{R}$ evolves under the matrix $C(k) = A - k^2 D$, $D = \operatorname{diag}(D_1, \dots, D_n)$. Assume $A$ is stable (every eigenvalue has negative real part). For a nonempty proper index set $J \subsetneq \{1, \dots, n\}$, write $A_J$ for the principal submatrix of $A$ on the rows and columns in $J$; the linear system $\dot v = A_J v$ is the **subsystem** on the species in $J$.

**Theorem (unstable subsystem implies Turing instability).** If some principal submatrix $A_J$ has an eigenvalue with positive real part, then there are diffusion coefficients $D_1, \dots, D_n > 0$ and a wavenumber $k \neq 0$ such that $C(k)$ has an eigenvalue with positive real part, i.e. the uniform state is stable for the kinetics but unstable for the reaction-diffusion system. One can take $D_j$ small for $j \in J$ and $D_j$ large for $j \notin J$.

Conversely, if every principal minor of $A$ satisfies $\operatorname{sgn} \det A_J = (-1)^{|J|}$ (all subsystems sign-stable), then $\det C(k) \ne 0$ for every $D \ge 0$ and every $k$, so no choice of $D \ge 0$ produces a steady Turing bifurcation (a real eigenvalue crossing zero). This sign condition does not exclude an oscillatory instability: for $n \ge 4$ there are stable matrices satisfying it for which $C(k)$ acquires a complex pair of eigenvalues with positive real part (see below). Satnoianu, Menzinger and Maini call a Turing bifurcation **of type $p$** when the smallest unstable subsystem has $p$ species, $1 \le p \le n - 1$: type I means some $a_{ii} > 0$ (a single self-activating species, the classical activator), type II means no $a_{ii} > 0$ but some $2 \times 2$ principal minor $\det A_{\{i,j\}} < 0$, and so on. For $n = 2$ only type I exists; for $n \ge 3$ the "activator" can be a network of several species none of which is self-activating.

## Expansion

For $n = 2$ this recovers the classical two-species condition of [diffusion-driven instability](turing-patterns-and-diffusion-driven-instability/turing-instability.md), where the unstable subsystem is a self-activating species. Order the species so that $J = \{1, \dots, p\}$ and write $A = \begin{pmatrix} A_J & B \\ E & A_K \end{pmatrix}$. Fix $k \ne 0$, set $D_j = 0$ for $j \in J$ and $D_j = L$ for $j \notin J$. For $\lambda$ in a fixed bounded set and $L$ large, $A_K - k^2 L I - \lambda I$ is invertible with inverse of norm $O(1/L)$, so by the Schur complement the eigenvalues of $C(k)$ in that set are the roots of $\det\big(A_J - \lambda I - B(A_K - k^2 L I - \lambda I)^{-1} E\big)$, which converge to the eigenvalues of $A_J$ as $L \to \infty$; the remaining $n - p$ eigenvalues tend to $-\infty$. Hence for $L$ large $C(k)$ inherits an eigenvalue of positive real part from $A_J$, and by continuity of eigenvalues the same holds after replacing the zero coefficients on $J$ by small positive ones. Because $A$ itself is stable, the $k = 0$ mode is stable, so the instability is diffusion driven. The original argument tracks instead the constant term $c_n = \det(-C(k))$ of the characteristic polynomial, which is multilinear in the $D_j$, and tunes one coefficient until $c_n$ changes sign, producing a zero eigenvalue (a steady Turing bifurcation). For the converse, expanding $\det(A - \Lambda)$ for a nonnegative diagonal $\Lambda = k^2D$ gives $\sum_J \det A_J \prod_{j \notin J}(-\Lambda_{jj})$, a sum of terms of sign $(-1)^n$ or zero whose term $J = \{1, \dots, n\}$ is $\det A \ne 0$; so $\det C(k)$ keeps the sign of $\det A$ and $0$ is never an eigenvalue. Stability of $A - \Lambda$ for every nonnegative diagonal $\Lambda$ ("strong stability") requires the weak sign condition ($(-1)^{|J|}\det A_J \ge 0$ for all $J$, with some minor of each order nonzero); the strict sign condition together with stability of $A$ implies strong stability for $n \le 3$, but not for $n = 4$: Satnoianu and van den Driessche (Example 3.1) give a stable $4\times4$ matrix with all signed principal minors positive for which $A - \operatorname{diag}(0,0,0,1)$ has an unstable complex pair of eigenvalues. The same example is a counterexample to Theorem 2 of Satnoianu, Menzinger and Maini as stated there; what the strict sign condition rules out is the steady (zero-eigenvalue) kind of diffusion-driven instability.
