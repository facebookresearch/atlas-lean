# Wave instability needs at least three components

Consider the $n$-component reaction–diffusion system $\partial_t u = D \nabla^2 u + f(u)$ with diagonal diffusion matrix $D = \operatorname{diag}(D_1, \dots, D_n)$, $D_i \ge 0$, and a homogeneous equilibrium $P$, $f(P) = 0$, that is stable without diffusion: every eigenvalue of $J = J_u f(P)$ has negative real part. A Fourier mode with $|k|^2 = \mu > 0$ evolves under $J - \mu D$. Three situations are distinguished. A **neutral mode** is a $\mu > 0$ at which $J - \mu D$ has an eigenvalue on the imaginary axis: a **Turing (neutral) mode** when that eigenvalue is $0$, i.e. $\det(J - \mu D) = 0$, and a **wave (neutral) mode** when $J - \mu D$ has eigenvalues $\pm i\omega$, $\omega \ne 0$. A **crossing** is a neutral mode at which, as $\mu$ (or a parameter) varies through it, the eigenvalue passes from the open left into the open right half-plane; touching zero is not enough: for $J = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ -4 & -3 \end{pmatrix}$ and $D = \operatorname{diag}(1, 9)$, $\det(J - \mu D) = (1 - 3\mu)^2$ vanishes at $\mu = 1/3$ while the trace stays negative, so no eigenvalue ever becomes positive. A **growing mode** is a $\mu > 0$ at which $J - \mu D$ has an eigenvalue with positive real part; it is a **Turing instability** if that eigenvalue is real and a **wave instability** if it is nonreal. For an increasing word $(i_1 < \dots < i_k)$, $M_{i_1 \dots i_k}$ is the principal minor of $J$ on rows and columns $i_1, \dots, i_k$.

**Proposition.** (i) If $n = 2$, no diagonal $D \ge 0$ gives a wave neutral mode or a wave instability around $P$. (ii) If $n = 3$ and there are $1 \le i_1 < i_2 \le 3$ with $M_{i_1} + M_{i_2} > 0$ and $M_{i_1 i_2} > 0$, then there are diffusion rates $D_1, D_2, D_3 \ge 0$ (for instance $D_{i_1} = D_{i_2} = 0 < D_{i_3}$, with $i_3$ the remaining index) and $\mu^* > 0$ such that $J - \mu^* D$ has eigenvalues $\pm i\omega$ with $\omega \ne 0$ (a wave neutral mode), and $\mu > \mu^*$ arbitrarily close to $\mu^*$ at which $J - \mu D$ has a nonreal pair of eigenvalues with positive real part (a wave instability). Hence three is the minimal number of components for a wave instability.

## Expansion

For $n = 2$, $\operatorname{tr}(J - \mu D) = \operatorname{tr} J - \mu(D_1 + D_2) < 0$ for all $\mu > 0$, while a nonreal pair $\alpha \pm i\omega$ has trace $2\alpha$, so it would need $\alpha < 0$. For $n = 3$, the conditions say that the $2 \times 2$ principal submatrix $B$ on $\{i_1, i_2\}$ has positive trace and positive determinant, so both its eigenvalues have positive real part (they may be real). Take $D_{i_1} = D_{i_2} = 0 < D_{i_3}$. Then $\det(J - \mu D) = \det J - \mu D_{i_3} M_{i_1 i_2} < 0$ for all $\mu \ge 0$, since stability of $J$ gives $\det J < 0$; so $0$ is never an eigenvalue. As $\mu \to \infty$ one eigenvalue tends to $-\infty$ and the other two tend to those of $B$, so for large $\mu$ two eigenvalues lie in the open right half-plane, while at $\mu = 0$ all three lie in the open left half-plane. Let $\mu^*$ be the supremum of the $\mu$ for which all eigenvalues have nonpositive real part. By continuity some eigenvalue of $J - \mu^* D$ lies on the imaginary axis and is not $0$, so it is a pair $\pm i\omega$, $\omega \ne 0$, and the third (real) eigenvalue is negative. For $\mu > \mu^*$ near $\mu^*$ with an eigenvalue in the open right half-plane, that eigenvalue is close to $\pm i\omega$ and hence nonreal. The paper locates such crossings through the coefficients of the purely imaginary eigenvalue condition $b_3\mu^3 + b_2\mu^2 + b_1\mu + b_0 = 0$.
