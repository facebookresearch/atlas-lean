# Parabolic limit of kinetic cell adhesion: myopic diffusion with non-local adhesion

**Mesoscopic model.** Cells at position $x \in \mathbb{R}^d$ with velocity $v$ in the closed unit ball $V$ are pulled by a damped adhesion force and reorient randomly along tissue fibers. With $c(t,x,v)$ the population density, $\bar c = \int_V c\,\mathrm{d}v$ the macroscopic density (total mass $1$), a radially symmetric interaction potential $H_r$ supported in $B_r$, an adhesion strength $\chi(t,x) \ge 0$, damping $a > 0$ and a fiber orientation distribution $q(x, \theta) \ge 0$ depending only on $x$ and $\theta = v/|v|$ with $\bar q = 1/d$, the density solves the doubly non-local kinetic equation

$$
\nabla_{(t,x,v)}\cdot\Big(\big(1,\ v,\ -a v + \chi\, \nabla_x H_r \star \bar c\big)\, c\Big) = d\, q\, \bar c - c ,
$$

with $c = 0$ on $\mathbb{R}^d \times \partial V$. The boundary condition is admissible when $a \ge \sup \chi \cdot \sup_{B_r}|\nabla H_r|$, since the drift then points into $V$ on $\partial V$. The adhesion velocity $\nabla_x H_r \star u$ coincides with the classical non-local adhesion operator

$$
\mathcal{A}_r u(x) = \frac{1}{r|B_r|}\int_{B_r} u(x + \xi)\, \frac{\xi}{|\xi|}\, F(|\xi|)\,\mathrm{d}\xi
$$

for the force profile $F$ generated by $H_r$.

**Mean-field step.** For $N$ cells obeying $\dot Z_i = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{j \ne i} K(t, Z_i, Z_j) + A(t, Z_i)$ with continuous $K, A$, the empirical measure $\mu_N = \frac1N\sum_i \delta_{Z_i}$ satisfies, in the sense of distributions,

$$
\partial_t \mu_N + \nabla_z\cdot\Big(\big(\mathcal{K}\mu_N - \tfrac1N \operatorname{tr}K + A\big)\mu_N\Big) = 0, \qquad \mathcal{K}\mu(t,Z) = \int K(t, Z, Z')\,\mu(\mathrm{d}Z'),\quad \operatorname{tr}K(t,Z) = K(t,Z,Z).
$$

Applied to the microscopic adhesion ODEs, this gives the transport part of the kinetic equation.

**Macroscopic limit (formal).** Under the parabolic scaling $\hat t = \varepsilon^2 t$, $\hat x = \varepsilon x$, $\hat r = \varepsilon r$, and if the mean fiber direction $\mathbb{E}[q] = \int_{S_1}\theta\, q(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\theta$ vanishes, the leading-order density $u = \overline{c^0}$ satisfies

$$
(a + 1)\,\partial_t u = \frac{1}{2a + 1}\,\frac{d}{d + 2}\,\nabla_x\nabla_x^\top : \big(\mathbb{D}[q]\, u\big) - \nabla_x\cdot\big(u\, \chi\, \mathcal{A}_r u\big), \qquad \mathbb{D}[q] = \int_{S_1}\theta\theta^\top q(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\theta .
$$

The first term is a **myopic** (non-Fickian) anisotropic diffusion, equivalently $\nabla\cdot(\mathbb{D}[q]\nabla u) + \nabla\cdot(u\,\nabla\cdot\mathbb{D}[q])$ up to the prefactor, and the second is the non-local adhesion advection. For constant $\chi$ and $q$ this reduces to the Armstrong-Painter-Sherratt adhesion model $u_t = D\Delta u - \nabla\cdot(u\,\chi\,\mathcal{A}_r u)$.

## Expansion

The empirical-measure identity follows by testing against smooth $\phi(Z)$ and differentiating $\frac1N\sum_i\phi(Z_i(t))$; the term $\frac1N \operatorname{tr}K$ removes the self-interaction that $\mathcal{K}\mu_N$ would otherwise include. In the limit $N \to \infty$ one obtains the Vlasov-type equation, to which the turning operator $c \mapsto d q \bar c - c$ (velocity-jump realignment with fibers) is added. Under the macroscopic rescaling, the adhesion convolution picks up a factor $\varepsilon$, $\nabla_x H_r \star \bar c = \varepsilon\,\nabla_{\hat x} H_{\hat r}\star \bar c$, so the adhesion drift enters as a small perturbation of the equilibrium velocity. A Hilbert expansion $c^\varepsilon = c^0 + \varepsilon c^0_1 + \dots$ and moment closure with respect to $v$ (the damping $-av$ relaxes speeds, giving the factors $a+1$ and $2a+1$) yield the macroscopic equations. As $r \to 0$ with $F(0) = d + 1$, the operator $\mathcal{A}_r$ tends to the local gradient, recovering a local Keller-Segel-type aggregation term.
