# Phylogenetic degree of the Jukes–Cantor variety

**Setting.** For a tree $T$ with edge set $E(T)$ and inner vertices $I(T)$, the Jukes–Cantor (group $\mathbb Z_2$) phylogenetic variety $X_T$ is, after the Fourier change of coordinates, a projective toric variety. Its polytope is

$$P_T = \operatorname{conv}\Big\{x \in \{0,1\}^{E(T)} : \textstyle\sum_{e \ni v} x_e \text{ is even for every } v \in I(T)\Big\} \subset \mathbb R^{E(T)},$$

and the lattice $L_T$ generated by its vertices is $\{x \in \mathbb Z^{E(T)} : \sum_{e\ni v} x_e \equiv 0 \pmod 2\ \forall v \in I(T)\}$, of index $2^{|I(T)|}$ in $\mathbb Z^{E(T)}$. $P_T$ is cut out by $0 \le x_e \le 1$ and, for each $v \in I(T)$ and each odd-size subset $A$ of the edges at $v$, $\sum_{e \ni v,\, e\notin A} x_e - \sum_{e\in A} x_e \ge 1 - |A|$.

**Theorem.** If $P_T$ is full-dimensional (no inner vertex of degree $2$), the degree of $X_T$ (its *phylogenetic degree*) satisfies

$$|E(T)|!\;\mathrm{vol}(P_T) = 2^{|I(T)|}\,\deg(X_T),$$

and $\mathrm{vol}(P_T)$ is given by an inclusion–exclusion over subsets of inner vertices of volumes of explicit "forest polytopes". For the star tree $S_n$ with $n \ge 3$ leaves and for the double star $S_{m,n}$ (two adjacent inner vertices of degrees $m+1$ and $n+1$):

$$\mathrm{vol}(P_{S_n}) = 1 - \frac{2^{n-1}}{n!}, \qquad \deg(X_{S_n}) = \frac{n! - 2^{n-1}}{2},$$

$$\mathrm{vol}(P_{S_{m,n}}) = 1 - \frac{2^m}{(m+1)!} - \frac{2^n}{(n+1)!} + 2^{m+n-1}\left(\frac{1}{(m+n+1)\,m!\,n!} + \frac{1}{(m+n+1)!}\right).$$

## Expansion

The degree of a projective toric variety equals the normalized volume of its polytope with respect to the lattice generated by its vertices; since $L_T$ has index $2^{|I(T)|}$, the Euclidean volume must be rescaled by that index. $P_T$ is the unit cube with, at each inner vertex, the corners of odd parity sliced off by the half-spaces above, so its volume is $1$ minus the volumes of these corner regions, corrected by inclusion–exclusion where cuts at adjacent vertices overlap. For the claw $S_3$ the polytope is the tetrahedron with volume $1/3$, giving $\deg X_{S_3} = 1$ (the variety is $\mathbb P^3$).
