# Hadamard conjugation for group-based models

Let $T$ be a phylogenetic tree with leaves $0, 1, \dots, n$ in which each edge $e$ carries Kimura three-substitution-type (K3ST) parameters $q_e(\alpha), q_e(\beta), q_e(\gamma) \ge 0$, the substitution types $\epsilon, \alpha, \beta, \gamma$ forming the Klein group $\mathbb{Z}_2 \times \mathbb{Z}_2$ acting on the four nucleotides. Index rows and columns of $2^n \times 2^n$ matrices by subsets of $[n] = \{1, \dots, n\}$, the first index giving the row and the second the column.

A site with character states $\chi(0), \chi(1), \dots, \chi(n)$ at the leaves determines the sets $S_\theta = \{i \in [n] : t_\theta(\chi(0)) = \chi(i)\}$ for $\theta \in \{\epsilon, \alpha, \beta, \gamma\}$, where $t_\theta$ is the substitution of type $\theta$; its site pattern is coded by the pair $(A, B) = (S_\alpha \cup S_\gamma, S_\beta \cup S_\gamma)$, from which $S_\gamma = A \cap B$, $S_\alpha = A \setminus B$, $S_\beta = B \setminus A$ and $S_\epsilon = [n] \setminus (A \cup B)$ are recovered. The sequence probability spectrum $P_T$ has entry $p_{A,B}$ in row $A$ and column $B$, the probability of the site pattern coded by $(A, B)$. The edge-length spectrum $Q_T$ is placed to agree with this coding: a single substitution of type $\alpha$, $\beta$ or $\gamma$ on the edge $e_C$ whose split separates $C \subseteq [n]$ from leaf $0$ produces the pattern $(C, \emptyset)$, $(\emptyset, C)$ or $(C, C)$ respectively, so $Q_{C, \emptyset} = q_{e_C}(\alpha)$, $Q_{\emptyset, C} = q_{e_C}(\beta)$, $Q_{C, C} = q_{e_C}(\gamma)$, the entry $Q_{\emptyset,\emptyset} = -K$ with $K$ the sum of all edge parameters, and all other entries are zero. (The source's display and its Figure 1 caption place $q(\alpha)$ and $q(\beta)$ in transposed positions; the placement here is the one under which the entrywise computation in the proof of Theorem 7 and the one-edge identities of Section 2 hold.) Let $H_n$ be the Sylvester Hadamard matrix of order $2^n$, so $H_n^{-1} = 2^{-n}H_n$. Hendy and Snir (2008, Theorem 7, equations (8) and (9)) prove that $P_T$ is determined by $Q_T$ through an entrywise exponential after Hadamard conjugation, and that $Q_T$ is recovered from $P_T$ by the entrywise logarithm when its arguments are positive; written in the form used in their proof and in their equation (9),

$$H_nP_TH_n = \mathrm{Exp}(H_nQ_TH_n), \qquad Q_T = H_n^{-1}\,\mathrm{Ln}(H_nP_TH_n)\,H_n^{-1}.$$

Hence all edge parameters, and the support of $Q_T$, are recovered from the expected pattern frequencies. The support records only the edges with positive total weight $q_e(\alpha) + q_e(\beta) + q_e(\gamma)$: contracting an internal edge whose three parameters vanish changes neither $Q_T$ nor $P_T$. So the expected pattern frequencies determine the tree obtained from $T$ by contracting its zero-weight edges, and they determine $T$ itself when every internal edge of $T$ has positive total weight. This is the invertible relationship between a tree with K3ST edge parameters and the expected nucleotide pattern frequencies reported by Hendy, Penny and Steel (1994), extending the transform for the two-state Cavender–Farris model.

## Expansion

1. The K3ST model, the Klein-group structure of the substitutions (Observation 1), and the diagonalization of the one-edge transition matrix by the $4 \times 4$ Hadamard matrix (Observation 2 and equations (2)–(3)).
2. The coding of site patterns by pairs of subsets $(A, B)$ of $[n]$, the sparse edge-length spectrum with the row and column placement matching that coding, and the invisibility of zero-weight edges.
3. Pathsets and the interpretation of the entries of $H_nQ_TH_n$ and $H_nP_TH_n$ through pathset distances.
4. Theorem 7 in the form $H_nP_TH_n = \mathrm{Exp}(H_nQ_TH_n)$, which matches the source's equation (9) and the entrywise computation of $[\mathrm{Exp}(H_nQ_TH_n)]_{A,B}$ in its proof, and the inverse under the positivity condition.
5. The two-state Cavender–Farris case and the vector form with $4^n$ components used by Hendy, Penny and Steel.
