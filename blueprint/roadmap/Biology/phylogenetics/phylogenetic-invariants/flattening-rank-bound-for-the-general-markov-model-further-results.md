# Flattening rank bounds for the general Markov model: further results

Allman and Rhodes (2008) prove more than the [rank bound for split flattenings](flattening-rank-bound-for-the-general-markov-model.md). (1) For the two-state general Markov model on a binary tree, the $3 \times 3$ minors of the flattenings along the edges of the tree generate the prime ideal of all phylogenetic invariants (their Theorem 4). (2) For any number of states $\kappa$, a set of polynomials defining the variety of the three-taxon tree yields, through flattenings at the nodes, polynomials whose zero set is the phylogenetic variety of any binary tree (their Theorem 17), so that for $\kappa = 2, 3$ explicit set-theoretic defining polynomials exist for every binary tree (their Corollary 18).

A separate target, not part of the results of that paper, is the four-taxon question whether the rank conditions on flattenings identify the tree topology: the flattening along the tree's split has rank at most $\kappa$, and the question is whether the flattenings along the other two splits have larger rank for generic parameters.

## Expansion

1. The general Markov model, its parametrization, and the ideal and variety of phylogenetic invariants.
2. Edge flattenings and their $(\kappa + 1) \times (\kappa + 1)$ minors; Theorem 4 for $\kappa = 2$.
3. Node flattenings and Theorem 17, with Corollary 18.
4. The four-taxon identifiability question, to be stated and proved from a source that treats it.
