# Linear independence and characterization of the canonical split summands

Let $d$ be a metric on a finite set $X$ (symmetric, vanishing on the diagonal, satisfying the triangle inequality, with $d(x, y) = 0$ allowed for $x \ne y$), and let $d = d_1 + \dots + d_k + d_0$ be its [canonical split decomposition](../../not-yet-classified/split-decomposition/exists-unique-canonical-decomp.md), with split metrics $d_1, \dots, d_k$ and split-prime residue $d_0$ (Bandelt and Dress 1992). Dress, Huber and Moulton (2001, Section 4) record the following further properties: the split metrics $d_1, \dots, d_k$ are linearly independent, and so are $d_1, \dots, d_k, d_0$ when $d_0 \ne 0$; and, up to scaling, $d_1, \dots, d_k$ are exactly the split metrics $d'$ for which $d - d'$ is a metric and $d'$, $d - d'$ form a coherent decomposition of $d$. For a tree-like metric, where $d_0 = 0$ by [total split-decomposability](../../not-yet-classified/split-decomposition/canonical-decomp-residue-eq-zero-of-tree.md), the summands $d_1, \dots, d_k$ correspond one to one to the edges of positive length of a weighted tree realizing $d$. An edge of length zero contributes nothing to $d$, so the decomposition recovers the realizing tree only after contracting its zero-length edges, and recovers the tree itself only when every internal edge has positive length.
