# Cherries and pitchforks in random phylogenetic trees

Cherries (two leaves sharing a parent) and pitchforks (a cherry plus a sibling leaf) are the simplest tree-shape statistics used to test models of diversification. Classifying pendant edges by the local shape they belong to turns leaf-by-leaf tree growth under the Yule–Harding–Kingman (YHK) and proportional-to-distinguishable-arrangements (PDA) models into an extended Pólya urn, whose strong law and central limit theorem give the joint asymptotic distribution of the two counts.
