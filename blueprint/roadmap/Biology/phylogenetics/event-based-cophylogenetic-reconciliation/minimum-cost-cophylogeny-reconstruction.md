# Minimum-cost cophylogeny reconstruction

**Setting.** Let $H$ be a rooted binary host tree and $P$ a rooted binary associate (parasite) tree, with a tip map $\varphi$ sending each leaf of $P$ to the leaf of $H$ it is associated with. A *reconstruction* maps every node of $P$ to a vertex or edge of $H$, consistently with $\varphi$ at the leaves and with ancestry, and thereby explains each associate divergence by one of four events: *cospeciation* (associate and host diverge together), *duplication* (the associate diverges within one host lineage), *host switch* (one daughter lineage moves to another host edge existing at the same time), and *loss* (an associate lineage is absent from a descendant host lineage); some formulations add *failure to diverge*. For event costs $c_{\mathrm{cosp}}, c_{\mathrm{dup}}, c_{\mathrm{sw}}, c_{\mathrm{loss}}$, the cost of a reconstruction is $\sum_e c_e\, N_e$ with $N_e$ the number of events of type $e$. Host switches must be time-consistent: a switch may only go to a host edge contemporaneous with its source.

**Proposition.**

1. (Intractability) Finding a minimum-cost time-consistent reconstruction, with host switches allowed, is NP-complete.
2. (Fixed timing) If a total order of the internal nodes of $H$ (a dating of host divergences into time zones) is fixed, a minimum-cost reconstruction consistent with that order can be computed in polynomial time by dynamic programming over the nodes of $P$ and the time zones of $H$.
3. (Search over timings) Hence a minimum-cost reconstruction over all timings equals the minimum, over total orders of the internal nodes of $H$ consistent with its ancestry, of the fixed-timing optimum; heuristics search this space of orders (for example with a genetic algorithm) and evaluate each order exactly.
4. (Significance) The cost $C^\ast$ of the best reconstruction is compared with the distribution of best costs for randomized instances, either with the tip map permuted or with associate trees of the same size drawn at random (for example from a Yule model); a small proportion of randomized costs at or below $C^\ast$ is evidence for congruence beyond chance.

## Expansion

(1) is proved by reduction from a known NP-complete problem; the difficulty comes from the global time-consistency of host switches, which (2) removes by fixing the order of host divergences, after which each associate node can be placed zone by zone with a recurrence over its two children. (3) is immediate from (1) and (2), and is the design of the Jane software: a genetic algorithm proposes host-node orders and the dynamic program scores each one, returning near-optimal solutions on data sets with more than a hundred associate pairs. Results depend on the cost scheme. The source compared three: cospeciation $0$ and all other events $1$ (the TreeMap scheme, in which a duplication contributes $2$ because both daughter lineages count), costs $(0, 1, 1, 2)$ for cospeciation, duplication, host switch and loss, and cospeciation $-1$ with all other events $0$, which maximizes the number of inferred cospeciations. Distance-based tests such as ParaFit, a permutation test on principal coordinates of the patristic distance matrices of both trees together with the association matrix, measure overall congruence but do not identify the events. For nearly 200 interacting fig and pollinator species pairs, ParaFit rejected random association, and under the TreeMap scheme Jane 2 inferred $198$ cospeciations, $204$ duplications, $102$ host switches and $61$ losses (optimal cost $367$); under every cost scheme the number of cospeciations exceeded that of randomized instances.
