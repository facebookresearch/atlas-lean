# Segregating sites and phylogenetic diversity

Population geneticists measure diversity of a set of sampled taxa by the probability that a site segregates among them, phylogeneticists by the total branch length spanning them. For bi-allelic sites evolving on one tree the two families of measures share the same combinatorial structure, both are determined by their values on subsets of even size, and under a symmetric substitution model they are linked by an explicit invertible transform that does not depend on the tree, a diversity analogue of the Hadamard transform.
