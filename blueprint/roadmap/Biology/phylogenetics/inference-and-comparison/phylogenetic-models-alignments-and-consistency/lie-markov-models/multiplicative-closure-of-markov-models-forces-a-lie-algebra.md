# Multiplicative closure of Markov models forces a Lie algebra

**Setting.** Let $\mathcal L$ be the space of real $k\times k$ matrices with zero column sums, and $\mathcal L^+\subset\mathcal L$ those with non-negative off-diagonal entries, the valid (stochastic) rate matrices of a continuous-time Markov chain on $k$ states, such as nucleotides. A *model* is a subset $\mathcal R^+\subseteq\mathcal L^+$. Consider three properties.

- **(P1)** $\mathcal R^+=\mathcal R\cap\mathcal L^+$, where $\mathcal R\subseteq\mathcal L$ is cut out by finitely many polynomial equations in the matrix entries and is minimal: no set $\mathcal R'\subsetneq\mathcal R$ cut out by polynomial equations has $\mathcal R^+=\mathcal R'\cap\mathcal L^+$. (Without minimality the theorem fails: for two states, $\mathcal R=\{a^2=b^2\}$ in the off-diagonal entries $a,b$ satisfies the other conditions, but $\mathcal R\ne\operatorname{span}(\mathcal R^+)=\{a=b\}$.)
- **(P2)** $\mathcal R^+$ is closed under non-negative scaling, $\alpha Q\in\mathcal R^+$ for all $\alpha\ge0$.
- **(P3)** $\overline{\mathcal R}\subseteq\mathcal R$. Here $\mathcal M$ is the monoid generated by $\{e^Q:Q\in\mathcal R^+\}$, and $\overline{\mathcal R}=\{\alpha\log M:\alpha\ge0,\ M\in\mathcal M\}$ is the set of scaled matrix logarithms, where the logarithm series converges. Equivalently, products of substitution matrices of the model are again (formally) generated by rate matrices satisfying the model's constraints.

**Theorem.** Suppose $\mathcal R^+$ satisfies (P1). Then $\mathcal R^+$ satisfies (P2) and (P3) if and only if $\mathcal R=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}(\mathcal R^+)$ and this linear space is a real Lie algebra, closed under $[Q,Q']=QQ'-Q'Q$. Consequently, the set of transition matrices of a model is multiplicatively closed exactly when the model is a *Lie Markov model*.

## Proof sketch

Multiplicative closure is natural in phylogenetics. Composing substitution processes along consecutive branches, or averaging rates over time, should not leave the model. For a linear space of generators, closure of the matrix group under products is equivalent to closure of the generators under commutators, by the Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff formula $\log(e^{\epsilon Q}e^{\epsilon Q'})=\epsilon(Q+Q')+\tfrac{\epsilon^2}2[Q,Q']+\dots$. In the stochastic setting the model is only a semi-algebraic cone, cut out by inequalities, so the standard argument does not apply directly. The key observation is that logarithms of products may be non-stochastic, yet under (P3) they must still satisfy the model's polynomial constraints. Expanding $\log(e^{\epsilon Q}e^{\epsilon Q'})$ in $\epsilon$ then forces the constraint set to contain sums and commutators of model generators. A standard model that fails the condition is the Hasegawa–Kishino–Yano (HKY) model of DNA substitution. Its defining constraints include non-linear ones such as $q_{12}q_{23}=q_{21}q_{13}$, and an explicit numerical example shows that $\log(e^{Q_1}e^{Q_2})$ for two HKY rate matrices is not an HKY rate matrix. The same non-linearity affects time-reversible models in general.
