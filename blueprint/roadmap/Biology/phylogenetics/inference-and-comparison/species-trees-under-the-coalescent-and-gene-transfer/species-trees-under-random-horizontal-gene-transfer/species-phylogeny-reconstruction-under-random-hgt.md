# Species phylogeny reconstruction from distorted gene trees under random HGT

**Model.** Let $T_s$ be a rooted binary ultrametric species phylogeny (all leaves contemporaneous) on $n$ leaves with branch durations $\tau(e)$; two points of $T_s$ are *contemporaneous* if they are at the same time distance from the root. Each branch $e$ carries an HGT rate $\lambda(e)$, and the substitution rate $\mu$ is the same constant on all branches and genes. A gene tree $T_g$ is generated as follows: recipient locations are a Poisson process of rate $\lambda(e)$ along each branch; for a recipient $x$ a donor $y$ is chosen uniformly among the locations contemporaneous with $x$; processing events chronologically from the root, each transfer is an SPR move that prunes the gene subtree below $x$ and regrafts it at $y$. Each gene-tree edge, which maps to a (partial) species branch over a time interval of length $\ell$, gets length $\mu\,\ell$, so gene trees are ultrametric. The data are, for $N$ independent genes, **$\epsilon$-distortions** of the gene trees: rooted binary trees on the same leaves that agree with $T_g$ up to suppression of degree-$2$ vertices, with leaf-to-leaf path lengths within $\epsilon$ of those of $T_g$.

**Bounded-rates model.** Constants $0\le\rho_\lambda\le1$, $0<\rho_\tau\le1$, $0<\bar\tau,\bar\lambda<\infty$ with, for all branches $e$, $\rho_\lambda\bar\lambda \le \lambda(e)\le\bar\lambda$ and $\underline\tau := \rho_\tau\bar\tau\le\tau(e)\le\bar\tau$.

**Theorem.**

1. (Reconstruction at low HGT rate.) Fix the constants above and $0\le\epsilon<\underline\tau\mu/2$. If $\bar\lambda$ is a sufficiently small constant (independent of $n$), the topology of $T_s$ can be reconstructed with probability at least $1 - 1/\mathrm{poly}(n)$ from $\epsilon$-distortions of $N = \Omega(\log n)$ independent gene trees.
2. (Impossibility at high HGT rate.) With $N = O(\log n)$ genes, there are constants $\rho_\tau,\rho_\lambda,\bar\tau$ and a constant $\bar\lambda$ large enough such that for every $n$ there are two species phylogenies that produce the same $N$ gene trees with probability at least $1/2$.

So, up to a constant in the transfer rate, $\Theta(\log n)$ gene trees are what a constant rate of transfer per branch allows.

## Expansion

At low constant HGT rate each gene tree is a few SPR moves away from $T_s$ near any given node, so for a pair of nodes $u, v$ of $T_s$ a typical gene tree contains, with probability close to $1$, real subtrees that are copies of "diluted" versions of the species subtrees below $u$ and $v$ (some leaves transferred away), and the graph distance between their roots estimates the species-tree distance. The algorithm reconstructs the species tree bottom-up from cherries, at each level identifying diluted subtrees in polynomial time and taking a majority over the $\Omega(\log n)$ genes, which gives the high-probability guarantee by a union bound over pairs; the bound $\epsilon<\underline\tau\mu/2$ lets single-linkage clustering of the distorted distances recover the bottom levels. The impossibility result couples two complete binary phylogenies that differ by swapping two subtrees of size $n^{1/3}$ at depth $\frac23\log_2 n$: with transfers only inside those subtrees at a large constant rate, each gene tree is identical under the two phylogenies with probability $1 - O(n^{-1/6})$, so $O(\log n)$ genes coincide simultaneously with probability at least $1/2$.
