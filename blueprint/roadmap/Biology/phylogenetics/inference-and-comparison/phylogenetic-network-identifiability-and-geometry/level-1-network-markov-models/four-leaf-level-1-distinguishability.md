# Distinguishability of four-leaf level-1 semi-directed networks

Let $N_1 \ne N_2$ be four-leaf level-1 semi-directed networks, each a quartet tree, a single-triangle network, a double-triangle network or a 4-cycle network, and impose the Jukes-Cantor, Kimura 2-parameter or Kimura 3-parameter constraints. Writing $V_N$ for the network variety (Lemma 1 of the source):

1. If $N_1$ and $N_2$ are both trees, they are distinguishable.
2. If both are single-triangle networks with different leaf-labelled unrooted skeletons, they are distinguishable.
3. If $N_1$ is a $k_1$-cycle network with $k_1 \le 4$ and $N_2$ is a tree or a $k_2$-cycle network with $k_2 < k_1$, then $V_{N_1} \not\subseteq V_{N_2}$.
4. If both are 4-cycle networks, they are distinguishable.
5. If $N_1$ is a double-triangle network and $N_2$ is a single-triangle network or a tree, then $V_{N_1} \not\subseteq V_{N_2}$.
6. If $N_1$ is a double-triangle network and $N_2$ is a 4-cycle network, they are distinguishable.
7. If both are double-triangle networks with different leaf-labelled unrooted skeletons, they are distinguishable.

These seven items are the complete Lemma 1 of the source. Two single-triangle, or two double-triangle, networks with the same leaf-labelled unrooted skeleton have the same variety under these constraints, so items 2 and 7 cannot be extended to them.

## Expansion

Under these constraints the variety of a triangle or double-triangle semi-directed network depends only on its unrooted skeleton, because all three-leaf triangle networks have the same ideal and a toric fiber product argument propagates this (Section 3). Each item is then checked by exhibiting invariants of one model that do not vanish on the other, partly from earlier Jukes-Cantor computations and partly from new ones; the results are tabulated in Table 1 of the source. Items 6 and 7 use one invariant $g_3$ that vanishes on exactly one double-triangle variety and on no 4-cycle variety, together with invariants vanishing on a 4-cycle variety but on no double-triangle variety (supplementary computations).
