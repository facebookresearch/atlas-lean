# Generic identifiability of triangle-free level-1 semi-directed networks

A level-1 semi-directed network is one in which every cycle of the unrooted skeleton contains exactly one reticulation vertex; it is triangle-free if every such cycle has length greater than three.

**Theorem** (Theorem 2 of the source). Under the Jukes-Cantor, Kimura 2-parameter or Kimura 3-parameter constraints, the network parameter of a network-based Markov model is generically identifiable with respect to the class of models whose network parameter is an $n$-leaf triangle-free level-1 semi-directed network with a fixed number $r \ge 0$ of reticulation vertices.

The key step (Lemma 8): if $N_1 \ne N_2$ are $n$-leaf triangle-free level-1 semi-directed networks on $X$ with $r(N_1) \ge r(N_2)$, then $V_{N_1} \not\subseteq V_{N_2}$.

Consequently, from a generic site-pattern distribution of such a model one can recover the unrooted skeleton of the network together with its reticulation vertices and reticulation edges; the root position is not identifiable.

## Expansion

Lemma 8 is proved by induction on $n$. For $n \le 4$, triangle-freeness forces each network to be a tree or a 4-cycle network, and the four-leaf lemma applies. For larger $n$, combinatorial lemmas on cut-edges, common splits and refinements of cycles (Lemmas 2 and 4-7) produce a four-leaf subset $A$, or a smaller leaf set, on which the restrictions already satisfy non-containment, and the restriction lemma lifts it to $V_{N_1} \not\subseteq V_{N_2}$. Applying Lemma 8 in both directions when $r(N_1) = r(N_2)$ gives distinguishability, hence generic identifiability. The earlier single-cycle case is Theorem 1 of the source, due to Gross and Long (2017) and Hollering and Sullivant (2020). Identifiability of networks from data generated under the multispecies coalescent is a separate question, recorded as a [research direction](../../identifiability-of-phylogenetic-networks-under-the-coalescent.md); this result does not use it.
