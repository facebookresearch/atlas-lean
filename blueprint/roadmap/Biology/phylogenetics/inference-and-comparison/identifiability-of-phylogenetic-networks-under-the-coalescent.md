# Identifiability of phylogenetic networks under the multispecies coalescent

This node records a research direction: which features of networks with gene flow (introgression or admixture) are identifiable from data generated under the multispecies coalescent, beyond the network classes already understood.

## What would settle it

Fix a class of networks and the data type (quartet concordance factors, gene-tree topologies, or metric gene trees) together with the gene-tree model. Then prove an explicit characterization of when two networks in the class give the same distribution of the data, or prove generic identifiability modulo exactly those equivalences, with the exceptional parameter set described. A statement that every network is identifiable is false: the cited work exhibits networks that the data cannot distinguish.

## Status

No cited source poses this question in the form above; it is a research direction motivated by the cited results. This page does not certify the status or novelty of any particular formulation.

Allman, Ané, Baños and Rhodes obtain identifiability results for large subclasses of galled tree-child semidirected networks from quartet concordance factor data under several gene-tree models, with the strongest results from two or more samples per taxon, and give examples in which parts of a network are not identifiable from quartet concordance factors.

This page records a research direction and asserts no theorem or present-status certification.
