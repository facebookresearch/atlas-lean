# Recoverability of the species tree under random lateral gene transfer

**Random LGT model.** Let $T_s$ be a rooted, time-calibrated species phylogeny with $n^+$ extant leaves (and possibly extinct lineages), branch durations $\tau(e)$ and per-branch LGT rates $\lambda(e)$. Write $\Lambda(e)=\lambda(e)\tau(e)$ for the expected number of transfers on $e$ and

$$\boldsymbol\Lambda=\sum_{e\in E(T_s|L_s^+)}\Lambda(e)$$

for the total LGT weight of the extant phylogeny. A gene tree is generated by:

1. **Recipient locations:** a Poisson process of rate $\lambda(e)$ along each branch $e$.
2. **Donor locations:** for a recipient location $x$, a donor $y$ is chosen uniformly among the locations contemporaneous to $x$ whose most recent common ancestor with $x$ is at time distance at most $R\in(0,\infty]$ ($R=\infty$: any contemporaneous lineage); the subtree below $x$ is pruned and regrafted at $y$ (an SPR move), transfers being applied chronologically from the root.
3. **Taxon sampling:** each extant leaf is kept independently with probability $p\in(0,1]$.

Genes evolve independently. The **bounded-rates model** requires $\rho_\lambda\bar\lambda\le\lambda(e)\le\bar\lambda$ on all branches and $\rho_\tau\bar\tau\le\tau^+(e)\le\bar\tau$ on extant branches, for constants $\rho_\lambda,\rho_\tau\in(0,1)$, $\bar\tau<\infty$, with $\bar\lambda$ allowed to depend on $n^+$. The **Yule model** draws $T_s$ from a pure-birth process with LGT rates bounded as above.

**Estimator (quartet majority).** For each four-tuple $X$ of extant taxa, take the most frequent quartet topology among the gene trees that display $X$; if the resulting quartet set is compatible, output the unique tree displaying it.

**Theorem.** With $N$ independent gene tree topologies:

1. (Bounded rates, $R=\infty$) If $\boldsymbol\Lambda=O(n^+/\log n^+)$, the extant species tree topology is recovered with high probability from $N=\Omega(\log n^+)$ gene trees.
2. (Sharpness) With $N=O(\log n^+)$, recovery with high probability is impossible in general when $\boldsymbol\Lambda=\Omega(n^+\log\log n^+)$.
3. (Yule, $R=\infty$) With probability arbitrarily close to $1$ over the species tree, recovery holds with high probability from $N=\Omega(\log n)$ gene trees if $\boldsymbol\Lambda=O(n/\log n)$.
4. (Preferential LGT) If $0<R<\log n^+$, recovery holds with high probability from $N=\Omega(\log n^+)$ gene trees if $\boldsymbol\Lambda=O(n^+/R)$; a similar statement holds under the Yule model.

Thus up to a logarithmic factor, a tree-like trend is statistically identifiable even when each gene experiences a constant expected number of transfers per branch, i.e. a total number of transfers linear in the number of species.

## Expansion

Fix a quartet $X$ of the species tree. Its gene-tree topology can differ from the species quartet only if some transfer hits the subtree spanned by $X$ (a "miss" otherwise leaves the quartet intact). With $R=\infty$ the donor is uniform among roughly $n$ contemporaneous lineages, so a transfer that does hit the quartet's paths produces each of the two wrong topologies with small, symmetric probability, while the probability that some transfer touches the quartet's paths is controlled by the LGT weight on those paths. Bounding this weight (the maximum quartet weight) under the bounded-rates assumptions shows that, when $\boldsymbol\Lambda=O(n/\log n)$, the correct quartet therefore keeps a frequency bounded away above the wrong ones, and an Azuma–Hoeffding bound plus a union bound over the $\binom n4$ quartets gives correctness of all majority quartets with $N=\Omega(\log n)$. Under the Yule model the quartet path weights are controlled with high probability over the random tree. The lower bound exhibits species trees for which, at $\boldsymbol\Lambda=\Omega(n\log\log n)$, some quartet is disrupted in a constant fraction of genes so that $O(\log n)$ gene trees cannot distinguish it from an alternative resolution.
