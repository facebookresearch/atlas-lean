# No bounded-size obstructions for 8-state perfect phylogeny

**Characters and perfect phylogenies.** Let $X$ be a finite set of taxa. For a positive integer $r$, an *$r$-state character* on $X$ is a partition $\chi = S_1 | \dots | S_{r'}$ of $X$ with $r' \le r$ (empty parts are ignored). A *tree on $X$* is an unrooted tree whose leaves are bijectively labelled by $X$; for $S \subseteq X$, $T[S]$ is the minimal subtree containing $S$. $T$ *displays* $\chi$ if the subtrees $T[S_1], \dots, T[S_{r'}]$ are pairwise vertex-disjoint (equivalently, the vertices of $T$ can be partitioned into connected classes $V_i$ with $V_i \cap X = S_i$). A set $C$ of characters is *compatible*, and $T$ is a *perfect phylogeny* for $C$, if $T$ displays every $\chi \in C$.

**Local obstructions conjecture.** For each $r \ge 1$ there is an integer $f(r)$ such that, for every finite $X$ and every set $C$ of $r$-state characters on $X$, $C$ is compatible iff every subset of at most $f(r)$ characters of $C$ is compatible. It holds for $r = 2$ with $f(2) = 2$ (pairwise compatibility of binary characters, Buneman 1971) and for $r = 3$ with $f(3) = 3$; if $f(r)$ exists then $f(r) \ge \lfloor r/2 \rfloor \lceil r/2 \rceil + 1$.

**Theorem.** For every integer $t$ there is a set $C$ of $8$-state characters such that $C$ is incompatible but every subset of at most $t$ characters of $C$ is compatible. Concretely, for every even $n \ge 4$ there is an $8$-state character set on $2n$ taxa with $2n - 4$ characters that has no perfect phylogeny, while removing any single character leaves a compatible set. Hence $f(8)$ does not exist, and neither does $f(r)$ for any $r \ge 8$.

## Expansion

The construction uses taxa $a_1, \dots, a_n, b_1, \dots, b_n$ and two lobster trees $A$ and $B$: both hang cherries off a central path in index order, but every taxon has a different sibling in $A$ than in $B$ (in $A$ the path runs $a_1 \to a_n$ with cherries $(b_1,b_2), (a_2,a_3), (b_3,b_4), \dots$; in $B$ the roles of $a$ and $b$ are swapped). The characters $\chi_j$, $\phi_j$ propagate structure along the path: roughly, if one segment of a compatible tree looks like $A$ then so does the next, and likewise for $B$, so $A$ and $B$ are the only trees compatible with all of them. Two end characters $\Omega_A = a_1b_1b_2 \mid \{a_2\} \cup X_{\ge 3}$ and $\Omega_B = X_{\le n-2} \cup \{b_{n-1}\} \mid a_{n-1}a_nb_n$ demand $A$-structure at one end and $B$-structure at the other, which makes the whole set incompatible. Removing any one character breaks the propagation at one position, and then $A$, $B$, or a hybrid tree $A_iB$ or $A^iB$ (following $A$ on one side of position $i$ and $B$ on the other) is a perfect phylogeny. Since $n$ is arbitrary, minimal incompatible subsets have unbounded size. A consequence is that the fixed-parameter approach to near-perfect phylogeny that works for binary characters through $f(2)$ cannot be extended through bounded obstructions for $r \ge 8$.
