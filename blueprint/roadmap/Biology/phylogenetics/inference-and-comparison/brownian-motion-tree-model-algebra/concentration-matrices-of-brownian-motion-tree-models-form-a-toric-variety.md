# Concentration matrices of Brownian motion tree models form a toric variety

Let $T$ be a rooted tree with leaves $1,\dots,n$ and vertex set $V$, and let $\tilde T$ be the unrooted tree obtained by attaching a new leaf $0$ at the root. In the Brownian motion tree model with non-negative parameters $\theta_v$, $v \in V$, the leaf values $(Y_1,\dots,Y_n)$ are centred Gaussian with covariances

$$
\sigma_{ij} = \sum_{v \le \operatorname{lca}(i,j)} \theta_v ,
$$

the sum running over the vertices on the path from the root to the last common ancestor of $i$ and $j$; let $\mathcal{L}_T$ be the linear space of symmetric matrices of this form. For a concentration matrix $K = \Sigma^{-1} = (\kappa_{ij})$ introduce the coordinates

$$
p_{ij} = -\kappa_{ij} \ (1 \le i < j \le n), \qquad p_{0i} = \sum_{j=1}^n \kappa_{ij} \ (1 \le i \le n),
$$

in which $K$ is the reduced Laplacian of an edge-weighted complete graph on $\{0,1,\dots,n\}$. Let $I_{\tilde T}$ be the toric ideal generated by the binomials $p_{ik}p_{jl} - p_{il}p_{jk}$ for all quadruples $i,j,k,l \in \{0,\dots,n\}$ whose induced four-leaf subtree of $\tilde T$ has cherries $\{i,j\}$ and $\{k,l\}$.

**Theorem.**

1. The reciprocal variety $\mathcal{L}_T^{-1}$ of concentration matrices, in the coordinates $p$, coincides with the toric variety of $I_{\tilde T}$.
2. The set $\mathcal{L}_{T,\ge}^{-1}$ of concentration matrices of the model itself is the set of positive definite $K$ with $p_{ij} \ge 0$ for all $0 \le i < j \le n$, $p_{ik}p_{jl} = p_{il}p_{jk} = p_{ij}p_{kl}$ for every star quartet $ijkl$ of $\tilde T$, and $p_{ik}p_{jl} = p_{il}p_{jk} \le p_{ij}p_{kl}$ for every trivalent quartet $ij|kl$ of $\tilde T$.

## Expansion

Via the Farris transform the covariance cone $\mathcal{L}_{T,\ge}$ is the cone of tree metrics on $\tilde T$, with $\theta_v$ the edge lengths. A trek expansion shows that $p_{ij}\det\Sigma$ is a sum of distinct monomials in the $\theta_v$, indexed by trek systems between $i$ and $j$; the binomial relations on quartets mirror the four-point condition for tree metrics, with the inequality on trivalent quartets playing the role of the four-point inequality. Toricity makes the model's likelihood geometry accessible: the maximum likelihood degree of the binary-tree model with $n = 2,\dots,8$ leaves is $1,1,5,17,61,233,917$, and of the star-tree model with $n = 2,\dots,9$ is $1,7,21,51,113,239,493,1003$.
