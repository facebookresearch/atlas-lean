# The space of phylogenetic trees is CAT(0)

Billera, Holmes and Vogtmann (2001) construct the space $\mathcal T_n$ of metric trees with leaves labelled $1, \dots, n$ and a root labelled $0$: a tree with $r$ interior edges ($r = n - 2$ for binary trees) and edge lengths $l_1, \dots, l_r$ is a point of the open orthant $(0, \infty)^r$ of its combinatorial type, and boundary points of the closed orthant, where some lengths are $0$, are identified with the trees obtained by shrinking those edges (Section 2). They prove that $\mathcal T_n$ with its natural path metric is a CAT(0) space (Lemma 4.1). In the proof, subdividing each orthant into unit cubes with integral vertices makes $\mathcal T_n$ a cubical complex; by a theorem of Gromov a cubical complex is CAT(0) if and only if the link of every vertex is a flag complex, and the link of a vertex in an orthant of dimension $k$ is the $k$-fold suspension of the subcomplex of the link of the origin spanned by the partitions compatible with that orthant's $k$ partitions, which is flag because compatibility of a set of partitions is a pairwise condition. Consequently geodesics between trees are unique, and in any CAT(0) space centroids of finite sets, defined by iterating the passage to centroids of subsets of size one less, starting from midpoints of geodesics, exist, and the centroid function is convex (Theorem 4.1).

## Expansion

1. The construction of $\mathcal T_n$ from orthants indexed by sets of pairwise compatible partitions of $\{0, \dots, n\}$, the gluing along faces, and the path metric.
2. The link of the origin as a flag complex, and the links of the other vertices as iterated suspensions; the alternative proof via the cone on the link and Berestovskii's theorem.
3. Uniqueness of geodesics, and the geodesic between two trees through the cone point when their angle is at least $\pi$ (Section 4.2).
4. Theorem 4.1: existence and convexity of centroids by induction on the size of the set.
