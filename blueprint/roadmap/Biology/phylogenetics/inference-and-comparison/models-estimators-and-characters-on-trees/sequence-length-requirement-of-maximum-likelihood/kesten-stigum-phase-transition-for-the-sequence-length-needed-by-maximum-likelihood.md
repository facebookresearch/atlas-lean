# Kesten–Stigum phase transition for the sequence length needed by maximum likelihood

**Setting.** A phylogeny is a binary tree with $n$ labelled leaves, internal vertices of degree $3$, and edge weights (branch lengths) $w_e$. Fix $0<\Delta<f\le g<\infty$ and let $\mathcal{Y}^{(n)}_{f,g}[\Delta]$ be the phylogenies with $n$ leaves whose branch lengths lie in $[f,g]$ and are multiples of $\Delta$ ($f$, $g$ and $\Delta$ are known to the method). Under the two-state symmetric (Cavender–Farris–Neyman) model, a uniform state in $\{-1,+1\}$ at the root is propagated along each edge $e$, flipping with probability $\tfrac12(1-e^{-w_e})$; each site of an alignment is an independent draw of the leaf states. A reconstruction method $\Psi$ has *sequence-length requirement* $k_0(\Psi,n)$ if it is the smallest sequence length such that, for every $T\in\mathcal{Y}^{(n)}_{f,g}[\Delta]$, that many i.i.d. sites suffice to output the leaf-labelled topology of $T$ with probability at least $1-\delta$. Let $\Psi_{ML}$ return a maximizer of the likelihood of the sites over $T\in\mathcal{Y}^{(n)}_{f,g}[\Delta]$ (the source's (2)).

**Theorem.** Let $0<\Delta<f<g^*:=\ln\sqrt2$. Then

$$k_0(\Psi_{ML},n)=\begin{cases}O(\log n), & g<g^*,\\ \mathrm{poly}(n), & g\ge g^*.\end{cases}$$

The previous best bound for maximum likelihood was $e^{O(n)}$. The threshold $g^*$ is the Kesten–Stigum bound for root state reconstruction: on a binary tree with branch length $g$, the second eigenvalue $\theta=e^{-g}$ of the transition matrix satisfies $2\theta^2>1$ exactly when $g<\ln\sqrt2$. The logarithmic rate is optimal up to constants for any method, since $\Theta(\log n)$ sites give $e^{\Theta(n\log n)}$ datasets, the order of the number of phylogenies. Distance methods such as Neighbor-Joining can require exponentially long sequences for any $g$.

## Expansion

The polynomial bound follows by comparison with distance-based short-quartet methods, which need polynomially many sites: on the event that empirical distances are accurate on short quartets, a likelihood argument shows that any tree with a higher likelihood than the truth must agree with the true tree on all short quartets, hence equal it. The logarithmic bound in the Kesten–Stigum regime confirms Steel's conjecture for maximum likelihood. Root states of deep subtrees can then be estimated from leaves with error bounded away from $1/2$, so a tree with $n$ leaves is effectively shallow. The proof compares the likelihood of the true phylogeny with that of any competitor by decomposing both into matching restricted subtrees and quartets: at least one quartet where they differ must be distinguishable from the leaf distribution, and total-variation bounds based on reconstructed internal states show that $O(\log n)$ sites distinguish all competitors simultaneously, by a union bound over the $e^{O(n\log n)}$ alternatives.
