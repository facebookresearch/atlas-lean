# Consistency of the transition-rate MLE under the two-state symmetric model

**Model.** A binary trait evolves along a rooted phylogenetic tree $\mathcal T$ with $n$ leaves and edge lengths $t_e$ by a continuous-time Markov chain on $\{0,1\}$ with equal rates $0\to1$ and $1\to0$, both equal to the unknown transition rate $\mu^\ast\in[\underline\mu,\overline\mu]\subset(0,\infty)$, started from its stationary distribution; along an edge of length $t$ the trait changes with probability $\frac12(1-e^{-2\mu t})$. Only the leaf states $\mathbf Y$ are observed, and $\hat\mu$ maximizes the log-likelihood $\ell_{\mathcal T,\mu}(\mathbf Y)$ over $[\underline\mu,\overline\mu]$. Traits at different leaves are dependent through shared ancestry, so classical i.i.d. MLE theory does not apply. The *large-tree limit* lets $n\to\infty$ with all leaves observed.

**Assumptions.** With $f(x)=\max\{\log2,\ \log\frac1{1-e^{-2x}}\}$ and $S_{\mathcal T,\mu}=\sum_{e}f(\mu t_e)^2$:

1. (no extremely short edges in aggregate) $S_{\mathcal T,\mu}=O(n^\gamma)$ for some $1\le\gamma<2$;
2. (enough independent contrasts) there are $\Omega(n)$ pairs of leaves whose connecting paths are pairwise node-disjoint and have lengths in a fixed range $[\underline d,\overline d]$.

**Theorem (consistency with rate).** Under Assumptions 1–2, for every $\delta>0$ and every $\alpha\in\big(\max\{\log2/\log(3/2),\gamma\},2\big)$ there is a constant $C$ such that, with probability at least $1-\delta$,

$$|\hat\mu-\mu^\ast|\le C\,\frac{\sqrt{\log n}}{n^{(2-\alpha)/6}} .$$

**Corollary (tree models).** The MLE is consistent if the edge lengths are bounded above and below, if $\mathcal T$ is a Yule (pure-birth) tree, and if $\mathcal T$ is generated by a regular coalescent point process.

## Expansion

The likelihood is invariant under rerooting, and the log-likelihood is uniformly Lipschitz in $\mu$ after dividing by $n$. The proof compares the empirical criterion $R_{\mathcal T,\mu}=\ell_{\mathcal T,\mu^\ast}-\ell_{\mathcal T,\mu}$ with its mean: node-disjoint leaf pairs give independent squared contrasts, which force $\mathbb E R_{\mathcal T,\mu}\ge Cn|\mu-\mu^\ast|^2$ (an information lower bound), while a recursive decomposition over subtrees bounds $\mathrm{Var}\,R_{\mathcal T,\mu}\le Cn^\alpha$ with $\alpha<2$; the threshold $\log2/\log(3/2)$ comes from balancing subtree sizes in that recursion. Chebyshev plus a union bound over a grid of $\mu$ values then concentrate $R/n$ around its mean uniformly, giving the rate. Assumption 1 fails only when many edges are so short that the per-edge likelihood factor degenerates, which does not happen for Yule or coalescent point process trees.
