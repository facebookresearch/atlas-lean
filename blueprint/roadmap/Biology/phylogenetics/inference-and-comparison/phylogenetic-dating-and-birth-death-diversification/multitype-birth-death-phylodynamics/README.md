# Multitype birth–death phylodynamics

A multitype birth–death process describes an epidemic in a population divided into discrete subpopulations (locations, risk groups, or infection stages): infected individuals transmit within and between types, recover, are sampled, and change type. Backward-in-time master equations for the probability of producing no sampled descendants and for the density of the observed subtree give the likelihood of a time-scaled, tip-typed phylogeny, from which type-specific reproduction numbers and migration rates are inferred.
