# Large-tree identifiability of phylogenetic mixtures

**Setting.** Fix $\Theta \ge 2$ and $0 < f \le g < \infty$. A $\Theta$-**mixture model** $(\mathbb T,\nu,Q)$ on leaf set $[n]$ consists of $\Theta$ weighted binary phylogenetic trees $T_1,\dots,T_\Theta$ on the same $n$ leaves, with all branch lengths in $[f,g]$, mixing weights $\nu_\theta \ge \underline\nu > 0$, and a common general time-reversible (GTR) rate matrix $Q$. The rate matrix $Q$ is fixed and known throughout. Each site of an alignment is generated by first drawing $\theta \sim \nu$ and then evolving a character along $T_\theta$ under $Q$. Let $\mathcal D_l[(\mathbb T,\nu,Q)]$ be the resulting distribution of site patterns at the leaves. Let $\Theta\text{-M}(f,g,\underline\nu,n)$ be the set of such models. A measure on it is **permutation-invariant** if it is invariant under relabelling the leaves of each tree separately.

**Theorem.** There are Borel sets $A_n \subseteq \Theta\text{-M}(f,g,\underline\nu,n)$ such that, for every sequence of permutation-invariant probability measures $\lambda_n$, $\lambda_n[A_n] = 1 - o_n(1)$ as $n \to \infty$, and:

1. (Identifiability.) Two models in $\bigcup_n A_n$ with the same rate matrix $Q$ and different collections of tree topologies always induce different leaf distributions.
2. (Asymptotic distinguishability.) For $\Theta = 2$ and $\nu = (1/2,1/2)$, every model in $\bigcup_n A_n$ satisfies

$$\bigl\|\mathcal D_l[T_1,Q] - \mathcal D_l[T_2,Q]\bigr\|_{\mathrm{TV}} = 1 - o_n(1).$$

So two typical large trees produce almost mutually singular single-site distributions.

3. (Efficient reconstruction.) For models in $\bigcup_n A_n$, all $\Theta$ topologies can be reconstructed with probability $1 - o_n(1)$ by an algorithm, given $Q$, running in time polynomial in $n$ and the number of sites $k$, from $k = \mathrm{poly}(n)$ sites.

Identifiability of mixtures can fail for small trees, but these exceptions form an asymptotically negligible fraction of large models.

## Expansion

The key statistic is the empirical correlation between pairs of leaves at bounded evolutionary distance. Under one tree, the number of pairs of leaves that are close in that tree concentrates sharply (Azuma/McDiarmid-type concentration over sites and leaf pairs). Under a "generic" second tree, obtained by an independent relabelling, almost no pair close in $T_1$ is close in $T_2$. A single site therefore reveals, with high probability, which component generated it, through a test based on correlations of nearby leaves. Clustering sites by this test separates the mixture into its components, and each component tree is then reconstructed by a standard distance-based method.
