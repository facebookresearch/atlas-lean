# One step of Wald's argument

Let `Θ` be a metric space, `M` and `M'` real functions on `Θ`, `θ₀` and `θ̂` points of `Θ`, `E ⊆ Θ`
any set and `ε`, `δ` real numbers. Suppose that the maximum of `M` is separated by `δ` away from
`E`, that is, `M θ + δ ≤ M θ₀` for every `θ` with `d(θ, E) ≥ ε`; that `M'` is uniformly within
`δ / 2` of `M`, that is, `|M' θ - M θ| < δ / 2` for every `θ`; and that `θ̂` does at least as well
as `θ₀` for `M'`, that is, `M' θ₀ ≤ M' θ̂`. Then `d(θ̂, E) < ε`, where
`d(θ, E) = inf_{θ' ∈ E} d(θ, θ')`, taken to be `0` when `E` is empty.
