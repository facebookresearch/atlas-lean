# Linear-delay ranking of the top-k support trees of a tree-based network

**Setting.** A rooted binary phylogenetic $X$-network $N$ is a finite directed acyclic graph with a unique root of in-degree $0$ and out-degree $1$ or $2$, leaf set $X$ (vertices of in-degree $1$, out-degree $0$), and every other vertex of in-/out-degree $\{1,2\}$. $N$ is **tree-based** if it has a spanning tree $\tau$ (a **support tree**) obtained from a rooted binary phylogenetic $X$-tree by subdividing arcs. By Francis and Steel, $N[S]$ is a support tree iff the arc set $S\subseteq A(N)$ is **admissible**: (i) $S$ contains every arc $(u,v)$ with in-degree of $v$ equal to $1$ or out-degree of $u$ equal to $1$; (ii) of two arcs with a common head, exactly one is in $S$; (iii) of two arcs with a common tail, at least one is in $S$.

Suppose each arc $a$ is present in the true history with probability $w(a)\in(0,1]$ and score a support tree by its likelihood $f(\tau)=\prod_{a\in A(\tau)}w(a)$, ties broken lexicographically. The **top-$k$ support tree ranking problem** asks, for $k$ at most the number $|\Omega|$ of support trees, for the $k$ highest-ranked support trees $\tau^{(1)},\dots,\tau^{(k)}$ in order.

**Theorem.** The top-$k$ support tree ranking problem can be solved with delay $O(|A(N)|)$ between consecutive outputs, hence in total time $O(k\,|A(N)|)$. Since every support tree has $\Omega(|A(N)|)$ arcs, this is optimal: $\Theta(k\,|A(N)|)$, the same cost as listing $k$ arbitrary support trees.

## Expansion

Hayamizu's structure theorem decomposes a tree-based $N$ uniquely into maximal zig-zag trails $Z_1,\dots,Z_d$ (arc sequences in which consecutive arcs share a head or a tail), each a crown, an N-fence or an M-fence, and the set of support trees factors as a direct product $\Omega=\prod_{i=1}^d\Omega_i$ of the admissible arc sets of the trails: a crown has exactly $2$ admissible sets, an N-fence exactly $1$, and an M-fence with $2m$ arcs exactly $m$, namely the vectors $1(01)^p(10)^q1$ with $p+q=m-1$. Because the likelihood is a product over trails, one first sorts each local family $\Omega_i$; for an M-fence consecutive candidates differ in one pair of arcs, so all local likelihoods, and the local ranking, cost $O(|A(Z_i)|)$ (Proposition 5.1).

Identify each support tree with its vector of local ranks in $\prod_i\{1,\dots,|\Omega_i|\}$; the global ranking is a linear extension of the componentwise order, with $\tau^{(1)}=(1,\dots,1)$, and each next tree differs from an already-output tree by $+1$ in one coordinate (Proposition 5.2). Linking each vector $\tau'\ne\tau^{(1)}$ to the parent obtained by decrementing its first coordinate exceeding $1$ gives a spanning tree $\Gamma$ of the Hasse diagram rooted at $\tau^{(1)}$ (Lemma 5.3). A candidate set $Q_j$ maintained by removing $\tau^{(j-1)}$ and inserting its best child and its next sibling in $\Gamma$ always contains $\tau^{(j)}$ and no earlier output (Lemma 5.4). With $Q_j$ in a priority queue of size at most $k\le 2^{|A(N)|}$, each step costs $O(\log k)+O(|A(N)|)=O(|A(N)|)$ (Theorem 5.5).
