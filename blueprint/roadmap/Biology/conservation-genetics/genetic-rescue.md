# Genetic rescue

The [one-locus heterosis comparison](heterosis-between-populations-equals-s-1-2h-time.md)
compares offspring viability within a randomly sampled population with
crosses of two independently sampled populations. Its advantage requires
both an appropriate dominance effect and nonzero frequency variance; it
is not an unconditional benefit of crossing.
Whitlock and coauthors report substantial heterosis at low to moderate
migration and small population size in a mutation-selection-drift-migration
model, with intermediate mutation and selection effects and recessive
alleles. Those stationary-distribution claims are distinct from the
identity proved on the linked page.

## Expansion

Specify a demographic-genetic model separating added individuals
(demographic rescue), masking of deleterious variants (genetic rescue),
and loss of local adaptation. Its persistence comparison is not supplied
by a one-generation viability difference. A multilocus model must also
state linkage and epistasis before adding single-locus effects.
