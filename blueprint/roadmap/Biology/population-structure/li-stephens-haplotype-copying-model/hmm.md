# The Li–Stephens copying model

Let $R$ be a field, $K$ a natural number and $\rho, \varepsilon \in R$. The Li–Stephens model is the hidden Markov model with hidden states $\{1, \dots, K\}$ (the copied reference haplotype) and [site observations](obs.md) as observations, with uniform initial weights $1/K$ (with $x/0 = 0$), [transition weights](trans.md) $\tau_\rho$ and [emission weights](emit.md) $e_\varepsilon$. Its forward variables for an observation sequence $y$ are $\alpha_0(k) = \frac{1}{K} e_\varepsilon(k, y_0)$ and $\alpha_{t+1}(k) = e_\varepsilon(k, y_{t+1}) \sum_{j} \alpha_t(j)\, \tau_\rho(j, k)$, and its likelihood up to site $t$ is $\sum_k \alpha_t(k)$. Since the panel enters only through the observations, it is a model of the target haplotype conditional on the panel; for $R = \mathbb{R}$, $K \ge 1$, $0 \le \rho, \varepsilon \le 1$ and a biallelic alphabet it is a hidden Markov model of conditional probabilities, and $\sum_k \alpha_t(k)$ is the conditional probability of the target alleles up to site $t$ given the panel.
