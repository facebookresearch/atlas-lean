# Lineage-count distribution of the Yule–Λ nested coalescent

**Model.** Sample $n_k$ individuals (possibly $n_k=\infty$) from each of $s$ species. Backwards in time, gene lineages and species merge as follows. *Lineage mergers:* within a species currently carrying $b$ lineages, each particular set of $k$ of them merges at rate $\lambda_{b,k}=\int_{[0,1]}x^{k-2}(1-x)^{b-k}\Lambda(dx)$, i.e. lineages in a species follow a $\Lambda$-coalescent, $\Lambda$ a probability measure on $[0,1]$. *Species mergers:* each species label dies at rate $c>0$, and its lineages are relabelled to a uniformly chosen remaining species. Reversed in time, the species tree is a Yule tree with speciation rate $c$. Let $X\sim\Lambda$, so $\mathbb E[1/X]=\int x^{-1}\Lambda(dx)$, which is finite exactly when the $\Lambda$-coalescent has dust. Let $L_n(t)$ be the number of blocks at time $t$ of the $\Lambda$-coalescent started from $n$ lineages, and define the map $G_c$ on laws on $\mathbb N^+\cup\{\infty\}$ by: $G_c(\mu)$ is the law of $L_{W_1+W_2}(Y)$ with $W_1,W_2\sim\mu$, $Y\sim\mathrm{Exp}(c)$, all independent.

**Theorem.**
1. If $c<\mathbb E[1/X]<\infty$, the recursive distributional equation $G_c(\nu)=\nu$ has a unique solution $\nu^*_c$ among laws on $\mathbb N^+$ with finite mean.
2. Under the same condition, if every species starts with one sampled lineage, then for fixed $m$, just before the species count drops from $m$ to $m-1$, the lineage counts of the $m$ surviving species are asymptotically independent as $s\to\infty$, each with limit law $\nu^*_c$. If instead each species starts with infinitely many lineages, the counts converge to a law $\nu'_c$ with $\nu'_c(\{\infty\})>0$ if $c>\mathbb P(X=1)$ and $\nu'_c(\{\infty\})=0$ if $c\le\mathbb P(X=1)$; in particular $\nu'_c=\delta_\infty$ when $\mathbb P(X=1)=0$.
3. If $0<c<\mathbb E[(1-(1-X)^{1/2})/X^2]$, $\mathbb E[1/X]<\infty$, and $n^{-p}\mathbb E[(1-(1-X)^n)/X^2]\to0$ for some $p<1$, the equation also has a solution $\bar\nu^*_c$ on $\mathbb N^+$ with **infinite** mean.

## Expansion

When a species dies and merges into another, the receiving species' lineages are the union of two independent pools whose sizes, deep in a large Yule tree, have the same stationary law; the merged pool then coalesces within the species for an $\mathrm{Exp}(c)$ time until the next species event affecting it, which is exactly the fixed-point map $G_c$. With dust, a $\Lambda$-coalescent started from infinitely many lineages keeps infinitely many, so (when $\mathbb P(X=1)=0$) $\delta_\infty$ is a trivial fixed point and uniqueness can only hold among finite-mean laws; the condition $c<\mathbb E[1/X]$ says that within-species coalescence outpaces the doubling caused by species mergers, which keeps the mean finite. This contrasts with the Yule–Kingman nested coalescent, where Kingman's coalescent comes down from infinity and the fixed point is unique on all laws.
