# $k$-spine representation of uniform samples from multitype Galton–Watson trees

**Setting.** Let $\mathbf Z=(\mathbf Z_t)_{t\ge0}$ be a continuous-time multitype Bienaymé–Galton–Watson (MBGW) process with types $[d]=\{1,\dots,d\}$. An individual of type $i$ lives an $\mathrm{Exp}(\alpha_i)$ time and is replaced by a random offspring vector $\mathbf L\in\mathbb Z_+^d$ with law $p_i$. Assume (H): $\mathbf Z$ is *non-simple* (not every reproduction gives exactly one child), *conservative* (no explosion in finite time), and *irreducible* (every type eventually leads to every type). No finite-mean assumption is needed. Let $N_T=\mathbf 1\cdot\mathbf Z_T$ be the population size at time $T$, and $N_T^{\lfloor k\rfloor}=N_T(N_T-1)\cdots(N_T-k+1)$. Start from one individual of type $r$. On $\{N_T\ge k\}$, pick $k$ individuals uniformly without replacement at time $T$, and write $\mathbb P^{(k)}_{\mathrm{unif},T,r}$ for the joint law of the tree and the sample. The *sample genealogy* records the splitting times $\tau_h$ of the ancestral lineages, the type $C_h$ and offspring vector $\mathbf L_{v(h)}$ of each branching ancestor, and the partition $\mathcal P_h$ of the marks among its children.

**Theorem.** For every $\boldsymbol\theta\in\mathbb R_+^d$ there is a change of measure $\mathbb Q^{(k),\boldsymbol\theta}_{T,r}$ under which $k$ marked *spines* start together at the root and evolve as follows. Lineages carrying marks are size-biased and $\boldsymbol\theta$-discounted. At each branching event the marks are split among the children according to explicit weights involving $\ell_i p_r(\boldsymbol\ell)\prod_m\mathbb E_m[e^{-\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf Z_{T-t}}]^{\ell_m}$. Unmarked subtrees are independent copies of $\mathbf Z$, $e^{-\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf Z}$-discounted. The process has the Markov branching property: a vertex of type $i$ carrying $j$ marks at time $t$ generates a subtree with law $\mathbb Q^{(j),\boldsymbol\theta}_{T-t,i}$. For events $A$ determined by time $t$,

$$\mathbb P^{(k)}_{\mathrm{unif},t,r}(A)=\mathbb E_r\big[N_t^{\lfloor k\rfloor}e^{-\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf Z_t}\,\big|\,N_t\ge k\big]\;\mathbb Q^{(k),\boldsymbol\theta}_{t,r}\Big[\frac{\mathbf 1_A}{N_t^{\lfloor k\rfloor}e^{-\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf Z_t}}\Big],$$

and the law of the sample genealogy $\Delta_T(n)$ (with $n$ spine-splitting events) is the mixture

$$\mathbb P^{(k)}_{\mathrm{unif},T,r}(\Delta_T(n))=\frac{1}{(k-1)!\,\mathbb P_r(N_T\ge k)}\int_0^\infty(e^{\phi}-1)^{k-1}\,\mathbb Q^{(k),\phi\mathbf 1}_{T,r}(\Delta_T(n))\,\mathbb E_r\big[N_T^{\lfloor k\rfloor}e^{-\phi\mathbf 1\cdot\mathbf Z_T}\big]\,d\phi .$$

## Expansion

Under $\mathbb Q^{(k),\boldsymbol\theta}$ the $k$ spines end at $k$ distinct individuals at time $T$ with Radon–Nikodym weight proportional to $N_T^{\lfloor k\rfloor}e^{-\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf Z_T}$. The factor $N_T^{\lfloor k\rfloor}$ is exactly the number of ordered $k$-samples, so dividing by it recovers uniform sampling. The discount $e^{-\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf Z_T}$ makes all expectations finite without moment assumptions. The identity $\int_0^\infty(e^\phi-1)^{k-1}e^{-\phi N}\,d\phi=(k-1)!/N^{\lfloor k\rfloor}$ (a Beta integral) then removes the discount after averaging over $\phi$. The result reduces the genealogy of a uniform sample at a fixed time to a Markovian forward-in-time construction. This is the starting point for limiting sample genealogies, the multitype analogues of Kingman-type coalescents, in the subcritical, critical and supercritical regimes, classified by the Perron root $\rho$ of $\mathrm{diag}(\boldsymbol\alpha)(\mathbf M-\mathbf I)$.
