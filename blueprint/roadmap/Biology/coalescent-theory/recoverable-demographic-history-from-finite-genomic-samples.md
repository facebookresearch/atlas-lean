# Recoverable demographic history from finite genomic samples

This node records a research direction: which features of a population-size history can be estimated from a finite sample of genomes, and at what optimal rate. Identifiability from infinite data and estimation from finite data are different questions.

## What would settle it

Fix a coalescent model of the sample, an explicitly restricted class of population-size histories, the observation process (for example the sample frequency spectrum of a finite sample), and the mutation and time-scale conventions. Then prove matching minimax upper and lower bounds for estimating a specified feature of the history over that class, with the loss and the sample-size regime stated. A statement that unrestricted histories are identifiable is false, as the cited work recalls.

## Status

No cited source poses this question in the form above; it is a research direction motivated by the cited results. This page does not certify the status or novelty of any particular formulation.

Bhaskar and Song recall that very different population histories can generate the same expected sample frequency spectrum for arbitrarily large sample sizes. They prove that, when the population size is piecewise defined with each piece from a family of biologically motivated functions, the expected sample frequency spectrum of a sufficiently large sample determines the history, with an explicit sufficient sample size depending on the number and the type of the pieces. These are identifiability results, not rates.

This page records a research direction and asserts no theorem or present-status certification.
