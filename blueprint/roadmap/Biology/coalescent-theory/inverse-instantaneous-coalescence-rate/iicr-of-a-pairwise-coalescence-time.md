# Inverse instantaneous coalescence rate of a pairwise coalescence time

Let $T_2 \ge 0$ be the coalescence time of a sample of two genes, an absolutely continuous random variable with distribution
function $F$, a density $f$ of $T_2$ (a nonnegative function with $F' = f$ almost everywhere) and survival function
$R(t) = \Pr(T_2 \ge t) = 1 - F(t)$. Let $t_* = \sup\{t : R(t) > 0\} \in (0, \infty]$. The **hazard** of $T_2$ is

$$h(t) = \frac{f(t)}{R(t)}, \qquad 0 \le t < t_* ,$$

defined almost everywhere on $\{R > 0\}$ and allowed to vanish: for $T_2 = 1 + \mathrm{Exp}(1)$ one has $f = 0$ and $h = 0$ on
$[0, 1)$. The **inverse instantaneous coalescence rate** (IICR) is its reciprocal, valued in $(0, \infty]$:

$$\mathrm{IICR}(t) = \frac{1}{h(t)} = \frac{R(t)}{f(t)} = -\frac{R(t)}{R'(t)}, \qquad 0 \le t < t_* ,$$

with $\mathrm{IICR}(t) = \infty$ where $f(t) = 0$ (and $1/\infty = 0$). The survival function is reconstructed from the hazard,

$$R(t) = \exp\Bigl(-\int_0^t h(s)\, ds\Bigr) \quad (0 \le t < t_*), \qquad R(t) = 0 \quad (t \ge t_*),$$

the value on $[t_*, \infty)$ being the limit of the exponential as $t \uparrow t_*$ when $t_* < \infty$. So the hazard, equivalently
the IICR, determines the law of $T_2$.

**Proposition (panmixia).** In a single panmictic population whose relative size at time $t$ before the present is $\lambda(t) > 0$
(time measured in units of $2N_0$ generations), the pair coalesces at rate $1/\lambda(t)$ under the
[time-changed coalescent](../site-frequency-spectrum-under-variable-populatio/derivation-of-the-time-changed-coalescent-from-a.md),
so $R(t) = \exp\bigl(-\int_0^t \lambda(s)^{-1} ds\bigr)$ and

$$\mathrm{IICR}(t) = \lambda(t).$$

For any other model (population structure, heterogeneous effective size along the genome) the IICR is still a well-defined
function of the law of $T_2$, but need not equal any population size.

## Expansion

The hazard $h(t) = f(t)/R(t)$ is the instantaneous probability per unit time that two lineages still distinct at $t$ coalesce. Under
panmixia this is exactly the reciprocal relative size, which is why methods inferring a "population size history" from $T_2$
(such as PSMC) estimate the IICR; this identification fails whenever the law of $T_2$ is not generated by a single panmictic
population. The reconstruction integrates $h$, which is finite almost everywhere on $\{R > 0\}$, rather than $1/\mathrm{IICR}$ with an undefined value where $f = 0$: $\log R$ is absolutely continuous on each $[0, t]$ with $t < t_*$, with derivative $-h$ almost everywhere. Under panmixia $h = 1/\lambda$, and the integral is the cumulative coalescence intensity. (Boitard et al. 2022, definition of the IICR and Equation 2, following Mazet et al. 2016, Equations 4–8.)
