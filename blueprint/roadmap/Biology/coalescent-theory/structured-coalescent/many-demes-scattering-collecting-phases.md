# Scattering and collecting phases of the many-demes coalescent

For a population subdivided into a large number of demes, Wakeley and Aliacar (2001, Abstract) state that the genealogy of any sample has two phases: a brief sample-size adjustment, followed by a coalescent process that dominates the history. They show that this holds for metapopulations with extinction and recolonization as well as migration, for any structure in which the number of possible source demes of migrants and colonists of each deme is large, and give the mean numbers of pairwise differences within and between demes. Wakeley (1999, Abstract), assuming symmetric migration among all pairs of a large number of demes, shows that a single abrupt change of deme size, number of demes or migration rate acts on the genealogy exactly as a change of the ancestral effective population size, and derives the expected numbers of sites segregating at given frequencies in a sample from several demes. Mazet, Rodríguez, Grusea, Boitard and Chikhi (2016) use Wakeley's term scattering phase for the initial adjustment and note that strong-migration approximations amount to assuming that this phase is very short: in their $n$-island computations with very large migration rates the inverse instantaneous coalescence rate of two genes from one deme stays near the value for the whole population except in the most recent past.

## Expansion

1. The island model with $D$ demes and its structured coalescent for a sample from a few demes.
2. The scattering phase: coalescence within demes against migration into distinct demes, and the law of the number of lineages that leave it.
3. The collecting phase: a Kingman coalescent for lineages in distinct demes on a time scale of order $D$, with a time change depending on the migration rate.
4. Convergence of the two-phase description as $D \to \infty$, and its extension to extinction and recolonization.
5. A change in deme size, number of demes or migration rate as a change in the ancestral effective size, and the resulting site-frequency predictions.
