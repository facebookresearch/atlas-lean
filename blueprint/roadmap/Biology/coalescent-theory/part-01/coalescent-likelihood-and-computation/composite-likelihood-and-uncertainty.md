# Composite likelihoods for genetic data and their uncertainty

A composite likelihood multiplies component likelihoods, marginal or conditional, such as those of single sites or pairs of sites (Varin, Reid and Firth 2011, Section 2.1). It need not be the full likelihood, and it need not satisfy the second Bartlett identity. Some products are exact: for dependent observations, $p_\theta(x)\,p_\theta(y \mid x)$ is the joint likelihood by the chain rule. Multiplying marginal densities as if the components were independent gives the full likelihood when they are independent, and in general not otherwise. The frequency spectrum of unlinked SNPs gives such an independent-site likelihood (Adams and Hudson 2004, abstract and Introduction), while linkage makes sites dependent, so the corresponding product over linked sites is a composite likelihood, as in the composite-likelihood estimator of the recombination parameter from more than two linked sites built from two-locus sampling probabilities (Hudson 2001, abstract). For a composite score $u(\theta; y)$, Varin, Reid and Firth (2011, Section 2) distinguish the sensitivity matrix $H(\theta) = E_\theta\{-\nabla_\theta u(\theta; Y)\}$ from the variability matrix $J(\theta) = \mathrm{var}_\theta\{u(\theta; Y)\}$, since the second Bartlett identity need not hold, and replace the Fisher information by the Godambe information

$$G(\theta) = H(\theta)J(\theta)^{-1}H(\theta)$$

(equation (2.4)). For $n$ independent replicates, under the usual regularity conditions ($\theta$ identifiable and interior to an open parameter set, the composite log-likelihood twice continuously differentiable in $\theta$ with its second derivatives dominated by an integrable function near $\theta$, the composite maximum likelihood estimator $\hat\theta_{CL}$ consistent, the score satisfying $n^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^n u(\theta; Y_i) \to N_p(0, J(\theta))$, and $H(\theta)$ and $J(\theta)$ finite and nonsingular), one has $\sqrt n(\hat\theta_{CL} - \theta) \to N_p(0, G(\theta)^{-1})$ at the true value $\theta$. The test limits are a separate statement. Write $\theta = (\psi, \tau)$ with a $q$-dimensional parameter of interest $\psi$, consider the hypothesis $H_0 : \psi = \psi_0$, and let $\hat\tau_{CL}(\psi_0)$ maximize the composite likelihood with $\psi$ fixed at $\psi_0$ (the constrained fit at $\psi_0$). When $H_0$ holds, that is, when the true $\theta$ has $\psi = \psi_0$, the Wald statistic built with $G$ and the score statistic evaluated at the constrained fit have $\chi^2_q$ limits, and the composite likelihood ratio statistic $W = 2\{c\ell(\hat\theta_{CL}) - c\ell(\psi_0, \hat\tau_{CL}(\psi_0))\}$, with $c\ell$ the composite log-likelihood, converges to $\sum_{j=1}^q \lambda_j Z_j^2$, with $Z_j$ independent standard normals and $\lambda_j$ the eigenvalues of $A^{-1}B$, where $A = (H^{-1})_{\psi\psi}$ and $B = (G^{-1})_{\psi\psi}$ are the $q \times q$ blocks, indexed by the components $\psi$ of interest, of the inverse matrices $H(\theta)^{-1}$ and $G(\theta)^{-1}$ (equation (2.5)); this limit is not in general $\chi^2_q$, and it is $\chi^2_q$ when $H = J$, since then $A = B$. All three are null distributions (Section 2.3) and do not describe the statistics when the true $\psi$ differs from $\psi_0$: for a normal sample with variance one, the likelihood ratio statistic for mean zero is $n\bar Y^2$, which tends to infinity when the true mean is one. The same authors note that for increasing dimension with fixed sample size, the case relevant to genetics, there is no rigorous proof that the composite maximum likelihood estimator is or is not consistent (Section 6.2).

## Expansion

1. Marginal and conditional components; products of marginal densities taken as if independent, which are full likelihoods for unlinked independent sites; composite likelihoods for linked sites.
2. Sensitivity and variability matrices, and the Godambe information (2.4).
3. Asymptotic normality of the composite maximum likelihood estimator with variance $G^{-1}$, under replication or a central limit theorem for the score of dependent sites along the genome.
4. For $H_0 : \psi = \psi_0$ and the constrained fit at $\psi_0$: the null limits of the Wald and score statistics, the weighted chi-square null limit of the composite likelihood ratio with weights the eigenvalues of $A^{-1}B$ built from blocks of the inverse matrices, and its Satterthwaite-type adjustments.
5. The open consistency question for long genomes with a fixed number of samples.
