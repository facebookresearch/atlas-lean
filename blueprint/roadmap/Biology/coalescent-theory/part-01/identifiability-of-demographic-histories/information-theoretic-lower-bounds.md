# Information-theoretic lower bounds for demographic inference

Terhorst and Song (2015) show that even when a population-size history is identifiable from the expected site-frequency spectrum, its estimation from $s$ independent segregating sites is slow: the minimax error of estimating the size history from the spectrum is at least of order $1/\log s$, for populations that have experienced a bottleneck, and the bound does not depend on the number of sampled individuals. Their Theorem 1 bounds the Kullback–Leibler divergence between the normalized expected spectra of two histories $\eta, \eta'$ that agree up to a time $t_c$ and satisfy $\max_{t > t_c}\eta(t) \le \min_{t > t_c}\eta'(t)$; their Theorem 3 gives an exponentially small bound, independent of $n$, for two histories $\eta, \eta'$ of the piecewise-constant bottleneck family $\mathcal F_{I,J}$ below that satisfy $\max_{t \ge t_I}\eta(t) \le \min_{t \ge t_I}\eta'(t)$; and the histories are normalized by $\eta(0) = N_{\mathrm{fix}}$, since $\eta$ and $\kappa\eta(\cdot/\kappa)$ give the same normalized spectrum.

The family is $\mathcal F_{I,J}$: piecewise-constant histories with $I + J$ epochs, fixed change points $t_1 < \dots < t_{I+J-1}$, and fixed sizes on the first $I$ epochs, the last of which is the smallest (the bottleneck, of size $\varepsilon$); on the next epoch the size is either $h$ or $h + \delta$, with $h > \varepsilon$ and $\delta > 0$, and at each later change point it either stays the same or jumps upward by $\delta$, the later epochs all having a fixed length $\tau_A$, which gives $2^J$ histories; write $\tau_B = t_I - t_{I-1}$ for the duration of the bottleneck. The data are $s$ independent segregating sites, each with a frequency drawn from the normalized expected spectrum of $n$ sampled individuals, and the error of an estimator $\hat\eta$ is measured by the risk $E_\eta\int_0^T|\hat\eta(t) - \eta(t)|\,dt$ over a fixed time horizon $T$ (the untruncated $L^1$ distance is infinite for histories that differ arbitrarily far back). Their Theorem 5 requires $J > 8$ strictly and $T \ge t_{I+J-1} + \tau_A$; at $J = 8$ the bound has a factor $(J - 8)^2$ and gives no positive constant. The hard family is not fixed in advance: it depends on the number $s$ of independent sites, through the bottleneck size $\varepsilon(s) = \tau_B/\log s$ and an increment $\delta$ chosen accordingly, as in their proof of Theorem 5. With this choice, over the bounded subfamily $\{\eta \in \mathcal F_{I,J} : \sup_t\eta(t) < M\}$, the minimax value of this risk, the infimum over estimators of the supremum over the subfamily, is at least $C/\log s$ for a positive constant $C$ that does not depend on $s$ or $n$. The dependence on $s$ is essential: for one fixed finite family of histories with distinct spectra, the laws are separated by a positive distance, and choosing the history whose spectrum is nearest to the empirical frequencies has an error probability that decays exponentially in $s$. A bound for this loss is not a bound for other measures of error. The target is this family, this observation model and risk, the divergence bounds and the minimax lower bound of Theorem 5.

## Expansion

1. The observation model: independent segregating sites with frequencies drawn from the normalized expected spectrum.
2. The bottleneck family and the KL divergence between the induced spectra, as a function of the bottleneck time.
3. The generalized Fano inequality (their Theorem 8 in the published version; Theorem 7 in the arXiv preprint) applied, for $J > 8$, $T \ge t_{I+J-1} + \tau_A$ and each $s$, to a packing of the bounded bottleneck family with $\varepsilon(s) = \tau_B/\log s$ and the increment chosen accordingly: many histories that are pairwise separated in the truncated $L^1$ distance on $[0, T]$ while their spectra have pairwise small KL divergence, giving the minimax lower bound of order $1/\log s$ for the risk $E_\eta\int_0^T|\hat\eta - \eta|\,dt$.
4. The dependence on sample size and the limitations of the bound.
