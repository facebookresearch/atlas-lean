# Binomial identity relating $C(j, k-1)$ to $C(n-k, i-1)$ when $i+j=n$

For natural numbers `n`, `k`, `i` and `j` with `2 ≤ k ≤ n`, `i ≥ 1` and `i + j = n`,

`C(j, k - 1) C(n - 1, i - 1) = C(n - k, i - 1) C(n - 1, k - 1)`.

Under these conditions none of the subtractions goes below `0`, and binomial coefficients are
extended by `C(u, v) = 0` for natural `v > u`: when `k - 1 > j`, both `C(j, k - 1)` and
`C(n - k, i - 1)` vanish (for example at `n = k = 5`, `i = 3`, `j = 2`).
