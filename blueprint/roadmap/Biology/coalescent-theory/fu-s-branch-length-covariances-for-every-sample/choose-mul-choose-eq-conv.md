# Binomial identity relating $C(n-i-1, k-2)$ to $C(n-k, i-1)$

For natural numbers `n`, `k` and `i` with `2 ≤ k ≤ n` and `1 ≤ i < n`,

`C(n - i - 1, k - 2) (n - 1) C(n - 2, i - 1) = (k - 1) C(n - k, i - 1) C(n - 1, k - 1)`.

Under these conditions none of the subtractions goes below `0`, and binomial coefficients are
extended by `C(u, v) = 0` for natural `v > u`: for example at `n = k = 5` and `i = 3` the
factors `C(1, 3)` and `C(0, 2)` vanish and both sides are `0`.
