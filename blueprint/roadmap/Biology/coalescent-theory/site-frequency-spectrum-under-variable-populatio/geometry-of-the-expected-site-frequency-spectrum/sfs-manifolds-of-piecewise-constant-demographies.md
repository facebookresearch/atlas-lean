# Geometry of the expected SFS under piecewise-constant demography

**Setting.** Let $\Pi_k$ be the population size histories (in coalescent units, looking back in time) that are piecewise constant with $k$ epochs: size $y_j>0$ on $[t_{j-1},t_j)$, $0=t_0<t_1<\dots<t_{k-1}<t_k=\infty$. For a sample of size $n$, let $\mathbf c=(c_2,\dots,c_n)$ be the expected first coalescence times of $m=2,\dots,n$ lineages, and let the *coalescence manifold* $\mathcal C_{n,k}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n-1}$ and the *SFS manifold* $\Xi_{n,k}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n-1}$ be the sets of all $\mathbf c$, respectively all expected sample frequency spectra, generated by histories in $\Pi_k$. The SFS is a fixed invertible linear image of $\mathbf c$, so $\Xi_{n,k}$ is a linear image of $\mathcal C_{n,k}$; hats denote normalization to the simplex $\Delta^{n-2}$. (These are semialgebraic sets, not manifolds in the strict sense.)

**Parametrization.** With $x_j=\exp[-(t_j-t_{j-1})/y_j]\in(0,1)$ for $j<k$, $x_0=1$, $x_k=0$, the vector $(x_1,\dots,x_{k-1},y_1,\dots,y_k)$ identifies the history and

$$c_m=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{y_j}{\binom m2}\Bigl(\prod_{i=0}^{j-1}x_i^{\binom m2}\Bigr)\bigl(1-x_j^{\binom m2}\bigr),\qquad m=2,\dots,n .$$

**Theorem.**

*(1)* $\mathcal C_{n,1}=\{\lambda(1,\tfrac13,\dots,1/\binom n2):\lambda>0\}$; $\mathcal C_{2,k}=\{a>0\}$ and $\mathcal C_{3,k}=\{(a,b):0<b<a\}$ for $k\ge2$. For $n=4$ the constant-size normalized SFS is the single point $\widehat\Xi_{4,1}=(\tfrac6{11},\tfrac3{11},\tfrac2{11})$, and $\widehat{\mathcal C}_{4,k}=\widehat{\mathcal C}_{4,3}$ for all $k\ge3$: the interior of the convex hull of the curve $t\mapsto(1,t^2,t^5)/(1+t^2+t^5)$, $0<t<1$.

*(2)* *(Dimension.)* $\dim\widehat{\mathcal C}_{n,k}=0$ for $k=1$ and $\min(2k-2,\,n-2)$ for $k\ge2$; in particular $\mathcal C_{n,k}\subsetneq\mathcal C_{n,k+1}$ for $k<\lceil n/2\rceil$.

*(3)* *(Saturation.)* For each $n\ge2$ there is $\kappa_n$ with $\Xi_{n,k}\subseteq\Xi_{n,\kappa_n}$ for every $k\ge1$, and

$$\lceil n/2\rceil\ \le\ \kappa_n\ \le\ 2n-1 .$$

*(4)* For $k\ge2$ the Zariski closure of $\mathcal C_{n,k}$ is the affine cone over the join of the point $p_n=[1:\tfrac13:\dots:1/\binom n2]$ with the $(k-2)$-nd secant variety of the monomial curve $C_n:[s:t]\mapsto[\binom m2^{-1}s^{\binom n2-\binom m2}t^{\binom m2}]_{m=2}^n$.

Consequently every expected SFS of a sample of size $n$ is already produced by a history with at most $2n-1$ epochs, and the manifolds are bounded by curves along which epoch lengths tend to $0$ or $\infty$; an observed SFS lying outside $\Xi_{n,k}$ has its best fit on this boundary, which explains runaway optimization in demographic inference.

## Expansion

In epoch $j$ the $m$ lineages coalesce at rate $\binom m2/y_j$, so the probability of surviving to the start of epoch $j$ is $\prod_{i<j}x_i^{\binom m2}$ and the conditional mean time spent in epoch $j$ before coalescing contributes $y_j(1-x_j^{\binom m2})/\binom m2$. Each $\mathbf c$ is thus a positive combination of points $p_n$ and of points on the curve $C_n$ (one per epoch boundary), which gives the join/secant description and the count of $2k-2$ free normalized parameters. Carathéodory-type arguments on these convex cones give the saturation bound $\kappa_n\le 2n-1$, while the strict growth of dimension up to $k=\lceil n/2\rceil$ gives the lower bound.
