# Non-identifiability of general histories

There are two measurable functions $\eta_1, \eta_2 : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ and constants $0 < a \le b$ such that

- $a \le \eta_1(s) \le b$ and $a \le \eta_2(s) \le b$ for every real $s$;
- $\eta_1$ and $\eta_2$ differ on a subset of $[0, \infty)$ of positive Lebesgue measure;
- their [first-coalescence times](first-coal-mean.md) agree: $c_m(\eta_1) = c_m(\eta_2)$ for every natural number $m \ge 2$.

Hence the two population-size histories have the same expected site frequency spectrum for every sample size.

## Sources

- S. Myers, C. Fefferman and N. Patterson, *Can one learn history from the allelic spectrum?*, Theoret. Population Biol. 73 (2008), 342–348.
