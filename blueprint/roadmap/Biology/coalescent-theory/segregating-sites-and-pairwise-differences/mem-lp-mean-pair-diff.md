# Square-integrable pair counts give a square-integrable mean $\pi$

Let $\mu$ be a measure on a measurable space $\Omega$ and $n$ a natural number. If, for every
[pair](sample-pairs.md) $s$ of genes of a sample of size $n$, the real function $D_s$ on $\Omega$
is square-integrable with respect to $\mu$, then so is the
[mean number of pairwise differences](mean-pair-diff.md)
$\pi = \binom{n}{2}^{-1} \sum_{s} D_s$. For $n \le 1$ there is no pair and $\binom{n}{2} = 0$;
with the convention $0^{-1} = 0$ the mean is then identically $0$, not a sample average. The
averaging case is $n \ge 2$.
