# Conditional moments of the site frequency spectrum given the genealogy

Let `μ` be a measure on a measurable space `Ω`, let `m` be a σ-algebra on `Ω`, let `θ` be a real
number, and let `ξ_i` and `L_i` (`i ∈ ℕ`) be real functions on `Ω`. The counts `ξ` have the
conditional moments of the site frequency spectrum given `m`, with branch lengths `L`, when for
all `i, j ∈ ℕ`:

- `ξ_i` and `L_i` are square integrable under `μ`;
- `E[ξ_i | m] = (θ / 2) L_i` almost everywhere;
- `E[ξ_i ξ_j | m] = (θ / 2)² L_i L_j + δ_{ij} (θ / 2) L_i` almost everywhere.

These are the first two conditional moments of counts `ξ_i` that, given `m`, are independent
Poisson variables with means `(θ / 2) L_i`: the conditional covariance of `ξ_i` and `ξ_j` is
`δ_{ij} (θ / 2) L_i`. In the coalescent, `ξ_i` is the number of sites whose derived allele is
carried by exactly `i` sample copies, `L_i` is the total length of the branches subtending
exactly `i` copies, and `m` is generated by the genealogy; neutral infinite-sites mutation at
rate `θ / 2` per lineage gives these moments, because distinct size classes occupy disjoint sets
of branches. The conditional expectation is `0` by convention when `m` is not contained in the
σ-algebra of `Ω`, when `μ` restricted to `m` is not σ-finite, or when the function is not
integrable; the results that use these conditions take `μ` a probability measure and `m`
contained in the σ-algebra of `Ω`.
