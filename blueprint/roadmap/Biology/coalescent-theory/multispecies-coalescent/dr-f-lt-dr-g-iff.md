# Degnan–Rosenberg criterion: $f(x, y) < g(x, y)$ iff $y < b(x)$

For every real $x \ge 0$ and every real $y$, the [Degnan–Rosenberg](dr-f.md) [probabilities](dr-g.md) satisfy $f(x, y) < g(x, y)$ if and only if $y < b(x)$, where $b$ is the [boundary for three anomalous gene trees](anomaly-b.md). So for branch lengths $x, y \ge 0$, the gene tree $((A, C), (B, D))$, equivalently $((A, D), (B, C))$, is more probable than the matching gene tree exactly when $y < b(x)$.

## Sources

- J. H. Degnan and N. A. Rosenberg, *Discordance of species trees with their most likely gene trees*, PLoS Genet. 2 (2006), e68.
