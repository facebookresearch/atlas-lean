# Variance of a site statistic decomposes into branch statistics

Use the notation of [site and branch statistics](site-and-branch-statistics.md): a tree sequence $T_{[i, j)}$ on a window $[i, j)$, sample weights $w$, a strict summary function $f$, and infinite-sites Poisson mutation at constant rate $\mu$ per unit time per unit sequence length. Write

$$\mathrm{Site}^{+}(f, w)_s = \sum_{a \in D_s} f(\bar x_s(a)), \qquad \mathrm{Branch}^{+}(f, w)_k = \sum_{u \in T_k} \beta_k(u)\, f(x_k(u))$$

for the **polarized** statistics ($D_s$ is the set of derived alleles at the site at position $s$), windowed exactly as the unpolarized ones: the site statistic of $[i, j)$ is $(j - i)^{-1}$ times the sum of $\mathrm{Site}^{+}(f, w)_s$ over the observed mutation positions $s \in [i, j)$, and the branch statistic weights each tree by its overlap $\ell_k(i, j)$ with the window. Assume the genealogy $T_{[i, j)}$ is itself random (for example generated by a coalescent or forward-time model) with $\operatorname{Var}[\mathrm{Branch}^{+}(f, w)_{[i,j)}]$ and $\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{Branch}^{+}(f^2, w)_{[i,j)}]$ finite, where $f^2(x) = f(x)^2$.

**Proposition (Ralph, Thornton and Kelleher 2020).** The variance of the windowed site statistic splits into a genealogical (drift) term and a mutational term, both of which are branch statistics:

$$\operatorname{Var}\big[\mathrm{Site}^{+}(f, w)_{[i, j)}\big] = \mu^2 \operatorname{Var}\big[\mathrm{Branch}^{+}(f, w)_{[i, j)}\big] + \frac{\mu}{j - i}\, \mathbb{E}\big[\mathrm{Branch}^{+}(f^2, w)_{[i, j)}\big].$$

Consequently the site statistic is a noisier estimator of $\mu\, \mathrm{Branch}^{+}(f, w)_{[i,j)}$ than the branch statistic itself, and the excess (mutational) variance decreases like $1/(\mu (j - i))$ relative to the squared mean scale.

## Expansion

Apply the law of total variance conditioning on $T_{[i, j)}$. By duality the conditional mean is $\mu\, \mathrm{Branch}^{+}(f, w)_{[i, j)}$, which gives the first term. Given the trees, each mutation on the branch above $u$ in $T_k$ at a position in $[i, j)$ adds $f(x_k(u))$ to the summed site statistic, and the numbers of mutations on distinct branch pieces are independent Poisson variables with means $\mu\, \beta_k(u)\, \ell_k(i, j)$. A constant $c$ times a Poisson variable of mean $m$ has variance $m c^2$, so the conditional variance of the window average is $(j - i)^{-2} \sum_{k,u} \mu\, \beta_k(u)\, \ell_k(i, j)\, f(x_k(u))^2 = \mu (j - i)^{-1} \mathrm{Branch}^{+}(f^2, w)_{[i, j)}$. Taking expectations gives the second term. The paper states the decomposition for general site statistics with the mutational term written as a branch statistic of $f^2$; for unpolarized statistics the same argument applies with $f(x)^2$ replaced by $(f(x) + f(w_{\mathrm{total}} - x))^2$ per branch.
