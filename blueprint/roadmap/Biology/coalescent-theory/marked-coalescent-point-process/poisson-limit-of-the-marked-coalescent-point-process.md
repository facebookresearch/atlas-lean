# Poisson limit of the marked coalescent point process with rare mutations at birth

**Model.** A splitting tree with lifespan measure $\Lambda$ (a measure on $(0,\infty)$ with $\int(1\wedge r)\,\Lambda(dr) < \infty$) is a population in which each individual gives birth during its life at the points of a Poisson process with intensity $dt\,\Lambda(dr)$, $r$ being the lifetime of the child, children behaving as independent copies. Each birth is independently a *mutant* birth (mark $1$) or a clonal birth (mark $0$); mutations are neutral. Its jumping chronological contour process (JCCP), which explores the tree and visits every level of every lifetime once, is a spectrally positive Lévy process with drift $-1$ and Lévy measure $\Lambda$, reflected below level $\tau$ and killed at $0$; the extant individuals at level $\tau$ correspond to its excursions below $\tau$.

For the $i$-th extant individual at level $\tau$ (in contour order), record the point measure $\sigma^{(i)} = \delta_{(a_0,0)} + \sum_{k\ge1}\delta_{(a_k,1)}$ on $(0,\tau)\times\{0,1\}$, where $a_0$ is the depth of its coalescence with the previous lineage and $a_1 < a_2 < \cdots \le a_0$ are the depths (measured down from $\tau$) of the mutations on its lineage before that coalescence. The **marked coalescent point process** of a tree with $I$ extant individuals is $\Sigma = \sum_{i=1}^{I-1}\delta_{(i/I',\,\sigma^{(i)})}$ for the appropriate population scale $I'$.

**Theorem (general rare-mutation regime).** Let a sequence of rescaled marked splitting trees, conditioned on their number of extant individuals at level $\tau$, have rescaled JCCPs converging to a spectrally positive Lévy process $Z$ with infinite variation, and let the mutation probability per birth vanish so that the rescaled number of marks converges to rate $\theta$. Let $H = \bar Z\circ L^{-1}$ be the ascending ladder height process of $Z$, $\Theta$ an independent Poisson process with rate $\theta$, and $\sigma$ the point measure that places mark $1$ at $H$ evaluated at the jump times of $\Theta$ and mark $0$ at the depth of the excursion. Then the marked coalescent point processes converge in distribution to a Poisson point measure on $[0,1]\times\mathcal M$ with intensity $\mathrm{Leb}\otimes\Pi_1$, where $\Pi_1$ is the law of $\sigma$ under the excursion measure of $Z$ away from $0$ restricted to excursions of depth less than $\tau$.

**Corollary (critical exponential lifetimes).** Take lifetimes exponential with mean $1$ and birth rate $1$ (critical), rescaled by $d_n = n^2/2$ so that the JCCP converges to standard Brownian motion, and mutation probability $\beta/n$ per birth, $\beta\in[0,1]$. Then $H(t) = t/2$ and

$$\Pi_1 = \int_0^\tau \frac{dh}{2h^2}\int \pi_{\beta,h}(d\Theta)\,\mathbf 1\Big\{\delta_{(h,0)} + \sum_{i}\delta_{(\Theta_i,1)} \in\cdot\Big\},$$

where $\pi_{\beta,h}$ is the law of a Poisson process of rate $\beta$ on $(0,h)$. Thus the coalescence depths form the Poisson point process of depths of Brownian excursions, with intensity $dh/(2h^2)$ on $(0,\tau)$, and the mutations on each lineage form an independent rate-$\beta$ Poisson process stopped at its coalescence depth. In the limit infinitely many lineages carry at least one mutation, but only finitely many carry at least two.

## Expansion

In the finite tree, the levels of birth events on the $i$-th lineage up to its coalescence are the values of the future infimum of the $i$-th excursion of the JCCP below $\tau$; by time reversal these are read from the ascending ladder height process of the Lévy process, and the marks they carry are those of the ladder jumps. Excursions are i.i.d., so the lineage records are i.i.d. and a law of rare events for null arrays gives the Poisson limit once the marked ladder height process converges, which in the constant rare-mutation regime yields $H$ independent of a Poisson process of marks. For Brownian motion the ladder height process is a pure drift, so the image of a Poisson process is again Poisson, and the excursion measure of the depth is $dh/(2h^2)$; $\int_0^\tau \beta h\,dh/(2h^2) = \infty$ while $\int_0^\tau(\beta h)^2\,dh/(2h^2) < \infty$ gives the last statement.
