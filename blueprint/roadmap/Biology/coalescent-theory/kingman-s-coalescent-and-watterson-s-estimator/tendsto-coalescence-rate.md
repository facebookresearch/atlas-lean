# Kingman's coalescence rate

For every natural number $k$, let $P_{\mathrm{nc}}(M, k) = \prod_{i=0}^{k-1} (1 - i/M)$. Then

$$M\bigl(1 - P_{\mathrm{nc}}(M, k)\bigr) \to \binom{k}{2}$$

as the natural number $M$ tends to infinity. Thus in a Wright–Fisher population of $M = 2N$ gene copies the probability that some of $k$ lineages coalesce in one generation is $\binom{k}{2}/M + o(1/M)$: in time units of $M$ generations, coalescences occur at rate $\binom{k}{2}$.
