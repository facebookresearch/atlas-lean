# Coalescence time of a sample from a multi-type supercritical Galton–Watson process

**Setting.** Let $(\mathbf Z_t)_{t \ge 0}$ be a discrete-time multi-type Galton–Watson process on a finite or countably infinite type set $S$, with mean matrix $\mathbf M = (m_{ij})$ and founder of type $i_0$. Assume $\mathbf M$ is irreducible with all entries of its powers finite, and that the process is supercritical and $R$-positive: the convergence parameter $R \in (0, 1)$ satisfies $\sum_n m^{(n)}_{ij}R^n = \infty$ for all $i, j$, with left and right $R$-invariant vectors $\boldsymbol\nu, \mathbf u$ satisfying $\mathbf u \cdot \boldsymbol\nu < \infty$. Then $R^T Z^{i_0, j}_T \to \nu_j W^{(i_0)}$ in $L^2$ for a nonnegative limit $W^{(i_0)}$ with mean $u_{i_0}$. Run the process for $T$ generations, sample $k \ge 2$ individuals uniformly from generation $T$, and let $X_{T,k}$ be the generation of their most recent common ancestor.

**Theorem.** For every fixed $t$,
$$\lim_{T \to \infty}\mathbb P\bigl(X_{T,k} < t \bigm| |\mathbf Z_T| \ge k\bigr) = 1 - \mathbb E\left[\frac{\sum_{i \in S}\sum_{s=1}^{Z^{i_0,i}_t}\bigl(W^{(i)}_{t,s}\bigr)^k}{\Bigl(\sum_{i \in S}\sum_{s=1}^{Z^{i_0,i}_t} W^{(i)}_{t,s}\Bigr)^k}\;\middle|\; |\mathbf Z_\infty| > 0\right],$$
where $W^{(i)}_{t,s}$ are independent copies of $W^{(i)}$, one for the subtree of the $s$-th type-$i$ individual alive at generation $t$. If moreover $\mathbb E[(W^{(i)})^{2k}] < \infty$ for all $i$ (implied, for finitely many types, by finite $2k$-th offspring moments), then this limit is bounded above and below by $1 - C\,\mathbb E\bigl[|\mathbf Z_t|^{-1} \mid |\mathbf Z_\infty| > 0\bigr]$-type expressions, and $\mathbb E\bigl[|\mathbf Z_t|^{-r} \mid |\mathbf Z_\infty| > 0\bigr]$ is squeezed between $(1 - \sup_i q_i)^r\bigl(\mathbb E\sup_i|\mathbf Y^i_1|\bigr)^{-tr}$ and $(1 - \sup_i q_i)^{-2}\bigl(\sup_i\mathbb E|\mathbf Y^i_1|^{-1}\bigr)^{tr}$, where $q_i$ are extinction probabilities and $\mathbf Y^i_1$ is the first generation of the Harris–Sevastyanov transform (the process of individuals with infinite lines of descent) started from type $i$; hence the sample's MRCA lies in the first few generations with probability converging to $1$ exponentially fast.

## Expansion

The $k$ sampled individuals descend from distinct generation-$t$ ancestors unless all $k$ fall into one generation-$t$ subtree. As $T \to \infty$ the fraction of generation $T$ descending from individual $s$ of type $i$ converges to its normalized limit $W^{(i)}_{t,s}$ divided by the total, so the probability that all $k$ samples share a generation-$t$ ancestor is the $k$-th power sum of these fractions. This extends Hong's finite-type, no-extinction formula to processes that can die out (hence the conditioning on survival) and to countably many types, where $R$-positivity replaces Perron–Frobenius theory.
