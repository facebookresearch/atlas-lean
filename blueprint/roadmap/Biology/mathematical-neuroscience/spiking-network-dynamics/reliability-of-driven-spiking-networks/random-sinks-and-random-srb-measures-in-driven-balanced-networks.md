# Random sinks and random SRB measures in driven balanced networks

**Reliability.** A network driven by a fixed temporal signal $\zeta(t)$ is **reliable** if trajectories started from different initial states converge to the same signal-dependent response. Model the driven network as a stochastic differential equation (random dynamical system) and let $\mu^t_\zeta$ be the conditional (sample) measure at time $t$: the image of the stationary distribution of initial conditions under the flow generated by the realized signal. Let $\lambda_1$ be the top Lyapunov exponent.

**Theorem (Le Jan; Baxendale).** If $\lambda_1 < 0$ and suitable nondegeneracy conditions hold, $\mu^t_\zeta$ is a **random sink**: $\mu^t_\zeta = \delta_{x_t}$ for a single solution $x_t$ of the SDE, so all initial states synchronize to one trajectory (reliability).

**Theorem (Ledrappier–Young).** If $\lambda_1 > 0$, $\mu^t_\zeta$ is a **random Sinai–Ruelle–Bowen measure**: it is supported on a time-dependent, fractal-like attractor with absolutely continuous conditional measures along unstable manifolds, so responses to repeated presentations of the signal differ (unreliability).

**Application (balanced networks of $\theta$-neurons).** For a randomly connected excitatory–inhibitory network of $1000$ $\theta$-neurons (Type I, equivalent to quadratic integrate-and-fire) with balanced-state $O(1/\sqrt K)$ weights and a common fluctuating drive, the top Lyapunov exponent stays positive for weak and strong drives, so the network-wide response is chaotic and, by the Ledrappier–Young dichotomy, globally unreliable. Nevertheless, at any moment only a few cells significantly support the expanding directions of the attractor, and single neurons and spike times show intermittent periods of highly reliable firing coexisting with unreliable activity.

## Proof sketch

The sign of $\lambda_1$ measures whether nearby trajectories, driven by the same realization of the signal, converge or separate. A negative exponent contracts the whole sample measure onto a point; a positive exponent stretches it along unstable directions while dissipation folds it, producing an SRB attractor. Isolated $\theta$-neurons have $\lambda_1 < 0$ (they are reliable), so unreliability is generated by recurrent interactions; but because the expanding directions are carried by few cells at a time, spike times of many cells remain locked to the stimulus during significant fractions of time.
