# Exponential ergodicity of stochastic spiking networks

**Setting.** Consider a network of $n$ spiking neurons with arbitrary internal structure (multi-compartment neurons, nonlinear dendrites, heterogeneous synapses with short-term dynamics) whose firing is stochastic, driven by input neurons that fire as Poisson processes with rate vector $\mathbf r$. Define the network's Markov state at time $t$ as the record of all spikes in the window $(t - \tau, t]$ (for a window $\tau$ at least as long as any memory in the neuron and synapse models), together with any internal variables needed to make the evolution Markov. Assume the network is *sufficiently stochastic*: the summed instantaneous firing rate of all network neurons is bounded, $\sum_i \rho_i(t) \le R < \infty$, for every state and every admissible input.

**Return to rest (Doeblin condition).** Starting from any state at time $t$, with probability at least $e^{-R\tau}$ no network neuron fires in $(t, t + \tau]$, after which the network is in its resting state $\mathbf 0$ (no spikes in the window). Thus the transition kernel $P^{\tau}$ over a window satisfies

$$P^{\tau}(\mathbf z, \cdot) \ \ge\ \varepsilon\, \delta_{\mathbf 0}(\cdot), \qquad \varepsilon = e^{-R\tau} > 0,$$

uniformly in the initial state $\mathbf z$ and in the input trajectory.

**Contraction lemma.** For any two initial distributions $\mu, \nu$ of network states,

$$\| \mu P^{\tau} - \nu P^{\tau} \|_{TV} \ \le\ (1 - \varepsilon)\, \| \mu - \nu \|_{TV}.$$

**Theorem (exponentially fast convergence).** If the input rates are constant, or are themselves generated by a uniformly ergodic Markov process, then the network (jointly with its input process) has a unique stationary distribution $p(\mathbf z)$ of network states, and the distribution of the network state converges to it exponentially fast in total variation from every initial state. The same holds for the marginal distribution of every subset of neurons. With inputs that are periodic with period $T_0$, the distribution of network states at each fixed phase of the input converges exponentially fast to a unique phase-specific stationary distribution.

## Expansion

The Doeblin minorization comes from the rate bound: the probability that a point process with total intensity at most $R$ produces no event in an interval of length $\tau$ is at least $e^{-R\tau}$, and an empty spike window is the resting state. Write $\mu - \nu = (\mu-\nu)^+ - (\mu-\nu)^-$; both parts have the same total mass, and after one window each part has placed at least the fraction $\varepsilon$ of its mass on the resting state, so these common parts cancel and the remaining mass is at most $(1-\varepsilon)$ times the original. Iterating the contraction over $k$ windows gives geometric convergence of the $\tau$-skeleton chain; non-explosiveness and stochastic continuity lift geometric ergodicity of the skeleton to exponential ergodicity of the continuous-time process. Marginals over subsets of neurons inherit the convergence because total variation does not increase under projection. Ergodicity also means that the fraction of time a sub-network spends in a given pattern converges almost surely to that pattern's stationary marginal probability, so marginals can be read out by time averaging. A deterministic network violates the rate bound (an input that forces firing with probability one corresponds to an infinite rate) and is outside the theorem.
