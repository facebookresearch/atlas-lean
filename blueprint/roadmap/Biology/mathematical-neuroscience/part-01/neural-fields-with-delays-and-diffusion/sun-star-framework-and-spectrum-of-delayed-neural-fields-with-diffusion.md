# Sun-star framework and spectrum of delayed neural fields with diffusion

**Model.** On $\Omega = [-1,1]$ with no-flux boundary conditions $\partial_x u(t,\pm 1) = 0$,

$$\partial_t u(t,x) = d\,\partial_x^2 u(t,x) - \alpha\, u(t,x) + \alpha \int_\Omega J(x,x')\, S\bigl(u(t - \tau(x,x'), x')\bigr)\,dx',$$

with $d \ge 0$ (gap-junction diffusion), $\alpha > 0$, continuous connectivity $J$ and delay $\tau$ with $0 \le \tau \le h$, and $S \in C^\infty(\mathbb{R})$ with $S(0) = 0$, $S'(0) \neq 0$. It arises from a chain of $M$ passive neurons with membrane rate $\alpha$, nearest-neighbour resistive gap junctions and delayed synapses in the limit $M \to \infty$ with the gap conductance scaled by $M^2$ and synaptic weights by $1/M$.

**Abstract form.** With $Y = C(\Omega)$ and $X = C([-h,0]; Y)$, the model is the abstract delay equation $\dot u(t) = Bu(t) + G(u_t)$, where

$$Bq = d\,q'' - \alpha q,\quad D(B) = \{q \in C^2(\Omega) : q'(\pm1) = 0\},\qquad G(\varphi)(x) = \alpha\int_\Omega J(x,x')\,S\bigl(\varphi(-\tau(x,x'),x')\bigr)\,dx'.$$

$G$ is compact, globally Lipschitz and $C^k$ for every $k$, with compact Fréchet derivatives.

**Theorem 12 (sun-reflexivity).** For the delay semigroup $T_0$ generated by $B$ with zero delayed term, $X$ is sun-reflexive with respect to $T_0$ iff $Y$ is reflexive. In particular, for the continuous-function state space $Y = C(\Omega)$, $X$ is not sun-reflexive, so the standard sun-star theory must be extended.

**Corollary 14 (well-posedness).** For globally Lipschitz $G$ and every initial history $\varphi \in X$ there is a unique global solution of the abstract integral equation (variation-of-constants formula), and these solutions correspond one-to-one with mild solutions of the delay equation (Theorem 18).

**Theorem 27 and essential spectrum.** If $DG(0)$ is compact then $\sigma_{ess}(A) = \sigma_{ess}(B)$ for the generator $A$ of the linearized semigroup. For the neural field,

$$\sigma_{ess}(A) = \begin{cases} \emptyset, & d > 0, \\ \{-\alpha\}, & d = 0. \end{cases}$$

**Theorem 28 (characteristic equation).** With $K_z y = DG(0)(y\,e^{z\theta})$ and $\Delta(z) = z - B - K_z$, $\lambda$ is an eigenvalue of $A$ with eigenvector $\psi$ iff $\psi(\theta) = e^{\lambda\theta} q$ with nontrivial $q \in D(B)$ solving $\Delta(\lambda)q = 0$; $z \in \rho(A)$ iff the corresponding equation $\Delta(z)q = W_z\varphi$ is uniquely solvable, and the resolvent is then explicit.

**Theorem 36 (exponential kernels).** Let $d > 0$, $J(x,x') = \sum_{j=1}^N \eta_j e^{-\mu_j|x-x'|}$ with $\eta_j \ne 0$, and $\tau(x,x') = \tau_0 + |x-x'|$ with $\tau_0 \ge 0$. Put $c_j(z) = S'(0)\,\alpha\,\eta_j e^{-\tau_0 z}$, $k_j(z) = \mu_j + z$ and

$$P_z(\rho) = (\alpha + z - d\rho^2)\prod_{p=1}^N\bigl(k_p(z)^2 - \rho^2\bigr) - 2\sum_{j=1}^N c_j(z)\,k_j(z)\prod_{p \ne j}\bigl(k_p(z)^2 - \rho^2\bigr),$$

an even polynomial of degree $2(N+1)$ in $\rho$ (Eq. 50). Let $\lambda \in \mathbb{C}$ be such that $P_\lambda$ has $2(N+1)$ distinct roots $\pm\rho_1, \dots, \pm\rho_{N+1}$ and $\lambda \notin L = \{z : k_j(z) = \pm\rho_m(z) \text{ for some } j, m\}$ (Eq. 52). Then every solution of $\Delta(\lambda)q = 0$ is a combination of $\cosh(\rho_m x)$ and $\sinh(\rho_m x)$, and $\lambda \in \sigma_p(A)$ iff $\det S_{\lambda,\mathrm{even}}\cdot\det S_{\lambda,\mathrm{odd}} = 0$, where for $j \le N$ and $m \le N+1$

$$(S_{\lambda,\mathrm{even}})_{j,m} = \frac{k_j\cosh\rho_m + \rho_m\sinh\rho_m}{k_j^2 - \rho_m^2},\qquad (S_{\lambda,\mathrm{odd}})_{j,m} = \frac{\rho_m\cosh\rho_m + k_j\sinh\rho_m}{k_j^2 - \rho_m^2},$$

and the last rows, from the boundary conditions, are $(S_{\lambda,\mathrm{even}})_{N+1,m} = \rho_m\sinh\rho_m$ and $(S_{\lambda,\mathrm{odd}})_{N+1,m} = \rho_m\cosh\rho_m$ (all evaluated at $z = \lambda$). The eigenvectors are $e^{\lambda\theta}\sum_m a_m\cosh(\rho_m x)$ (even) or $e^{\lambda\theta}\sum_m b_m\sinh(\rho_m x)$ (odd) with $a$, $b$ in the corresponding null space. With a repeated root of $P_\lambda$ the general solution has terms such as $x\cosh(\rho_m x)$ and this test does not apply.

## Expansion

Diffusion turns $B$ from a bounded multiplication operator into an unbounded sectorial one. The isolated point $-\alpha$ of essential spectrum present at $d = 0$ (from the local decay term) disappears because the Neumann Laplacian on a bounded interval has compact resolvent, and the delayed term is a compact perturbation, which leaves the essential spectrum unchanged. Theorem 28 reduces the eigenvalue problem on the history space $X$ to a boundary value problem on $\Omega$, which for exponential kernels becomes an ODE with constant coefficients and hence a finite determinant condition. In a numerical example with the wizard-hat kernel $J(x,x') = \tfrac{25}{2}e^{-2|x-x'|} - 10\,e^{-|x-x'|}$ and sigmoid $S(u) = 1/(1+e^{-\gamma u}) - 1/2$, adding diffusion $d = 0.2$ shifts the Hopf point only slightly (gain $\gamma = 3.3482 \to 3.3094$, critical eigenvalue $1.2403\,i \to 1.2379\,i$): eigenvalues off the real axis barely move, while real eigenvalues become more negative, consistent with the near-flat spatial profile of the critical eigenvector, since diffusion penalizes curvature.
