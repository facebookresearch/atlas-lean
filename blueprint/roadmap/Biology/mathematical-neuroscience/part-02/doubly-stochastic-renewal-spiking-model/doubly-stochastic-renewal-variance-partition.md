# Doubly stochastic renewal variance partition

A **doubly stochastic renewal (DSR) point process** is a pair $\{g(\cdot), \lambda(t)\}$: $g$ is an inter-spike-interval density with mean $\mu_g = 1$ in operational time $t'$, and $\lambda(t) \ge 0$ is a stochastic firing-rate process. Spikes are generated by drawing a rate path, sampling an equilibrium (stationary) renewal process with interval density $g$ in operational time, and mapping spike times to real time through $t = \Lambda^{-1}(t')$, where $t' = \Lambda(t) = \int_0^t \lambda(s)\,ds$.

Let $\phi = \sigma_g^2/\mu_g^2$ be the squared coefficient of variation of $g$ (the spiking irregularity) and $N_T$ the spike count in a bin of length $T$. Assume $\lambda$ is constant within the bin (varying across trials) and $T > 1/\mathbb{E}[\lambda]$. Then $\mathbb{E}[N_T \mid \lambda] = \lambda T$ and

$$\operatorname{Var}(N_T) = \operatorname{Var}(\lambda T) + \phi\,\mathbb{E}[N_T] + \frac{1}{6} + \frac{\phi^2}{2} - \frac{\mu_{3,g}}{3} + O(T^{-1}),$$

where $\mu_{3,g}$ is the third central moment of $g$ (Eq. 11). If $g$ is a gamma density, $\mu_{3,g} = 2\phi^2$ and the partition becomes

$$\operatorname{Var}(N_T) = \operatorname{Var}(\lambda T) + \frac{1}{6}\left(1 - \phi^2\right) + \phi\,\mathbb{E}[N_T] + O(T^{-1})$$

(Aghamohammadi, Chandrasekaran and Engel 2025, Eq. 5). The first term is firing-rate (trial-to-trial) variance; the remaining terms are point-process variance controlled by the single parameter $\phi$, with $\phi = 1$ recovering the Poisson case.

## Expansion

The law of total variance gives $\operatorname{Var}(N_T) = \mathbb{E}[\operatorname{Var}(N_T \mid \lambda)] + \operatorname{Var}(\mathbb{E}[N_T \mid \lambda])$. Within a bin with constant $\lambda$, time rescaling turns the process into a renewal process with interval mean $1/\lambda$, variance $\phi/\lambda^2$ and third central moment $\mu_{3,g}/\lambda^3$; inserting these into the asymptotic renewal variance and averaging over $\lambda$ gives $\phi\,\mathbb{E}[N_T]$ plus the $\lambda$-free constant. For a gamma density with shape $k$ and scale $\theta$ and unit mean, $\sigma_g^2 = k\theta^2 = \phi$ and $\mu_{3,g} = 2k\theta^3 = 2\phi^2$, so the constant is $\tfrac16 + \tfrac{\phi^2}{2} - \tfrac{2\phi^2}{3} = \tfrac{1-\phi^2}{6}$.
