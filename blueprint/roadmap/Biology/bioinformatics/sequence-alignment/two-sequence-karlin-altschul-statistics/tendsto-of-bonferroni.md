# Poisson limit from Bonferroni bounds and factorial moments

Let `P₀(N)` and `S_k(N)`, for natural numbers `k` and `N`, be real numbers and `c` a real
number. Suppose that for every natural number `r`, eventually in `N`,
`∑_{k=0}^{2r+1} (-1)^k S_k(N) ≤ P₀(N) ≤ ∑_{k=0}^{2r} (-1)^k S_k(N)`, and that `S_k(N) → c^k / k!`
as `N → ∞` for every `k`. Then `P₀(N) → e^{-c}`. In use, `P₀(N)` is the probability that none of
a family of events occurs, the bounds are the Bonferroni inequalities and the `S_k(N)` are the
factorial moments of the number of events that occur; the statement itself needs no probability.
