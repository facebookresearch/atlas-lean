# Binomial rank statistic for gene-level hit calling in perturbation screens

Consider a pooled genetic screen in which $N$ perturbations (single-guide RNAs) are ranked by a per-perturbation readout, for instance log fold change between selected and control populations. Fix a gene targeted by $n$ perturbations and a threshold keeping the top $\theta N$ perturbations of the whole experiment. Let the perturbations of this gene that pass the threshold be indexed by their within-gene rank $k = 1, 2, \dots$ in the same order, and let the $k$-th of them sit at overall rank $R_k$ in the experiment, so that $p_k = R_k / N$ is the fraction of all perturbations ranking at least as high.

**Definition (gene score).** For each such $k \ge 2$ compute the binomial probability

$$\Pr(X = k) \;=\; \binom{n}{k} p_k^{\,k} (1 - p_k)^{\,n-k},$$

the chance that exactly $k$ of this gene's $n$ perturbations fall in the top $p_k$ fraction when perturbations are ranked at random. The gene is assigned the score of its least probable such $k$, and receives no score unless at least two of its perturbations pass the threshold.

**Calibration.** A null distribution of gene scores is generated by permuting the ranked list of perturbations, and the empirical tail of that distribution converts a gene score into a $p$-value and, after multiple-testing correction across genes, into a false discovery rate.

## Expansion

Under the null hypothesis that the gene's perturbations are placed uniformly at random in the ranking, the number landing above a fixed quantile $p$ is binomial with parameters $n$ and $p$. Evaluating that probability at the data-dependent quantile $p_k$ rewards a gene whose perturbations are both numerous near the top and tightly packed, and the minimum over $k$ lets the statistic choose the depth at which the gene's evidence is strongest, rather than fixing one cutoff in advance. Because $p_k$ is estimated from the same ranking, the statistic is not a valid tail probability on its own, which is exactly why significance is read from the permutation null rather than from the binomial formula. Requiring two passing perturbations suppresses genes carried by a single outlier guide, the dominant failure mode when individual guides have off-target activity.
