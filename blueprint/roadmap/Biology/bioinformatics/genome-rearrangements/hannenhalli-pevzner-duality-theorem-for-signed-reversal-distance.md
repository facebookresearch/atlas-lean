# Hannenhalli–Pevzner formula for the signed reversal distance

For a [signed permutation](../../../Combinatorics/permutations/permutation-statistics/sorting-by-reversals/svalid.md) $\pi$ of $\{1, \dots, n\}$, let $d(\pi)$ be its [signed reversal distance](../../../Combinatorics/permutations/permutation-statistics/sorting-by-reversals/sdist.md), the minimum number of [reversals of signed segments](../../../Combinatorics/permutations/permutation-statistics/sorting-by-reversals/srev-block.md) transforming $\pi$ into the identity. Replace each element $+x$ by $2x - 1, 2x$ and each $-x$ by $2x, 2x - 1$ to obtain the [unsigned image](../../../Combinatorics/permutations/permutation-statistics/sorting-by-reversals/dbl.md) $\pi' = \pi'_1 \cdots \pi'_{2n}$ of $\pi$, and frame it by $\pi'_0 = 0$ and $\pi'_{2n+1} = 2n + 1$. A pair $(\pi'_i, \pi'_{i+1})$, $0 \le i \le 2n$, is an adjacency if $|\pi'_i - \pi'_{i+1}| = 1$ and a breakpoint otherwise. The breakpoint graph $G(\pi)$ has vertices $0, 1, \dots, 2n + 1$, a black edge joining $\pi'_i$ and $\pi'_{i+1}$ for each breakpoint, and a gray edge joining two consecutive values $\pi'_i \sim \pi'_j$ (that is, $|\pi'_i - \pi'_j| = 1$) whenever they are not adjacent in $\pi'$, that is, $|i - j| \ne 1$. Adjacencies contribute no edges, so the graph has no trivial cycles; its degrees are at most $2$, so its decomposition into alternating cycles is unique. Let $b(\pi)$ be the number of breakpoints of $\pi'$ (the [unsigned breakpoint count](../../../Combinatorics/permutations/permutation-statistics/sorting-by-reversals/ubreaks.md) of $\pi'$ framed by $0$ and $2n + 1$) and $c(\pi)$ the number of alternating cycles of $G(\pi)$; for the identity both are $0$. (Counting adjacencies as trivial cycles, as in the cycle graph of all position pairs, would give the identity $2n + 1$ cycles and a negative value below.)

A gray edge joining $\pi'_i$ and $\pi'_j$, $i < j$, has position interval $[i, j]$. Its two ends lie at breakpoints, and each end is incident to exactly one black edge, which joins it to its predecessor or to its successor in position order. The gray edge is oriented if these black neighbours lie on the same side at both ends, that is, the incident black edges are $(\pi'_{i-1}, \pi'_i)$ and $(\pi'_{j-1}, \pi'_j)$ (both predecessors) or $(\pi'_i, \pi'_{i+1})$ and $(\pi'_j, \pi'_{j+1})$ (both successors), and unoriented otherwise; by Hannenhalli and Pevzner (1999, Lemma 1) this is equivalent to their definition that a reversal acting on the two incident black edges is proper. A cycle is oriented if it has an oriented gray edge. Two gray edges interleave if their position intervals overlap without containment, cycles interleave if they have interleaving gray edges, and the components of the resulting interleaving graph are oriented if they contain an oriented cycle and unoriented otherwise. The extent of a component $U$ is the interval $[U_{\min}, U_{\max}]$ from the smallest to the largest position of a vertex of its cycles. A component $U$ separates components $U'$ and $U''$ if some gray edge of $U$ has a position interval $[i, j]$ that contains the extent of $U'$ and is disjoint from the extent of $U''$ (Section 3). Order the unoriented components by containment of their extents. The number $h(\pi)$ of hurdles counts the minimal unoriented components, together with the greatest unoriented component when it does not separate any two minimal hurdles. A hurdle $K$ protects a nonhurdle $U$ if deleting $K$ turns $U$ into a hurdle; a superhurdle is a hurdle that protects some nonhurdle, and a fortress is a permutation with an odd number of hurdles, all of them superhurdles. Hannenhalli and Pevzner (1999, Theorem 5) prove

$$d(\pi) = \begin{cases} b(\pi) - c(\pi) + h(\pi) + 1 & \text{if } \pi \text{ is a fortress},\\ b(\pi) - c(\pi) + h(\pi) & \text{otherwise}. \end{cases}$$

The lower bound $d(\pi) \ge b(\pi) - c(\pi) + h(\pi)$ (Theorem 1) uses the fact, due to Bafna and Pevzner, that one reversal decreases $b - c$ by at most $1$.

## Expansion

1. Definitions: signed permutations, reversals, the framed unsigned image with $0$ and $2n + 1$, breakpoints and adjacencies, the breakpoint graph with edges only at breakpoints and its unique alternating cycle decomposition, and the value $b = c = h = 0$ for the identity.
2. The lower bound $b - c$ from the change of $b - c$ under one reversal, and Theorem 1 with hurdles.
3. Oriented gray edges by the same-side test (Lemma 1), oriented and unoriented cycles and components, extents and separation, hurdles, protection of a nonhurdle and superhurdles, fortresses (Section 7), and the extra reversal needed for fortresses.
4. The matching upper bound: safe paddings (Theorems 2–3), safe reversals in oriented components (Theorem 4), and hurdle merging and cutting (Lemmas 14–21), giving Theorem 5. The algorithm is [polynomial-time optimal sorting of signed permutations by reversals](polynomial-time-optimal-sorting-of-signed-permutations-by-reversals.md).
5. Related distances: the [double-cut-and-join distance formula](double-cut-and-join-distance-formula.md) and the [greedy breakpoint algorithm for sorting by reversals](greedy-breakpoint-bound-for-unsigned-sorting-by-reversals.md).
