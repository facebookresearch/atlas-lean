# Intra-strand parity as balanced persymmetric generation

A stationary Markov chain $(W,\omega)$ on the nucleotides $N=\{1,2,3,4\}$ ($A,C,G,T$, complement $\alpha(i)=5-i$) satisfies *intra-strand parity* (Chargaff's second parity rule at the dinucleotide level) if

$$\omega_iW_{ij}=\omega_{\alpha(j)}W_{\alpha(j)\alpha(i)}\qquad\text{for all } i,j\in N,$$

i.e. every dinucleotide is as frequent as its reverse complement on the same strand.

**Statement.** $(W,\omega)$ satisfies intra-strand parity if and only if $W$ is $\aleph$-generated (the row normalization of a positive persymmetric matrix) and the persymmetric matrix $L=\varphi(W,1)$ generating it has balanced row sums,

$$S_i=S_{\alpha(i)},\qquad S_i:=\sum_{k=1}^4L_{ik}\quad(i\in N).$$

In that case the stationary law is explicit:

$$\omega=\frac{1}{2(S_1+S_2)}\,(S_1,S_2,S_2,S_1).$$

Hence $\aleph$-generation is a strictly weaker property than intra-strand parity: chains of the persymmetric concatenation model can violate parity, as the two chirochores of a bacterial chromosome typically do, while the parity observed for whole chromosomes corresponds to the balanced subclass.

## Expansion

If parity holds, $D(\omega)W$ is persymmetric, so $W$ is $\aleph$-generated by the diagonal scaling criterion; summing the parity identity over $j$ gives $\omega_i=\omega_{\alpha(i)}$, and comparing the diagonal entries $W_{ii}=L_{ii}/S_i$ with $W_{\alpha(i)\alpha(i)}=L_{ii}/S_{\alpha(i)}$ forces $S_i=S_{\alpha(i)}$. Conversely, if $W_{ij}=L_{ij}/S_i$ with $S_i=S_{\alpha(i)}$, the vector proportional to $(S_1,S_2,S_2,S_1)$ is stationary (column sums of the persymmetric $L$ equal row sums of complementary rows), and $\omega_iW_{ij}\propto L_{ij}=L_{\alpha(j)\alpha(i)}\propto\omega_{\alpha(j)}W_{\alpha(j)\alpha(i)}$.
