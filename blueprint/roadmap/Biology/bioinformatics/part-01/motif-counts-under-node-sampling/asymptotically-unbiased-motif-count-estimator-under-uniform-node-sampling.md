# Asymptotically unbiased motif-count estimator under uniform node sampling

**Model.** Let $G$ be a (directed or undirected) network on $n$ known nodes with adjacency matrix $A$, and let $\mathcal M$ be a motif on $m$ nodes. Write the number of occurrences of $\mathcal M$ in $G$ as

$$N = \sum_{1\le i_1<\cdots<i_m\le n} f\big(A[\{i_1,\dots,i_m\}]\big),$$

where $f$ counts the occurrences of $\mathcal M$ on the induced submatrix. Each node is included independently with probability $p \in (0,1)$, $X_i \sim \mathrm{Bernoulli}(p)$, and the observed network $G_{\mathrm{obs}}$ is the subgraph induced on the $n_{\mathrm{obs}} = \sum_i X_i$ sampled nodes, in which the motif count $\tilde N = \sum f(A[\{i_1,\dots,i_m\}])\,X_{i_1}\cdots X_{i_m}$ is observed exactly. The estimator scales by the ratio of the numbers of $m$-node subsets:

$$\hat N = \frac{\binom{n}{m}}{\binom{n_{\mathrm{obs}}}{m}}\,\tilde N \qquad (\hat N = 0 \text{ if } n_{\mathrm{obs}} < m).$$

**Theorem.**

1. For every motif,

    $$\mathbb E[\hat N] = N\cdot P\big(n_{\mathrm{obs}} \ge m\big) = N\Big(1 - \sum_{r=0}^{m-1}\binom{n}{r}p^r(1-p)^{n-r}\Big),$$

    so $\hat N$ is asymptotically unbiased, $\mathbb E[\hat N]/N \to 1$ as $n \to \infty$, and the convergence is exponentially fast in $n$.

2. For the link count ($m = 2$), the variance of $\hat N$ has a closed form in the numbers of links and three-node paths of $G$, and $\operatorname{Var}(\hat N/N) \to 0$ as $n \to \infty$ when $G$ is generated by the Erdős–Rényi, preferential-attachment, duplication or geometric random-graph models; so $\hat N/N$ is consistent in those models.

## Expansion

By linearity it suffices to treat one $m$-subset. Conditioning on its $m$ nodes being sampled (probability $p^m$), $n_{\mathrm{obs}} = m + Z$ with $Z \sim \mathrm{Binomial}(n-m,p)$, so

$$\mathbb E[\hat N] = N\binom{n}{m}p^m\,\mathbb E\Big[\binom{m+Z}{m}^{-1}\Big] = N\sum_{z=0}^{n-m}\binom{n}{m+z}p^{m+z}(1-p)^{n-m-z},$$

using $\binom{n}{m}\binom{n-m}{z} = \binom{n}{m+z}\binom{m+z}{m}$; the last sum is $P(\mathrm{Binomial}(n,p) \ge m)$. The notions of unbiasedness and consistency here refer to the network size growing, not to repeated samples from a fixed network. Applied to the human interactome, the estimator projects the totals of 14 small motifs from the currently mapped fraction of proteins.
