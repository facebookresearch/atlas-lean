# Asymptotic number of dangle-annotated secondary structures

**Setting.** In the homopolymer model with minimum hairpin loop size $1$, a secondary structure is a noncrossing matching of positions $1,\dots,n$ in which every hairpin loop contains at least one unpaired base. Energy models such as those of Zuker-type folding software also annotate **dangles**: unpaired bases immediately adjacent to a helix end that stack on the closing pair (5' or 3' dangle). A dangle-annotated structure is a secondary structure together with a choice of which eligible unpaired neighbors are marked as dangles; distinct annotations of the same base pairing count as distinct structures.

**Theorem (Theorem 1).** The number $S_n$ of dangle-annotated secondary structures of length $n$ generated by the Markham–Zuker recursions (all energies set to zero) satisfies

$$
S_n \sim 0.63998 \cdot n^{-3/2} \cdot 3.06039^n .
$$

**Theorem (Theorem 2).** The number $S_n$ of secondary structures with annotated external dangles only (a 5′-dangle immediately before an opening bracket or a 3′-dangle immediately after a closing bracket, allowed in every loop: bulges, internal loops, multiloops and the exterior loop; no dangle inside a loop next to its closing pair), generated by the unambiguous context-free grammar $G_5$ of the source, satisfies

$$
S_n \sim 0.96691 \cdot n^{-3/2} \cdot 3.079596^n .
$$

Both growth rates exceed the Stein–Waterman rate $2.618034^n$ for unannotated structures, quantifying how much larger the state space of dangle-aware folding algorithms is.

## Expansion

Counting structures equals evaluating the partition function with all energies zero, so one writes an unambiguous context-free grammar whose derivations are in bijection with the annotated structures, and converts it by the Delest–Schützenberger–Viennot method into an algebraic system for the generating function $S(z)$. The dominant singularity $\rho$ is a square-root branch point, and the Flajolet–Odlyzko transfer theorem gives $S_n \sim c\,\rho^{-n} n^{-3/2}$; the constants are evaluated numerically from the algebraic system ($1/\rho = 3.06039$ resp. $3.079596$).
