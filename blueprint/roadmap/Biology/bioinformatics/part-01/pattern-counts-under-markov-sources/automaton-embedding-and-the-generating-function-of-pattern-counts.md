# Automaton embedding and the generating function of pattern counts

**Setting.** Let $X_1,\dots,X_\ell$ be an order-$d$ Markov chain over a finite alphabet $\mathcal A$, with starting distribution $\mu$ on $\mathcal A^d$ and position-dependent transition probabilities $\pi_{i+d}(a,b)$ from the $d$-letter context $a$ to the letter $b$. Let $\mathcal W$ be a finite set of words (no word shorter than $d$), and let $N_\ell$ be the number of positions $i$ at which some word of $\mathcal W$ ends, $N_\ell = \sum_{i} \mathbf 1\{X_1\cdots X_i \in \mathcal A^*\mathcal W\}$. Let $(\mathcal Q, \sigma, \mathcal F, \delta)$ be a minimal deterministic finite automaton recognizing $\mathcal A^*\mathcal W$ (texts ending with an occurrence), chosen non $d$-ambiguous, i.e. each state is reached by at most one length-$d$ suffix.

**Theorem (Markov chain embedding).** The state sequence $Y_i = \delta(\sigma, X_1\cdots X_i)$, restricted to the reachable set $\mathcal Q'$ of states, is a (heterogeneous) order-1 Markov chain whose transition probabilities from $p$ to $q = \delta(p,b)$ equal the source probability of letter $b$ after the context determined by $p$. Split its transition matrix as $T_{i+d} = P_{i+d} + Q_{i+d}$, where $Q_{i+d}$ holds the transitions into final states $\mathcal F$ (each completing an occurrence) and $P_{i+d}$ the others. The pattern complexity is $L = |\mathcal Q'|$.

**Proposition (generating function).** With $u_d$ the distribution of $Y_d$ (a row vector) and $\mathbf 1$ the all-ones vector,

$$F_\ell(y) = \mathbb E\big[y^{N_\ell}\big] = u_d\Big(\prod_{i=1}^{\ell-d}\big(P_{i+d} + yQ_{i+d}\big)\Big)\mathbf 1^{\mathsf T};$$

for a homogeneous chain, $F_\ell(y) = u_d\,(P + yQ)^{\ell-d}\,\mathbf 1^{\mathsf T}$.

**Set of sequences.** For $r$ independent sequences of lengths $\ell_1,\dots,\ell_r$ with counts $N^{(j)}$ and generating functions $F^{(j)}(y)$, the total count $N = N^{(1)} + \cdots + N^{(r)}$ has

$$\mathbb E[y^N] = \prod_{j=1}^r F^{(j)}(y),\qquad \text{and in the homogeneous case}\qquad \mathbb E[y^N] = \prod_{j=1}^r u_d\,(P + yQ)^{\ell_j - d}\,\mathbf 1^{\mathsf T}.$$

Truncating polynomials at degree $n_{\max}$, the distribution of $N$ up to $n_{\max}$ is obtained by propagating polynomial-valued state vectors through the sequences (no convolutions needed), or, for homogeneous models, by recycling the powers $(P+yQ)^\ell$ across sequence lengths, with binary exponentiation reducing the dependence on length to logarithmic for low-complexity patterns.

## Expansion

Because the automaton is deterministic and non $d$-ambiguous, its current state determines the last $d$ letters, so the next letter's law depends only on the state; this gives the embedding. Marking every transition that enters a final state with a formal variable $y$ makes each path's weight $y^{(\text{number of occurrences})}$ times its probability, and summing over end states gives the generating function. Independence of the sequences turns the total count's generating function into a product. Counting in a set of sequences differs from counting in their concatenation: the concatenation creates spurious junction occurrences and uses the wrong starting distribution for each sequence, an edge effect that biases P-values when the chain converges slowly to stationarity.
