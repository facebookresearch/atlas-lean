# The RNA branching polytope and its normal fan

**Setting.** Represent an RNA secondary structure with $n$ helices by a rooted plane tree $T$ with $n$ edges. The root is the exterior loop, the other vertices are loops, and the edges are helices. Let $r$ be the root degree, the number of helices in the exterior loop, and let $d_k$ be the number of non-root vertices with $k$ children, that is, loops with $k+1$ helices: $d_0$ counts hairpins, $d_1$ counts bulges and internal loops, and $d_k$ for $k\ge2$ counts multibranch loops. Under the linear multibranch-loop model $E(L)=a+bn_1+cn_2+q$, the identities $\sum_{k\ge2}d_k=n-d_0-d_1$ and $\sum_{k\ge2}(k-1)d_k=d_0-r$ reduce the branching energy, up to a constant depending only on $n$, to

$$E'(T)=\theta_2\,r+\theta_3\,d_0+\theta_4\,d_1,$$

with $(\theta_2,\theta_3,\theta_4)$ affine in the loop parameters. Minimizing energy over trees therefore minimizes a linear functional over the **count vectors** $(r,d_0,d_1)$. Let $P_n$ be the convex hull of all count vectors of plane trees with $n$ edges, the *RNA polytope*.

**Theorem.** Let $n\ge5$.

1. *(Vertices.)* For $n$ odd,

$$P_n=\operatorname{conv}\big\{(1,\tfrac{n+1}{2},0),\ (1,n-1,0),\ (1,1,n-1),\ (n,n,0)\big\},$$

a tetrahedron. For $n$ even, $P_n=\operatorname{conv}\{(1,\frac{n+2}{2},0),(1,\frac n2,1),(2,\frac{n+2}{2},0),(1,n-1,0),(1,1,n-1),(n,n,0)\}$.
2. *(Inequalities and normal fan.)* $P_n$ is cut out by

$$-r+2d_0+d_1\ge n,\qquad r\ge1,\qquad r+(1-n)d_0+(2-n)d_1\ge2n-n^2,\qquad d_1\ge0,$$

plus $d_0+d_1\ge\frac{n+2}{2}$ when $n$ is even. For odd $n$, its normal fan is generated by the rays $(1,0,0)$, $(0,0,1)$, $(-1,2,1)$ and $(1,1-n,2-n)$; for even $n$ there is a fifth ray $(0,1,1)$.
3. *(Lattice points.)* The facet $\operatorname{conv}\{(1,1,n-1),(1,n-1,0),(n,n,0)\}$ has no interior lattice points. For odd $n$, each of the other three facets of the tetrahedron has $\frac14(n-3)^2$ interior lattice points, and $\partial P_n$ contains $\frac14(3n^2-8n+13)$ lattice points in total, all realized by plane trees.
4. *(Biological meaning of vertices.)*
 - $(n,n,0)$ is the unique tree with all $n$ helices closing hairpins off the exterior loop.
 - $(1,1,n-1)$ is the path, a single stem with only internal loops.
 - $(1,n-1,0)$ is one stem leading to a single $n$-way multiloop.
 - For odd $n$, $(1,\frac{n+1}{2},0)$ consists of the trees with no internal loops and only 3-way multiloops. These correspond to full binary trees, so there are $C_{(n-1)/2}$ of them, a Catalan number.

A structure whose count vector lies in the interior of $P_n$ is never a minimum free energy structure, whatever the parameters. A structure on the relative interior of a face $F$ is optimal exactly for parameters in the normal cone of $F$. So the normal fan partitions parameter space by the branching type that is optimal, and the predicted branching is either minimal (only 3-way junctions) or maximal (one $n$-way junction, or all-hairpin).

## Expansion

The count vectors of plane trees with $n$ edges fall into four families of linear inequalities, according to whether $r=1$, $r=d_0$, and so on. Each family is a small polytope whose vertices depend on the parity of $n$. One checks that every family's vertices satisfy the four or five inequalities above, and that the claimed vertices are realized, which gives $P_n=\Psi_n$. Interior lattice points of edges are counted by $\gcd$ of coordinate differences minus one, and of facets by Pick's theorem on the 2-dimensional sublattice. For the facet $F$ above, the normalized area is $\frac12(n-1)$ and there are $n+1$ boundary points, so it has $\frac12(n-1)-\frac12(n+1)+1=0$ interior points. Varying the three experimentally undetermined multiloop parameters over the normal fan has no dramatic effect on the predicted structures. A collection of known secondary structures is also placed in $P_n$ through their count vectors.

These are the propositions on $P_n$, its lattice points and the biological meaning of its vertices in Section 3 of the source.
