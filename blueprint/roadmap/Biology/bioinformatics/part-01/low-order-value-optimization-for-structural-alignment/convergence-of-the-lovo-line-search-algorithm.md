# Convergence of the LOVO line-search algorithm

**Setting.** Let $f_1, \dots, f_m : \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}$ be continuously differentiable, $f_{\min}(x) = \min_i f_i(x)$ and $I_{\min}(x) = \{i : f_i(x) = f_{\min}(x)\}$. Fix $\theta \in (0,1)$, $\alpha \in (0, 1/2)$ and $\beta > 0$. Algorithm A1 iterates from $x_0$:

1. choose $\nu(k) \in I_{\min}(x_k)$; stop if $\nabla f_{\nu(k)}(x_k) = 0$;
2. choose a direction $d_k$ with the angle and length conditions

$$\nabla f_{\nu(k)}(x_k)^T d_k \le -\theta \, \|d_k\| \, \|\nabla f_{\nu(k)}(x_k)\|, \qquad \|d_k\| \ge \beta \, \|\nabla f_{\nu(k)}(x_k)\|$$

(for instance a regularized Newton direction $d = -(\nabla^2 f_{\nu(k)}(x_k) + \lambda I)^{-1} \nabla f_{\nu(k)}(x_k)$);
3. find $t_k > 0$ by backtracking with safeguarded quadratic interpolation such that the Armijo condition

$$f_{\min}(x_k + t_k d_k) \le f_{\min}(x_k) + \alpha\, t_k\, \nabla f_{\nu(k)}(x_k)^T d_k$$

holds, and set $x_{k+1} = x_k + t_k d_k$.

**Theorem 1.** Algorithm A1 is well defined, and it terminates at $x_k$ only if $x_k$ is critical.

**Theorem 2.** If $x^*$ is a limit point of a sequence generated by Algorithm A1, then $x^*$ is critical. Moreover, if $x_k \to x^*$ along $k \in K$ and the same index $i = \nu(k)$ is chosen for infinitely many $k \in K$, then $i \in I_{\min}(x^*)$ and $\nabla f_i(x^*) = 0$. Finally, $\lim_{k \in K} \|\nabla f_{\nu(k)}(x_k)\| = 0$.

Applied to protein structural alignment (the minimization form of the score envelope), every limit point of the DP-LS and NB-LS iterations is a critical point of the alignment score, independently of the initial superposition.

## Expansion

For Theorem 1: if $\nabla f_i(x_k) \ne 0$ with $i = \nu(k)$, the angle condition gives $\nabla f_i(x_k)^T d_k < 0$, so $f_i(x_k + t d_k) \le f_i(x_k) + \alpha t \nabla f_i(x_k)^T d_k$ for small $t$, and $f_{\min} \le f_i$ with $f_{\min}(x_k) = f_i(x_k)$ transfers this to $f_{\min}$; backtracking therefore ends. For Theorem 2, finiteness of $\{1, \dots, m\}$ yields a subsequence with a constant active index $i$, continuity places $i$ in $I_{\min}(x^*)$, and the monotone decrease of $f_{\min}$ forces $t_k \nabla f_i(x_k)^T d_k \to 0$. Either the gradients vanish along a subsequence, or the steps shrink; in the latter case the last rejected trial step and the mean value theorem give $\nabla f_i(x^*)^T s \ge \alpha \nabla f_i(x^*)^T s$ for a limit direction $s$, which with $\alpha < 1$ and the angle condition forces $\nabla f_i(x^*) = 0$.
