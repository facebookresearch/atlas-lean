# Monte Carlo null for the goodness of fit of a deconvolution

Let $B \in \mathbb{R}^{c \times g}$ be a signature matrix on $g$ marker genes, let $m$ be a bulk
mixture profile restricted to those genes, taken from a parent profile $M$ on the whole transcriptome,
and let $\hat f(m)$ be the estimated cell-type fractions returned by a deconvolution procedure such as
[nu-support vector regression deconvolution](nu-support-vector-regression-deconvolution-of-cell-type-fractions.md).
The goodness-of-fit statistic is the Pearson correlation

$$R = \operatorname{corr}\bigl(m,\ \hat f(m) \times B\bigr)$$

between the mixture and its reconstruction. It is defined only when the deconvolution returns fractions and both $m$ and $\hat f(m) \times B$ are nonconstant across the $g$ genes; otherwise the sample receives no goodness-of-fit statistic. To test the null hypothesis that none of the $c$ cell
types of $B$ is present in the mixture, draw for $i = 1, \dots, I$ a random mixture $m_i^*$ of the same
length $g$ by sampling $g$ expression values at random from $M$, rerun the deconvolution to get
$\hat f(m_i^*)$, and record

$$R_i^* = \operatorname{corr}\bigl(m_i^*,\ \hat f(m_i^*) \times B\bigr).$$

(each $R_i^*$ is defined under the same nonconstancy conditions, which the procedure assumes hold for every draw). The empirical $P$ value of the deconvolution is the proportion of the null correlations
$R_1^*, \dots, R_I^*$ that are at least as large as the observed $R$ ($I = 500$ in the original work).

## Remarks

Any regression of $m$ on the rows of $B$ produces some fit, even when the mixture contains none of the
reference cell types, so the size of $R$ alone does not say whether the fractions mean anything. The
random mixtures keep the marginal expression distribution of the sample, including its scale and
skew, but break the gene-by-gene correspondence with the signature, so $R^*$ measures how well the
procedure fits expression values unrelated to $B$. A small $P$ value says the sample matches the
signature better than random genes from the same sample do. Because the null is generated by rerunning
the whole estimator, it automatically accounts for the adaptive gene selection inside the regression.
