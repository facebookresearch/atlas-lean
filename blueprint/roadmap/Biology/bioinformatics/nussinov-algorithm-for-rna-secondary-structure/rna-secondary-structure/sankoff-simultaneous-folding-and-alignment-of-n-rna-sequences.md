# Simultaneous folding, alignment and protosequence reconstruction of RNA (Sankoff)

Sankoff (1985) combines free-energy secondary structure prediction with parsimony alignment over a given phylogeny. Restrict structures so that interior loops (2-loops) have at most $U$ unpaired positions and multiple loops have energy linear in their degree $k$ and number $u$ of unpaired positions, $e(s) = A + (k - 1)P + uQ$ (equation (8), Theorem 3). For two sequences he gives dynamic-programming recurrences over 4-tuples of positions computing the minimum cost, the sum of the free energies and a constrained alignment cost, of a pair of equivalent secondary structures (Theorem 4). Two structures are equivalent when they have the same branching configuration: their external pairs and the accessible pairs of their multiple loops are equal in number and pair in the same pattern (Section 4, pp. 817–819). The alignment is then constrained so that these positions are aligned to each other and each multiple loop is aligned with a single multiple loop of the same degree or inserted or deleted whole; interior loops (2-loops) need not correspond and may be inserted or deleted, so their numbers may differ. For $N$ sequences at the leaves of a tree, the multiple-alignment recurrence (16) charges each alignment column, a choice of one symbol or gap $\phi$ per sequence, the cost $\gamma$ of its most parsimonious labelling of the tree; Theorem 5 computes $\gamma$ for one fixed column by the recursion (17) over the vertices of the tree, with effort proportional to $N\,|\Sigma|^2$ for the alphabet $\Sigma$ (stated in the text after the proof of Theorem 5, not in the theorem), and backtracking through (16) and (17) reconstructs the protosequences at interior vertices for most parsimonious alignments. The bound $N\,|\Sigma|^2$ is for one fixed column and is paid at each application of (16); it is not a bound on reconstructing whole protosequences of arbitrary length. The $2N$-dimensional generalization of Theorem 4 computes the minimum total cost of $N$ equivalent structures together with alignments and protosequences (Theorem 6). For fixed $N$ and sequences of length $n$, letting all positions vary freely requires time proportional to $n^{3N}$, with an extra factor $U^{2N}$ from the search over 2-loops, and memory of order $n^{2N}$; under the band constraint (21) of width $W$ on corresponding positions ("cutting corners"), time becomes proportional to $n^3(U^2W)^N$ and memory to $n^2(U^2W)^N$.

## Expansion

1. The model: sequences, alignments and their costs (Section 2, Theorem 1), secondary structures with their loop decomposition and the energy restrictions (Theorems 2–3), and equivalence of structures (same branching configuration) with the loop constraints on the alignment.
2. Theorem 4: the recurrences (13)–(15) for two sequences and their correctness.
3. Theorem 5: the most parsimonious labelling cost of one fixed alignment column by the recursion (17), with effort proportional to $N$ and the square of the alphabet size per column, and protosequence reconstruction by backtracking through (16) and (17).
4. Theorem 6 and the recurrences (18)–(20) for $N$ sequences.
5. The time and memory bounds (Sections 5–6), the banded variant with (21), and the generalization to the multiple-loop energy (12).
