# Intron clusters from shared splice junctions

Each RNA-seq split read that aligns with at least a minimum overhang into the exons on both sides defines an excised intron, an interval $[a, b]$ of the genome with donor end $a$ and acceptor end $b$ (on a given strand). Given the set $\mathcal{I}$ of introns observed in a group of overlapping introns, form the **junction-sharing graph** with vertex set $\mathcal{I}$ and an edge between two introns whenever they share a splice junction (the same donor $a$ or the same acceptor $b$). An **intron cluster** is a connected component of this graph with at least two vertices; singleton introns are discarded, since a lone intron has no alternative within the data.

**Filtering.** For each cluster $C$ with read counts $y_{ij}$ (sample $i$, intron $j$), remove every intron $j$ with $\sum_i y_{ij}$ below an absolute threshold (default $30$ reads) or below a fraction (default $0.1\%$) of the cluster total $\sum_{i,j'} y_{ij'}$, then rebuild the junction-sharing graph on the remaining introns and recompute its connected components; repeat until no intron is removed. The output is, for each cluster and sample, the vector of intron counts $(y_{i1}, \dots, y_{iJ})$ and its total $n_{iC} = \sum_{j'} y_{ij'}$. The excision proportions $y_{ij}/n_{iC}$ are defined only for samples with $n_{iC} > 0$: the filter acts on counts pooled over samples, so an individual sample can have no reads in a retained cluster, and then its counts (all zero) are kept but it has no proportions for that cluster.

## Expansion

Two introns sharing a donor (or an acceptor) are mutually exclusive uses of that splice site. This exclusivity does not extend through a whole component: the introns $(1, 3)$, $(1, 7)$ and $(5, 7)$ form one cluster, yet $(1, 3)$ and $(5, 7)$ are disjoint and can both be spliced out of one transcript. The proportions $y_{ij}/n_{iC}$ are therefore shares of the observed junction counts in the cluster, not probabilities of mutually exclusive choices made by each molecule; they are comparable across samples without reference to annotated transcripts. Connected components are unique and are found in linear time by breadth-first search. Removing a low-count intron can disconnect a component (when that intron was the only link between two groups of introns), which is why the procedure re-clusters after each filtering pass; since every pass removes at least one intron or stops, the iteration terminates after at most $\lvert\mathcal{I}\rvert$ passes.
