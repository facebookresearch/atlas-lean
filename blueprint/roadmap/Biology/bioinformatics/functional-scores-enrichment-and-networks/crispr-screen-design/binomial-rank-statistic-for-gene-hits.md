# Binomial rank statistic for gene-level hit calling in perturbation screens

Consider a pooled genetic screen in which $N$ perturbations (single-guide RNAs) are ranked by a per-perturbation readout, for instance log fold change between selected and control populations. Fix a gene targeted by $n$ perturbations and a threshold keeping the top $\theta N$ perturbations of the whole experiment. Let the perturbations of this gene that pass the threshold be indexed by their within-gene rank $k = 1, 2, \dots$ in the same order, and let the $k$-th of them sit at overall rank $R_k$ in the experiment, so that $p_k = R_k / N$ is the fraction of all perturbations ranking at least as high.

**Definition (gene score).** For every such $k \ge 1$, the first passing perturbation included, compute the binomial mass

$$b_k \;=\; \binom{n}{k} p_k^{\,k} (1 - p_k)^{\,n-k}.$$

The gene score is the smallest of these values, $\min_k b_k$, the value of the gene's least probable passing perturbation.

**Eligibility.** A gene receives a score only if at least two of its perturbations pass the threshold. This condition decides whether the gene is scored at all; it does not remove $k = 1$ from the minimum.

**Calibration.** A null distribution of gene scores is generated by permuting the ranked list of perturbations and recomputing every score with the same threshold and eligibility rule; the empirical tail of that distribution converts a gene score into a $p$-value and, after multiple-testing correction across genes, into a false discovery rate.

## Remarks

The binomial mass is a ranking score, not a null probability. Under a uniformly random ranking of $N$ distinct perturbations, the number of a gene's $n$ perturbations among the top $R$ ranks is hypergeometric (population $N$, $R$ marked ranks, $n$ draws), not binomial; the binomial law with $p = R/N$ is its with-replacement approximation, close when $n$ is small compared with $N$ and $R$. With $n = N$ and $0 < R < N$ the hypergeometric count equals $R$ with probability $1$, while the binomial law with $n$ trials and $p = R/N$ is not a point mass. Evaluating the mass at the data-dependent quantile $p_k$ also rewards a gene whose perturbations are both numerous near the top and tightly packed, and the minimum over $k$ lets the score choose the depth at which the gene's evidence is strongest. Because neither the binomial form nor the data-dependent $p_k$ yields a valid tail probability, significance is read from the permutation null. Requiring two passing perturbations suppresses genes carried by a single outlier guide, the dominant failure mode when individual guides have off-target activity.
