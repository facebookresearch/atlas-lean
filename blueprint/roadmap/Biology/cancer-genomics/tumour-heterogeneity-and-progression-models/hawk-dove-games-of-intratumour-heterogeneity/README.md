# Hawk–dove games of intratumour heterogeneity

Game-theoretic models of competition among cancer cell types for a shared resource. Cells play a hawk–dove game, can recognize the type of the cell they meet but not their own, and choose how aggressively to treat each type; comparing evolutionarily stable strategies in tumours with two versus three cell types shows how heterogeneity can lower the payoff of the most aggressive clone.
