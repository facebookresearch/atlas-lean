# Number of samples for unique clone-tree reconstruction

This node records a research direction: when do additional, independently sampled bulk mixtures make a clone tree uniquely recoverable, rather than merely improving a fitting score?

## What would settle it

For a fixed perfect phylogeny model and a stated distribution of the mixture proportions, bound the probability that the reconstruction is unique as a function of the number of samples, the number of clones and a separation parameter, and prove matching impossibility bounds. Observational equivalence must not be assumed away.

## Status

No cited source poses this question in the form above; it is a research direction motivated by the cited results. This page does not certify the status or novelty of any particular formulation.

Pradhan and El-Kebir show that the perfect phylogeny mixture problem typically has multiple solutions; in their simulations the number of solutions increases with the number of mutations and decreases with the number of bulk samples. This is an empirical observation, not a theorem about the number of samples.

This page records a research direction and asserts no theorem or present-status certification.
