# Average absolute copy number of a tumor sample

For real numbers $\alpha$ (the purity) and $q$ (a copy number in the cancer cells), the average absolute copy number of the mixture is

$$D(\alpha, q) = \alpha q + 2(1 - \alpha),$$

the normal cells being diploid. With $q = \tau$, the cancer-cell ploidy, it is the sample ploidy (Carter et al. 2012).

## Sources

- S. L. Carter et al., *Absolute quantification of somatic DNA alterations in human cancer*, Nat. Biotechnol. 30 (2012), 413–421 (Eqs. 1, 2, 10, 11).
