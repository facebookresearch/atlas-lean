# Minimum viable volume and resection dynamics of Gompertz growth with surface loss

**Setting.** Tumor volume $V(t) \ge 0$ follows a Gompertz law with a necrotic loss proportional to surface area,

$$\frac{dV}{dt} = r_g V \ln\Big(\frac{K}{V}\Big) - \lambda V^{2/3}, \qquad r_g, K, \lambda > 0.$$

A non-trivial equilibrium $V_\infty \in (0,K)$ satisfies $r_g\ln(K/V_\infty) = \lambda V_\infty^{-1/3}$. Surgery at time $t_{op}$ acts by the **resection operator** $\mathcal R(V) = (1-\eta)V + \epsilon$ with efficiency $\eta \in (0,1)$ and minimum residual volume $\epsilon > 0$; afterwards growth resumes with postoperative parameters $(r_p, K_{post})$.

**Theorem.**
1. (Minimum viable volume, Theorem 2.1) Write $\frac{dV}{dt}=V^{2/3}g(V)$ with $g(V)=r_gV^{1/3}\ln(K/V)-\lambda$. Since $g(V)\to-\lambda$ as $V\to0^+$, there is $\delta_0>0$ with $g\le-\lambda/2$ on $(0,\delta_0)$, so $\frac{dV}{dt}\le-\frac\lambda2V^{2/3}$ there, and every solution with $V(0)\in(0,\delta_0)$ reaches $V=0$ by time $6V(0)^{1/3}/\lambda$. Moreover there is $\delta > 0$ such that every solution with $V(0) \in (0,K)$ eventually satisfies $V(t) \le K - \delta$.
2. (Convergence rate, Theorems 2.2 and 3.1) $V_\infty$ is locally asymptotically stable when $V_\infty > Ke^{-3}$, with linearized rate

$$\alpha = r_g - \tfrac13\lambda V_\infty^{-1/3} = r_g\Big[1 - \tfrac13\ln\Big(\frac{K}{V_\infty}\Big)\Big].$$

3. (Resection, Theorem 3.2 and Proposition 1) $\mathcal R$ is a contraction with Lipschitz constant $1-\eta$, its unique fixed point is $V^* = \epsilon/\eta$, and $\mathcal R(V) \ge \epsilon$. Surgery reduces the volume if and only if $V(t_{op}^-) > V^*$, i.e. $\eta V(t_{op}^-)/\epsilon > 1$.
4. (Adjuvant kill rate, Proposition 2) After surgery let $\dot V = r_pV\ln(K_{post}/V) - \lambda V^{2/3} - \delta V = V\,h(V)$ with $h(V) = r_p\ln(K_{post}/V) - \lambda V^{-1/3} - \delta$, a kill rate $\delta \ge 0$ and a threshold $V_{crit} > 0$. If $\delta \ge r_p\ln(K_{post}/V_{crit}) - \lambda V_{crit}^{-1/3}$, then $h(V_{crit}) \le 0$, and $[0,V_{crit}]$ is forward invariant: $V(t_0) \le V_{crit}$ implies $V(t) \le V_{crit}$ for all $t \ge t_0$.
5. (Cube-root linearization, Theorem 4.1) For the early postoperative law $\dot V = rV - \kappa V^{2/3}$ with $r \ne 0$, $\kappa > 0$,

$$V(t) = \Big[\Big(V_{res}^{1/3} - \frac{\kappa}{r}\Big)e^{rt/3} + \frac{\kappa}{r}\Big]^3;$$

if $r < 0$ the tumor is cleared at $T_{ext} = \frac{3}{|r|}\ln\big(1 + |r|V_{res}^{1/3}/\kappa\big)$, and if $r > 0$ the volume $(\kappa/r)^3$ is an unstable threshold separating regression from regrowth.
6. (Radiotherapy, Theorem 4.4) For $\dot V = -\Psi(t)D(t)V$ with radiosensitivity $\Psi(t)=\alpha+\beta\sin(2\pi t/\tau)\ge0$ on $[0,T]$ and doses $0 \le D \le D_{max}$, $\int_0^T D \le D_{total}$, every dose schedule minimizing $V(T)$ satisfies, for a constant threshold $\lambda^*$, $D^* = D_{max}$ almost everywhere on $\{\Psi > \lambda^*\}$ and $D^* = 0$ almost everywhere on $\{\Psi < \lambda^*\}$; on $\{\Psi=\lambda^*\}$ the dose may take intermediate values, subject only to the budget.

## Expansion

Near $V = 0$ the loss $\lambda V^{2/3}$ dominates $r_gV\ln(K/V)$, because $V^{1/3}\ln(K/V) \to 0$; on that interval $\dot V \le -cV^{2/3}$, so $\frac{d}{dt}V^{1/3}\le -c/3$ and the volume reaches zero in finite time, unlike the pure Gompertz law where every small tumor grows. Away from zero the sign of $g$ decides the long-time behavior, and since $g'(V)=r_gV^{-2/3}\big[\tfrac13\ln(K/V)-1\big]$ the equilibria are the at most two zeros of $g$, separated by $Ke^{-3}$. Differentiating the equilibrium condition gives $f'(V_\infty) = \frac13\lambda V_\infty^{-1/3} - r_g$, hence the rate formula and the stability condition $\ln(K/V_\infty) < 3$. For the kill-rate clause, $h'(V) = V^{-4/3}\big(\tfrac\lambda3 - r_pV^{1/3}\big)$, so $h$ increases below $(\lambda/(3r_p))^3$ and decreases above it; $h(V_{crit}) \le 0$ makes the right-hand side nonpositive at $V = V_{crit}$, which by uniqueness of solutions near $V_{crit}$ keeps trajectories from crossing it upward, while a solution started above $V_{crit}$ can be trapped at a zero of $h$ above $V_{crit}$ (see (b) below). The substitution $u = V^{1/3}$ turns $\dot V = rV - \kappa V^{2/3}$ into the linear equation $\dot u = \frac r3 u - \frac\kappa3$. The radiotherapy problem is linear in the control: $\ln V(T) = \ln V(0) - \int_0^T \Psi D$, so the problem is to maximize the linear functional $\int_0^T\Psi D$, an optimal schedule spends the dose budget where radiosensitivity is highest, and doses on a level set of $\Psi$ can be exchanged without changing the objective.

**Supplementary facts (elementary consequences, not stated in the source).** The following are not part of the theorem; each is proved here in full.

(a) *Extinction basin.* Since $g'(V)=r_gV^{-2/3}\big[\tfrac13\ln(K/V)-1\big]$, $g$ is strictly increasing on $(0,Ke^{-3})$ and strictly decreasing on $(Ke^{-3},\infty)$, with maximum $g(Ke^{-3})=3r_gK^{1/3}e^{-1}-\lambda$, $g(0^+)=-\lambda$, $g(K)=-\lambda$ and $g(V)\to-\infty$ as $V\to\infty$. If $\lambda>3r_gK^{1/3}/e$, then $g\le-c$ with $c=\lambda-3r_gK^{1/3}/e>0$, so $\frac{d}{dt}V^{1/3}=\tfrac13g(V)\le-c/3$ while $V>0$, and every positive solution reaches $0$ by time $3V(0)^{1/3}/c$: negative drift near zero alone does not decide extinction, but a globally negative $g$ does. If $\lambda<3r_gK^{1/3}/e$, then $g(Ke^{-3})>0$ and strict monotonicity on each side give exactly two zeros $V_1<Ke^{-3}<V_\infty$, and $V_\infty<K$ because $g(K)<0$. The right-hand side is locally Lipschitz on $(0,\infty)$, so solutions cannot cross the equilibria $V_1,V_\infty$. For $V(0)\in(0,V_1)$, $V$ decreases and $g(V(t))\le g(V(0))<0$ (as $g$ is increasing there), so $\frac{d}{dt}V^{1/3}\le g(V(0))/3$ and $V$ reaches $0$ in finite time. For $V(0)\in(V_1,V_\infty)$, $g>0$, so $V$ increases, stays below $V_\infty$ and converges to an equilibrium in $(V_1,V_\infty]$, i.e. to $V_\infty$; for $V(0)>V_\infty$, $g<0$, so $V$ decreases to $V_\infty$ although the drift is negative. Hence the basin of finite-time extinction is exactly $(0,V_1)$. Example: for $K=r_g=1$ and $\lambda=2^{-1/3}\ln2\approx0.550<3/e$, $g(1/2)=2^{-1/3}\ln2-\lambda=0$, so $V_\infty=1/2$ and the solution from $V(0)=3/4$ decreases to $1/2$ without reaching $0$.

(b) *Kill rate and larger tumours.* Since $h'(V)=V^{-4/3}\big(\tfrac\lambda3-r_pV^{1/3}\big)$, $h$ increases on $(0,(\lambda/(3r_p))^3)$ and decreases afterwards. If $h(V_{crit})\le0$ and $V_{crit}\ge(\lambda/(3r_p))^3$, then $h<0$ on $(V_{crit},\infty)$; a solution started above $V_{crit}$ then decreases while it stays above $V_{crit}$, and it cannot converge to a limit $L>V_{crit}$ (that would force $h(L)=0$), so $\limsup_{t\to\infty}V(t)\le V_{crit}$. Without the extra hypothesis this fails: for $r_p=K_{post}=1$, $\lambda=0.3$, $V_{crit}=10^{-4}$ and $\delta=\ln(10^4)-0.3\cdot10^{4/3}\approx2.747$ (so $h(V_{crit})=0$), $h$ increases on $(V_{crit},10^{-3})$ and decreases afterwards, $h(10^{-3})\approx1.16>0$, and $h$ has a unique zero $V_2\approx0.022$ above $10^{-3}$; hence $h>0$ on $(V_{crit},V_2)$, $h<0$ on $(V_2,\infty)$, and the solutions from $V(0)=10^{-3}$ and from $V(0)=0.5$ converge to $V_2>V_{crit}$.

(c) *Radiotherapy schedules.* Minimizing $V(T)=V(0)\exp(-\int_0^T\Psi D)$ means maximizing the linear functional $J(D)=\int_0^T\Psi D$. Given an optimal $D^*$ with threshold $\lambda^*$ and $E=\{\Psi=\lambda^*\}$, let $m=\int_ED^*\le D_{max}|E|$; since Lebesgue measure has no atoms there is $E'\subseteq E$ with $|E'|=m/D_{max}$, and replacing $D^*$ on $E$ by $D_{max}\mathbf 1_{E'}$ keeps $J$, the bounds and the budget, so a bang–bang optimum ($D^*\in\{0,D_{max}\}$ almost everywhere) can always be chosen. If $E$ is a null set, every optimum is determined almost everywhere by the threshold rule and is bang–bang; this holds for $\beta\ne0$, since then $\Psi-\lambda^*$ is a non-constant trigonometric function with finitely many zeros in $[0,T]$. If $\beta=0$, then $\Psi\equiv\alpha\ge0$ and $J(D)=\alpha\int_0^TD$, so for $0<D_{total}<TD_{max}$ every schedule spending the whole budget is optimal, the constant schedule $D\equiv D_{total}/T$ included. If $D_{total}\ge TD_{max}$, the budget does not bind and $D\equiv D_{max}$ maximizes $\Psi D$ pointwise, so it is optimal (and it is the unique optimum when $\Psi>0$ almost everywhere).
