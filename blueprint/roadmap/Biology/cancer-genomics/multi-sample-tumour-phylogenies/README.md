# Multi-sample tumour phylogenies

Sequencing several spatially distinct samples (primary sections and metastases) of one cancer gives, for each somatic variant, a presence/absence pattern across samples. Because somatic evolution is asexual and the normal tissue carries none of the variants, the patterns of truly co-inherited variants must form a nested-or-disjoint family, and reconstructing the phylogeny reduces to selecting the most reliable such family from noisy, impure sequencing data.
