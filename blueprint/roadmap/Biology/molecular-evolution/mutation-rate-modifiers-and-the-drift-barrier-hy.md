# Mutation-rate modifiers and the drift-barrier hypothesis

This page covers the evolution of the mutation rate through modifier alleles, a topic whose results depend on the model chosen. A modifier that lowers the mutation rate is favoured indirectly, through the deleterious mutations it avoids, and may carry a direct cost. The drift-barrier hypothesis holds that selection lowers the mutation rate only until the selective advantage of a further reduction becomes small compared with the power of random genetic drift, so that the attainable rate depends on the effective population size.

## Expansion

Model the costs and benefits of a modifier explicitly, then prove comparisons of fixation probabilities in a finite population, or an explicit weak-selection limit, each as a page of its own with a source. A sharp universal boundary at a chosen value of $N_e s$ is not assumed.
