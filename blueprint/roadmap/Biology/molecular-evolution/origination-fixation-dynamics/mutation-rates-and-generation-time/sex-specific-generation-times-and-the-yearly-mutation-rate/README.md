# Sex-specific generation times and the yearly mutation rate

Pedigree studies measure mutations per generation at particular paternal and maternal ages, while dating evolutionary events needs substitutions per year. Converting one to the other depends on the male and female generation times separately, through the paternal age effect, so the yearly rate is not a constant of the species. A dated ancient genome bypasses the conversion by measuring the divergence its lineage never accumulated.
