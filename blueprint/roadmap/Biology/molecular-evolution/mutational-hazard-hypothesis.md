# Mutational hazard hypothesis

Added genomic sequence can increase the target for deleterious mutation.
The [explicit target and diffusion-fixation comparison](fixation-of-a-feature-that-enlarges-the-target-f.md)
states an independent lethal-hit model, its fitness deficit, its exact
relative fixation expression in a diffusion approximation, and the joint
weak-effect scaling limit. The finite expression has an additional
per-copy factor; it is not universally just a function of target size times
population size.

## Expansion

Lynch and Conery argue that population-size reductions allowed many genomic
complexity increases to emerge passively. That is an empirical explanatory
hypothesis, not a theorem proved by the simplified target comparison.
Partly deleterious target effects and recurrent gain/loss maintenance need
specified effect distributions and processes. Strong suppression limits
must state which parameters vary; the fitness deficit saturates with target
size, so a large target-size product alone is not a universal limit.
