# Birth, death and innovation models of gene family size

A birth–death–innovation model (BDIM) treats the size of each paralogous gene family as a birth–death chain driven by duplication, loss and innovation. Its stationary distribution is determined by the ratio of birth to death rates, so power-law family-size distributions arise exactly when the rates are balanced to second order, and many different rate laws share the same stationary distribution while differing in their dynamics.
