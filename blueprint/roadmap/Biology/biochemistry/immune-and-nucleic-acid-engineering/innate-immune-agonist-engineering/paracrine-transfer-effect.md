# The paracrine transfer effect as a nanomedicine design concept

Cellular export of a nanomaterial has usually been treated as loss. The paracrine transfer effect (PTE) reframes it: a nanomaterial is first internalized by a "waypoint" cell and then exported - as the intact particle or as a therapeutically active subunit - to a "destination" cell, influencing both.

The concept as the Perspective states it:

- *Definition.* Administered nanomedicines internalize within a waypoint cell and may act there; exported material, comprising either the initial nanoparticle or a therapeutically active subunit, can travel through the extracellular matrix and internalize into a destination cell, where it can act again.
- *Reservoir case.* Where the waypoint cell is unaffected by the nanomedicine, it serves as a reservoir releasing the agent to neighboring or more distant cells.
- *Candidate waypoint cells* named include cancer cells, tissue-resident macrophages, endothelial cells, ex vivo cell therapies, glial cells and circulating monocytes.
- *Export routes and determinants* reviewed include exosome secretion, transcytosis, lysosomal exocytosis and secretory autophagy, with particle size, colloidal stability and surface charge influencing export; the Perspective notes that export can occur with first-order kinetics.
- *Case studies discussed* are STING-stimulating polymeric cyclic-dinucleotide nanoparticles and TLR9-stimulating liposomal spherical nucleic acids.

This is a Perspective: it organizes and names a concept and discusses existing case studies. It does not itself demonstrate PTE, and the endogenous-messenger transfer described elsewhere in this folder is an analogy to it, not an instance of the same definition.

## Context

Research level. The page names a pattern that recurs in this subfield - a cell that takes up material and later supplies it, or an active fragment of it, to its neighbors. The Perspective is co-authored and co-corresponded by the Artzi and Mirkin groups.

## Expansion

Making this quantitative would require measured uptake and export rates for a defined particle in a defined cell type; a statement of whether the exported species is the particle or a subunit, since the two have different fates; evidence that the exported material remains active; and identifiability analysis for any transfer parameter. The Perspective's own observation that export can follow first-order kinetics is a starting point, not a parameterised model, and its case studies do not validate one.
