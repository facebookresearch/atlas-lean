# Pairing immunogenic cell death with STING activation in one carrier-free conjugate

A peptide-cyclic dinucleotide nanoconjugate is reported to induce immunogenic cell death in tumor cells and to activate STING signaling in dendritic cells. The stated rationale is that efficient cross-priming requires dendritic cells integrating antigenic, co-stimulatory and migratory programs at once. Only the abstract was accessible, so every statement below is abstract-scope.

Reported content and its limits:

- *Construct.* A carrier-free peptide-cyclic dinucleotide nanoconjugate; immunogenic-cell-death-inducing peptides were identified by library screening and described as enriched in hydrophobic and cationic residues. No sequences, formulations or procedures are reproduced here.
- *Mechanistic report.* The peptides are described as triggering overlapping pyroptosis, necroptosis and apoptosis, with lysosomal damage and cathepsin release as the dominant upstream drivers.
- *Immune consequence.* Tumour-antigen release with cytosolic cyclic-dinucleotide delivery for STING activation; early cytokine and chemokine responses; migratory dendritic cells enriched for the cDC1 subset trafficking from tumors to draining lymph nodes; enhanced antigen-specific CD8+ T-cell activation and memory. The authors' phrase "three-signal" dendritic cells is their own terminology and is not the standard numbered signals of T-cell activation.
- *Preclinical outcome.* Complete tumor rejection is reported in 70% of "B16-F10- or CT26-bearing mice". The abstract does not give per-model estimates, group sizes or denominators, so the figure cannot be assigned to either model individually. Durable protection against rechallenge is reported for the combination with anti-PD-1, and the rechallenge interval is not stated.

No human data; no strain-to-arm mapping is asserted, since indexing terms do not establish which strain was used in which experiment.

## Context

Research level, and the most recent empirical page in this folder. It completes an arc: deliver an agonist, make the agonist endogenously, or couple agonism to antigen release from dying tumor cells. He and Artzi are the lead and corresponding authors.

## Expansion

Before any mathematical treatment: the dendritic-cell state would have to be defined as a measurable vector rather than a label; antigen release would have to be linked to cross-priming probability; the rejection fraction needs its denominators and a binomial interval, per model; and the anti-PD-1 arm needs an explicit non-interaction reference. Full-text review is required before this page is used as evidence.
