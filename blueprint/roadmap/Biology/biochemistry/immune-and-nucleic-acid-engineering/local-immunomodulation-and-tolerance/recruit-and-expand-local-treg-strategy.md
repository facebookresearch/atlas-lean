# Recruit and expand: a two-signal local strategy for Treg

The strategy pairs two signals in one local device: a chemoattractant to bring regulatory T cells to the site, and a cytokine to support and expand them there. Implemented as a hydrogel microneedle patch delivering CCL22 and IL-2, it aims to shift the local balance toward regulation without systemic immunosuppression.

The strategy and what each claim rests on:

- *Signal 1, attraction.* CCL22, described in both sources as a chemoattractant for Treg. The receptor-level basis for that preference is not established by the sources read here and is not asserted.
- *Signal 2, survival and expansion.* IL-2, described as a Treg survival factor and linked to expansion. "Retention" at the site is a separate quantity and was not measured in the material read here.
- *Format.* A hydrogel microneedle patch applied to skin, chosen for accessibility and for acting at the site of antigen encounter. The earlier study in a skin-allograft setting also reports a correlation between the cell populations sampled by the device and those in the allograft.
- *Device properties reported as translation-relevant.* High loading capacity and shelf-life stability.

Neither signal is novel alone; the claim concerns delivering both locally, and the evidence for effect is the model-specific outcome on the next page.

## Context

## Expansion

Formalisation requires: a coupled attraction-expansion model with a chemokine field and a proliferation term; a measurement of residence time if retention is to be claimed; identification of which signal limits the outcome under which conditions; a dose pair as the control variable with its own response surface; and explicit treatment of the non-monotone dose behavior documented for chemokine-releasing depots elsewhere in this folder. A two-signal design is not additive by assumption.
