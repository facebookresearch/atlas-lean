# A polymorphism inside a microRNA binding site switches regulation on and off

A single-nucleotide polymorphism inside a predicted miR-96 and miR-182 binding site of the *PALLD* transcript determines whether those microRNAs repress it in cell assays: the ancestral allele gives full seed complementarity and repression, the alternate allele introduces a mismatch and abolishes it.

Reported measurements, with their systems:

- *Reporter assays* in two human cell lines: about 30% reduction of the reporter by miR-96 and about 70% by miR-182 when the complementary allele is present; no regulation with the alternate allele.
- *Endogenous expression.* Two human breast cancer lines heterozygous at the site showed opposite Palladin and microRNA expression patterns.
- *Compensatory binding-site rescue, not genome editing.* In a human line homozygous for the alternate allele, wild-type microRNAs did not change Palladin isoform 4 levels, whereas engineered microRNA variants restoring complementarity reduced them. The genomic sequence is unchanged; what is altered is the delivered microRNA. A parallel trend for a second isoform did not reach statistical significance.
- *Cohort analysis.* The associated patient cohort was underpowered for small allelic effects, as the authors state. A stratification hypothesis follows; clinical validation does not.

The claim concerns this site, these two microRNAs and this transcript, in the assays described.

## Context

Graduate level. This is the clearest case in the subfield of genotype entering a design decision: the same oligonucleotide therapy is predicted to act differently depending on a patient-level variant, and the response taken was to engineer the microRNA rather than the target. The work is a collaboration between the Shomron and Artzi groups.

## Expansion

A quantitative version requires: a binding model in which one seed mismatch changes occupancy, with energetics supplied by the thermodynamics subfield; a reporter measurement model separating transcript-level from translation-level effects; isoform-resolved quantification, since the isoforms behaved differently; and population-genetic treatment with explicit power analysis before any stratification claim.
