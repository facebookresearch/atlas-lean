# Staggered fluorescence maxima for two co-delivered RNA cargos

Two labeled cargos delivered inside one construct did not reach their fluorescence maxima together: the reported maximum occurred at 24 h for the mimic and at 48 h for the antagomiR, in the in vivo imaging series. The observation is an ordering of fluorescence peaks at the sampled timepoints, and the sequential-processing explanation is the authors' suggestion.

The observation with its measurement conditions:

- *System.* Orthotopic human triple-negative breast tumors in female immunodeficient mice carrying the implanted RNA-triple-helix hydrogel, imaged over 13 days.
- *Observable.* Unquenching of a labeled strand, which reports separation of that strand from the assembled structure. It is not a calibrated per-cargo concentration and it is not an assay of functional engagement with the silencing machinery.
- *Reported ordering.* Maximum fluorescence at 24 h for one cargo and at 48 h for the other, reported from the imaging series rather than as paired per-animal peak estimates.
- *Interpretation by the authors.* The ordering "suggests" that the mimic is processed first and the antagomiR afterwards. This remains a hypothesis: no timing-mechanism experiment is reported.

## Context

Research level. One construct yielded different sampled reporter-fluorescence maxima for its two cargos. The page illustrates the gap between that observation and evidence about the timing of cargo release, processing or biological activity.

## Expansion

Before a timing claim could be made quantitative: per-animal paired peak estimates rather than series-level maxima; calibration converting fluorescence to amount; denser sampling, since a peak located between two sampled times is not resolved; an independent readout of engagement with the silencing machinery; and a kinetic description whose parameters are identifiable from the available measurements. Any model of sequential processing must be fitted and tested, not inferred from the ordering alone.
