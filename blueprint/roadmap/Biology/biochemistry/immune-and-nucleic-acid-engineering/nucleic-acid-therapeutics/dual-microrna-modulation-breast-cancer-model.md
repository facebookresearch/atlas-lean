# Dual microRNA modulation in an orthotopic triple-negative breast cancer mouse model

An RNA-triple-helix hydrogel carrying a microRNA mimic and an antagomiR was evaluated in female immunodeficient mice bearing orthotopic tumors of a human triple-negative breast-cancer line. The study reports tumor measurements thirteen days after implantation and humane-endpoint survival comparisons across arms.

The result with its exact envelope:

- *Model.* Female SCID hairless congenic mice with orthotopic MDA-MB-231 human breast-cancer xenografts; n = 5 per group. This is a preclinical implant study, not a clinical efficacy study.
- *Tumor measurement.* Figure 5 reports tumor burden after treatment, including the thirteen-day observation, with hydrogel-alone and scrambled-triplex controls and other therapeutic arms. The percentage reference is not defined clearly enough in the cited paragraph to interpret it as shrinkage from pretreatment volume, so the percentages are not reproduced. The separate single-cargo comparisons referenced through supplements are not treated as one matched comparison.
- *Survival.* Kaplan-Meier curves for the triplex, scrambled-triplex and drug-loaded arms were compared with a log-rank (Mantel-Cox) test reported at the figure level as P = 0.006 (n = 5), with humane-endpoint cutoffs stated. The figure does not report a pairwise test, so the value is not assigned here to any single two-arm contrast.
- *Supporting observations.* Reduced Ki67 staining and reduced vascularization on stained sections relative to the scrambled-triplex group; gene-expression changes measured by quantitative PCR on resected tumors are correlates, not demonstrations of direct target engagement.

Mouse data with human tumor cells in an immunodeficient host: no human-efficacy inference, and no extension to other tumor types or routes.

## Context

Research level, read as a property of a specific device in a specific model. The informative comparison is scrambled versus active triplex in the same hydrogel, which separates sequence-dependent action from the material's presence.

## Expansion

Toward a mathematical statement one would need: a tumor-growth model with identifiable parameters instead of a single endpoint ratio; an uncertainty decomposition across animals, measurement and model choice; a stated inferential target, such as a difference in growth rate rather than per-cent reduction on a fixed day; and pairwise comparisons with multiplicity control if arm-to-arm survival claims are wanted. A measurement prerequisite on relative expression estimates is still needed and is not yet linked. The transcriptional correlates would require separate target-validation experiments.
