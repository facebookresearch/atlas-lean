# Watson-Crick and Hoogsteen pairing in nucleic-acid therapeutics

A nucleobase offers more than one hydrogen-bonding edge. Duplex nucleic acids use the Watson-Crick edge; a third strand can bind an existing duplex through the Hoogsteen edge of a purine, giving a triple helix. This is the structural vocabulary behind triplex-based RNA constructs used for local gene modulation.

Conventions, and the separation of rationale from measurement:

- Pairing is described here by hydrogen-bonding edges, not by free energies; binding energetics belong to the molecular-thermodynamics subfield.
- A *triplex-forming oligonucleotide* binds a duplex via Hoogsteen or reverse-Hoogsteen hydrogen bonds while the duplex retains Watson-Crick pairing; orientation is stated relative to the purine-rich strand.
- *Motif rationale versus observation.* For the RNA construct in this folder, the authors argue that the chosen antiparallel motif requires no base protonation and therefore is not pH-dependent in the way protonation-requiring motifs are. That is a design rationale.
- *What was measured.* Stability of that assembled triplex was reported across a finite tested range, pH 5 to 9, together with tests of ionic strength, temperature, urea and serum. The rationale does not establish stability at arbitrary pH, and the measured range is the claim that is supported.
- *Modelled versus measured structure.* The published secondary-structure depiction of the triplex is a software model; the experimental support for assembly comes from separate assays (fluorescence proximity, gel electrophoresis, melting behavior).

## Context

Undergraduate level. The page exists so later pages can say "triple helix" without importing unstated structural or stability assumptions. Its motivation is practical: a single-stranded RNA cargo is degraded quickly, and assembling several strands changes the physical object that must survive delivery.

## Expansion

A quantitative development would need: base-triple geometry; stacking contributions; dependence of stability on sequence composition, ionic strength and pH as a measured surface rather than a range; and melting thermodynamics linking a transition midpoint to free energy. Those belong to the molecular-foundations and thermodynamics material. Nothing here asserts how stable any particular triplex is outside the tested conditions.
