# pH dependence of imine-crosslinked adhesives (empirical)

On treated rat duodenal tissue ex vivo, a PEG–dextran aldehyde sealant's ultimate adhesive strength was higher at pH 7.4 than at pH 6 (60% lower) and pH 9 (77% lower); a different imine-crosslinked sealant retained over 50% of its initial solids after one week in pH 2 buffer and over 80% at day 16 at pH 7.

Findings (empirical). Artzi et al. (2009) explain qualitatively that amines add to aldehydes and the intermediate dehydrates to the imine: the addition is slow at acidic pH, where many amines are protonated, and the dehydration is slow at basic pH. In their test on treated rat duodenal tissue ex vivo, ultimate adhesive strength was highest of the three values at pH 7.4 and fell by 60% at pH 6 and 77% at pH 9, with the adhesive modulus following the same trend. Muñoz Taboada et al. (2024), with a polyethyleneimine-modified Pluronic/oxidized-dextran sealant, note that imine bonds are less stable in acid; their formulations retained over 50% of initial solid content after one week in a large excess of pH 2 buffer renewed daily, and over 80% at day 16 at pH 7, with water content stable near 80% in both conditions. These are separate lower bounds at different times and do not establish an ordering between the two conditions at a common time.

## Context

Graduate. The pH dependence links material chemistry to the tissue microenvironment, a theme of the cited sealant studies. The two studies use different formulations, assays and observation times; retained dry solids are neither bond-cleavage rates nor evidence of in vivo lesion protection, and three pH points establish only the tested ordering, not an optimum.

## Expansion

A model would need pH-dependent rate constants for carbinolamine formation, dehydration and imine hydrolysis (with amine $pK_a$ values), a crosslink-density balance, and a link from crosslink density to adhesive strength and to mass retention; each is a separate, source-backed step.
