# Nanoparticle penetration in brain extracellular space (empirical)

In fresh human and rat brain tissue ex vivo, tested polymer nanoparticles as large as 114 nm diffused through extracellular space, but only when densely coated with poly(ethylene glycol).

Study design and findings (empirical). Nance and co-workers tracked nanoparticles of several diameters and surface coatings in fresh human and rat brain tissue ex vivo and in mouse brain in vivo. Tested particles as large as 114 nm in diameter diffused within human and rat tissue, but only when densely coated with poly(ethylene glycol). From the motion of these minimally adhesive particles the authors estimated, with a model, that human brain extracellular space has some pores larger than 200 nm and that more than one quarter of pores are at least 100 nm. In mice in vivo, 40 nm and 100 nm but not 200 nm densely PEGylated particles spread rapidly; paclitaxel-loaded biodegradable particles of about 85 nm with similar surfaces behaved similarly in rat brain tissue.

## Context

Research-level evidence on nanoparticle transport in tissue, contradicting the earlier view that only objects below about 64 nm move appreciably through brain extracellular space. It is relevant to local delivery in glioblastoma, the setting of Dosta et al. (2024), but it is a different experiment and not a validation of that system. The pore sizes are inferred from particle tracking with a model, not measured directly.

## Expansion

A mathematical treatment would need a model of hindered diffusion in a porous medium with a pore-size distribution and particle adhesion, the estimator used to infer pore sizes from multiple-particle tracking, and its uncertainty. Statements about other tissues, tumors or humans in vivo require separate evidence.
