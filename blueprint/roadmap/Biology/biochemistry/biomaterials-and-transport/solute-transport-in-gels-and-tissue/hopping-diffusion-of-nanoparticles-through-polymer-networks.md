# Hopping diffusion of nanoparticles through polymer networks

In the scaling theory of Cai, Panyukov and Rubinstein, non-sticky nanoparticles moderately larger than the mesh of a polymer solid are trapped at short times but diffuse at long times by hopping between confinement cells; much larger particles stay trapped in solids.

Claims of the source (abstract level). Particles larger than the mesh size $a_x$ of unentangled networks, or the tube diameter $a_e$ of entangled polymer liquids, are trapped by network or entanglement cells. At long times they can diffuse by overcoming a free-energy barrier between neighbouring cells, and the terminal diffusion coefficient dominated by hopping is appreciable for particles moderately larger than $a_x$ or $a_e$. Much larger particles are permanently trapped by local network cells in polymer solids (networks and gels), whereas in polymer liquids (melts and solutions) they can still move once entanglement cells rearrange on the liquid's relaxation time. Hopping diffusion in entangled polymer liquids and networks has a weaker dependence on particle size than in unentangled networks, because entanglements can slide along chains.

## Context

Research frontier for nanoparticle transport in gels and tissue; the source mentions nano drug carriers in biological gels such as mucus. The page records the regime structure only; quantitative step sizes, barriers, diffusion coefficients and the time windows of ballistic, subdiffusive and confined motion depend on the regime and are in the source's Section 2 and later sections. The regimes bear on nanoparticles or in-situ nanogels released into gels or tissue: whether they are smaller than, comparable to, or much larger than the local mesh changes their mobility.
