# Sprayable gastrointestinal hydrogel sealant (empirical)

A sprayable PEI-modified Pluronic/oxidized-dextran sealant gelled in under 2 s at 37 °C, held a burst pressure of 56.3 ± 12 mmHg 24 h after application to ex vivo porcine stomach, and blocked over 98% of pepsin in an in vitro transwell assay.

Findings (empirical), by assay. Bench: high-concentration formulations gelled in under 2 s at 37 °C (a PAMAM-dendrimer analogue gelled in 6.75 ± 0.25 s); swelling at 24 h, defined from wet masses (Methods Eq. (1)), was about 50% for formulations containing 15% and 20% PluPEI; formulations retained over 50% of initial solids after one week in daily renewed pH 2 buffer and over 80% at day 16 at pH 7. Under compression, the 10:20 and 20:20 formulations had strains at fracture of 57% and 63% and stresses at fracture of 677 and 1646 kPa (Young's moduli are also reported, but the text and the Fig. 2j caption label the formulations differently, so they are not reproduced here); tensile adhesion strengths were 10.4 and 10.1 kPa. Ex vivo burst test (porcine stomach, 3 mm lesion): 64.7 ± 11 mmHg at 0 h and 56.3 ± 12 mmHg at 24 h, versus 110.0 ± 50.1 and 43.0 ± 37 mmHg for BioGlue. In vitro transwells with pH 2 fluid and 1.8 mg/mL pepsin: over 50% of pepsin crossed unprotected membranes within 1 h, while fresh and 7-day-aged sealant blocked over 98%; pepsin penetrated about 250 µm into a gel about 2 mm thick. Animals, separate studies: rabbit subcutaneous implantation (pathology scoring at 2 and 4 weeks), guinea-pig sensitization (no sensitization reported), and swine colon lesions, where sealant was present in all 4 analysed lesions at day 3 and in none of the 4 analysed lesions at each of days 7 and 14; one perforation occurred in a control lesion and none in protected lesions, with no statistically significant difference. In a separate clipped-perforation swine model, hydrogel was detected in 33% of lesions at day 7.

## Context

Research-level, preclinical. The design requirements the authors state (ultrafast gelation, low viscosity for long catheters, low swelling, sustained adhesion under acid and enzymes) map onto models elsewhere in this folder: gel point, swelling equilibrium, imine hydrolysis and protease diffusion into a gel. The paper also reports compression toughness values (5.97 and 16.59 MJ/m³) that cannot be reconciled with the stated break stresses and strains; they are withheld here, and a volumetric compression work would not be comparable to a fracture or peel energy in J/m². No human efficacy, complication prevention or uniform multi-day protection is established by these studies.

## Expansion
