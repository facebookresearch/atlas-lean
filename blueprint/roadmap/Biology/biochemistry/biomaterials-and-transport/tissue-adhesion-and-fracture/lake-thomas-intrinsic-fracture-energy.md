# Lake–Thomas intrinsic fracture energy of a gel

In the Lake–Thomas model the intrinsic fracture energy is the work to rupture the strands crossing unit area of the crack plane, $\Gamma_0 = U_f m$; for a fixed network topology, isotropic swelling dilutes the crossing strands as $C^{2/3}$.

Let $U_f$ be the modelled work required to rupture one network strand bridging the crack plane and $m$ the number of strands crossing unit area of the undeformed gel. The Lake–Thomas intrinsic fracture energy is

$$\Gamma_0 = U_f\,m, \qquad m = m_{dry}\,C^{2/3},$$

where, for a gel obtained by isotropic swelling of a given dry network, $m_{dry}$ is the areal strand density of that dry network and $C$ the polymer volume fraction of the gel. With $U_f m_{dry} \approx 50$ J/m² for common elastomers, a gel with about 90% water gives the estimate $50 \times 0.1^{2/3} \approx 10.8$ J/m², i.e. about 10 J/m²; Zhao notes that Eq. (4) tends to overestimate dilute, loosely crosslinked gels.

## Context

Graduate. The estimate explains the brittleness of single-network gels and motivates adding dissipation, as in double-network and alginate–polyacrylamide gels and tough tissue adhesives. $U_f$ is the elastic work stored in a strand at rupture, not the chemical bond dissociation energy: Wang et al. (2019) estimate the stored energy per bond at fracture at about 60 kJ/mol for typical hydrocarbon polymers, well below typical C–C dissociation energies. Trends with strand length are not unconditional: changing crosslink density changes both $U_f$ and $m$.

## Expansion

Needed: the Lake–Thomas expression of $U_f$ as the number of segments per strand times a per-segment rupture work (with the assumption that all segments of a bridging strand are loaded near rupture), its molecular adjustment (Wang et al.), the dependence of $m$ on strand length and polymer density for a stated topology (for monodisperse Gaussian strands at fixed polymer density $m$ decreases with strand length), and the geometric argument for $C^{2/3}$ under isotropic swelling of a fixed topology.
