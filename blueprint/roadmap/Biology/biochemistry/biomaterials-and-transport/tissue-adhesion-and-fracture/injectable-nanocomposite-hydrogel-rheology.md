# Rheology of injectable nanocomposite hydrogels (empirical)

In water, laponite nanoplatelet dispersions reached yield stresses of about 300 Pa at 6% loading and were strongly shear thinning; in dextran-aldehyde/dendrimer precursors, platelet aspect ratio shifted the effect between flow behaviour and gel stiffness in the tested formulations.

Findings (empirical), with media. In water: laponite (LAP) dispersions developed yield stresses up to about 300 Pa at 6% loading, montmorillonite (MMT) high yield stresses only at higher loadings, carbon black about 30 Pa and hydroxyapatite about 1 Pa or less; nanoplatelet dispersions were strongly shear thinning, with viscosity falling by about three orders of magnitude over the tested shear rates and peak viscosities of 1788 Pa·s for 6% LAP and 63 Pa·s for 6% MMT. In macromer solutions the effects depended on the macromer: dispersion in dendrimer lowered yield stress, consistent with poor exfoliation. Across the tested formulations, high-aspect-ratio platelets acted mainly on compressive modulus and low-aspect-ratio platelets mainly on rheology, which the authors describe as nearly independent control of injectability and mechanics.

## Context

Graduate rheology of injectable biomaterials. The authors describe apparent Bingham-fluid behaviour qualitatively; strong decrease of apparent viscosity with shear rate neither proves nor rules out a Bingham law (which has a constant post-yield plastic viscosity), and no constitutive fit is given in the sections read. The aspect-ratio trend is specific to the tested chemistry and dispersion state, not a general law.

## Expansion

A mathematical treatment needs a source-backed constitutive model of yield-stress fluids with fitted parameters, flow of such a fluid through a needle or catheter (pressure drop versus flow rate, plug flow), and a micromechanical model of platelet reinforcement depending on aspect ratio and dispersion.
