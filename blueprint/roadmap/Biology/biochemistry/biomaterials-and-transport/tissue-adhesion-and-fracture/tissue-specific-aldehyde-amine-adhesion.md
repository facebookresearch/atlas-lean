# Tissue-specific aldehyde–amine adhesion (empirical)

Aldehyde-bearing sealants adhered differently to different rat tissues: in one study the interfacial modulus of a high-aldehyde PEG–dextran sealant was 72 kPa on lung and 724 kPa on duodenum, and in another the serosal ileum showed higher amine density and adhesion than duodenum and jejunum.

Findings (empirical). Artzi et al. (2009) varied the dextran-aldehyde solid content (8.8, 14.0, 20.0 wt%) of a star PEG-amine/dextran-aldehyde sealant. On excised rat tissues the interfacial modulus of the high-aldehyde formulation was 724 ± 86 kPa on duodenum, 72 ± 7 kPa on lung, and intermediate on heart (296 ± 60 kPa) and liver (431 ± 15 kPa). An aldehyde-affinity proxy from conjugation of aldehyde-coated fluorescent microspheres correlated with interfacial modulus across the tested tissues and formulations (R = 0.92). In mice, nine-day subcutaneous implants with more aldehyde produced thicker fibrous capsules and higher gelatinase activity. Oliva et al. (2012) used dendrimer-amine/dextran-aldehyde adhesives on rat serosal duodenum, jejunum and ileum: the microsphere-based estimate of amine density was significantly higher in the ileum than in both the duodenum and the jejunum (P < 0.05), and correlated with interfacial fluorescence (R = 0.99) and adhesion strength measured with a mechanical tester (R = 0.96); interfacial fluorescence correlated linearly with maximum load (R = 0.98).

## Context

Graduate biomaterials; the empirical principle of Artzi's adhesive design ("matching material and tissue properties"). The correlations are over a handful of rodent tissues and formulations and do not establish a quantitative or causal law. Inflammation scores in the 2012 subcutaneous study stayed mild to moderate at 7 and 30 days; subcutaneous responses do not establish safety in the GI tract.

## Expansion

A model would need: a surface-reaction balance between material aldehydes (consumed by network amines and tissue amines) and tissue amine density, a link from interfacial crosslink density to interfacial modulus, a validated relation between the microsphere proxy and true amine density, and a dose–response for aldehyde-related tissue reaction. Each is a hypothesis requiring data.
