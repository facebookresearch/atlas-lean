# Interfacial toughness from a 90-degree peel test

When a sheet with an inextensible backing is peeled from a rigid substrate at 90 degrees and the crack advances steadily along the interface, the apparent interfacial toughness under that protocol equals the peeling force per unit width, $\Gamma = F/w$.

Let a hydrogel sheet of width $w > 0$, bonded to a fixed rigid substrate and backed so that it cannot stretch along the peel direction, be peeled at a constant angle of 90 degrees and a steady rate. If the debonding front advances steadily along the intended interface under a constant force $F$, and no energy is stored or dissipated outside the debonding region (for example in the backing or by fixture friction), then the energy consumed per unit area of debonded interface is

$$\Gamma = \frac{F}{w} .$$

## Context

Undergraduate mechanics. Yuk et al. (2016) use this test (50 mm/min, stiff backing) to measure hydrogel–solid interfacial toughness, which they attribute to intrinsic work of adhesion plus dissipation in the bulk hydrogel; $\Gamma$ is therefore a protocol-specific apparent toughness, not an intrinsic work of adhesion. If the failure path is a cohesive tear within the gel, a constant $F/w$ measures that different process. Without the stiff backing, elastic stretching of the arm stores energy and the relation fails.

## Expansion

Energy balance: when the front advances by $da$, the debonded area grows by $w\,da$; with an inextensible arm at 90 degrees the load point moves by $da$ along the force, doing work $F\,da$, and in steady state the stored energy of the arm is unchanged, so $\Gamma w\,da = F\,da$. The general-angle result $F(1 - \cos\theta)/w$ (Rivlin) is conditional on the same assumptions and needs its own source.
