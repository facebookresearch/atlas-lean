# Tough adhesion to wet tissue (empirical)

On wet porcine skin ex vivo, adhesives combining an amine-rich bridging polymer with a dissipative alginate–polyacrylamide matrix reached adhesion energies above 1000 J/m², versus about 10 J/m² cited for brittle adhesives.

Findings (empirical), each with its own endpoint. Li et al. (2017): a two-layer tough adhesive (a positively charged bridging polymer such as chitosan or polyallylamine, coupled with carbodiimide chemistry, on a hysteretic alginate–polyacrylamide matrix) gave adhesion energies above 1000 J/m² on porcine skin ex vivo for those two bridging polymers; coupling reagents alone gave 14 J/m² and the bridging polymer alone 303 J/m²; on skin first covered with blood the adhesion energy was 1116 J/m². They cite cartilage–bone adhesion energy of about 800 J/m² and brittle adhesives of about 10 J/m² for comparison. Yuk et al. (2016): hydrogel–solid interfacial toughness above 1000 J/m² on non-porous solids (glass, silicon, ceramics, titanium, aluminium) with chemically anchored tough hydrogels. Sun et al. (2012): bulk alginate–polyacrylamide gels with fracture energy about 9000 J/m², stretchable beyond 20 times their length unnotched and to 17 times with a notch. Yuk et al. (2019): a dry double-sided tape adhering to wet tissues and devices (abstract only; no numbers used here).

## Context

Research-level, preclinical evidence. The design principle, a strong interfacial bond plus bulk dissipation, follows the decomposition $\Gamma = \Gamma_0 + \Gamma_D$. The numbers come from different substrates, tests and failure modes (tissue adhesion, interfacial toughness on solids, bulk fracture) and are not one comparison. Artzi's sealants are characterized by burst pressure and adhesive modulus, so values are not directly comparable.

## Expansion

Linking the data to models requires the peel geometry and analysis behind each number (supplementary methods, not read), estimates of $\Gamma_0$ from bridging-chain density and of $\Gamma_D$ from measured hysteresis, and uncertainty across samples. Claims for other tissues or in vivo use need separate evidence.
