# Invasion growth rates and permanence of flow-kick systems

**Setting.** A **flow-kick system** tracks species densities $x\in[0,\infty)^k$ and auxiliary variables $y\in\mathbb R^\ell$, $z=(x,y)$. Between disturbances the state follows the flow $\dot x_i=x_if_i(z)$, $\dot y=g(z)$; at disturbance times it is kicked by a map $z\mapsto H(z)$ that maps each species' density multiplicatively (so absent species stay absent), and the waiting time until the next kick is $\tau(z)>0$. Examples are Lotka-Volterra communities with periodic harvesting or fire, serial-passage competition experiments with periodic dilution, and pulsed consumer-resource models in patches. The *kick-to-kick map* $\kappa$ sends the state just after one kick to the state just after the next. Under dissipativity assumptions (A1-A5), $\kappa$ has a compact global attractor $\Gamma$; $\mathcal S_0$ is the extinction set (some $x_i=0$) and $\mathcal S_+$ its complement. For an ergodic invariant measure $\mu$ of $\kappa$ supported on $\mathcal S_0$, the **invasion growth rate** of species $i$ is $r_i(\mu)=\int r_i(z)\,\mu(dz)$, the average over $\mu$ of species $i$'s per-capita growth over one flow-kick cycle; $r_i(\mu)=0$ for species present in the support of $\mu$. The system is *permanent* if there is a compact set in $\mathcal S_+$ that eventually contains every trajectory starting in $\mathcal S_+$.

**Theorem 4.2 (permanence via Morse decompositions).** Let $\{M_1,\dots,M_\ell\}$ be a Morse decomposition of $\Gamma\cap\mathcal S_0$. If for every Morse set $M_m$, $\max_ir_i(\mu)>0$ for all ergodic $\mu$ supported in $M_m$, then the flow-kick system is permanent, and permanence is robust to small perturbations of $\kappa$ (Theorem 9.1).

**Propositions 4.4 and 4.5 (extinction).** If $\kappa$ is $C^2$ and there is an ergodic $\mu$ on $\mathcal S_0$ with $r_i(\mu)<0$ for all species absent from its support, then some trajectory starting in $\mathcal S_+$ has its $\omega$-limit set in $\mathcal S_0$, so the system is not permanent. If an attractor of $\kappa$ restricted to the face of a subcommunity $I$ satisfies $r_i(\mu)<0$ for all $i\notin I$ and all ergodic $\mu$ on it, it is an attractor of the full system.

**Theorem 4.7 (invasion graphs).** Under additional genericity assumptions (A6-A7), suppose the invasion graph (vertices: subcommunities supporting ergodic measures; edges: $I\to J$ when all species in $J\setminus I$ have positive invasion growth rate at $I$ and all species in $I\setminus J$ are lost) is acyclic. If $\max_{i\notin I}r_i(I)>0$ for every proper subcommunity $I$, the system is permanent; in particular it suffices that each species can invade the community formed by the others (Corollary 4.8).

## Expansion

Discretizing at kicks reduces the impulsive system to a map, to which the Hofbauer-So characterization (permanence if and only if no Morse set of the boundary attracts interior points) and ergodic Lyapunov-exponent arguments apply: by the Birkhoff ergodic theorem, $r_i(\mu)$ is the long-run per-cycle growth rate of a rare species $i$ along $\mu$-typical boundary orbits, so a positive value repels nearby interior orbits and a negative value attracts them. Permanence of the kick-to-kick map transfers to the continuous-time flow-kick dynamics. For serial-passage competition the invasion rates are explicit in terms of dilution factor and growth curves, giving coexistence and exclusion criteria.
