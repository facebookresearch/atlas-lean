# Non-equilibrium positive solutions are periodic with a least period

Let $\alpha, \beta, \gamma, \delta > 0$, and let $x, y : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ have two-sided
derivatives at every $t \ge 0$ with

$$x'(t) = \alpha x(t) - \beta x(t) y(t), \qquad y'(t) = \delta x(t) y(t) - \gamma y(t)$$

there, $x(0) > 0$, $y(0) > 0$ and $(x(0), y(0)) \ne (\gamma/\delta, \alpha/\beta)$, the coexistence
equilibrium. Then there is $T > 0$ such that $x(t + T) = x(t)$ and $y(t + T) = y(t)$ for every
$t \ge 0$, and $(x(s), y(s)) \ne (x(0), y(0))$ for every $s$ with $0 < s < T$. So $T$ is the least
positive time of return to the initial state, and hence the minimal period.
