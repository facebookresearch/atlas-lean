# Clone-consistent growth terms are per capita

**Setting.** Consider population models $\dot x_j=R_j$ or $x_j(t+1)=x_j(t)+R_j$, in which every effect of populations other than $j$ on the change of $x_j$ enters through an impact function $\psi_j(x,a)$, so $R_j=\beta_j(x_j,\psi_j(x,a))$. Require *growth clone consistency*: if populations $j$ and $k$ have identical parameters and abundances $y$ and $z$, then

$$
R_j(x_j=y)+R_k(x_k=z)=R_j(x_j=y+z)+R_k(x_k=0)=R_j(x_j=y+z),
$$

that is, splitting a population into identical halves does not change total growth, and an extinct population does not grow.

**Statement.** Under this requirement (and regularity making additive functions linear), the right-hand side must have the form

$$
R_j=x_j\,\phi_j(x,a),
$$

with $\phi_j$ an impact function. All dependence of $R_j$ on abundances occurs inside impact functions except a single multiplicative factor $x_j$. For example, the generalized Lotka-Volterra model $\dot x_j=g_jx_j\bigl(c_j-\sum_i a_{ji}x_i\bigr)$ is clone-consistent, while $\dot x_j=x_j\bigl(a_j+\sum_i b_{ji}\log x_i\bigr)$ is not.

## Expansion

Equal parameters make $\psi_j$ take the same value $w$ whether the clones are split or lumped, so the requirement reduces to Cauchy's equation $\beta_j(y,w)+\beta_j(z,w)=\beta_j(y+z,w)$ in the first argument. Additivity plus regularity gives homogeneity, $\beta_j(x,w)=x\,\beta_j(1,w)$, hence $R_j=x_j\hat\beta_j(\psi_j)$, and $\hat\beta_j\circ\psi_j$ is again an impact function. Biologically: each individual multiplies at a rate set by the impacts it experiences, including crowding by its own population. In the log-interaction example, splitting a predator population into two identical halves turns predator-prey oscillations into convergence to a fixed point, because $\log y+\log z\ne\log(y+z)$.
