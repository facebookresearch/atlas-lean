# Invasion growth rates and permanence of flow-kick systems

**Setting.** A **flow-kick system** tracks species densities $x\in[0,\infty)^k$ and auxiliary variables $y\in\mathbb R^\ell$, $z=(x,y)$. Between disturbances the state follows the flow $\dot x_i=x_if_i(z)$, $\dot y=g(z)$; at disturbance times it is kicked by a map $z\mapsto H(z)$ that maps each species' density multiplicatively (so absent species stay absent), and the waiting time until the next kick is $\tau(z)>0$. Examples are Lotka-Volterra communities with periodic harvesting or fire, serial-passage competition experiments with periodic dilution, and pulsed consumer-resource models in patches. The *kick-to-kick map* $\kappa$ sends the state just after one kick to the state just after the next. The standing assumptions are: (A1) the $f_i$ and $g$ are locally Lipschitz on a set $\mathcal K$ open in $[0,\infty)^k\times\mathbb R^\ell$; (A2) there are a closed set $\mathcal S\subset\mathcal K$ of post-kick states and a continuous $\tau:\mathcal S\to(0,\infty)$ such that the solution $z.t$ from each $z\in\mathcal S$ exists and stays in $\mathcal K$ for $0\le t\le\tau(z)$; (A3) the kick is $H(z)=(x_1F_1(z),\dots,x_kF_k(z),G(z))$ on the pre-kick states $\{z.\tau(z):z\in\mathcal S\}$, with each $F_i$ continuous and positive; (A4) $G$ is continuous; (A5) the kick-to-kick map $\kappa(z)=H(z.\tau(z))$ is dissipative: there is $K>0$ with $\limsup_{n\to\infty}\|\kappa^n(z)\|_1\le K$ for all $z\in\mathcal S$. Under (A1)-(A5), $\kappa$ has a compact global attractor $\Gamma$; $\mathcal S_0$ is the extinction set (some $x_i=0$) and $\mathcal S_+$ its complement. For an invariant probability measure $\mu$ of $\kappa$ on the global attractor (ergodic or not), the **invasion growth rate** of species $i$ is $r_i(\mu)=\int r_i(z)\,\mu(dz)$, where $r_i(z)=\int_0^{\tau(z)}f_i(z.t)\,dt+\ln F_i(z.\tau(z))$ is species $i$'s per-capita growth over one flow-kick cycle; $r_i(\mu)=0$ for species present in the support of an ergodic $\mu$. The system is *permanent* if there is a compact set in $\mathcal S_+$ that eventually contains every trajectory starting in $\mathcal S_+$.

**Theorem 4.2 (permanence via Morse decompositions; Theorem 4.3, conditions (22)–(23), in the arXiv version).** Assume (A1)-(A5) and let $\{M_1,\dots,M_\ell\}$ be a Morse decomposition of $\Gamma\cap\mathcal S_0$. If for every Morse set $M_m$

$$\max_ir_i(\mu)>0\quad\text{for all invariant probability measures }\mu\text{ with }\mu(M_m)=1,$$

equivalently (by the minimax theorem) there are weights $p_1,\dots,p_k>0$, possibly depending on $m$, with $\sum_ip_ir_i(\mu)>0$ for all ergodic $\mu$ with $\mu(M_m)=1$, then the flow-kick system is permanent.

**Propositions 4.4 and 4.5 (extinction).** Assume (A1)-(A5). If $\kappa$ is $C^2$ and there is an ergodic $\mu$ on $\mathcal S_0$ with $r_i(\mu)<0$ for all species absent from its support, then some trajectory starting in $\mathcal S_+$ has its $\omega$-limit set in $\mathcal S_0$, so the system is not permanent. If an attractor of $\kappa$ restricted to the face of a subcommunity $I$ satisfies $r_i(\mu)<0$ for all $i\notin I$ and all ergodic $\mu$ on it, it is an attractor of the full system.

**Theorem 4.7 (invasion graphs).** Assume (A1)-(A5) and two genericity assumptions: (A6) for every ergodic $\mu$ supported on $\mathcal S_0$, $r_j(\mu)\ne0$ for every species $j$ absent from the support $I(\mu)$ of $\mu$; (A7) $\operatorname{sgn}r_j(\mu)=\operatorname{sgn}r_j(\nu)$ for all $j$ and all ergodic $\mu,\nu$ with $I(\mu)=I(\nu)$. Write $r_j(I)=\operatorname{sgn}r_j(\mu)$ for any ergodic $\mu$ with $I(\mu)=I$. Suppose the invasion graph (vertices: the communities $I(\mu)$ of ergodic measures; an edge $I\to J$, $I\ne J$, when $r_j(I)>0$ for all $j\in J\setminus I$ and $r_i(J)<0$ for all $i\in I\setminus J$) is acyclic. If $\max_{i\notin I}r_i(I)>0$ for every proper subcommunity $I$, the system is permanent; in particular it suffices that each species can invade the community formed by the others (Corollary 4.8).

The condition must hold for all invariant measures on each Morse set, not only the ergodic ones: in the May–Leonard system ($0<\alpha<1<\beta$, $\alpha+\beta>2$, as the flow of a flow-kick system with trivial kicks) every ergodic measure on the boundary is a Dirac mass at an equilibrium that some missing species invades, yet the boundary heteroclinic cycle attracts interior orbits and the system is not permanent.

## Expansion

Discretizing at kicks reduces the impulsive system to a map, to which the Hofbauer-So characterization (permanence if and only if no Morse set of the boundary attracts interior points) and ergodic Lyapunov-exponent arguments apply: by the Birkhoff ergodic theorem, $r_i(\mu)$ is the long-run per-cycle growth rate of a rare species $i$ along $\mu$-typical boundary orbits, so a positive value repels nearby interior orbits and a negative value attracts them. Permanence of the kick-to-kick map transfers to the continuous-time flow-kick dynamics. For serial-passage competition the invasion rates are explicit in terms of dilution factor and growth curves, giving coexistence and exclusion criteria.
