# Resilience estimation from quasi-stationary fluctuations

Engineering resilience of an ecosystem is minus the largest real part of the eigenvalues of the Jacobian at its stable equilibrium. Populations are counted, not described by differential equations, so it must be inferred from noisy abundance data. For multi-species birth-and-death processes with a large scaling parameter $K$, the long-lived quasi-stationary distribution carries the Jacobian in its covariance structure: time-lagged covariances propagate by the matrix exponential of the Jacobian, and equal-time covariances satisfy a fluctuation-dissipation (Lyapunov) relation. Time averages of a single trajectory, long but much shorter than the exponentially long extinction time, therefore estimate resilience consistently.
