# Retention fraction from a random-walk generating function

Keep the weighted graph $G$ with $\sum_j e_{ij} = 1$, the diffusion parameter $\lambda > 0$ and the [stationary public-goods distribution](public-goods-diffusion-equilibrium.md). Let $p^{(n)}_{ij}$ be the $n$-step transition probability of the random walk with weights $e_{ij}$ and

$$G_{ij}(z) = \sum_{n = 0}^{\infty} p^{(n)}_{ij} z^n$$

its generating function, convergent for $|z| < 1$.

**Retention.** The fraction of a cooperator's public good retained by the producer is

$$\phi_0 = \frac{1}{1 + \lambda}\, G_{ii}\!\left( \frac{\lambda}{1 + \lambda} \right),$$

and $\phi_1$ follows from the identity $(1 + \lambda)\phi_0 = 1 + \lambda \phi_1$.

**Simpson degree.** The local structure enters through

$$\kappa = \Big( \sum_{j \in G} e_{ij}^{2} \Big)^{-1},$$

the Simpson diversity of a cell's neighbours, which equals the ordinary degree on a regular unweighted graph and does not depend on $i$ by symmetry. Expanding the return generating function for small $\lambda$ — the walk returns at step $0$ with probability $1$ and at step $2$ with probability $1/\kappa$ — gives

$$\phi_0 = 1 - \lambda + \lambda^2\Big(1 + \tfrac{1}{\kappa}\Big) + O(\lambda^3), \qquad \phi_0 + \phi_1 = 1 - \frac{\lambda(\kappa - 1)}{\kappa} + O(\lambda^2).$$

On a lattice generated by vectors $v_1, \dots, v_k \in \mathbb{R}^n$ with weights $w_1, \dots, w_k$, the generating function has the Fourier representation

$$G_{0x}(z) = \frac{1}{(2\pi)^n} \int_{-\pi}^{\pi} \!\!\cdots \int_{-\pi}^{\pi} \frac{e^{-i\, x \cdot y}}{1 - z\, \chi(y)} \, \mathrm{d}^n y, \qquad \chi(y) = \sum_{m} w_m e^{i\, v_m \cdot y},$$

which yields $\phi_0$ in closed form for the one- and two-dimensional lattices and for the complete graph.

## Expansion

The formula for $\phi_0$ is the Neumann series of the stationary equation read at the producer: the good returns to its source along closed walks, a walk of length $n$ contributing $p^{(n)}_{ii}$ weighted by $\lambda^n (1+\lambda)^{-n-1}$, and summing the geometric weight is exactly evaluating the return generating function at $z = \lambda/(1+\lambda)$. Everything about the spatial arrangement that matters for retention is thus compressed into one scalar function of one variable, the return probability generating function, and $\lambda$ enters only through $z$. The Simpson degree appears at leading order because the first nontrivial return is the two-step one, whose probability is $\sum_j e_{ij} e_{ji} = \sum_j e_{ij}^2$ for symmetric weights; a cell with many weakly weighted neighbours (large $\kappa$) loses more of its product per unit of diffusion than one with few strong neighbours. For a monosaccharide hydrolysis system in budding yeast the authors estimate $\lambda \sim 3$, which is in the regime where neither limit applies and the graph structure matters.
