# Invariant compact set and attractor of the Yoccoz–Birkeland integral equation

**Setting.** The **Yoccoz–Birkeland model** describes the number $N(t)$ of sexually mature females (time in years) of a species with maturation age $A_0$, maximal age $A_1$, density-dependent fertility $m(N)$ (female offspring per female per year in good conditions), seasonal reproduction fraction $\rho(t) \in [0,1]$ (typically 1-periodic) and the linear survival function $\varsigma(a) = 1 - a/A_1$ on $[0,A_1]$ (the source's choice (2.2), on which $N_{max}$ and $L$ below depend):

$$N(t) = \int_{A_0}^{A_1} N(t-a)\,m\big(N(t-a)\big)\,\rho(t-a)\,\varsigma(a)\,da.$$

Taking the season phase $t_0 \pmod 1$ together with a history $N$ on $[-A_1, 0]$ satisfying the endpoint compatibility condition $N(0) = \int_{A_0}^{A_1} N(-a)\,m(N(-a))\,\rho(t_0 - a)\,\varsigma(a)\,da$ (the phase-labelled history space $Y^\sharp$ of Section 2), this determines $N$ for all later times and defines a semiflow $T^s$ on $Y^\sharp$.

**Theorem (Theorem 2.1, from Arlot).** Assume $N \mapsto Nm(N)$ is uniformly continuous on $[0,\infty)$ and
1. $m_0/2 \le m(N) \le m_0$ for $N \le 1$ and $\min\{1/2, N^{-\gamma}\}m_0 \le m(N) \le m_0N^{-\gamma}$ for $N \ge 1$, with $\gamma \ge 1$;
2. $0 \le \rho \le 1$ and $\rho = 1$ on an interval of length $1 - \rho_w$ each year, with $0 < \rho_w < 1$ (a positive winter length);
3. $A_1 \ge \max\{2A_0, A_0 + 1\}$ and $c_0m_0 > 2$, where $c_0 = \int_{A_0+\rho_w}^{A_0+1}\varsigma(a)\,da$.

Let $N_{max} = m_0\frac{A_1}{2}\big(1 - \frac{A_0}{A_1}\big)$, $L = m_0\big(3 - \frac{A_0}{A_1}\big)$ and $\mathcal K \subset Y^\sharp$ the set of phase-labelled, endpoint-compatible histories $(t_0, N) \in Y^\sharp$ with $\frac{c_0m_0}{2}N_{max}^{1-\gamma} \le N \le N_{max}$ on $[-A_1, 0]$ that are $L$-Lipschitz. Then $\mathcal K$ is compact and forward invariant ($T^s(\mathcal K)\subseteq\mathcal K$ for all $s\ge0$), and $\Lambda = \bigcap_{n\ge0}T^n(\mathcal K)$ is compact and invariant ($T^s(\Lambda)=\Lambda$ for all $s\ge0$). The source's Theorem 2.1 also prints, for every history, that the solution enters $\mathcal K$ in finite time and that $\Lambda$ is a global attractor; this page omits those clauses because they fail as printed: the zero history stays zero, and with the source's seasonal choice (2.2), where $\rho=0$ on $[0,\rho_w)$ modulo $1$, so does a continuous non-zero history that vanishes outside the set where $\rho=0$ (in particular $N(0)=0$), because every birth term $N(t-a)\,m(N(t-a))\,\rho(t-a)$ is zero; neither enters $\mathcal K$, whose histories are bounded below by $\frac{c_0m_0}{2}N_{max}^{1-\gamma}>0$.

**Coupling with prices (Theorems 4.10–4.11).** In the coupled livestock–market model (3.8a)–(3.8c), assuming no mortality before the end of reproduction (no survival factor $\varsigma(a)$ in the integral), a price-dependent fraction $R(P) \in [R_0, R_1] \subseteq [0,1]$ of newborn females is kept for reproduction while all males and the remaining females (weight $2 - R$) are sent to slaughter over ages $[\Omega_0, \Omega_1]$: $N_r(t) = \int_{A_0}^{A_1} N_r(t-a)\,m(N_r(t-a))\,m_\rho(t-a)\,R(P(t-a))\,da$ (Eq. (3.8a)), slaughter supply $S(t)$ averages $N_r(t-a)\,m(N_r(t-a))\,m_\rho(t-a)\,(2 - R(P(t-a)))$ over $[\Omega_0, \Omega_1]$ (Eq. (3.8c)), and the commodity price obeys $P'/P = \lambda\,\frac{D(P) - S}{D(P) + S}$ (Eq. (3.8b)). Under the standing assumptions at the start of Section 4—in particular, strictly decreasing, locally Lipschitz demand $D$ with $\lim_{P\to\infty} D(P) = 0$, seasonal integral bounds $0 < c_0 \le \int_{A_0}^{A_1} m_\rho(t-a)\,da \le c_1$ (Eq. (4.1)), and the induced supply bounds $S_{min}, S_{max}$ (Eqs. (4.2)–(4.3))—if $m_0 R_0 c_0 > 2$, $D(0) > S_{max}$, and $S_{min} > 0$ (Eqs. (4.7)–(4.9)), then the time-one map $\Pi$ of the coupled system has an attractor $\Lambda_P$, a non-empty compact set with $\Pi(\Lambda_P)=\Lambda_P$, whose basin contains every initial datum $(N_0,P_0)$ with $N_0>0$ almost everywhere on $[-A_1,0]$ and $P_0(0)>0$ (semi-trivial solutions such as $P\equiv0$ when $P_0(0)=0$ show that the basin is not everything), and the coupled system admits a non-trivial 1-periodic solution, while simulations show chaotic price cycles.

## Expansion

The model was proposed for the Svalbard vole *Microtus epiroticus*, whose high average fertility coexists with large, near-extinction fluctuations without significant predation: a shortage of good breeding sites makes fertility decrease with density ($m(N) \sim N^{-\gamma}$ for large $N$), and a short summer makes early-summer births mature within the same season. The upper bound $N_{max}$ comes from $Nm(N) \le m_0$ for $\gamma \ge 1$, which caps births, and the lower bound from a full good season ($\rho = 1$ on an interval) recruiting at least $c_0m_0/2$ times $\min\{\inf N, N_{max}^{1-\gamma}\}$; condition $c_0m_0 > 2$ keeps a history in $\mathcal K$ above that lower bound one generation later. The integral form makes histories Lipschitz after one generation, which gives the compactness needed for an attractor; the dynamics on $\Lambda$ can be irregular, which is how the model reproduces erratic abundance cycles.
