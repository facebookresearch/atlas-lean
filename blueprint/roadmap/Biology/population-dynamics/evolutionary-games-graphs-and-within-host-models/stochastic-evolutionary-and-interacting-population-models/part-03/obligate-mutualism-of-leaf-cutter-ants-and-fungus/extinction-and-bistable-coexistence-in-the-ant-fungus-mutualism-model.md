# Extinction and bistable coexistence in the ant–fungus mutualism model

Let $A(t)$ be the biomass of worker ants and $F(t)$ that of the fungus garden in a young leaf-cutter ant colony. With all parameters positive,

$$

\frac{dA}{dt} = (r_a F - d_a A)\,A, \qquad
\frac{dF}{dt} = \Big(\frac{r_f\,a A^2}{b + aA^2} - d_f F - r_c A\Big)F,

$$

where $r_a$ is the ants' conversion of fungus, $d_a$ and $d_f$ are self-limitation rates, $r_c = c\,r_a$ is fungus consumption, $r_f$ the maximal fungal growth driven by ant labour, $b$ a half-saturation constant, and $a = p^2 q(1-q) \in [0,\tfrac14]$ measures the division of labour ($p$ the fraction of biomass in adult workers, $q$ the fraction of them tending the fungus). Set

$$

a_c = 4b\Big(\frac{r_c r_a + d_f d_a}{r_a r_f}\Big)^2, \qquad \bar A = \frac{r_f r_a}{2(r_c r_a + d_f d_a)} .

$$

**Theorem.** The positive quadrant is invariant, solutions are bounded, there are no periodic orbits, and every trajectory converges to an equilibrium. Moreover:

1. (Extinction.) If $a < a_c$, the only equilibrium is $(0,0)$, and it is globally asymptotically stable.
2. (Bistable coexistence.) If $a > a_c$, there are two interior equilibria $(A^{i1}, \tfrac{d_a}{r_a}A^{i1})$ and $(A^{i2}, \tfrac{d_a}{r_a}A^{i2})$ with

$$

A^{i1,i2} = \bar A \mp \sqrt{\bar A^2 - b/a};

$$

$(A^{i1},F^{i1})$ is a saddle (unstable) and $(A^{i2},F^{i2})$ is locally asymptotically stable.
3. $(0,0)$ is locally asymptotically stable whenever $a \ne a_c$. If $a > a_c$, the system has exactly the two attractors $(0,0)$ and $(A^{i2},F^{i2})$; initial conditions in

$$

\Big\{(A,F) \in (0,\infty)^2 : \frac{r_f\,aA^2}{d_f(b + aA^2)} - \frac{r_c A}{d_f} \le F < F^{i1}\Big\},

$$

off the stable manifold of the saddle, converge to $(0,0)$, while sufficiently large initial conditions converge to $(A^{i2},F^{i2})$. At $a = a_c$ the two interior equilibria merge at $(\bar A, \tfrac{d_a}{r_a}\bar A)$ in a saddle-node.

So a colony survives only if labour is divided efficiently enough ($a > a_c$) and the founding ant and fungus biomasses are large enough.

## Expansion

Interior equilibria lie on the ant nullcline $F = \tfrac{d_a}{r_a}A$; substituting into the fungus nullcline gives a quadratic in $A$, whose discriminant is positive iff $a > a_c$. The Dulac function $1/(AF)$ rules out closed orbits, so by Poincaré–Bendixson all bounded trajectories converge to equilibria. At the origin the fungal growth term vanishes to second order in $A$, so both per-capita rates are negative near $(0,0)$, making extinction locally stable: an Allee effect generated by the mutualism. Linearization at the interior equilibria gives one saddle and one sink; the region described in item 3 is bounded by the fungus nullcline and the line $F = F^{i1}$, where the fungus declines.
