# Coexistence versus bistability in the oxygenation model

In the competition model of anoxygenic and oxygenic photosynthesis with negligible migration, an
interior equilibrium $\hat E$ with both bacterial types present exists exactly when the two
invasion conditions for $E_1$ and $E_2$ are either both satisfied or both violated. If both are
satisfied, $\hat E$ is unstable and the system is bistable; if both are violated, $\hat E$ is a
stable mixed equilibrium, and the two types coexist. The mixed equilibrium is the stable case
precisely when

$$b \ >\ c\,(a - 1).$$

In particular, if $a < 1$ the inequality holds for every $b > 0$ and $c > 0$, so bistability is
impossible.

## Remarks

The threshold compares how fast oxygen is removed geochemically, at rate $b$ per unit oxygen, with
how fast cyanobacteria can build it up, measured by $c(a-1)$. When removal dominates, oxygen never
accumulates enough to make the cyanobacterial equilibrium stable before the anoxygenic equilibrium
loses stability, and the two regimes overlap only through coexistence: the transition driven by a
slowly falling reductant influx is then gradual and reversible. When removal is weak, the two
stable regimes overlap and the transition is abrupt and hysteretic.
