# Exponential exit times from evolutionary stable conditions across fitness valleys

**Model.** Traits are the vertices of a finite (possibly directed) graph $G = (V,E)$. In a population with carrying-capacity parameter $K$, an individual of trait $v$ gives birth at rate $b(v)$, its offspring mutating with probability $\mu_K = K^{-1/\alpha}$ ($\alpha > 0$, $\alpha \notin \mathbb{N}$) to a neighbor $w$ with probability $m(v,w)$ (positive exactly on edges), and dies at rate $d(v) + \sum_w c(v,w)N_w/K$, with $c(v,v) > 0$. For a set $\mathbf{v}$ of coexisting traits with Lotka–Volterra equilibrium $\bar n(\mathbf{v})$, the invasion fitness of $w$ is $f(w,\mathbf{v}) = b(w) - d(w) - \sum_{u\in\mathbf{v}}c(w,u)\bar n_u(\mathbf{v})$, assumed nonzero. Write population sizes as $N^K_w \approx K^{\beta^K_w}$.

**Definitions.** The *mutation spreading neighborhood* is $V_\alpha(\mathbf{v}) = \{w : d(\mathbf{v},w) < \alpha\}$. The *stability degree* $L(\mathbf{v})$ is the graph distance from $\mathbf{v}$ to the nearest trait of positive invasion fitness (the width of the fitness valley). $\mathbf{v}$ with exponents $\beta_w = (1 - d(\mathbf{v},w)/\alpha)_+$ is an *evolutionary stable condition* (ESC) if $f(w,\mathbf{v}) < 0$ for all $w \in V_\alpha(\mathbf{v}) \setminus \mathbf{v}$, equivalently $L(\mathbf{v}) > \alpha$. Let $V_{\mathrm{mut}}(\mathbf{v})$ be the fit traits at distance $L(\mathbf{v})$. For a shortest path $\gamma : \mathbf{v} \to w \in V_{\mathrm{mut}}(\mathbf{v})$, with $\rho(u,\mathbf{v}) = b(u)/(b(u) + d(u) + \sum_{v\in\mathbf{v}}c(u,v)\bar n_v(\mathbf{v}))$ and $\lambda(\rho) = \sum_{\ell\ge1}\frac{(2\ell)!}{(\ell-1)!(\ell+1)!}\rho^\ell(1-\rho)^{\ell+1}$,

$$R(\mathbf{v},\gamma) = \bar n_{\gamma_0}(\mathbf{v})\prod_{i=1}^{\lfloor\alpha\rfloor}\frac{b(\gamma_{i-1})m(\gamma_{i-1},\gamma_i)}{|f(\gamma_i,\mathbf{v})|}\; b(\gamma_{\lfloor\alpha\rfloor})m(\gamma_{\lfloor\alpha\rfloor},\gamma_{\lfloor\alpha\rfloor+1})\prod_{j=\lfloor\alpha\rfloor+1}^{L(\mathbf{v})-1}\lambda(\rho(\gamma_j,\mathbf{v}))\,m(\gamma_j,\gamma_{j+1})\;\frac{f(\gamma_{L(\mathbf{v})},\mathbf{v})}{b(\gamma_{L(\mathbf{v})})},$$

$R(\mathbf{v},w) = \sum_\gamma R(\mathbf{v},\gamma)$ over shortest paths ending at $w$, and $R(\mathbf{v}) = \sum_{w\in V_{\mathrm{mut}}(\mathbf{v})}R(\mathbf{v},w)$.

**Theorem (exit from an ESC).** Start from an asymptotic ESC $\mathbf{v}$ and let $T^K_{\mathrm{fix}}$ be the first time some trait outside $V_\alpha(\mathbf{v})$ reaches size $K^{1/\alpha}$. There are $\varepsilon_0, c > 0$ such that for $0 < \varepsilon < \varepsilon_0$ there are exponential variables $E_\pm(\varepsilon)$ with rates $R(\mathbf{v})(1 \pm c\varepsilon)$ satisfying

$$\liminf_{K\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\big(E_-(\varepsilon) \le T^K_{\mathrm{fix}}\,K\mu_K^{L(\mathbf{v})} \le E_+(\varepsilon)\big) \ge 1 - c\varepsilon,$$

and the trait triggering $T^K_{\mathrm{fix}}$ is $w$ with limiting probability $R(\mathbf{v},w)/R(\mathbf{v})$ for $w \in V_{\mathrm{mut}}(\mathbf{v})$ (zero otherwise). If the subsequent deterministic $\ln K$-time-scale dynamics reach a new ESC $\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{ESC}}(\mathbf{v},w)$, they do so in time $O(\ln K)$, so the time to the next ESC obeys the same exponential law, and the construction iterates.

**Theorem (time-scale $L$ limit).** Let $\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{ESC}}$ be the metastability graph whose vertices are ESCs and whose edges are the possible transitions $\mathbf{v} \to \mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{ESC}}(\mathbf{v},w)$. For $L > \alpha$, let $S^{(L')}$ be the set of ESCs of stability degree $L'$ and $V^L$ those of degree $\ge L$, and assume: for every ESC $\mathbf v\in S^{(L)}$ (stability degree exactly $L$), the jump chain $(\mathbf v^{(k)})_{k\ge0}$ of successive ESCs started at $\mathbf v^{(0)}=\mathbf v$ almost surely has some later ESC $\mathbf v^{(k)}$, $k\ge1$, of stability degree $\ge L$ (Assumption 3 of the source; cycles of lower time scale elsewhere in $\mathcal G_{\mathrm{ESC}}$ are allowed); the initial state is an asymptotic ESC $\mathbf v^L(0)\in V^L$ satisfying the hypotheses of the exit theorem; and for every $\mathbf v\in\bigcup_{L'\le L}S^{(L')}$ and $w\in V_{\mathrm{mut}}(\mathbf v)$ the $\ln K$-time-scale dynamics reach a new ESC without early termination. Then $(N^K_v(t/(K\mu_K^L))/K)_{v\in V}$ converges in finite-dimensional distributions to $\mathbf{1}\{v \in \mathbf{v}^L(t)\}\,\bar n_v(\mathbf{v}^L(t))$, where $\mathbf{v}^L$ is a Markov jump process on $V^L$ with rates $R_L(\mathbf v,\mathbf w)=R(\mathbf v)\,p_L(\mathbf v,\mathbf w)$; here $p_L(\mathbf v,\mathbf w)$ is the sum, over paths $\Gamma:\mathbf v\to\mathbf w$ in $\mathcal G_{\mathrm{ESC}}$ whose intermediate ESCs have stability degree $<L$, of the products along $\Gamma$ of the one-step transition probabilities $p(\Gamma_{i-1},\Gamma_i)$ of $\mathcal G_{\mathrm{ESC}}$ (the limiting probability, from the exit theorem, that the exit from $\Gamma_{i-1}$ leads to $\Gamma_i$).

## Expansion

At an ESC, resident traits sit at their Lotka–Volterra equilibrium and traits within distance $<\alpha$ are maintained at sizes $K^{1-d/\alpha}$ by mutation flow, but cannot invade. Crossing a valley of width $L(\mathbf{v}) > \alpha$ requires a lineage to produce successive mutants through unfit intermediate traits during short subcritical excursions; $\lambda(\rho)$ is the expected number of births in such an excursion, and the last factor is the survival probability $f/b$ of the fit mutant. Each crossing attempt is rare and nearly independent, so the exit time is exponential with rate $R(\mathbf{v})K\mu_K^{L(\mathbf{v})}$. Valleys of different widths give separated time scales $K\mu_K^{L}$, which is what makes the coarse-grained process on the metastability graph Markov at each scale.
