# Competitive exclusion at equilibrium and coexistence on cycles

Armstrong and McGehee (1980) review the competitive exclusion principle, the statement, going back to Volterra (1928), that $n$ species cannot coexist on fewer than $n$ resources or limiting factors. (i) Coexistence at fixed densities: if $n$ species with densities $x_i$ interact only through $k < n$ limiting factors $R \in \mathbb{R}^k$, with per capita growth rates affine in $R$, $\dot x_i = x_i (b_i \cdot R - m_i)$, an equilibrium with all $x_i > 0$ requires the $n$ linear equations $b_i \cdot R = m_i$ in $k$ unknowns, which for generic $(b_i, m_i)$ have no solution; Armstrong and McGehee conclude (their Section 5) that in this special case $n$ species cannot coexist on fewer than $n$ resources. (ii) Off equilibrium the principle fails: their Section 2, following Koch and their earlier work, shows that two consumer species can coexist on one biotic resource in a time-invariant, spatially homogeneous environment, the populations oscillating rather than resting at fixed densities.

## Expansion

1. The model: consumers coupled through limiting factors, linear and nonlinear (saturating) dependence of growth on resources, and the meaning of "generic" (the complement of a closed set of measure zero in parameter space).
2. The linear-algebra statement (i), including the role of the affine dependence on $R$.
3. The coexistence example of item (ii): the equations, the periodic orbit, and the sense in which both consumers persist.
4. Exclusion for time averages along periodic orbits when growth is linear in the resources, as a separate statement.
