# Sharpe–Lotka–Feller renewal theorem for births

In Lotka's renewal model the births $B(t)$ at time $t$ are the births $G(t)$ to women alive at time $0$ plus the births to women born since, $B(t) = G(t) + \int_0^t B(t - a)\, \varphi(a)\, da$, where $\varphi(a) = l(a) m(a)$ is the net maternity function, $l$ the survivorship and $m$ the age-specific fertility (the model of Sharpe and Lotka 1911, treated rigorously by Feller 1941, Section 2). Let $\varphi \ge 0$ be measurable with $\int_0^\infty \varphi > 0$, and assume that the characteristic equation $\int_0^\infty e^{-ra}\varphi(a)\, da = 1$ has a real root $r$; it is unique, because the left side is strictly decreasing in $r$ where it is finite. When the total fertility satisfies $1 \le \int_0^\infty \varphi < \infty$ the root exists, with $r \ge 0$ (Hyrien and Yanev 2012, Section 4; Feller 1941, Theorem 3 (iii), for the positive root when $\int_0^\infty \varphi > 1$); otherwise it need not exist, and its existence is a hypothesis. Assume also $\mu_r = \int_0^\infty a\, e^{-ra}\varphi(a)\, da < \infty$. Let $G \ge 0$ be such that $t \mapsto e^{-rt}G(t)$ is integrable on $[0, \infty)$ and non-increasing, and let $B$ be the solution of the renewal equation that is bounded on compact sets. Then

$$e^{-rt} B(t) \to Q = \frac{1}{\mu_r}\int_0^\infty e^{-rt} G(t)\, dt \qquad (t \to \infty),$$

so births ultimately grow like $Q e^{rt}$. A nonzero exponential $c\,e^{rt}$ does not solve the homogeneous half-line equation ($G = 0$): at $t = 0$ that equation forces $B(0) = 0$. The exponentials solve instead the full-history equation $B(t) = \int_0^\infty B(t - a)\,\varphi(a)\,da$ for every real $t$, and these solutions are characterized by the [renewal equation for exponential growth](../../../epidemiology/euler-lotka-equation-and-the-reproduction-number/renewal-exp-iff.md). On the half-line, $c\,e^{rt}$ solves the renewal equation above with the inherited forcing $G(t) = c\,e^{rt}\int_t^\infty e^{-ra}\varphi(a)\,da$, the births to women born before time $0$ in a population that has always grown at rate $r$; this $G$ satisfies the hypotheses of the theorem and gives $Q = c$. The theorem shows that every solution of the half-line equation with $G$ as above is asymptotic to $Q e^{rt}$.

The derivation is explicit. Put $B_r(t) = e^{-rt}B(t)$, $G_r(t) = e^{-rt}G(t)$ and $f(a) = e^{-ra}\varphi(a)$. Multiplying the renewal equation by $e^{-rt}$ gives $B_r = G_r + B_r * f$ (Feller 1941, Section 4, Lemma), and $f$ is a probability density with mean $\mu_r$. Hence $B_r(t) = G_r(t) + \int_0^t G_r(t - x)\, u(x)\, dx$ with $u = \sum_{n \ge 1} f^{*n}$ the renewal density of the renewal process with inter-renewal density $f$, and the key renewal theorem for directly Riemann integrable functions (Hyrien and Yanev 2012, Section 4, Theorem A(i)), applied to the distribution with density $f$ and to $G_r$, gives $\int_0^t G_r(t - x) u(x)\, dx \to \mu_r^{-1}\int_0^\infty G_r$, while $G_r(t) \to 0$ because $G_r$ is non-increasing and integrable. This uses the direct Riemann integrability of $G_r$, a separate step (Expansion item 3). For $\int_0^\infty \varphi > 1$, Feller (1941, Theorem 4 and the Remark after it) obtains the same limit $B(t) \sim Q e^{rt}$ under moment and bounded-variation hypotheses on the tilted functions in place of the monotonicity of $G_r$.

## Expansion

1. The model: the renewal equation for births with the initial-population term $G$, and existence and uniqueness of its solution bounded on compact sets (Feller 1941, Theorem 2, for $\varphi$ and $G$ bounded on finite intervals with convergent Laplace integrals).
2. The reduction by the factor $e^{-rt}$ to a renewal equation with a probability density $f$, and the representation of its solution through the renewal density $u$.
3. The key renewal theorem for directly Riemann integrable functions in the form used here (Hyrien and Yanev 2012, Theorem A(i)), and the direct Riemann integrability of the non-increasing integrable $G_r$.
4. The limit $Q$, and the case $\mu_r = \infty$, where $e^{-rt} B(t) \to 0$, as a separate statement.
