# Nonlocal selection–mutation equations

A phenotypically structured population with density $n(t,x)$ over a trait $x \in \mathbb R^d$ grows at a trait-dependent net rate, competes through its total size $\rho(t) = \int n\,dx$, and mutates by diffusion in trait space. These articles treat the Lotka–Volterra-type equation $\partial_t n - \sigma\Delta n = n\,[a(t,x) - \rho(t)]$ with a time-periodic growth rate: the periodic logistic equation, whose positive periodic solution is the limiting population size when the population persists, the long-time selection of the trait with the largest time-averaged growth rate, and the Hamilton–Jacobi description of the periodic solution as mutations become rare.
