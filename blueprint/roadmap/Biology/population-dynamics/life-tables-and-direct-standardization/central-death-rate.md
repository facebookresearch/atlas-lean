# Central death rate as deaths over positive exposure

For real numbers $D$ and $E$ with $E > 0$, the central death rate is $m = D / E$; it is defined only
when $E > 0$. With $D$ the number of deaths in an age interval and $E$ the exposure to risk, the
person-years lived in that interval, $m$ is the number of deaths per person-year. It is nonnegative
when $D \ge 0$. Unlike a [death probability](death-probability.md) with $0 \le d_x \le l_x$, which
lies in $[0, 1]$, it is not bounded by $1$ even for nonnegative deaths: $m > 1$ whenever $D > E$.

Source: Methods Protocol for the Human Mortality Database, version 6, eq. (49).
