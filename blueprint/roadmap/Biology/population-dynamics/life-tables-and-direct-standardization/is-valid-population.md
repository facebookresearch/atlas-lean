# Valid population: nonnegative category sizes with a positive total

For a finite set $\iota$ of categories, real sizes $N_i$ form a valid population when $N_i \ge 0$
for every category $i$ and $\sum_{i \in \iota} N_i > 0$. This excludes any negative size, the
all-zero population and the empty set of categories, whose total is $0$; a valid population has at
least one category of positive size. It is the hypothesis of the results that the
[population shares](population-share.md) lie in $[0, 1]$ ([bounds](population-share-mem-icc.md)) and
that the crude rate and the directly standardized rate, both
[weighted means](../../statistical-genetics/mendelian-randomization/weighted-mean.md) of the
category rates, lie between the smallest and the largest category rate
([bounds](crude-rate-mem-icc.md)).
