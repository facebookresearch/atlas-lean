# Directly standardized rate of the category rates under a standard

For a finite set $\iota$ of categories, real sizes $S_i$ of a standard population and real category
rates $T_i$, the directly standardized rate of $T$ under the standard $S$ is the
[crude rate](crude-rate.md) of a population with sizes $S_i$ and rates $T_i$,
$(\sum_i S_i T_i) / \sum_i S_i$: the rates weighted by the composition of the standard rather than
by that of the population in which they were measured. With the convention $x / 0 = 0$ it is $0$
when the total $\sum_i S_i$ of the standard is $0$. The standard need not be normalised: its sizes
are divided by their total, and when they already sum to $1$ the rate is $\sum_i S_i T_i$.

For a [valid](is-valid-population.md) standard, with nonnegative sizes and a positive total, the
directly standardized rate is a weighted average of the rates and lies between the smallest and the
largest rate ([bounds](directly-standardized-rate-mem-icc.md)); for signed standard sizes it can lie
outside the range of the rates.

Source: P. Das Gupta, *Standardization and Decomposition of Rates: A User's Manual*, U.S. Bureau of
the Census (1993), chapter 1.
