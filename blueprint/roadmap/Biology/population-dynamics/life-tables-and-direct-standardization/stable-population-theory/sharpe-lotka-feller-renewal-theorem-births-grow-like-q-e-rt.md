# Sharpe–Lotka–Feller renewal theorem for births

In Lotka's renewal model the births $B(t)$ at time $t$ are the births $G(t)$ to women alive at time $0$ plus the births to women born since, $B(t) = G(t) + \int_0^t B(t - a)\, \varphi(a)\, da$, where $\varphi(a) = l(a) m(a)$ is the net maternity function, $l$ the survivorship and $m$ the age-specific fertility (the model of Sharpe and Lotka 1911, treated rigorously by Feller 1941, Section 2). Let $\varphi \ge 0$ be measurable with total fertility $1 < \int_0^\infty \varphi < \infty$. Then the characteristic equation $\int_0^\infty e^{-ra}\varphi(a)\, da = 1$ has a real root $r > 0$ (Feller 1941, Theorem 3 (iii); Hyrien and Yanev 2012, Section 4); it is unique, because the left side is strictly decreasing in $r$ where it is finite. Put $f(a) = e^{-ra}\varphi(a)$, a probability density, and $G_r(t) = e^{-rt}G(t)$, and assume the hypotheses of Feller (1941, Theorem 4 and the Remark after it, p. 253), applied to these exponentially tilted functions: for some integer $n \ge 2$ the moments $m_k = \int_0^\infty a^k f(a)\, da$ are finite for $k \le n$, the functions $a^k f(a)$ are of bounded total variation over $(0, \infty)$ for $k \le n - 2$, and $G \ge 0$ is measurable and bounded on finite intervals with $\int_0^\infty G_r(s)\, ds < \infty$, $t^{n-2} G_r(t) \to 0$ and $t^{n-2}\int_t^\infty G_r(s)\, ds \to 0$ as $t \to \infty$. Write $\mu_r = m_1 = \int_0^\infty a\, e^{-ra}\varphi(a)\, da$, and let $B$ be the solution of the renewal equation that is bounded on compact sets. Then

$$e^{-rt} B(t) \to Q = \frac{1}{\mu_r}\int_0^\infty e^{-rt} G(t)\, dt \qquad (t \to \infty),$$

so births ultimately grow like $Q e^{rt}$. A nonzero exponential $c\,e^{rt}$ does not solve the homogeneous half-line equation ($G = 0$): at $t = 0$ that equation forces $B(0) = 0$. The exponentials solve instead the full-history equation $B(t) = \int_0^\infty B(t - a)\,\varphi(a)\,da$ for every real $t$, and these solutions are characterized by the [renewal equation for exponential growth](../../../epidemiology/euler-lotka-equation-and-the-reproduction-number/renewal-exp-iff.md). On the half-line, $c\,e^{rt}$ solves the renewal equation above with the inherited forcing $G(t) = c\,e^{rt}\int_t^\infty e^{-ra}\varphi(a)\,da$, the births to women born before time $0$ in a population that has always grown at rate $r$; this $G$ satisfies the hypotheses of the theorem and gives $Q = c$. The theorem shows that every solution of the half-line equation with $G$ as above is asymptotic to $Q e^{rt}$.

The derivation is explicit. Put $B_r(t) = e^{-rt}B(t)$. Multiplying the renewal equation by $e^{-rt}$ gives $B_r = G_r + B_r * f$ (Feller 1941, Section 4, Lemma), a renewal equation whose kernel $f$ is a probability density with mean $\mu_r$. Applied to this equation, Feller's Theorem 4 gives, as in the Remark after it (p. 253), $B(t) \sim (b'/m_1')\, e^{\sigma' t}$ with $\sigma' = r$, $b' = \int_0^\infty e^{-rt} G(t)\, dt$ and $m_1' = \mu_r$, which is the limit above. The same limit for a monotone $G_r$, through the key renewal theorem for directly Riemann integrable functions, is a separate target (Expansion item 3).

## Expansion

1. The model: the renewal equation for births with the initial-population term $G$, and existence and uniqueness of its solution bounded on compact sets (Feller 1941, Theorem 2, for $\varphi$ and $G$ bounded on finite intervals with convergent Laplace integrals).
2. The reduction by the factor $e^{-rt}$ to a renewal equation with a probability density $f$, and the representation of its solution through the renewal density $u$.
3. Separate target, not used above: the same limit $e^{-rt}B(t) \to Q$ when the characteristic equation has a real root $r$ (for example when $1 \le \int_0^\infty \varphi < \infty$), $\mu_r < \infty$, and $G_r$ is non-increasing and integrable. It combines the reduction above, the representation $B_r(t) = G_r(t) + \int_0^t G_r(t - x)\, u(x)\, dx$ with $u = \sum_{n \ge 1} f^{*n}$, the key renewal theorem for directly Riemann integrable functions (Hyrien and Yanev 2012, Section 4, Theorem A(i)), and the direct Riemann integrability of a non-increasing integrable function, a separate step.
4. The limit $Q$, and the case $\mu_r = \infty$, where $e^{-rt} B(t) \to 0$, as a separate statement.
