# Pangraphs of higher-order ecological interactions

Ecological interaction modifications, such as a predator suppressing the effect of one species on another, act on interactions rather than on species. Pangraphs let directed, weighted edges have heads and tails made of vertices and of other edges, recursively, so that nested higher-order interactions keep the asymmetric roles of their participants; their incidence (Levi) digraphs are layered, and recursive centralities computed on a pangraph differ from those computed on its digraph or dihypergraph surrogates.
