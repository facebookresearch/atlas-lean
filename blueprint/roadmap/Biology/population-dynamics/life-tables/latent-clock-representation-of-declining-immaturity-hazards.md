# Latent clock representation of declining immaturity hazards

**Setting.** Let the hazard at age $u$ be $\mu_1(u)+a_2+h(u)$, with an immaturity component $\mu_1\ge0$ integrable at infinity, a constant $a_2\ge0$, and an adult hazard $h\ge0$. Write $A_x=\int_x^\infty\mu_1(u)\,du$ for the remaining cumulative immaturity hazard and ${}_tp_x$ for the survival of a life aged $x$ for $t$ more years.

**Proposition (clock representation).** For all $x,t\ge0$,

$${}_tp_x=\mathbb{E}\left[\Big(\frac{A_{x+t}}{A_x}\Big)^{K_x}\exp\Big\{-a_2t-\int_x^{x+t}h(u)\,du\Big\}\right],\qquad K_x\sim\mathrm{Poisson}(A_x),$$

and $e_x=e^{-A_x}\sum_{n\ge0}\frac{A_x^n}{n!}\int_0^\infty\big(A_{x+t}/A_x\big)^n\exp\{-a_2t-\int_x^{x+t}h\}\,dt$.

**Proposition (constant-rate clocks).** The immaturity hazard is generated by Poisson-many clocks with independent constant rates drawn from one distribution if and only if $\mu_1$ is completely monotone. Then there is a positive random rate $R$, unique in law, with

$$\mu_1(t)=A_0\,\mathbb{E}(Re^{-tR}),\qquad A_x=A_0\,\mathbb{E}(e^{-xR}).$$

A survivor at age $x$ carries $K_x\sim\mathrm{Poisson}(A_x)$ clocks whose rates are i.i.d., independent of $K_x$, with the tilted law $\mathbb{E}g(\Lambda_x)=\mathbb{E}[g(R)e^{-xR}]/\mathbb{E}[e^{-xR}]$; with $Y_x$ the sum of these rates,

$${}_tp_x=\mathbb{E}\Big[\exp\Big\{-(a_2+Y_x)t-\int_x^{x+t}h(u)\,du\Big\}\Big],\qquad e_x=\mathbb{E}\big[e_{x,h}(a_2+Y_x)\big],$$

where $e_{x,h}(c)$ is the remaining life expectancy under hazard $c+h$. The Siler case is $R\equiv b_1$, $\mu_1(t)=a_1e^{-b_1t}$.

## Expansion

Each clock present at age $x$ survives to $x+t$ with probability $A_{x+t}/A_x$ when clocks are thinned in proportion to the remaining cumulative hazard; averaging $s^{K}$ over $K\sim\mathrm{Poisson}(A_x)$ gives $\exp\{-A_x(1-s)\}=\exp\{-\int_x^{x+t}\mu_1\}$, which proves the first identity. Constant individual rates mixed over $R$ yield a population immaturity hazard $A_0\mathbb{E}(Re^{-tR})$, which is completely monotone, and Bernstein's theorem gives the converse and uniqueness of the law of $R$. Selection on survivors tilts the rate distribution by $e^{-xR}$, so fast clocks disappear first; this is a selection explanation of ontogenescence that is exact at the level of population survival, though not identified as a biological mechanism.
