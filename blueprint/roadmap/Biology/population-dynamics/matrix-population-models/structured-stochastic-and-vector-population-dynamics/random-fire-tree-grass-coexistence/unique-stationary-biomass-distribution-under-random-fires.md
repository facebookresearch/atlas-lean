# Unique stationary distribution of tree and grass biomass under random fires

**Model.** Let $w(t), g(t) \in [0,1]$ be tree and grass biomass relative to their carrying capacities. Between fires they follow the competition system

$$ w' = r_w w (1 - w), \qquad g' = r_g g (1 - g - w), $$

under which, without fires, the woodland state $(1, 0)$ is the only stable equilibrium. Fires occur at random times $t_{n+1} = t_n + \tau_{n+1}$ with state-dependent intensity: $\Pr(\tau_{n+1} > t \mid (w,g)(t_n)) = \exp\bigl( -\int_0^t \lambda(\pi_s(w(t_n), g(t_n)))\, ds \bigr)$, where $\pi_s$ is the flow of the system above and $\lambda : [0,1]^2 \to [0,\infty)$ is bounded and continuous with $\lambda(w, 0) = 0$ and $\lambda(w,g) > 0$ for $g > 0$ (fire needs grass fuel). At a fire, biomass drops by fixed fractions, $(w,g) \mapsto S(w,g) = ((1 - M_w) w, (1 - M_g) g)$ with $M_w, M_g \in (0,1)$. If the state has a density $p(t,w,g)$, it solves

$$ \partial_t p + \partial_w\bigl( r_w w(1-w) p \bigr) + \partial_g\bigl( r_g g (1 - g - w) p \bigr) = -\lambda(w,g) p + \frac{\lambda(S^{-1}(w,g))\, p(t, S^{-1}(w,g))}{(1 - M_w)(1 - M_g)}. $$

**Theorem 1.** There exists a unique probability density $p_*(w,g)$ that is a stationary solution of this equation, and every solution with an initial probability density $f$ converges to it in $L^1$:

$$ \lim_{t \to \infty} \int_0^1 \!\! \int_0^1 \bigl| p(t,w,g) - p_*(w,g) \bigr|\, dw\, dg = 0. $$

So random grass-fueled fires turn the deterministic dominance of woodland into a unique, globally attracting statistical equilibrium of tree-grass coexistence.

## Expansion

The process is a piecewise-deterministic Markov process: deterministic flow between jumps, jumps at state-dependent rate $\lambda$, and the deterministic jump map $S$. Its densities evolve by a stochastic (positive, mass-preserving) semigroup $\{P(t)\}$ on $L^1$, generated by the transport operator plus the jump operator on the right-hand side. Asymptotic stability follows from a general criterion for stochastic semigroups: if the semigroup is partially integral (its kernel has a nontrivial absolutely continuous part, condition (K)), weakly irreducible (WI), and weakly tight (WT, mass does not escape to the boundary of the state space), then it is asymptotically stable. Partial integrality comes from combining two jumps with flows along different directions, which spreads mass over a two-dimensional set; irreducibility uses that from any state with grass the trajectory can reach a common neighborhood by suitable fire times; tightness uses that fires remove a fixed fraction of biomass while grass regrows, so solutions do not concentrate on the grass-free boundary.
