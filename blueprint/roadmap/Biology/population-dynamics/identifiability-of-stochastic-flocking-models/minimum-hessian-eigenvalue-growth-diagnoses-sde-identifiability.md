# Minimum Hessian eigenvalue growth diagnoses identifiability of SDE models

**Setting.** Consider Itô SDEs $dX_i=a_i(X,Y,p)\,dt+\sigma_i(X,Y,p)\,dW_i$, $i=1,\dots,N$, with independent Wiener processes, an auxiliary state $Y$ determined by $Y(0)$ and the history of $X$, parameters $p$ in an open subset of $\mathbb{R}^{d_p}_{>0}$, $\sigma_i>0$, and $a_i,\sigma_i$ of class $C^2$ in $p$ (with integrability of the gradient and Hessian terms). With Euler-Maruyama residuals $F_{i,n}=X_{i,n}-X_{i,n-1}-a_{i,n-1}\Delta t$ at fixed step $\Delta t$, the quasi-likelihood loss is

$$\mathcal{L}_t(p)=\sum_{n=1}^{t}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Big[\tfrac12\log(2\pi\Delta t)+\log\sigma_{i,n-1}+\frac{F_{i,n}^2}{2\sigma_{i,n-1}^2\Delta t}\Big],$$

and $\mathcal{H}_t$ denotes its log-parameter Hessian $(\mathcal{H}_t)_{jk}=\partial^2\mathcal{L}_t/\partial\log p_j\,\partial\log p_k$ at the true parameter $q^*=\log p^*$.

**Submartingale structure.** With $\mathcal{F}_t$ generated by $X_0,\dots,X_t$ and $Y_0$, the raw Hessian $\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_t$ satisfies

$$E\big[\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{t+1}-\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_t\mid\mathcal{F}_t\big]=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1}{\sigma_{i,t}^2}\Big[2\,\nabla_p\sigma_{i,t}\nabla_p\sigma_{i,t}^{\top}+\Delta t\,\nabla_p a_{i,t}\nabla_p a_{i,t}^{\top}\Big]\succeq0,$$

so it is a matrix-valued submartingale with Doob decomposition into a predictable non-decreasing part (accumulated information) and a martingale part (fluctuation); the same holds for $\mathcal{H}_t$.

**Theorem.**
1. (Supplementary Theorem S21.) If $\mathcal{H}_t/t\to H_\infty$ almost surely in Frobenius norm, then $\lambda^{\min}(\mathcal{H}_t)=\lambda^{\min}_\infty\,t+o(t)$ almost surely, where $\lambda^{\min}_\infty=\lambda^{\min}(H_\infty)$. Thus the minimum eigenvalue grows linearly when $\lambda^{\min}_\infty>0$ (identifiable) and only sublinearly when $\lambda^{\min}_\infty=0$.
2. (Supplementary Theorem S22.) If the model is completely degenerate (an exact parameter redundancy, e.g. two parameters entering only through their product) and the martingale increments satisfy $E[\|\mathcal{M}_{t+1}-\mathcal{M}_t\|_F^2\mid\mathcal{F}_t]\le C$, then $\lambda^{\min}(\mathcal{H}_t)/(\sqrt t\log t)\to0$ almost surely.

**Diagnostic.** Plot $t\mapsto\lambda^{\min}(\mathcal{H}_t(\hat q))$ at the fitted parameter: linear growth indicates identifiability (and its slope the attainable precision per observation), fluctuation-dominated stagnation indicates structural unidentifiability, and linear growth with a tiny slope indicates practical unidentifiability.

## Expansion

The conditional mean of each Hessian increment kills the terms linear in $F_{i,t+1}$ and replaces $F_{i,t+1}^2$ by $\Delta t\,\sigma_{i,t}^2$, leaving a sum of rank-one positive semidefinite matrices: information accumulates monotonically. Dividing by $t$ and using continuity of the minimum eigenvalue under the Frobenius norm gives part 1. Heuristically, under complete degeneracy the accumulated-information part gains nothing along the redundant direction, so the minimum eigenvalue is controlled by the martingale fluctuation along it; a martingale with bounded conditional increment variance grows like $o(\sqrt t\log t)$ almost surely. The practical diagnostic substitutes the estimate for the unknown true parameter, a step the theory does not yet cover but which numerics support.
