# Invariant bounds and L2 stability for a multiphase tumor–nutrient model

**Model.** A domain $\Omega$ is split by a prescribed moving interface $S(t)$ into a tumor region $\Omega_T(t)$ and a host region $\Omega_H(t)$. The cell volume ratio $\phi$ (tumor cells in $\Omega_T$, host cells in $\Omega_H$) and nutrient concentration $c$ solve, for $0<t\le T_{\max}$,

$$
\partial_t\phi-\kappa_m\Delta\Phi(\phi)=\Gamma_\alpha(\phi,c)\ \text{ in }\Omega_\alpha(t),\ \alpha\in\{T,H\},\qquad \partial_tc-D\Delta c=Q_\alpha(\phi,c),
$$

$$
\Gamma_\alpha(\phi,c)=\sum_{\nu\in\{p,d\}}\gamma_\alpha^\nu f_\alpha^\nu(\phi)g_\alpha^\nu(c)-\delta\phi,\qquad Q_\alpha(\phi,c)=-\lambda_\alpha h_\alpha(\phi)q_\alpha(c),
$$

with no-flux for $\phi$ and Robin exchange $-D\nabla c\cdot n=\eta(c-c_b)$ with blood concentration $c_b$ on the vascularized boundary $\partial_b\Omega$, Dirichlet data $\phi=\phi_\theta$ (stress-free ratio), $c=c_b$ on the far boundary $\partial_f\Omega$, flux continuity of $\Phi(\phi)$ across $S(t)$, and initial data $\phi_0,c_0$. Here $\Phi$ is the nonlinear diffusion potential generated by the cell stress, and $\phi_{\max}\in(0,1]$ is the packing limit.

**Hypotheses.** (H1) $\Phi$ smooth, strictly increasing, $\Phi(0)=0$. (H2) proliferation rates $\gamma^p_\alpha>0$, death rates $\gamma^d_\alpha<0$, natural apoptosis $\delta>0$. (H3) $f^\nu_\alpha$ bounded, nonnegative and $\Phi$-Lipschitz on $[0,\phi_{\max}]$, i.e. $|f(s_2)-f(s_1)|^2\le L\,(\Phi(s_2)-\Phi(s_1))(s_2-s_1)$, with $f^p_\alpha(0)=f^p_\alpha(\phi_{\max})=0$, $f^p_\alpha\ge0$ below $0$ and $\le0$ above $\phi_{\max}$, $f^d_\alpha(0)=0$, $f^d_\alpha\le0$ below $0$, $\ge0$ above $\phi_{\max}$ and nondecreasing on $[0,\phi_{\max}]$. (H4) $g^\nu_\alpha\ge0$, locally bounded and Lipschitz. (H5)–(H7) $\lambda_\alpha>0$, $h_\alpha\ge0$ $\Phi$-Lipschitz, $q_\alpha\ge0$ locally bounded. (H8) $0\le\phi_0\le\phi_{\max}$, $0\le c_0\le c_b$.

**Theorem (invariant bounds, Theorem 1).** Every weak solution satisfies $0\le\phi\le\phi_{\max}$ and $0\le c\le c_b$ a.e. in $\Omega\times(0,T_{\max})$.

**Theorem (continuous dependence and uniqueness, Theorem 2).** For two weak solutions with initial data $(\phi_{i,0},c_{i,0})\in L^2(\Omega)^2$,

$$
|||\phi_2-\phi_1|||^2_{L^2_tL^2_x}+\int_0^{T_{\max}}\!\!\int_\Omega\bigl(\Phi(\phi_2)-\Phi(\phi_1)\bigr)(\phi_2-\phi_1)+\|c_2-c_1\|^2_{L^2_tH^1_x}+\int_0^{T_{\max}}\!\|c_2-c_1\|^2_{L^2(\partial_b\Omega)}\lesssim\|\phi_{2,0}-\phi_{1,0}\|^2_{L^2}+\|c_{2,0}-c_{1,0}\|^2_{L^2},
$$

where $|||f|||^2=\langle\mathcal Pf,f\rangle$ is the negative-order norm defined by the Poisson solution operator $\mathcal P$ of the mixed boundary problem. In particular weak solutions are unique.

## Expansion

Testing the equations with the negative parts $\phi^-$, $c^-$ and the excesses $(\phi-\phi_{\max})^+$, $(c-c_b)^+$ and using the sign conditions in (H2)–(H7) shows that these quantities have nonincreasing $L^2$ norms, which vanish initially by (H8). For uniqueness, the degenerate diffusion is handled by testing the $\phi$-difference equation with $\mathcal P(\phi_2-\phi_1)$ (an $H^{-1}$-type energy method), which produces the monotone term $\int(\Phi(\phi_2)-\Phi(\phi_1))(\phi_2-\phi_1)$; the $\Phi$-Lipschitz condition on $f^\nu_\alpha$ and $h_\alpha$ lets this term absorb the reaction differences, and Gronwall's lemma closes the estimate. The stationary analogue also has these bounds, uniqueness when a coefficient-dependent constant is small, and existence in one space dimension.
