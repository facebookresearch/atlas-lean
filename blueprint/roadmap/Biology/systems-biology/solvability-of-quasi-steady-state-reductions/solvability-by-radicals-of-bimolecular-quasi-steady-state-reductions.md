# Solvability by radicals of bimolecular quasi-steady-state reductions

**Setting.** A chemical reaction network $(\mathcal S,\mathcal C,\mathcal R)$ with mass-action kinetics has rate-law polynomials $\Phi_s(\mathbf x)=\sum_{c\to c'}(c'-c)_sk_{c\to c'}\mathbf x^c$. It is *at-most-bimolecular* if every reactant complex is of the form $2A$ or $A+B$ (so each $\Phi_s$ has degree $\le2$). The *quasi-steady-state assumption* (QSSA) for a set $\mathcal Q$ of intermediate species sets $\Phi_q=0$ for $q\in\mathcal Q$ and solves for the intermediate concentrations in terms of the other concentrations, the rate constants and the totals $T$ of linear conservation laws; these generate the ground field $\Bbbk_{\mathcal Q}$, and the *quasi-steady-state ideal* is $I_{\mathcal Q}=\langle\Phi_q:q\in\mathcal Q\rangle+\mathrm{LCL}(\mathcal R,\mathcal Q)$ in $\Bbbk_{\mathcal Q}[x_q:q\in\mathcal Q]$. *Reduction by QSSA is possible* if every quasi-steady state is expressible by radicals over $\Bbbk_{\mathcal Q}$; equivalently, for each $q$, $I_{\mathcal Q}\cap\Bbbk_{\mathcal Q}[x_q]\ne\{0\}$ and its generator is solvable by radicals (a condition checkable with lexicographic Gröbner bases).

**Theorem.** Consider an at-most-bimolecular network with two intermediates $\mathcal Q=\{q_1,q_2\}$ whose rate-law polynomials are neither constant nor both univariate in the same variable. Then the saturation $V^a\big(I_{\mathcal Q}:(x_{q_1}x_{q_2})^\infty\big)$ (quasi-steady states off the coordinate axes) is finite and each of its points is solvable by radicals over $\Bbbk_{\mathcal Q}$. If in addition one of the two rate-law polynomials has a nonzero constant term, then $V^a(I_{\mathcal Q})$ itself is finite. Consequently, for at-most-bimolecular networks, reduction by QSSA is always possible with one intermediate, and with two intermediates under the last hypothesis.

**Theorem (graph criterion).** Let $G$ be the oriented species–reaction graph, $\mathcal Q$ a set of intermediates without linear conservation laws, and $H$ the induced graph on intermediates. If there is a $\mathcal Q$-compatible equivalence relation on $H$ whose quotient has no directed cycles, such that QSSA reduction is possible for each equivalence class taken as the set of intermediates (and stays finite under algebraic specialization of constant terms), then QSSA reduction is possible for all of $\mathcal Q$. In particular, networks whose intermediates decompose into acyclically ordered blocks of at most two bimolecular intermediates have finitely many physically meaningful positive quasi-steady states, all expressible by radicals.

## Expansion

Two bimolecular equations in two unknowns define two conics; after removing boundary components (saturation by $x_{q_1}x_{q_2}$), Bézout bounds the intersection by $4$ points, and eliminating one variable yields a polynomial of degree at most $4$ in the other, always solvable by radicals. Finiteness uses the structure of mass-action coefficients: coefficients of distinct monomials of a rate law depend on disjoint sets of rate constants, so two rate laws can share a factor only if they are rational multiples, which would be a linear conservation law. For larger networks the acyclic block decomposition lets one solve block by block, substituting radical expressions downstream. This explains why QSSA succeeds on common mechanisms even though Pantea–Gupta–Rawlings–Craciun constructed networks whose QSSA polynomials are not solvable by radicals; the examples show their counterexample is minimal.
