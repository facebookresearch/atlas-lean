# Exact approximate Bayesian computation with a stochastic acceptor for measurement noise

**Setting.** Let $\pi(\theta)$ be a prior on parameters, $p(y \mid \theta)$ a (possibly stochastic) mechanistic model that can be simulated but whose density is unavailable, and $\pi(\bar y \mid y, \theta)$ a measurement-noise density (it may depend on $\theta$, e.g. through an unknown noise scale). The observed data $D$ are assumed generated by the noisy model

$$q(\bar y \mid \theta) = \int \pi(\bar y \mid y, \theta)\, p(y \mid \theta)\, dy, \qquad \text{posterior } \pi(\theta \mid D) \propto q(D \mid \theta)\, \pi(\theta).$$

**Theorem (exact noisy ABC).** Let $c \ge \sup_{y, \theta} \pi(D \mid y, \theta)$ be a constant. Repeat: draw $\theta \sim \pi(\theta)$, simulate $y \sim p(y \mid \theta)$, and accept $\theta$ with probability $\pi(D \mid y, \theta)/c$ (draw $u \sim U[0,1]$ and accept iff $\pi(D \mid y, \theta)/c \ge u$). Accepted parameters are exact samples from $\pi(\theta \mid D)$.

**Comparison with the usual practice.** (A1) Standard ABC with a uniform kernel $\mathbf 1[d(y, D) \le \varepsilon]$ and $\varepsilon > 0$ is exact only for a uniform noise model, so with Gaussian or other noise it gives a visibly wrong posterior; (A2) adding simulated noise to the model output and running standard ABC is exact only as $\varepsilon \to 0$ and has low acceptance rates; (A3) keeping the noise-free simulator and replacing the deterministic acceptance by the stochastic acceptor above gives exact inference for any non-degenerate noise model. Ignoring measurement noise altogether ($\varepsilon \to 0$ against the noisy data with a noise-free simulator) makes the posterior concentrate on wrong values.

## Expansion

The accepted pairs $(\theta, y)$ have joint density proportional to $\pi(\theta)\, p(y \mid \theta)\, \pi(D \mid y, \theta)/c$. Integrating out $y$ gives $\pi(\theta)\, q(D \mid \theta)/c$, which is proportional to the posterior because $c$ does not depend on $\theta$; the bound $c \ge \sup \pi(D \mid y,\theta)$ makes the acceptance probability valid. This is "ABC gives exact inference, but for the wrong model" read in reverse: choose the acceptance kernel to be the actual noise model. For deterministic models the scheme reduces to likelihood-based rejection sampling. A uniform kernel corresponds to the noise model $\pi(\bar y \mid y) \propto \mathbf 1[d(y, \bar y) \le \varepsilon]$, which explains approach (A1).
