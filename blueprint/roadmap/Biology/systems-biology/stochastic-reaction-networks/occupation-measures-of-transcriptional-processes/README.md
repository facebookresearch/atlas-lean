# Occupation measures of transcriptional processes

Single-cell RNA sequencing samples cells at unknown positions along their own stochastic trajectories. Describing the observed counts requires both a chemical master equation for transcription, splicing and degradation and a sampling law over the latent process time; the resulting mixture is the occupation measure of the process. For constitutive transcription the instantaneous laws are Poisson (products of Poissons for unspliced and spliced RNA), and their occupation measures have closed-form generating functions.
