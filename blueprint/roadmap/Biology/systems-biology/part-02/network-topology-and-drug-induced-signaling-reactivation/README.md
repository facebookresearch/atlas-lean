# Network topology and drug-induced signaling reactivation

When a drug inhibits an upstream kinase, the steady-state response of a pathway output follows from local responses (connection coefficients) of each node by modular response analysis. Proportional negative feedback only attenuates inhibition and never reverses it; complete reactivation or paradoxical activation needs at least two routes of opposite sign from the drug target to the output, and antithetic integral feedback cannot restore a set-point when the drug target's total abundance is conserved.
