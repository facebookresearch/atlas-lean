# Distance between model and data varieties for validation and selection of polynomial steady-state models

**Setting.** A model with polynomial dynamics $\dot x = f(a, x)$ (e.g. a mass-action reaction network with rate constants $a \in \mathbb{R}^k$ and species $x \in \mathbb{R}^n$) and polynomial observables $y = g(x) \in \mathbb{R}^m$ defines the *real model variety* of steady states

$$(\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal M})_{\mathbb{R}} = \{(a, x, y) \in \mathbb{R}^{k+n+m} : f(a, x) = 0,\ y - g(x) = 0\},$$

and a steady-state measurement $\hat y$ defines the *data variety* $(\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal D})_{\mathbb{R}} = \{(a, x, y) : y = \hat y\}$, an affine space of dimension $k + n$.

**Goodness of fit.** With independent Gaussian measurement errors of known variances $\sigma_i^2$, the maximum-likelihood estimate solves

$$d^2 = \min\Big\{\sum_{i=1}^m\frac{(y_i - \hat y_i)^2}{\sigma_i^2} : (a, x, y) \in (\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal M})_{\mathbb{R}}\Big\},$$

the squared weighted distance between the two varieties, and its minimiser $(a^*, x^*, y^*)$ gives the parameter estimate $a^*$. If the data are generated by the model, the distribution of $d^2$ is dominated by $\chi^2_m$, so the model is rejected at level $\alpha$ when $d^2$ exceeds the upper $\alpha$-quantile of $\chi^2_m$; $d^2 = 0$ (intersecting varieties) means compatibility. Among competing models, $d^2$ (optionally with an AIC/BIC-type complexity penalty) selects the best fit; ties at $d^2 = 0$ indicate the need for more informative experiments.

**Proposition (critical equations).** Let $c = \operatorname{codim}V_{\mathcal M}$ and let $f' = (f'_1, \dots, f'_c)$ cut out $V_{\mathcal M}$ on a Zariski-open set. If $(a, x, y) \in (\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal M})_{\mathbb{R}}$ is a local minimum of $\sum_i(y_i - \hat y_i)^2$, there is $\lambda \in \mathbb{R}^c$ with

$$f' = 0, \qquad y - g(x) = 0, \qquad \sum_{l=1}^c\lambda_l\nabla f'_l + \begin{pmatrix}0\\ y - \hat y\end{pmatrix} = 0.$$

Since this is a square polynomial system, probability-one homotopy continuation computes all its complex solutions, and hence the global minimum $d^2$ (restricted, if needed, to the positive orthant), avoiding the local minima that trap standard nonlinear optimisation.

## Proof sketch

The Gaussian log-likelihood is, up to constants, $-\frac12\sum_i(\hat y_i - y_i)^2/\sigma_i^2$, so maximum likelihood is a nearest-point problem between real algebraic sets. Restricting to a Zariski-open set where $V_{\mathcal M}$ is a complete intersection makes the Lagrange system well posed; the number of its complex solutions for generic data is the Euclidean-distance degree of the model variety, which bounds the work of the homotopy. Applied to cell-death activation, synthetic-biology circuits, HIV progression and multisite phosphorylation, the method discriminates mechanisms using steady-state data alone.
