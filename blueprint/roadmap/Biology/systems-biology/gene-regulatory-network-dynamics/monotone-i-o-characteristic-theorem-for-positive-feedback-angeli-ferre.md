# Monotone systems with positive feedback: the input–output characteristic test

Let $\dot x = f(x, \omega)$, $\eta = h(x)$ be a strongly monotone input–output system (monotone with respect to orthant orders on states, input and output) that has a well-defined input–output characteristic $k$: for each constant input $\omega$ there is a globally attracting equilibrium, whose output is $k(\omega)$. Close the loop with unity positive feedback, $\omega = \eta$. Angeli, Ferrell and Sontag (2004) show that the equilibria of the closed loop correspond one-to-one to the fixed points of $k$, and that if $k'(\omega) \ne 1$ at every fixed point and all trajectories are bounded, then for all initial conditions outside a set of measure zero the solutions converge to equilibria corresponding to fixed points with $k'(\omega) < 1$. This turns bistability of positive-feedback loops into a test on a scalar curve.

## Expansion

1. The model: monotone control systems, input–output characteristics and the closed loop.
2. The correspondence between closed-loop equilibria and fixed points of $k$.
3. Stability of the equilibria according to $k' < 1$ or $k' > 1$.
4. Generic convergence: Hirsch's theorem for strongly monotone flows (convergence of almost every bounded solution to equilibria), applied to the closed loop.
5. The biochemical applications (MAPK cascades, Cdc2–cyclin feedback), as separate statements.
