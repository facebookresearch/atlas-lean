# Flux topes as cells of a hyperplane arrangement

**Setting.** Let $C = \{ v \in \mathbb{R}^r : N v = 0,\ v_i \ge 0\ (i \in I_{\mathrm{irr}})\}$ be a
consistent flux cone, so that all flux topes have full support. Let the columns of
$K \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times d}$ be a basis of $\ker N$, with rows $K_1,\dots,K_r \in \mathbb{R}^d$.
Every flux mode can be written uniquely as $v = K x$ with $x \in \mathbb{R}^d$, and
$\operatorname{sign}(v)_i = \operatorname{sign}(K_i x)$.

**Statement.** The flux topes of $C$ correspond bijectively to the cells (full-dimensional regions)
of the central hyperplane arrangement

$$h_i = \{ x \in \mathbb{R}^d : K_i x = 0 \}, \qquad i = 1, \dots, r,$$

that satisfy the irreversibility constraints $K_i x > 0$ for $i \in I_{\mathrm{irr}}$. In particular,
the number of flux topes is at most the number of cells of a central arrangement of $r$ hyperplanes
in $\mathbb{R}^d$,

$$2 \sum_{i=0}^{d-1} \binom{r-1}{i},$$

which equals $2^r$ when $d \ge r$, and it is also at most $2^{|I_{\mathrm{rev}}|}$; with no
reversible reactions there is exactly one flux tope. If no two rows of $K$ are proportional (the
hyperplanes are distinct), the flux topes can be enumerated by reverse search over full sign
vectors, moving between adjacent cells that differ in one sign.

For flux balance analysis, maximizing $c^{\mathsf T} v$ over a polyhedron
$P = \{v : Nv = 0,\ \mathrm{lb} \le v \le \mathrm{ub}\}$ with optimal value $d^*$, the optimal face
$P^* = \{ v \in P : c^{\mathsf T} v = d^* \}$ has its own flux topes, which (after making $P^*$
consistent) are the cells of a non-central arrangement satisfying the box constraints, and can be
enumerated directly without listing all flux topes of $C$.

## Expansion

Since $v = Kx$ is a linear bijection between $\mathbb{R}^d$ and $\ker N$, the sign pattern of $v$
records on which side of each hyperplane $h_i$ the point $x$ lies. A full sign vector is realized by
a flux mode exactly when the corresponding open region of the arrangement is nonempty, and it is a
flux tope exactly when that region also lies on the positive side of every irreversible hyperplane.
Irreversible reactions therefore shape the cone, while reversible reactions cut it into flux topes.
The bound $2\sum_{i<d}\binom{r-1}{i}$ is the classical count of regions of a central arrangement in
general position (Buck 1943). In the paper's six-reaction toy network with three reversible
reactions, only five of the $2^3 = 8$ full sign vectors define flux topes, and each flux tope is
generated by three elementary modes, the minimum allowed by $\dim \ker N = 3$.
