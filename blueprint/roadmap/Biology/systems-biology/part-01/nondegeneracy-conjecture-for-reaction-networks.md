# Nondegeneracy Conjecture for reaction networks

Can a network's capacity for finitely many positive steady states always be realized with nondegenerate steady states? Nondegeneracy makes switches robust to perturbations; it is not itself stability.

## What would settle it

Assume the positive steady-state set is finite for every admissible choice of rates and conserved totals. If some choice gives ℓ positive steady states, prove another gives ℓ nondegenerate ones. Define nondegeneracy on the stoichiometric subspace, or equivalently through the properly augmented steady-state system. The finiteness hypothesis is essential; dropping it produces false statements.

## Status

The cited sources pose this as an open question in the versions cited; this page does not certify its present status.

Feliu, Henriksson and Pascual-Escudero record that the conjecture is proved for small networks and for stoichiometric rank one, state that the general case remains open, and prove the case ℓ = 2 under additional assumptions that include nondegenerate networks.

This page records the cited research question and asserts no theorem or present-status certification.
