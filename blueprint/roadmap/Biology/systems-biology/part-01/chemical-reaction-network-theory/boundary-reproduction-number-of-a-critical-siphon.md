# Boundary reproduction number of a critical siphon

**Setting.** A chemical reaction network $(\mathcal S,\mathcal R)$ with species $X_1,\dots,X_n$ evolves by $\dot{\mathbf x}=\Gamma R(\mathbf x)$ with stoichiometric matrix $\Gamma$ and reaction rates $R_j$ satisfying (A1) $R_j\in C^1$; (A2) $R_j(\mathbf x)\ge0$, with $R_j(\mathbf x)>0$ iff every reactant of reaction $j$ has positive concentration; (A3) $\partial R_j/\partial x_i\ge0$ whenever $X_i$ is a reactant of reaction $j$. Theorem 3 also assumes (A4): at every $\mathcal X$-free boundary steady state $\mathbf x^*$, $\partial R_j/\partial x_i(\mathbf x^*)>0$ whenever $X_i$ is a reactant of reaction $j$ (true for mass action with reactant coefficients $1$). A set $\mathcal X\subseteq\mathcal S$ is a *siphon* if every reaction producing a species of $\mathcal X$ also consumes a species of $\mathcal X$; its complement $\mathcal Y$ is the antisiphon. The siphon is *critical* if no nonnegative conservation vector is supported inside $\mathcal X$. Order coordinates as $\mathbf x=(\tilde{\mathbf x},\tilde{\mathbf y})$ with $\tilde{\mathbf x}$ the siphon species, so the face $\{\tilde{\mathbf x}=0\}$ is forward invariant and contains the $\mathcal X$-free boundary steady states $\mathbf x^*=(\mathbf 0,\tilde{\mathbf y}^*)$. Split the siphon dynamics as $\dot{\tilde{\mathbf x}}=\mathcal F(\tilde{\mathbf x},\tilde{\mathbf y})-\mathcal V(\tilde{\mathbf x},\tilde{\mathbf y})$, where $\mathcal F$ collects some of the positive terms of the right-hand side (production of siphon species, the analogue of new infections), and let $F=\partial_{\tilde{\mathbf x}}\mathcal F(\mathbf x^*)$ and $V=\partial_{\tilde{\mathbf x}}\mathcal V(\mathbf x^*)$. Here a matrix or vector is *nonnegative*, $A\ge0$, if all its entries are $\ge0$ **and it is not zero**, and a *$Z$-matrix* has nonpositive off-diagonal entries. The splitting is *admissible* (Definition 6) if $F\ge0$ and $V$ is a $Z$-matrix with $V^{-1}\ge0$; then the *boundary reproduction number* is

$$\mathscr R_{\mathbf x^*}=\rho(FV^{-1}).$$

**Theorem 1.** Assume (A1)–(A3). Let $\mathcal X$ be a critical siphon, $\mathbf x^*$ an $\mathcal X$-free boundary steady state and $F,V$ from an admissible splitting.
(a) If the network has no conservation laws and the Jacobian of the antisiphon dynamics with respect to $\tilde{\mathbf y}$ at $\mathbf x^*$ is Hurwitz, then $\mathbf x^*$ is locally asymptotically stable if $\mathscr R_{\mathbf x^*}<1$ and unstable if $\mathscr R_{\mathbf x^*}>1$.
(b) If the number of antisiphon species equals the number $n-s$ of independent conservation laws and the conservation matrix restricted to $\mathcal Y$, $W_{\mathcal Y}$, is invertible, then $\mathbf x^*=(\mathbf 0,W_{\mathcal Y}^{-1}\boldsymbol\Lambda)$ is the unique steady state in its stoichiometric compatibility class and is locally asymptotically stable relative to that class if $\mathscr R_{\mathbf x^*}<1$ and unstable if $\mathscr R_{\mathbf x^*}>1$.

**Theorem 2.** Call a square matrix $A$ *block triangularizable* (Definition 7) if a permutation $P$ brings it to block upper-triangular form $PAP^{\mathsf T}=\begin{pmatrix}A_1&J_{12}&\cdots\\0&A_2&\cdots\\ \vdots&&\ddots\end{pmatrix}$ with **irreducible** square diagonal blocks $A_i$. If $F\ge0$ and $V$ is block triangularizable with off-diagonal blocks $J_{ij}\le0$ (entrywise) and diagonal blocks $A_i$ that are $Z$-matrices with $\mathbb 1^{\mathsf T}A_i\ge0$ in the above sense (entrywise nonnegative and nonzero), then $V^{-1}\ge0$. The nonzero requirement is needed: read entrywise only, the statement fails, since $V=\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\-1&1\end{pmatrix}$ is an irreducible $Z$-matrix with $\mathbb 1^{\mathsf T}V=0$ but is singular.

**Theorem 3.** Assume (A1)–(A4). The $\mathcal X$-reduced network has vertices $\mathcal X\cup\{\emptyset\}$ and edges $X_i\to X_j$ when a reaction has reactant $X_i$ and product $X_j$, self-loops $X_i\to X_i$ when a reaction increases $X_i$ while consuming it, and $X_i\to\emptyset$ when a reaction consumes $X_i$ and produces no siphon species or decreases $X_i$ while producing it. Suppose its edges split into $E^{\mathcal F}\sqcup E^{\mathcal V}$ with (1) $E^{\mathcal F}\ne\emptyset$; (2) $\{\emptyset\}$ is the unique terminal strongly connected component of $(\mathcal X\cup\{\emptyset\},E^{\mathcal V})$; (3) all self-loops lie in $E^{\mathcal F}$; and (4) any two edges of $E^{\mathcal V}$ coming from the same reaction have the same source and targets in distinct strongly connected components. Then putting into $\mathcal F$ the rate terms of the edges in $E^{\mathcal F}$ and into $\mathcal V$ the rest gives $F\ge0$ and $V^{-1}\ge0$, an admissible splitting.

## Expansion

At $\mathbf x^*$ the Jacobian is block triangular because siphon species cannot be produced without siphon reactants: its eigenvalues are those of $F-V$ (siphon block) and of the antisiphon block. When $F\ge0$ and $V$ is a nonsingular M-matrix, $F-V$ is Hurwitz iff $\rho(FV^{-1})<1$, the van den Driessche–Watmough next-generation argument; Theorems 2 and 3 provide checkable graph conditions that make $V$ an M-matrix even with dissociation reactions $X_1\to X_2+X_3$ inside the siphon, which the epidemic-model setting excluded. Conservation laws are handled by restricting to the compatibility class, where the invertibility of $W_{\mathcal Y}$ pins down the antisiphon concentrations. For the EnvZ–OmpR osmoregulation network this gives $\mathscr R_{\mathbf x^*}=k_5Y_{tot}/k_2$, so the boundary state is stable iff $Y_{tot}<k_2/k_5$.
