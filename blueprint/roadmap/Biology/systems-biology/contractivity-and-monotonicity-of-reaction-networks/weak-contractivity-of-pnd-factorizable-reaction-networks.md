# Weak contractivity of PND-factorizable reaction networks

**Setting.** A reaction network with $n$ species and stoichiometric matrix $\Gamma\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}$ evolves by $\dot x=\Gamma R(x)$ on $\mathbb{R}^n_{\ge 0}$, where the reaction-rate vector $R$ satisfies the standard kinetic assumptions (each $R_j$ is $C^1$, vanishes when a reactant is absent, and is nondecreasing in its reactants and nonincreasing in its products for reversible reactions); mass-action, Michaelis–Menten and Hill kinetics qualify. A system is *weakly contractive* with respect to a norm $\|\cdot\|$ if any two distinct trajectories satisfy $\|x(t_2)-y(t_2)\|<\|x(t_1)-y(t_1)\|$ for all $t_2>t_1$. A network is weakly contractive if, for every admissible kinetics, the dynamics on the relative interior of each stoichiometric compatibility class $(x_0+\operatorname{im}\Gamma)\cap\mathbb{R}^n_{\ge0}$ are weakly contractive with respect to some norm. The network is *non-catalytic* if no species appears on both sides of a reaction.

**Matrix classes.** Let $S$ be the set of real matrices with entries in $\{-1,0,1\}$ and at most two nonzero entries per column, $S^t=\{A: A^t\in S\}$ and $\mathcal{N}=S\cup S^t$. Let $\mathcal{P}$ be the set of matrices with at most one nonzero entry in each row and no zero column, and $\mathcal{D}$ the set of square diagonal matrices with positive diagonal.

**$\mathcal{RI}$-graph.** Its nodes are the reactions; there is a directed edge $\Gamma_i\to\Gamma_j$ if $\Gamma_j$ is irreversible and some species of $\Gamma_i$ is a reactant of $\Gamma_j$, or if $\Gamma_j$ is reversible and the two reactions share a species.

**Theorem (main theorem).** Let a non-catalytic reaction network have stoichiometric matrix $\Gamma=PND$ with $P\in\mathcal{P}$, $N\in\mathcal{N}$, $D\in\mathcal{D}$. If the $\mathcal{RI}$-graph is strongly connected, then the network is weakly contractive.

The proof splits into three structural cases for $N$: (i) at most two nonzero entries per column (type C networks, weakly contractive by Corollary 5, via strong monotonicity with respect to a cone whose face poset is a cross-polytope or a simplex, Theorem 7); (ii) at most two nonzero entries per row with linearly independent columns (cubical networks, Theorem 10); (iii) at most two nonzero entries per row with linearly dependent columns (Theorem 13). Cases (ii) and (iii) are unified in Theorem 15: an aligned matrix with at most two nonzero entries per row gives a weakly contractive system.

## Expansion

The bridge is a lifting argument (the theorem of Section 2): if the dynamics are forward invariant on every translate $S_a=\mathbb{R}^n_{\ge0}\cap(a+S)$ of a subspace $S$ and the lifted system is strongly monotone with respect to a cone $K$ with $K\cap S=\{0\}$ and $\operatorname{span}K\supseteq S$, then the section $P=K\cap\operatorname{AffHul}(S_a)$ is bounded, $P-P$ is the unit ball of a norm, and strong monotonicity makes the flow strictly decrease distances in that norm. Strong monotonicity is checked reaction by reaction: a network is monotone with respect to $K$ iff each single reaction is, and a viable set of vectors $M$ whose affine hull has the dimension of the compatibility classes lifts to a cone for which the network is strongly monotone when the $\mathcal{RI}$-graph is strongly connected (Theorem 5). Left multiplication by $P\in\mathcal{P}$ transports the cone (Theorem 6), and $D$ only rescales reaction rates, so it suffices to build cones for $N$. The theorem recovers previously studied classes (Angeli–De Leenheer–Sontag reaction-coordinate networks, Banaji's type S networks, and the type I networks) as special cases.
