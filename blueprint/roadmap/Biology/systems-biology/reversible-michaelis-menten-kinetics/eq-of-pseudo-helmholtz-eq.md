# Positive c*: only c* has pseudo-Helmholtz value G(c*) among states ≥ 0

Let $c^* \in \mathbb{R}^4$ have positive coordinates and $y \in \mathbb{R}^4$ nonnegative ones, and
let $G(x) = \sum_i x_i (\log x_i - \log c^*_i - 1)$ be the
[pseudo-Helmholtz function](../complex-balancing-and-the-deficiency-zero-theore/pseudo-helmholtz.md)
relative to $c^*$; a term with $y_i = 0$ is $0$. If $G(y) = G(c^*)$, then $y = c^*$. The proof uses
the Gibbs inequality
$G(y) - G(c^*) = \sum_i \bigl(y_i (\log y_i - \log c^*_i - 1) + c^*_i\bigr) \ge 0$, each term being
nonnegative, and positive when $y_i \ne c^*_i$.
