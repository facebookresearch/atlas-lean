# The two ends of a reaction lie in the same linkage class

Let $N$ be a [chemical reaction network](../../../Dynamics/smooth-dynamics/reaction-network.md) with
species set $S$ and reaction set $\rho$. If $y$ and $y'$ are
[complexes](../../../Dynamics/smooth-dynamics/complexes.md) of $N$ with $y \to y'$, that is, some
reaction has source $y$ and product $y'$ (the
[reaction relation](../../../Dynamics/smooth-dynamics/reacts.md)), then $y$ and $y'$ lie in the same
linkage class: they are joined by a path in the
[linkage graph](../../../Dynamics/smooth-dynamics/linkage-graph.md) of $N$. This includes the case
$y = y'$ of a reaction whose source and product coincide.
