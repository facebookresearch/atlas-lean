# Flux balance analysis as a linear program

**Setting.** A metabolic network has $m$ internal metabolites and $n$ reactions, with stoichiometric matrix $S \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$ ($S_{ij}$ is the net number of molecules of metabolite $i$ produced by one unit of reaction $j$), flux vector $v \in \mathbb{R}^n$, bounds $\ell \le v \le u$ with $\ell_j \in [-\infty, \infty)$, $u_j \in (-\infty, \infty]$ (irreversible reactions have $\ell_j = 0$; exchange reactions carry uptake and secretion limits), and an objective vector $c \in \mathbb{R}^n$, typically selecting a biomass reaction. Metabolite concentrations $x$ obey $\dot x = S v$; at steady state $S v = 0$. Flux balance analysis (FBA) solves

$$\max_{v \in \mathbb{R}^n} \; c^\top v \quad \text{subject to} \quad S v = 0, \quad \ell \le v \le u .$$

**Proposition.**

1. The feasible set $\mathcal{F} = \{v : Sv = 0,\ \ell \le v \le u\}$ is a convex polyhedron, and a polytope when all bounds are finite.
2. If $\mathcal{F}$ is non-empty and bounded, the optimal value $z^\ast$ is attained, and attained at a vertex of $\mathcal{F}$. The optimal value is unique but the set of optimal flux vectors $\mathcal{F}^\ast = \mathcal{F} \cap \{c^\top v = z^\ast\}$ is a face of $\mathcal{F}$ and may contain more than one point.
3. (Flux variability) For each reaction $j$, the range of $v_j$ over $\mathcal{F}^\ast$ is $[\min_{\mathcal{F}^\ast} v_j, \max_{\mathcal{F}^\ast} v_j]$, obtained by two further linear programs; $v_j$ is uniquely determined by optimality exactly when the two values coincide.
4. (Monotonicity in bounds) Tightening bounds can only lower $z^\ast$. In particular, simulating a gene knockout by setting $\ell_j = u_j = 0$ for the reactions that require the deleted gene gives an optimal value no larger than the wild-type one; the knockout is predicted lethal under the model when the reduced problem is infeasible or its optimal biomass flux is zero.
5. (Duality) When $z^\ast$ is finite, the dual linear program has an optimal solution with equal value. Its metabolite prices $y$ (the multipliers of the rows of $S v = 0$) are the supergradients at $b = 0$ of the concave map $b \mapsto z^\ast(b)$, the optimal value with $S v = b$ in place of $S v = 0$. When the optimal price vector is unique and $0$ is interior to the set of feasible right-hand sides, $z^\ast$ is differentiable at $0$ with gradient $y$, the marginal change of $z^\ast$ per unit of imposed net production of each metabolite; without uniqueness it need not be differentiable (one metabolite with $v_1 - v_2 = b$, $0 \le v_1, v_2 \le 1$, maximizing $v_1$, gives $z^\ast(b) = \min(1, 1 + b)$ near $0$, and every $y \in [0, 1]$ is an optimal price).

## Expansion

(1): the feasible set is an intersection of a linear subspace with a box. (2) is the fundamental theorem of linear programming; optimal sets of linear programs are faces, which can be positive-dimensional when the network contains alternative pathways of equal yield. (3) is linear programming with the extra constraint $c^\top v = z^\ast$. (4): shrinking the feasible set cannot increase a maximum. (5) is strong duality, applied with the right-hand side of $Sv = 0$ perturbed; the [supergradient characterization of optimal prices](../../stochastic-mass-action-metabolic-control-and-flu/exists-optimal-dual-iff-supergradient.md) and [differentiability for a unique price vector](../../stochastic-mass-action-metabolic-control-and-flu/has-fderiv-at-fba-value-of-unique.md) are stated for finite bounds. For finite bounds and a general right-hand side $Sv = b$, the [feasible set](../../stochastic-mass-action-metabolic-control-and-flu/fba-feasible.md), the [optimal value](../../stochastic-mass-action-metabolic-control-and-flu/fba-value.md), [attainment of the optimum](../../stochastic-mass-action-metabolic-control-and-flu/exists-fba-value-eq.md) and [strong duality](../../stochastic-mass-action-metabolic-control-and-flu/exists-fba-dual-value-eq.md) have pages of their own; this page records the problem with possibly infinite bounds and the uses made of it. FBA needs no kinetic parameters; its predictions rest on the steady-state assumption, the bounds and the choice of objective, and the non-uniqueness in (2) is why reconstruction tools also report flux ranges or uniform random samples of the feasible space rather than a single flux vector.
