# Uniqueness of Sinkhorn scaling

Let $A$ be a real $n \times n$ matrix with positive entries, and let $r, c, r', c' \in \mathbb{R}_{>0}^n$ be
such that $\operatorname{diag}(r)\, A \operatorname{diag}(c)$ and $\operatorname{diag}(r')\, A \operatorname{diag}(c')$ are
both doubly stochastic (nonnegative entries, all row and column sums $1$). Then there is $t > 0$ with $r' = t\, r$ and $c' = t^{-1} c$.
