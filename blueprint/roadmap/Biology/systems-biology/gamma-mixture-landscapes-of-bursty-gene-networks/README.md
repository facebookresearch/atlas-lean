# Gamma-mixture landscapes of bursty gene networks

Single-cell differentiation is modeled as a stochastic process driven by a gene regulatory network in which proteins are produced in random bursts and degrade deterministically. When promoter switching is fast relative to protein turnover, cells spend long times near the attractors of the mean-field dynamics, each cell type corresponding to a basin of attraction, and the stationary protein distribution is well approximated by a mixture of products of Gamma distributions, one component per basin. For one specific choice of state-dependent burst rates the mixture is exactly stationary.
