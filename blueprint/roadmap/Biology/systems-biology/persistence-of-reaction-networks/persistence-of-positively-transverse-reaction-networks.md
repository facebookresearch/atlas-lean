# Persistence of positively transverse reaction networks

**Setting.** A reaction network $G=(V,E)$ on $n$ species with complexes $y\in\mathbb Z^n_{\ge0}$ defines, for every choice of positive rate constants, the mass-action system $\dot x=\sum_{y\to y'\in E}k_{y\to y'}x^y(y'-y)$ on $\mathbb R^n_{\ge0}$, with stoichiometric subspace $S$. A nonempty set of species $Z$ is a **siphon** if every reaction producing a species of $Z$ consumes some species of $Z$ (so the face $\{x_i=0,\ i\in Z\}$ is forward invariant); it is *relevant* if $Z$ does not contain the support of a nonnegative conservation law. For a siphon $Z$, the paper associates a subnetwork $G_Z$ (the reactions whose reactants involve $Z$, with complexes projected to the coordinates in $Z$), the polytope $P_Z$ spanned by its projected complexes, the normal cones $N_Z(\Gamma)$ of faces $\Gamma$ of $P_Z$, and the positive tropicalization $\mathrm{Trop}^+(S_Z)$ of the projected stoichiometric subspace. A face $\Gamma$ is *$Z$-transverse* for $G_Z$ if some reaction of $G_Z$ has its reactant in $\Gamma$ and its product outside $\Gamma$. The siphon $Z$ is **positively transverse** if every face $\Gamma$ of $P_Z$ either has no strictly positive inner normal direction, or is $Z$-transverse, or has $N_Z(\Gamma)\cap\mathrm{Trop}^+(S_Z)$ disjoint from the simplex $\Delta_Z$. The network is **positively transverse** if every relevant siphon is.

**Theorem 1 / Theorem 3.2.** If a reaction network is positively transverse, then for every choice of positive rate constants no bounded trajectory of the mass-action system with initial condition in $\mathbb R^n_{>0}$ has an $\omega$-limit point on the boundary of $\mathbb R^n_{\ge0}$. In particular (Corollary 3.3) no positive trajectory converges to a boundary steady state.

**Theorem 3.4 (global attractor property).** Suppose the mass-action system has a positive complex-balanced steady state and every relevant siphon $Z\ne\{1,\dots,n\}$ with $|Z|\ge2$ is positively transverse. Then every positive trajectory converges to the unique positive complex-balanced steady state of its stoichiometric compatibility class.

## Expansion

If a bounded positive trajectory accumulated at a boundary point with maximal zero support $Z$, the rates at which the coordinates in $Z$ approach $0$ define a *negative logarithmic limit* direction $a_\infty$ (Proposition 2.1). This direction must lie in the positive tropicalization of $S_Z$, because the trajectory moves within a translate of $S$ (Proposition 2.3), and it must select a face $\Gamma$ of $P_Z$ on which the dominant reactant monomials sit, with no reaction leaving that face, since otherwise the dominant flux would push the trajectory back away from the boundary (Proposition 2.2, Corollary 2.4). Positive transversality excludes exactly these configurations, so the zero support could be enlarged, contradicting maximality (Proposition 3.1). For complex-balanced systems, the boundary is the only obstruction to global convergence, giving Theorem 3.4; the condition is checkable by enumerating siphons and faces.
