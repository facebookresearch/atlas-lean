# Unique positive solution of the self-regulating gene recursion

In the self-regulating gene model with rates $\lambda, \psi, \theta, \mu > 0$ and $\tilde\lambda \ge 0$, the unbound-state probabilities $x_n = \mathbb{P}(X = (n,1))$ satisfy, for $n \ge 1$, the three-term recursion
$$x_{n+2} = \frac{1}{n(n+2)\mu^2}\Bigl[x_{n+1}\,\mu\bigl(n((n+1)(\psi+\mu)+\lambda) + (\tilde\lambda+\theta)(n+1)\bigr) - x_n\bigl(\tilde\lambda(n(\psi+\mu)+\lambda) + n\lambda\mu + \lambda\theta\bigr) + x_{n-1}\,\lambda\tilde\lambda\Bigr],$$
with $x_0 = (\mu/\lambda)\, x_1$. For every $n \ge 1$ and every prescribed $x_{n-1} > 0$ there is exactly one pair $x_n, x_{n+1} > 0$ such that the sequence generated by the recursion stays positive and summable, i.e. is (proportional to) a measure. The bound-state probabilities $\mathbb{P}(X=(n,0))$ then follow from the balance equation of the unbound states, and normalization gives the full steady-state law and $\mathbb{P}(A=1)$.

## Expansion

A third-order linear recursion has a three-dimensional solution space, and generic solutions eventually change sign or grow factorially. Positivity and summability pick out a one-dimensional ray, the minimal solution, which can be computed stably by shooting on the two free initial values. This gives a numerical scheme for the steady-state distribution that avoids truncating the master equation.
