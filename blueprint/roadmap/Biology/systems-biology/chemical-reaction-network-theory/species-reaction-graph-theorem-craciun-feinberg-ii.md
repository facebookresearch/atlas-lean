# Species–reaction graph criterion for unique equilibria (Craciun–Feinberg)

The SR graph of a reaction network is a signed, labelled bipartite multigraph with one vertex for each species and one for each reaction; edges join a species to the reactions in which it participates and carry as label its stoichiometric coefficient (Banaji 2012, Section 3). In Banaji's formulation (Section 4), a cycle is an e-cycle if its parity $(-1)^{|C|/2}\operatorname{sign}(C)$ equals $1$ and an o-cycle otherwise; it is an s-cycle if the alternating product of its edge labels, $\prod_i \mathrm{lbl}(e_{2i-1}) - \prod_i \mathrm{lbl}(e_{2i})$, vanishes; and two cycles have S-to-R intersection if each component of their intersection is a path between a species vertex and a reaction vertex. Condition $(*)$ requires that all e-cycles be s-cycles and that no two e-cycles have S-to-R intersection. For reaction systems with outflow of every species, $\dot x = x_{\mathrm{in}} + \Gamma v(x) - Q(x)$, whose kinetics are N1C (Banaji 2012, Section 2: with $V_{ji} = \partial v_j/\partial x_i$, $\Gamma_{ij}V_{ji} \le 0$ for all $i$ and $j$, and $\Gamma_{ij} = 0$ implies $V_{ji} = 0$), an SR graph satisfying $(*)$ makes the vector field injective (Banaji 2012, Proposition 1). Mass action and Michaelis–Menten kinetics are N1C provided no species occurs on both sides of any reaction (Banaji 2012, Section 2); when a species occurs on both sides, its net stoichiometric coefficient need not record how the rate depends on it, so N1C is then an independent hypothesis to check. An injective vector field has at most one zero, so injectivity excludes multiple equilibria, as in the result that [injectivity precludes multiple equilibria (Craciun–Feinberg)](../../../Biology/systems-biology/chemical-reaction-network-theory/injectivity-criterion-precludes-multistationarity-craciun-feinberg-i.md); Condition $(*)$ is therefore a graph-theoretic sufficient condition for injectivity, and hence for at most one equilibrium. For mass action kinetics in the continuous-flow stirred-tank reactor, this is the species–reaction graph theorem of Craciun and Feinberg (2006), whose case of natural-number coefficients is stated in the [species-reaction graph theorem for natural-number coefficients](../../not-yet-classified/species-reaction-graph/not-has-capacity-for-multiple-equilibria.md).

## Expansion

1. Definitions: the SR graph, edge signs and labels, parity, e-cycles, o-cycles, s-cycles and S-to-R intersection, as in the source.
2. The expansion of the Jacobian determinant into terms indexed by cycle structures, and the argument that the cycle condition makes the determinant nonzero for all concentrations and all positive rate constants.
3. Condition $(*)$ implies injectivity of the reaction vector field, and an injective vector field has at most one equilibrium, as in the [Craciun–Feinberg injectivity result](../../../Biology/systems-biology/chemical-reaction-network-theory/injectivity-criterion-precludes-multistationarity-craciun-feinberg-i.md).
4. The original mass-action statement of Craciun and Feinberg in its own terminology (odd-cycles, stoichiometric cycles, even-cycles), fixed from their paper, for complexes with nonnegative real coefficients beyond the [natural-number case](../../not-yet-classified/species-reaction-graph/not-has-capacity-for-multiple-equilibria.md).
5. Systems without outflow: at most one nondegenerate positive equilibrium in each stoichiometry class (Banaji 2012, Section 5.1), as a separate statement.
