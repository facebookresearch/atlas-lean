# Toric dynamical systems: the moduli of complex-balancing rate constants

A mass-action system with reaction graph $G$ on $n$ complexes and complex matrix $Y$ is toric (complex balanced) for a choice of rate constants $\kappa$ when it has a positive complex-balanced equilibrium. Craciun, Dickenstein, Shiu and Sturmfels (2009, Section 2) define the moduli ideal $M_G$ of rate constants for which the system is toric. Assume that every linkage class (connected component) of $G$ is strongly connected; without this, there is no positive complex-balanced equilibrium for any $\kappa$, and the source takes $M_G$ to be the unit ideal. For each complex $i$ let $K_i$ be the sum, over the spanning trees of the linkage class containing $i$ that are directed toward $i$, of the product of the rate constants $\kappa_{jk}$ on the tree's edges; by the matrix-tree theorem $K_i$ is, up to sign, a maximal minor of the Laplacian block of that linkage class, and it is a nonzero polynomial with positive coefficients. Under this assumption Theorem 9 of the source states that $M_G$, written in the coordinates $K = (K_1, \dots, K_n)$, equals the toric ideal of the Cayley matrix $\mathrm{Cay}_G(Y)$: it is generated by the binomials $K^u - K^v$ with $\mathrm{Cay}_G(Y)(u - v) = 0$, and its codimension equals the [deficiency](../../../Dynamics/smooth-dynamics/deficiency.md) $\delta$.

3. The Cayley matrix, its kernel, and the [toric ideal](../../../RingTheory/ideals-and-constructions/toric-ideals-and-markov-bases/binomials.md) it defines.
4. Theorem 9: the equality of ideals and the codimension count.
5. Detailed balancing and the source's proof of the global attractor conjecture for toric systems with detailed balancing in a bounded polygon, as separate statements.
