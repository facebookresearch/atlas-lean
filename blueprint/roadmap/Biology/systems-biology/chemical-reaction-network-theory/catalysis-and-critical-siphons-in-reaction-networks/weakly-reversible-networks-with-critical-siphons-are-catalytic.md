# Weakly reversible networks with critical siphons are catalytic

**Setting.** A chemical reaction network (CRN) on $s$ species is a finite directed graph $G$ on complexes $1, \dots, n$, each labeled by a distinct monomial $\psi_i(x) = \prod_{j=1}^s x_j^{y_{ij}}$; it is *weakly reversible* if every connected component is strongly connected. With positive rate constants $k$ it defines the mass-action system $\dot x = \psi(x) A_k Y$, whose trajectories stay in the invariant polyhedra $P_x = (x + S)\cap\mathbb{R}^s_{\ge0}$, $S$ the stoichiometric subspace.
- The **event-graph** $\overline G$ has all monic monomials as vertices and edges $(N\psi_i, N\psi_j)$ for every reaction $(i,j)$ and monomial $N$ (a reaction can fire in the presence of bystander molecules $N$).
- $G$ is **catalytic** if there are path-connected $M, N$ in $\overline G$ such that $M/\gcd(M,N)$ and $N/\gcd(M,N)$ are not path-connected: the common factor $\gcd(M,N)$ is a catalyst that switches a reaction pathway on. Equivalently, with $(\mathcal E_G)$ the binomial ideal generated by $\psi_i - \psi_j$ over reactions, $G$ is catalytic iff $(\mathcal E_G) \subsetneq (\mathcal E_G) : (x_1\cdots x_s)^\infty$ (the ideal is not saturated).
- A nonempty $Z \subseteq [s]$ is a **siphon** if every reaction producing a species in $Z$ consumes some species in $Z$ (absence of $Z$ is forward invariant); it is **critical** if some $z \ge 0$ with zero set exactly $Z$ has invariant polyhedron meeting $\mathbb{R}^s_{>0}$.

**Theorem.** Every weakly reversible CRN with a critical siphon is catalytic.

**Corollaries.**
1. Every weakly reversible, non-catalytic CRN is persistent: for every choice of rate constants, no positive trajectory has an $\omega$-limit point on the boundary of the orthant.
2. Horn's global attractor conjecture holds for non-catalytic complex-balanced mass-action systems: each positive invariant polyhedron contains a complex-balanced equilibrium that attracts every positive trajectory in it.
3. If the ideal $(\mathcal E_G)$ is prime, then $G$ is not catalytic, so the previous two conclusions apply.

## Expansion

If $Z$ is a critical siphon, take a boundary point $z$ with zero set $Z$ that is stoichiometrically compatible with a positive point $x$. Compatibility gives a linear relation between the monomials of $z$-supported and positive states, and weak reversibility turns it into a path in the event-graph between monomials $M, N$ that share all the non-$Z$ variables. Dividing out the common factor leaves monomials in the $Z$ species alone, which cannot be connected because the absence of $Z$ is forward invariant; so the shared factor acts as a catalyst. Contrapositively, non-catalytic networks have no critical siphons, and the Angeli–De Leenheer–Sontag theorem (no critical siphons implies persistence for weakly reversible networks) gives the corollaries. The definition captures pathways such as $x + y \to p$, $p + q \to x + z$, where $x$ is regenerated: synthetic DNA strand-displacement amplifiers are catalytic in this sense and do have critical siphons.
