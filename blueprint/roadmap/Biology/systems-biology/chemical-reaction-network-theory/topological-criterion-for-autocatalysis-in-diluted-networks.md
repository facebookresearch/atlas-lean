# Topological criterion for autocatalysis in diluted reaction networks

Consider an autonomous, unambiguous reaction network on dynamical species $\mathcal{S} = \{A_1, \dots, A_n\}$ (no species is both reactant and product of the same reaction; chemostatted species are omitted), with stoichiometric matrix $S \in \mathbb{Z}^{n \times N}$ whose column $R$ records the net change of each species in reaction $R$. The network is **stoichiometrically autocatalytic** (Blokhuis–Lacoste–Nghe) if there is a reaction vector $c \in \mathbb{R}^N$ with $(Sc)_i > 0$ for every species $i$, where a reaction whose orientation is free may be used in either direction ($c_R$ of either sign) and a reaction with a prescribed forward direction must have $c_R > 0$. When every orientation is free, Gordan's alternative makes this equivalent to the absence of a nonzero **nonnegative** conservation law, $m \in \mathbb{R}^n_{\ge 0} \setminus \{0\}$ with $m^\top S = 0$; a strictly positive $m$ is not required (the columns $(-1,1,0)^\top$, $(1,-1,1)^\top$ admit $m = (1,1,0)$, so their span misses the positive orthant, yet no strictly positive vector is left-null). When the reactions split into freely oriented columns $S_F$ and forward-oriented columns $S_P$, Motzkin's alternative makes it equivalent to the absence of $m \in \mathbb{R}^n_{\ge 0} \setminus \{0\}$ with $m^\top S_F = 0$ and $m^\top S_P \le 0$.

A **diluted network** keeps only reactions with a single reactant of stoichiometry one: reversible one-to-one reactions $A_i \rightleftharpoons A_j$ and irreversible **one-to-several** reactions $A_i \to s_1 A_{i_1} + \dots + s_m A_{i_m}$ with $\sum_\ell s_\ell \ge 2$ (their reverses are dropped, being negligible at low concentration). In the stoichiometric condition the reversible reactions are freely oriented and the one-to-several reactions have their forward orientation. Its **split graph** $G(S)$ has an arrow $A \leftrightarrow B$ for each reversible reaction and arrows $A \to B_1, \dots, A \to B_m$ for each one-to-several reaction $A \to \sum s_\ell B_\ell$. Decompose $G(S)$ into strongly connected classes, partially ordered by reachability; a class is **minimal** if no arrow enters it from another class. A one-to-several reaction $A \to \sum_\ell s_\ell B_\ell$ with $A \in C$ is **internal** to the class $C$ if its truncation $A \to \sum_{\ell : B_\ell \in C} s_\ell B_\ell$ still has total product stoichiometry $\ge 2$, and **purely external** to $C$ if no $B_\ell$ lies in $C$. Mass-action kinetics linearized at zero concentration give $\dot{[A]} = M [A]$ for a generalized Markov matrix $M$ with graph $G(S)$; write $\lambda_{\max} = \max \operatorname{Re} \operatorname{spec}(M)$.

**Theorem (Unterberger–Nghe).**
1. A diluted network is stoichiometrically autocatalytic if and only if
   **(Top)**: every minimal class of $G(S)$ contains at least one internal one-to-several reaction.
2. If (Top) holds and there are neither degradation reactions $A \to \varnothing$ nor purely external one-to-several reactions, or the rates of both kinds are small enough, then the network is **weakly dynamically autocatalytic**: $\lambda_{\max} > 0$ and it is an eigenvalue of $M$ with an eigenvector $v \ge 0$, $v \ne 0$. In the reducible case this is a spectral statement only: $\lambda_{\max}$ may have nontrivial Jordan blocks, and species outside the support of $v$, or initial data with no component along $v$, need not grow at rate $\lambda_{\max}$. If moreover $G(S)$ is strongly connected (so that no reaction is purely external) and degradation rates are zero or small enough, then $\lambda_{\max} > 0$ is a simple eigenvalue with a strictly positive eigenvector $v$ and every other eigenvalue has smaller real part (**strong** dynamical autocatalysis); for every nonzero initial concentration vector $x_0 \ge 0$ the linearized solution $x(t) = e^{tM}x_0$ satisfies $e^{-\lambda_{\max} t}x(t) \to \kappa v$ with $\kappa > 0$, so all species grow at the common rate $\lambda_{\max}$.

## Expansion

Necessity: if some minimal class $C$ has no internal one-to-several reaction, then every reaction consuming a species of $C$ produces at most one copy of a species of $C$ (other products leave $C$ for downstream classes), and nothing outside $C$ produces species of $C$. Summing the rows of $S$ over $C$ gives a nonpositive combination of reactions, so $Sc$ cannot be positive on all of $C$. For sufficiency in the irreducible case, each column of $S$ has one entry $-1$ (its reactant) and nonnegative entries summing to at least $1$ in total, with strict excess for some column. Averaging, for each species $j$, the columns of the reactions consuming $j$ gives a matrix $A - I$ with $A \ge 0$ irreducible, column sums $\ge 1$ and one column sum $> 1$. Perron–Frobenius gives a positive eigenvector $c'$ of $A$ with eigenvalue $\lambda > 1$, so $(A - I)c' = (\lambda - 1)c' > 0$, and $c'$ lifts to a positive reaction vector $c$ with $Sc > 0$. The general proof perturbs the stationary flow of an auxiliary irreducible Markov chain on species, built by splitting each one-to-several reaction into one-to-one transitions. The dynamical statement uses the Perron–Frobenius theory of the generalized Markov matrix $M$ (nonnegative off-diagonal entries), whose column sums are nonnegative and positive at species with an internal one-to-several reaction, so $\lambda_{\max} > 0$. Degradation and purely external reactions subtract from the column sums of the block of $M$ belonging to a minimal class, so without a bound on their rates $\lambda_{\max}$ can be $\le 0$ even under (Top): in the source's network $A_1 \to A_2$, $A_2 \to 2A_1 + B$, $B \to mB$, $A_2 \xrightarrow{k} B$ (Equation (47), other rates $1$), which satisfies (Top), the purely external reaction $A_2 \to B$ acts on the minimal class $\{A_1, A_2\}$ as a degradation of rate $k$; for $m = 1$ and $k > 1$ both eigenvalues of that block are negative and $\lambda_{\max} = 0$, while for $k < 1$ it is positive. In the strongly connected case $M + sI$ is a nonnegative irreducible matrix with positive diagonal for large $s$, hence primitive, which gives the simple, strictly dominant eigenvalue and the convergence of $e^{-\lambda_{\max} t}e^{tM}$ to a positive rank-one projection.
