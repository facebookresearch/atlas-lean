# Injectivity precludes multiple equilibria (Craciun–Feinberg)

For a reaction network $\mathcal N$ in a continuous-flow stirred-tank reactor, with an outflow reaction for every species, let $p_{\mathcal N}(c, k)$ be the mass-action species-formation rate, a function of positive concentrations $c$ and positive rate constants $k$. The network is injective if $c \mapsto p_{\mathcal N}(c, k)$ is injective on the positive orthant for every $k$. Craciun and Feinberg (2005) prove: $\mathcal N$ is injective if and only if $\det(\partial p_{\mathcal N}/\partial c\,(c, k)) \ne 0$ for all positive $c$ and $k$ (Theorem 3.1), and if and only if all coefficients in the polynomial expansion of this determinant (in the source's sign convention) are nonnegative (Theorem 3.3). An injective network cannot have two distinct positive equilibria for any rate constants.

## Expansion

1. The model: CFSTR networks with outflow of every species, mass-action rates, and the species-formation function.
2. Theorem 3.1: the equivalence of injectivity with nonvanishing of the Jacobian determinant, via a monomial change of variables that makes the determinant polynomial in $k$.
3. Theorem 3.3: the coefficient-sign criterion, and the source's sign convention.
4. The consequence for multiple equilibria, and the extension to networks without full outflow (restricted to compatibility classes) as a separate statement.
