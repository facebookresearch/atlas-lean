# Kinetic proofreading squares the error fraction

Hopfield (1974) defines the error fraction $f$ as the rate of incorrect product formation divided by the rate of correct product formation when the correct and incorrect substrates $C$ and $D$ are present in equal concentrations. For a single Michaelis-type discrimination in which only the off-rates distinguish the substrates, the minimum error fraction is $f_0 = k_C/k_D = K_D/K_C = \exp(-\Delta G_{CD}/RT)$ (Eqs. [2]–[3]). In the proofreading scheme (Eqs. [4], [8], [9]) the enzyme–substrate complex is converted by a substrate-insensitive step, driven by an energy source such as nucleoside triphosphate hydrolysis, into a high-energy intermediate, which either goes on to product at rate $W$ or releases the substrate with the same off-rate discrimination. Without the drive the error fraction is never below $f_0$ (constraint [6]). When the drive removes that constraint, and (i) the first complex nearly equilibrates, (ii) incorrect substrate reaches the high-energy intermediate mainly through the driven step, (iii) the intermediate is lost mainly by discard rather than by product formation or reversal, and (iv) the driven step is pushed out of equilibrium by a factor of at least $1/f_0$ (Eq. [10]), the error fraction approaches $f_0^2$, the value of a double discrimination. Stacking $n$ such driven stages in sequence reaches discrimination levels $f_0^{n+1}$, as the source notes.

## Expansion

1. The model: the single discrimination scheme [1] and the two-stage scheme [4] with mass action kinetics, and the error fraction.
2. The steady-state error fraction [5] and the equilibrium constraint [6], under which $f \ge f_0$.
3. The driven scheme [8]–[9], the four rate conditions and inequality [10], and the limit $f \to f_0^2$.
4. The free-energy cost of proofreading and the stacked $n$-stage generalization.
