# Deficiency one theorem

Consider a mass action system whose reaction network has $\ell$ linkage classes, each containing just one terminal strong linkage class. Let $\delta$ be the [deficiency](../../../Dynamics/smooth-dynamics/deficiency.md) of the network and $\delta_1, \dots, \delta_\ell$ the deficiencies of the individual linkage classes, and suppose that (i) $\delta_\theta \le 1$ for $\theta = 1, \dots, \ell$ and (ii) $\sum_{\theta=1}^{\ell} \delta_\theta = \delta$. Feinberg's Deficiency One Theorem (Feinberg 1987, Theorem 6.2.1) states: no matter what positive values the rate constants take, the differential equations admit no more than one steady state within a positive [stoichiometric compatibility class](../../../Dynamics/smooth-dynamics/stoichiometric-subspace.md); and if the network is weakly reversible, they admit precisely one steady state in each positive stoichiometric compatibility class. For a network with one linkage class condition (ii) holds trivially, so multiple positive steady states in a compatibility class require the deficiency or the number of terminal strong linkage classes to exceed one (Corollary 6.2.2). The source states that the proof appears in a separate article of Feinberg.

## Expansion

1. Definitions: complexes, linkage classes, strong and terminal strong linkage classes, the network and linkage-class deficiencies, and stoichiometric compatibility classes (Sections 2.4–2.9 of the source).
2. Condition (ii) as the statement that the stoichiometric subspace is the direct sum of the stoichiometric subspaces of the linkage classes (condition (5.45) of the source).
3. Uniqueness of a positive steady state in each compatibility class under (i) and (ii).
4. Existence for weakly reversible networks, compared with the [deficiency-zero theorem, weakly reversible case](../../../Biology/systems-biology/complex-balancing-and-the-deficiency-zero-theore/exists-unique-equilibrium.md).
5. Corollary 6.2.2 and the worked examples of Section 6.2 of the source, including networks of deficiency two to which the theorem applies.
