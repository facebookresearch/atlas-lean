# Deficiency, persistence and periodic entrainment of generalized ribosome flow models

**Model.** A compartmental graph $D = (Q, A)$ has compartments $q_1, \dots, q_m$ with capacities $c_i$ and directed transitions $(q_i, q_j) \in A$ on an arbitrary network (the classical ribosome flow model is a path, the ribosome flow model on a ring a cycle). Its kinetic representation is the chemical reaction network with species $N_i$ (occupied sites) and $S_i$ (free sites) and one reaction per transition,

$$N_i + S_j \xrightarrow{\ \mathcal{K}_{ij}\ } N_j + S_i,$$

with a nonnegative, possibly time-varying rate function $\mathcal{K}_{ij}(n, s, t)$. Since $n_i + s_i = c_i$ is conserved, the dynamics reduces to

$$\dot n_i = \sum_{j \in \mathcal{D}_i}\mathcal{K}_{ji}(n, c - n, t) - \sum_{j \in \mathcal{R}_i}\mathcal{K}_{ij}(n, c - n, t),$$

summing over donors $\mathcal{D}_i$ and receivers $\mathcal{R}_i$ of $q_i$. Let $|D|$ be the undirected graph of $D$ with parallel edges merged.

**Theorem.**
1. *(Deficiency.)* The deficiency of the reaction network assigned to $D$ equals the number of chordless cycles of $|D|$; in particular it is $0$ when $|D|$ is a forest.
2. *(Persistence.)* If $D$ is strongly connected, every siphon of the network contains both $N_i$ and $S_i$ for some compartment, or all of $N_1, \dots, N_m$, or all of $S_1, \dots, S_m$; each therefore supports a positive conservation law, and if the rates are sandwiched between time-independent, monotone mass-action-like bounds, the dynamics is persistent (no trajectory from the interior approaches the boundary).
3. *(Entrainment.)* Suppose the rates have the form $\mathcal{K}_{ij}(n,s,t)=k_{ij}(t)\,\theta_i(n_i)\nu_j(s_j)/\big(1+\Psi_{ij}(n,s)\big)$, where $\theta_i,\nu_j\in C^1$ are nondecreasing with $\theta_i(0)=\nu_j(0)=0$ and $\int_0^1|\log\theta_i|,\int_0^1|\log\nu_j|<\infty$, each $\Psi_{ij}$ is a nonnegative combination of monomials in the $\theta_l(n_l)$ and $\nu_l(s_l)$, and $0<\underline k_{ij}\le k_{ij}(t)\le\overline k_{ij}$. Assume the $k_{ij}$ are $C^1$ and periodic with a common period $T$ (phases may differ), and write the reduced system as $\dot n=F(t,n)$. Assume the cooperativity condition $F_i(t,x)\le F_i(t,y)$ whenever $x\ne y$ lie in the reduced state space with $x_i=y_i$ and $x_j\le y_j$ for $j\ne i$; it holds, for example, when $\Psi_{ij}\equiv0$ (no inhibition), and can fail otherwise, since a denominator depending on $n_l$ makes $\mathcal{K}_{ij}$ decrease in $n_l$. Then on each level set $L_r$ of the total occupancy there is a unique $T$-periodic solution $\phi_r$, and every solution in $L_r$ converges to it in $L^1$: $\lim_{t\to\infty}\|\rho(t,a) - \phi_r(t)\|_{L^1} = 0$ for $a \in L_r$.
4. *(Input-to-state stability.)* Mass-conservative systems in this class, with rates driven by external inputs, are uniformly input-to-state stable with respect to the corresponding input-value set; for strongly connected structures, an entropy-like logarithmic function is a Lyapunov function.

## Expansion

Complexes of the network are the pairs $N_i + S_j$ and $N_j + S_i$, one pair per edge of $|D|$; linkage classes and the rank of the stoichiometric matrix can be read off the graph, and the deficiency counts independent cycles not generated by shorter ones, namely chordless cycles. Siphons are classified using strong connectivity: if a siphon contains $N_i$, the reactions producing $N_i$ force it to contain either $S_k$ or $N_k$ for each donor, which propagates around the graph. The cooperativity condition makes the reduced system cooperative and $L^1$-nonexpansive on each conservation class, and a common period turns this into contraction to a unique periodic orbit.
