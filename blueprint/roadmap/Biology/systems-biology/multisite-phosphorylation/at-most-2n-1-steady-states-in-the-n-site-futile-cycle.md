# Number of steady states of the multisite futile cycle

For the mass action model $\Sigma(\kappa, C)$ of the $n$-site sequential distributive phosphorylation–dephosphorylation cycle, with kinetic constants $\kappa$ and conserved totals $C = (E_{tot}, F_{tot}, S_{tot})$ of kinase, phosphatase and substrate, Wang and Sontag (2008, Theorem 3) prove that for every $\kappa$ and $C$ the system has at most $2n - 1$ positive steady states; for $n = 2$ this gives at most three. They also prove (Theorem 1) that for each $S_{tot}$ and ratio of the enzyme totals there are kinetic constants and a threshold such that, when the enzyme totals are small enough relative to the substrate, the system has at least $n + 1$ positive steady states for $n$ even and at least $n$ for $n$ odd; that near the Michaelis–Menten quasi-steady-state regime there are at most $n + 1$ (Theorem 4); and that when enzyme is in excess there is at most one (Theorem 5). The bound is obtained by reducing the steady-state equations to the positive roots of a single polynomial in one variable.

## Expansion

1. The model: the $n$-site cycle, its mass action equations and the three conservation laws.
2. The reduction of positive steady states to positive roots of a one-variable polynomial of degree $2n + 1$, and the count giving at most $2n - 1$ (proof of Theorem 3).
3. The lower bound (Theorem 1) by perturbation of a polynomial with prescribed roots, and the regime bounds (Theorems 4 and 5).
4. Whether the bound $2n - 1$ is attained for every $n$ is the question of [maximal multistationarity of multisite phosphorylation](../../../Biology/systems-biology/maximal-multistationarity-of-multisite-phosphorylation.md).
