# A common scaling of F, V and CL leaves the oral curve unchanged

For real `F`, `D`, `ka`, `V`, `CL` and `t` with `V ≠ 0` and distinct rates `ka ≠ CL / V`, and every
`s ≠ 0`, the oral concentration curve
`C(t) = F * D * ka * (exp (-(CL / V) * t) - exp (-ka * t)) / (V * (ka - CL / V))` is unchanged by
the common scaling `(F, V, CL) ↦ (F * s, V * s, CL * s)`, which keeps the elimination rate `CL / V`.
So the scale is not identified from oral curves alone; in the model, admissible scalings have
`s > 0` and `F * s ≤ 1`, and the absorption rate `ka > 0` is known.
