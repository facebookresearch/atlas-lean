# The fifteen-qubit quantum Reed–Muller code

For a function $f : \mathbb{F}_2^4 \to \mathbb{F}_2$ let $[f] \in \mathbb{F}_2^{15}$ be the table of its values at the
fifteen nonzero points $0001, 0010, 0011, \dots, 1111$. Let $L_1 \subseteq \mathbb{F}_2^{15}$ be the span of
$[x_1], \dots, [x_4]$ (the linear functions, dimension $4$) and $L_2$ the span of the $[x_i]$ and the six
$[x_i x_j]$, $i < j$ (polynomials of degree at most $2$ vanishing at $0$, dimension $10$); these are shortened
Reed–Muller codes of orders $1$ and $2$ (restricted to $f(0) = 0$ and then with the coordinate $0$ deleted; puncturing alone
would keep the all-ones word and give dimensions $5$ and $11$), and $L_1^{\perp}$ is the $15$-bit Hamming code.

The fifteen-qubit code is the stabilizer code on fifteen qubits whose stabilizer is generated by $X^u$, $u \in L_1$, and
$Z^v$, $v \in L_2$. Every word of $L_1$ has weight divisible by $8$, every word of $L_2$ has even weight, and
$L_2^{\perp} = L_1 \oplus \langle 1 \rangle$, where $1$ is the all-ones word; in particular $L_1 \subseteq L_2^{\perp}$, so
the generators commute, and there are $4 + 10 = 14$ independent generators: the code encodes one qubit. Bravyi and
Kitaev take $X^{\otimes 15}$ and $-Z^{\otimes 15}$ as its logical $X$ and $Z$. As a CSS code it is $Q_{C_1, C_2}$ with
$C_2 = L_1$ and $C_1 = L_2^{\perp}$, and by the [CSS distance formula](../css-codes/distance-of-a-css-code.md) it has
distance $3$, the least weight of a Hamming codeword outside $L_2$.
