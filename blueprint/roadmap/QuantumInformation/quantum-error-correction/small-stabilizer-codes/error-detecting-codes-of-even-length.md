# The $[[2m, 2m - 2, 2]]$ error-detecting codes

Let $n = 2m$ with $m \ge 1$. The stabilizer group generated by $X^{\otimes n}$ and $Z^{\otimes n}$, which commute because
$n$ is even, has a code space of dimension $2^{n-2}$, spanned by the states $(|x\rangle + |\bar x\rangle)/\sqrt{2}$ for even-weight
strings $x$ (where $\bar x$ is the complement of $x$, and $x$ and $\bar x$ give the same state). No single-qubit Pauli operator
commutes with both generators, so every single-qubit error is detected.

For $m \ge 2$ it is an $[[n, n - 2, 2]]$ code: the distance is at least $2$ by the above, and at most $2$ since $Z_1 Z_2$
commutes with both generators and is not in the stabilizer $\{I, X^{\otimes n}, Z^{\otimes n}, X^{\otimes n} Z^{\otimes n}\}$.
For $m = 2$ this is the $[[4, 2, 2]]$ code.

For $m = 1$ the code space is one-dimensional, spanned by the Bell state $(|00\rangle + |11\rangle)/\sqrt 2$. The detection
distance of the $[[n, k, d]]$ page does not apply to a one-dimensional code, for which the compression of every operator $E$ is a scalar,
$P E P = \langle \psi | E | \psi \rangle P$. With the convention used for one-dimensional stabilizer codes, the minimum weight of
a nonidentity element of the stabilizer, it is a $[[2, 0, 2]]$ code: the stabilizer elements other than $I$ are
$X_1 X_2$, $Z_1 Z_2$ and $-Y_1 Y_2$, all of weight $2$.
