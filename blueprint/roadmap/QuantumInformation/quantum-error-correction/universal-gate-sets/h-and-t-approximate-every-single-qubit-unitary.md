# $H$ and $T$ approximate every single-qubit unitary

Let $T = \operatorname{diag}(1, e^{i\pi/4})$. For every $U \in SU(2)$ and every $\varepsilon > 0$ there are a finite
product $W$ of the gates $H$ and $T$ and a phase $\theta$ with $\| U - e^{i\theta} W \| < \varepsilon$. Equivalently,
the subgroup of $SU(2)$ generated by the determinant-one multiples $iH$ and $e^{-i\pi/8} T$ is dense in $SU(2)$.

Proof (Boykin, Mor, Pulver, Roychowdhury and Vatan): up to phases, $T^{*} H T H$ and a conjugate of it by
$\sqrt{H}$ are rotations of the Bloch sphere by the same angle $2\pi\lambda$ about two orthogonal axes $n_1$, $n_2$, with
$\cos \pi\lambda = \cos^2 (\pi/8)$. The number $\lambda$ is irrational: $e^{2\pi i \lambda}$ is a root of
$x^4 + x^3 + \tfrac14 x^2 + x + 1$, which is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$ and hence its minimal polynomial; a root of unity
has a cyclotomic minimal polynomial, with integer coefficients, so $e^{2\pi i \lambda}$ is not a root of unity. Hence powers of each rotation
approximate every rotation about its axis; every element of $SU(2)$ is a product of rotations about $n_1$, $n_2$, $n_1$
(Euler angles).

## Expansion

The proof uses two results that need their own pages before this page is ready: the density of the orbit of an irrational
rotation of the circle, which gives that the powers of a rotation by $2\pi\lambda$ with $\lambda$ irrational approximate every
rotation about the same axis; and the Euler decomposition, that every element of $SU(2)$ is a product of rotations about
two orthogonal axes in the order $n_1$, $n_2$, $n_1$.
