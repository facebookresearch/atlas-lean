# Canonical form of Clifford circuits

Call two unitary circuits of CNOT, Hadamard and phase gates on $n$ qubits equivalent if they act identically on every
[stabilizer state](../../../TheoryOfComputation/randomized-and-quantum-complexity/bounded-error-quantum-polynomial-time-bqp/stabilizer-groups-and-stabilizer-states.md);
by linearity, equivalent circuits implement the same unitary up to a global phase. Every such circuit is equivalent to one
in *canonical form*: eleven rounds, each consisting of gates of a single type, in the order
H–C–P–C–P–C–H–P–C–P–C (H for Hadamard, C for CNOT, P for phase gates). The proof reduces the $2n \times 2n$ tableau of
destabilizer and stabilizer rows of the circuit's action to the identity by a fixed sequence of such rounds, using that a
binary symmetric matrix can be made a Gram matrix $M M^T$ by phase gates.

A consequence: since the reduction uses only the commutation relations of the tableau rows (the first $n$ rows commute pairwise, rows $n + 1, \dots, 2n$ commute
pairwise, row $h$ anticommutes with row $h + n$, and row $i$ commutes with row $h + n$ for $i \ne h$), every tableau with
these relations, that is every binary symplectic matrix together with any choice of signs, is the tableau of some
Clifford circuit.
