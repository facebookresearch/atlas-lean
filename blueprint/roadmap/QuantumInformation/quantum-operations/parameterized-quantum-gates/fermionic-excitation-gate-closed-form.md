# Closed form of fermionic excitation gates

A unitary generated by a fermionic excitation generator is a cosine–sine rotation between each occupation state and its partner configuration, and the identity on the generator's null space.

Let $G$ be a Hermitian operator on a finite-dimensional Hilbert space with $G^3=G$, and $P_0=\mathbf 1-G^2$. Then for all $\theta\in\mathbb R$

$$
U(\theta)=e^{-i\frac{\theta}{2}G}=\cos\frac\theta2\,\mathbf 1-i\sin\frac\theta2\,G+\Bigl(1-\cos\frac\theta2\Bigr)P_0 .
$$

Hence $U(\theta)\Phi=\Phi$ if $P_0\Phi=\Phi$, and $U(\theta)\Phi=\bigl(\cos\frac\theta2-i\sin\frac\theta2\,G\bigr)\Phi$ if $P_0\Phi=0$. For an $n$-fold fermionic excitation generator ($2n$ distinct indices), $-iG$ has real entries in the occupation basis, so on an occupation state outside the null space $U(\theta)$ produces a real superposition of that state and its partner configuration, the $n$-fold excitation or de-excitation of it.

## Context

Graduate/research. This closed form is the building block of the encoding-independent gradient rules of Kottmann, Anand and Aspuru-Guzik for unitary coupled-cluster circuits. The identity for operators with $G^3=G$ is generic matrix-function algebra; the chemistry content is its application to excitation generators.

## Expansion

From $G^3=G$: $G^{2k+1}=G$ and $G^{2k}=G^2=\mathbf 1-P_0$ for $k\ge1$. Summing the matrix exponential series separately over even and odd powers gives $\mathbf 1+(\cos\frac\theta2-1)(\mathbf 1-P_0)-i\sin\frac\theta2\,G$, which rearranges to the formula. The action on states follows from $GP_0=0$; realness of $-iG=A-A^\dagger$ holds because $A$ has entries $0,\pm1$ in the occupation basis.
