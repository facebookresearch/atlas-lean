# Synchronous values of the 3-coloring game

The 3-coloring game of a graph asks two players, each given a vertex, to answer colors that agree on equal vertices and differ on adjacent ones; its synchronous value in a model $t$ is the best winning probability of a synchronous $t$-correlation.

**The game.** Let $G = (V, E)$ be a finite simple undirected graph without loops. The 3-coloring game $\operatorname{Hom}(G, K_3)$ has question set $V$, answer set $\{1, 2, 3\}$, and rule function

$$
\lambda(a, b, x, y) = \begin{cases} 0 & \text{if } x = y \text{ and } a \ne b, \\ 0 & \text{if } x \sim y \text{ and } a = b, \\ 1 & \text{otherwise,} \end{cases}
$$

where $x \sim y$ means $(x, y) \in E$. The questions are drawn from $\pi_{\mathrm{edges}}$, the uniform distribution on ordered pairs forming edges: $\pi_{\mathrm{edges}}(x, y) = 1/(2|E|)$ if $(x, y) \in E$ and $0$ otherwise; this requires $G$ to have at least one edge.

**Synchronous values.** A correlation $p = (p(a, b \mid x, y))$ is *synchronous* when $p(a, b \mid x, x) = 0$ for all $x$ and $a \ne b$. For $t \in \{q, qa, qc\}$ let $C_t^s(n, m)$ be the synchronous correlations with $n$ questions and $m$ answers in the [quantum, quantum-approximate or commuting-operator correlation set](../../tsirelson-problem-and-connes-embedding/quantum-correlation-sets-q-qs-qa-qc.md), and let $C_{\mathrm{loc}}^s(n, m)$ be the synchronous correlations among the local ones, the convex combinations of deterministic correlations $p(a, b \mid x, y) = [a = f(x)]\,[b = g(y)]$ for maps $f, g$ from questions to answers. The *synchronous $t$-value* of a game $\mathcal{G}$ with question distribution $\pi$ is

$$
\omega_t^s(\mathcal{G}, \pi) = \sup \Big\{ \sum_{x, y} \pi(x, y) \sum_{a, b} \lambda(a, b, x, y)\, p(a, b \mid x, y) : p \in C_t^s(n, m) \Big\}.
$$

Synchronicity here is a condition on the rule and on correlations only, as in the source: the rule rejects different answers to equal questions, and $\pi_{\mathrm{edges}}$ gives no weight to equal questions, since $G$ has no loops. The [synchronous two-prover games](../../../../TheoryOfComputation/randomized-and-quantum-complexity/interactive-proofs/entangled-provers-and-the-halting-problem/synchronous-two-prover-games.md) of the linked page also ask the question distribution to weight each pair $(x, x)$ positively, which $\pi_{\mathrm{edges}}$ does not. A synchronous correlation in $C_t^s(n, m)$ is given by a tracial state on the algebra generated by projection-valued measures $\{e_{a,x}\}_a$, one for each question $x$; this is the description of synchronous correlations used by the source.
