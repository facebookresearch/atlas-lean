# Bond comminution energy

Take as a stipulated empirical law Bond's specific energy $E = 10\,W_i\bigl(1/\sqrt{P_{80}} - 1/\sqrt{F_{80}}\bigr)$, in kWh per tonne,
with work index $W_i > 0$ expressed in the same kWh-per-metric-tonne units, and positive 80%-passing sizes $F_{80}$ (feed) and $P_{80}$ (product) in micrometres. Then $E \ge 0$ exactly
when $P_{80} \le F_{80}$, and two stages $F_{80} \to M_{80} \to P_{80}$ with the same work index use energies adding to that of the
single reduction $F_{80} \to P_{80}$. The coefficient $10$ belongs to micrometres: with sizes in metres the same law reads
$E = 0.01\,W_i\bigl(1/\sqrt{P_{80}} - 1/\sqrt{F_{80}}\bigr)$. The cited source instead expresses both $E$ and $W_i$ per US short ton; convert both to the metric-tonne basis by multiplying
by $1000/907.18474$ before using them here. The empirical validity of the law, size-distribution bookkeeping and
fragmentation models are outside this result.
