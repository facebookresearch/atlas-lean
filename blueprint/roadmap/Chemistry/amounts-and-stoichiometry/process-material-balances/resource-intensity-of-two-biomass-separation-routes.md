# Resource intensity of two biomass-separation routes

Consider a stipulated toy account: dry feed of composition $(1/2, 1/2)$ in two nonreacting pseudo-components, the main product $P$
and the coproduct $C$, separated completely by route A at throughput $u \ge 0$ or route B at throughput $v \ge 0$, measured in
kg/h, giving $(u+v)/2$ kg/h of each product. Fix the main-product rate at 1 kg/h, hence $u+v=2$, and report intensity per kg of
main product. Each route consumes 1 MJ per kg of feed: route A takes in $u$ MJ/h of heat, route B takes in $v$ MJ/h of work,
and the account rejects $(u+v)$ MJ/h of heat. Equal feed/product component specific enthalpies and zero mixing enthalpy make
stream-enthalpy changes zero. With zero storage, kinetic and potential changes, the net heat input and outward shaft work
are both $-v$ MJ/h, so their difference is zero. With feed burden $1$
per unit of feed and burden factors $c$ and $1 - c$ for heat and work, one shared scenario parameter $c \in [0, 1]$, the total burden is
$B = (1 + c)u + (2 - c)v$, and with mass allocation $1/2$ to each product the main-product intensity is $B/2$. The plans $(2, 0)$ and
$(0, 2)$ have intensities $1 + c$ and $2 - c$. This is an exact account of a declared model, not measured yields, reaction chemistry,
equipment feasibility or a life-cycle assessment.
