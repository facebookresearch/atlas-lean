# Rydberg formula for inverse transition wavelengths

In the three-dimensional Coulomb model (a particle of mass `m > 0` in the potential
`V(x) = -k / ‖x‖` on `ℝ³`, with levels `E_n` counted from `n = 0`), the signed inverse wavelength of
the transition from level `n₂` to level `n₁` is `(R_ν / c) * (1 / (n₁ + 1) ^ 2 - 1 / (n₂ + 1) ^ 2)`:
the Rydberg wavenumber for the principal quantum numbers `n₁ + 1` and `n₂ + 1`, in this order, with
`Z = 1`. Here `R_ν = m * k ^ 2 / (2 * ħ ^ 2 * h)` is the Rydberg frequency and `c` the speed of
light.

The wavelength is `c / ν` with `ν = (E_{n₂} - E_{n₁}) / h`, so for `k ≠ 0` the inverse wavelength is
positive for `n₁ < n₂` (emission) and negative for `n₂ < n₁`. For `n₁ = n₂`, `ν = 0`, the wavelength
is `c / 0 = 0` by the convention `x / 0 = 0`, and both sides are `0`.
