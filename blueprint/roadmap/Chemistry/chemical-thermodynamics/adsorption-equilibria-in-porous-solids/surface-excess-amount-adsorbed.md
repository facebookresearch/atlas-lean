# Surface excess amount adsorbed

The surface excess amount is the gas introduced minus the gas a homogeneous gas phase would hold up to the Gibbs dividing surface; adding $c^gV^a$ gives the amount adsorbed.

Let $n$ be the total amount of adsorptive introduced into a closed adsorption system, and let a Gibbs dividing surface
bound a volume $V^g$ filled by the homogeneous gas phase at concentration $c^g$, so that this gas phase holds
$n^g = c^gV^g$. The surface excess amount is

$$
n^\sigma = n - n^g.
$$

When the dividing surface coincides with the adsorbing surface and the adsorption space has volume $V^a$, the amount
adsorbed is

$$
n^a = n^\sigma + c^gV^a.
$$

Manometric and gravimetric experiments measure $n^\sigma$. For vapours below about $0.1$ MPa, with the dividing surface
close to the adsorbent surface and the void volume (or buoyancy) determined accurately, $n^a$ and $n^\sigma$ are nearly
equal; at higher pressures the conversion needs $V^a$, which a skeletal or pore volume determines only together with
the geometry of the adsorbent. Because $c^gV^a$ grows with pressure, the excess can pass through a maximum and
decrease.

## Context

Graduate level. Excess versus total (absolute) uptake is the bookkeeping behind high-pressure H2 and CH4 capacities reported for metal–organic frameworks.

## Expansion

The decomposition is a definition; whether the excess has a maximum depends on how $n^a$ and $c^gV^a$ grow with
pressure.
