# Deliverable adsorption capacity

The deliverable (usable) capacity of an adsorbent store is the uptake at the charging pressure minus what stays adsorbed at the discharge pressure.

Let $q(p,T)$ be the equilibrium uptake of a gas at pressure $p$ and temperature $T$. For a store charged at
$p_{\max}$ and emptied down to $p_{\min}$ with $0 \le p_{\min} < p_{\max}$, both at temperature $T$, the
deliverable capacity is

$$
k(p_{\max}, p_{\min}, T) = q(p_{\max}, T) - q(p_{\min}, T).
$$

For a temperature–pressure swing from $(p_{\max}, T_1)$ to $(p_{\min}, T_2)$ the second term is evaluated at $T_2$.
For a nondecreasing isotherm $k \ge 0$; gas adsorbed below $p_{\min}$ is unusable.

## Context

Graduate level. Deliverable capacity, not total uptake, is the figure of merit for vehicular H2 and natural-gas storage in metal–organic frameworks.
