# Standard cell potential and the equilibrium constant

The standard cell potential determines the standard molar reaction Gibbs energy and the equilibrium constant of the cell reaction: $\Delta_rG^\circ=-zFE^\circ_{\mathrm{cell}}$ and $\ln K=zFE^\circ_{\mathrm{cell}}/(RT)$.

For a cell reaction with electron number $z>0$ at temperature $T>0$, let $E^\circ_{\mathrm{cell}}$ be its
[standard cell potential](cell-reaction-and-its-electron-number.md) (unit activities, liquid junction potential
set to zero), let $\Delta_rG^\circ$ be its [standard molar reaction Gibbs energy](../reaction-gibbs-energy-and-equilibrium-constants/molar-reaction-gibbs-energy.md)
and let $K$ be its [thermodynamic equilibrium constant](../reaction-gibbs-energy-and-equilibrium-constants/reaction-quotient-and-equilibrium-constant.md),
all with the same standard states. Then

$$\Delta_rG^\circ=-zFE^\circ_{\mathrm{cell}},\qquad \ln K=\frac{zF}{RT}E^\circ_{\mathrm{cell}} .$$

In particular $K>1$ exactly when $E^\circ_{\mathrm{cell}}>0$. At $T=298.15$ K, $RT/F\approx0.02569$ V.

## Context

Undergraduate electrochemistry. A volt of standard potential corresponds to a very large equilibrium constant ($\ln K\approx39z$ at 298.15 K); how far a real cell reaction proceeds also depends on its initial composition and on competing processes.

## Expansion

In the hypothetical standard cell every activity is one and the junction potential is zero, so $\Delta_rG_{\mathrm{cell}}=\Delta_rG^\circ$ and the cell relation gives $\Delta_rG^\circ=-zFE^\circ_{\mathrm{cell}}$ (DeVoe Eq. 14.3.15). Substituting $\Delta_rG^\circ=-RT\ln K$ (Eq. 11.8.10) gives Eq. 14.3.16.
