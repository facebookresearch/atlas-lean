# Standard electrode potential

The standard electrode potential $E^\circ$ of an electrode is the standard cell potential of the cell with a standard hydrogen electrode on the left and that electrode on the right; the standard hydrogen electrode has $E^\circ=0$ at every temperature.

A **standard hydrogen electrode** is a hydrogen electrode in which $\mathrm H_2(\mathrm g)$ and
$\mathrm H^+(\mathrm{aq})$ are in their standard states (unit activity of $\mathrm H^+$, hydrogen at the standard
pressure $p^\circ=10^5$ Pa behaving ideally); it is a hypothetical electrode. The **standard electrode
potential** $E^\circ$ of an electrode (half-cell) is the
[standard cell potential](cell-reaction-and-its-electron-number.md) of the cell whose left electrode is the
standard hydrogen electrode and whose right electrode is the electrode in question, so that the cell
reaction is the reduction at that electrode coupled with the oxidation of $\mathrm H_2$; for $\mathrm M^{z+}/\mathrm M$
it refers to $\mathrm M^{z+}+\tfrac z2\mathrm H_2(\mathrm g)\to\mathrm M+z\,\mathrm H^+$ and is written $E^\circ(\mathrm M^{z+}/\mathrm M)$.
By construction $E^\circ=0$ for the hydrogen electrode at all temperatures, and every $E^\circ$ is a
function of temperature only. The definition is limited to aqueous solutions.

## Context

Undergraduate electrochemistry; tables of standard electrode potentials are the starting point for the voltage of any proposed battery couple.

## Expansion

A definition. The hydrogen electrode value is zero because a cell with standard hydrogen electrodes on both sides has zero standard cell potential.
