# Cell reaction, electron number and cell potential

A galvanic cell couples an oxidation at its left electrode and a reduction at its right electrode, each with the same electron number $z=|\nu_e|$; their sum is the cell reaction, whose advancement $d\xi$ passes the charge $-zF\,d\xi$ into the right terminal.

A galvanic cell is described by a cell diagram whose left electrode reaction is written as an oxidation
producing electrons and whose right electrode reaction is written as a reduction consuming them, both
scaled so that the electron has stoichiometric number $\pm z$ with the same positive **electron number**
$z=|\nu_e|$. Their sum, in which the electrons cancel, is the **cell reaction**, with stoichiometric numbers
$\nu_i$ for the chemical species $i$. As the cell reaction advances by $d\xi$, the charge entering the right
terminal is

$$dQ_{\mathrm{sys}}=-zF\,d\xi .$$

Its **molar reaction Gibbs energy** is $\Delta_rG_{\mathrm{cell}}=\sum_i\nu_i\mu_i$ with each $\mu_i$ evaluated in the
phase, at the electric potential, where species $i$ actually is. The **cell potential** is
$E_{\mathrm{cell}}=\phi_{\mathrm R}-\phi_{\mathrm L}$, the potential of the right terminal minus that of an identical
left terminal; its zero-current value with all local charge-transfer and chemical equilibria established
is the **equilibrium cell potential** $E_{\mathrm{cell,eq}}$. The **standard cell potential** $E^\circ_{\mathrm{cell}}$
is $E_{\mathrm{cell,eq}}$ of the hypothetical cell in which every reactant and product of the cell reaction is
in its standard state and any liquid junction potential is taken to be zero (DeVoe: negligible).

## Context

Undergraduate electrochemistry. Every battery model, organic or inorganic, starts from this bookkeeping: the electron number fixes the charge stored per unit advancement, the basis of the theoretical specific capacity.

## Expansion

A definition. The charge relation follows because $z\,d\xi$ moles of electrons, each mole of charge $-F$, enter the right electrode through its terminal when the cell reaction advances by $d\xi$ (DeVoe Eq. 14.1.1).
