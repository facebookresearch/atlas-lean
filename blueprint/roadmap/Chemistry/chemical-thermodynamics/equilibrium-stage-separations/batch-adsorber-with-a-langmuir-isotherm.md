# Batch adsorber with a Langmuir isotherm

Let a liquid of volume $V > 0$ and initial solute concentration $c_0 \ge 0$ contact an initially unloaded adsorbent of mass $m \ge 0$ until
equilibrium, with the Langmuir isotherm $q(c) = q_{\max} K c/(1 + K c)$ on $c \ge 0$, $q_{\max} > 0$ and $K \ge 0$. The balance
$V(c_0 - c) = m\,q(c)$ has exactly one root $c \in [0, c_0]$, because its left side decreases and its right side increases in $c$,
with the signs at the endpoints; the fractional occupancy $q(c)/q_{\max}$ lies in $[0, 1)$. If $K = 0$ or $m = 0$, the root is
$c = c_0$ and nothing is adsorbed. Competitive and nonideal adsorption are outside this result. The concentration-form law above is a stipulated liquid-phase model,
with $K$ in inverse-concentration units. The cited Equation 31.3 uses gas partial pressure; no identification of pressure with
concentration, or derivation of that conversion, is claimed here.
