# Shifting to a measurable random time is measurable

Let $S$ be a measurable space, and give step sequences $\omega = (\omega_m)_{m \in \mathbb{N}}$,
$\omega_m = (\xi_m, E_m) \in S^S \times \mathbb{R}$, the product σ-algebra built from the product
σ-algebra on maps $S \to S$ and the Borel σ-algebra on $\mathbb{R}$. Let $\tau$ be a measurable
function from step sequences to $\mathbb{N} \cup \{\infty\}$: every level set $\{\tau = n\}$,
$n \in \mathbb{N} \cup \{\infty\}$, is measurable. Then the map $\omega \mapsto \theta_\tau \omega$
to the [steps after $\tau$](shift-at.md), the shift of $\omega$ by $\tau(\omega)$ steps (by $0$
steps where $\tau(\omega) = \infty$), is measurable. No stopping-time property of $\tau$ is needed.
