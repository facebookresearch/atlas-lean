# A rate-one exponential clock is almost surely nonnegative

Let $\mathrm{Exp}(1)$ be the exponential law with rate $1$: the probability measure on
$\mathbb{R}$ with density $e^{-x}$ for $x \ge 0$ and $0$ for $x < 0$ with respect to Lebesgue
measure. Then $x \ge 0$ for $\mathrm{Exp}(1)$-almost every real $x$; equivalently,
$\mathrm{Exp}(1)\bigl((-\infty, 0)\bigr) = 0$. Since $\mathrm{Exp}(1)$ is the gamma law
$\Gamma(1, 1)$ with shape $1$ and rate $1$, this is the case $a = b = 1$ of the
[concentration of the gamma law on the nonnegative reals](../../../Probability/distributions/continuous/gamma-distribution/gamma-moments/ae-nonneg-gamma-measure.md).
