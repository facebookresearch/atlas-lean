# A visit makes the visit counts at all later steps strictly larger

Let $S$ be a set, $x_0, x \in S$ and $\omega$ a step sequence, and write $N_n = N_n(x_0, x, \omega)$
for the [number of visits](n-visits.md) to $x_0$ before step $n$ of the jump chain $Y$ started at
$x$. If the chain is at $x_0$ at step $m$, $Y_m(x, \omega) = x_0$, then

$$N_m < N_n \qquad \text{for every } n > m .$$
