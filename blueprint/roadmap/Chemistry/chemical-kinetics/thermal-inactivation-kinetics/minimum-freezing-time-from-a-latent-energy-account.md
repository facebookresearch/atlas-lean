# Minimum freezing time from a latent-energy account

For a lumped sample of mass $m > 0$ cooled from $T_0$ through the freezing point $T_f$ to $T_{\mathrm{end}}$, with
$T_0 \ge T_f \ge T_{\mathrm{end}}$, assume a constant-pressure enthalpy account with constant liquid and solid heat capacities
$c_l,c_s>0$, latent enthalpy $L>0$, no shaft work and negligible kinetic and potential energy changes. In this account the heat to remove is
$Q = m\,[c_l(T_0 - T_f) + L + c_s(T_f - T_{\mathrm{end}})]$. If the net rate of heat removal $P$ satisfies $0 \le P \le P_{\max}$,
$P_{\max} > 0$, and integrates to $Q$, the duration is at least $Q/P_{\max}$. $P_{\max}$ bounds heat extraction, not electrical input,
which would need a coefficient of performance and heat leaks. Free-boundary freezing models are outside this result.
