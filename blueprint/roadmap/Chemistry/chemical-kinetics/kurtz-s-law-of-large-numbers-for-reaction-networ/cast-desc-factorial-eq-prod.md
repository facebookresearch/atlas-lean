# Falling factorial as a product of real factors

For natural numbers $n$ and $k$, the falling factorial
$n^{\underline{k}} = n (n - 1) \cdots (n - k + 1)$, a natural number, is the real product

$$n^{\underline{k}} = \prod_{i=0}^{k-1} (n - i)$$

with the subtractions taken in the real numbers. Both sides are $1$ for $k = 0$ and $0$ for $n < k$,
where the factor with $i = n$ vanishes.
