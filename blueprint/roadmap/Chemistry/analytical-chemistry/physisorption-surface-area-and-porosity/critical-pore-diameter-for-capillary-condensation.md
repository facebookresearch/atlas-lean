# Critical pore diameter for capillary condensation

The critical pore diameter $D_c = 4\sigma T_c/(T_c - T)$ separates reversible continuous pore filling from hysteretic capillary condensation.

For an adsorbate with van der Waals diameter $\sigma > 0$ and critical temperature $T_c$, at an adsorption temperature
$0 < T < T_c$, the critical pore diameter is

$$
D_c = \frac{4\,\sigma\,T_c}{T_c - T} .
$$

In this model pores narrower than $D_c$ fill continuously and reversibly, while wider pores condense with hysteresis.
$D_c$ increases with $T$ on $(0, T_c)$, exceeds $4\sigma$, and diverges as $T \to T_c^-$. For water at
25 °C the cited value is $D_c = 20.76$ Å.

## Context

Research level. Rieth, Yang, Wang and Dincă used this criterion to design M2Cl2(BTDD) frameworks with 22 Å channels that take up water at the reversibility limit; the material performance is experimental context, not part of the statement.

## Expansion

The formula is a semi-empirical criterion quoted from the capillary-condensation literature; the qualitative claims
follow from $D_c = 4\sigma/(1 - T/T_c)$.
