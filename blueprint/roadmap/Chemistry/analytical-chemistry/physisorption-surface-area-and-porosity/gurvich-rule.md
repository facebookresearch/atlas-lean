# Gurvich rule

The Gurvich rule converts a limiting uptake into a pore volume by assuming the pores are filled with the adsorptive as bulk liquid.

Let $n_p$ be a limiting uptake of a vapour at the operational temperature and $V_{m,\ell}$ the molar volume of the bulk
liquid adsorptive at that temperature, with $n_p$ total or per unit mass. The Gurvich pore volume is

$$
V_p = n_p\,V_{m,\ell},
$$

total or specific accordingly. It is used in two settings: the micropore volume from the plateau of a Type I isotherm,
where external surface area and mesopores make the plateau slope and obscure $n_p$; and the total pore volume of a
mesoporous solid without macropores from the nearly horizontal upper part of its Type IV isotherm, at a relative
pressure close to unity (for example $0.95$). If macropores are present the upper part is not horizontal and the total
pore volume cannot be read this way. Confinement can make the adsorbate density differ from that of the bulk liquid.

## Context

Undergraduate level. Pore volumes of frameworks quoted from N2 uptake at high relative pressure use this rule.

None.

None.

## Expansion

The rule is a stipulated conversion, not a derived result.
