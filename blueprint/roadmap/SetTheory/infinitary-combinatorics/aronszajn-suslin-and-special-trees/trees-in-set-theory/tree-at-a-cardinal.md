# Tree at a cardinal

Let $\kappa$ be a cardinal and $(T, \le)$ a partial order whose strict order is well-founded; write $\operatorname{rank}(x)$ for the rank (height) of $x$ in $<$ and $\operatorname{Lev}_a(T) = \{x \in T : \operatorname{rank}(x) = a\}$ for the $a$-th level. The proposition that $T$ is a tree at $\kappa$ (a $\kappa$-tree):

1. $T$ is a tree: for every $x \in T$ the set $\{y \in T : y < x\}$ of predecessors of $x$ is a chain (hence well-ordered);
2. every element has rank below $\kappa$: each $x \in T$ lies in $\operatorname{Lev}_a(T)$ for some ordinal $a$ less than the initial ordinal of $\kappa$;
3. for every ordinal $a$ less than the initial ordinal of $\kappa$, the level $\operatorname{Lev}_a(T)$ is nonempty and has cardinality less than $\kappa$.

For $\kappa = 0$ this says $T$ is empty.

Source: Kunen.
