# The tower number

An infinite set $C \subseteq \mathbb{N}$ is a pseudo-intersection of a family $\mathcal{T}$ when $C \setminus A$ is finite for every $A \in \mathcal{T}$. The tower number $\mathfrak{t}$ is the least cardinality (the infimum) of a family $\mathcal{T}$ of infinite subsets of $\mathbb{N}$ that is linearly quasi-ordered by almost inclusion (for all $A, B \in \mathcal{T}$, $A \setminus B$ or $B \setminus A$ is finite) and has no pseudo-intersection.

Source: B. Tsaban, Selection principles and special sets of reals, in Open Problems in Topology II (E. Pearl, ed.), Elsevier 2007, 91–108; revised version arXiv:math/0609601, Problem 11.4.
