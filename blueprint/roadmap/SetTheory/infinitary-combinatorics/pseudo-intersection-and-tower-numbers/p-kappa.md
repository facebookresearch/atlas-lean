# The pseudo-intersection cardinals p_κ

A family $\mathcal{F}$ of subsets of $\mathbb{N}$ is centered when every finite subfamily (including singletons) has infinite intersection; an infinite set $B \subseteq \mathbb{N}$ is a pseudo-intersection of $\mathcal{F}$ when $B \setminus A$ is finite for every $A \in \mathcal{F}$. For a cardinal $\kappa$, $\mathfrak{p}_\kappa$ is the least cardinality (the infimum) of a centered family $\mathcal{F}$ that cannot be partitioned into fewer than $\kappa$ pieces each having a pseudo-intersection: for every index set $I$ with $|I| < \kappa$ and every map $c \colon \mathcal{F} \to I$, some fibre $c^{-1}(i)$ has no pseudo-intersection. Empty fibres always have one, so only the nonempty pieces matter. Cardinals and index sets are taken in the lowest universe.

Source: B. Tsaban, Selection principles and special sets of reals, in Open Problems in Topology II (E. Pearl, ed.), Elsevier 2007, 91–108; revised version arXiv:math/0609601, Problem 11.4.
