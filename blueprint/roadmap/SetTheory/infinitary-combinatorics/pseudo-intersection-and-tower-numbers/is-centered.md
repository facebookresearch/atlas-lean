# Centered families of subsets of the naturals

A family $\mathcal{F}$ of subsets of $\mathbb{N}$ is centered when for every finite subfamily $\mathcal{G} \subseteq \mathcal{F}$ the intersection $\bigcap_{A \in \mathcal{G}} A$ is infinite. Taking singletons, every member of a centered family is infinite; the empty subfamily has intersection $\mathbb{N}$.

Source: B. Tsaban, Selection principles and special sets of reals, in Open Problems in Topology II (E. Pearl, ed.), Elsevier 2007, 91–108; revised version arXiv:math/0609601, Problem 11.4.
