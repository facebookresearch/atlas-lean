# Pseudo-intersections of families of subsets of the naturals

Let $\mathcal{F}$ be a family of subsets of $\mathbb{N}$. A set $B \subseteq \mathbb{N}$ is a pseudo-intersection of $\mathcal{F}$ when $B$ is infinite and $B \setminus A$ is finite for every $A \in \mathcal{F}$. Every infinite set is a pseudo-intersection of the empty family.

Source: B. Tsaban, Selection principles and special sets of reals, in Open Problems in Topology II (E. Pearl, ed.), Elsevier 2007, 91–108; revised version arXiv:math/0609601, Problem 11.4.
