# Strongly inaccessible cardinal: an uncountable regular strong limit cardinal

A cardinal $\kappa$ is *(strongly) inaccessible* if it satisfies three conditions. First, $\kappa$ is uncountable: $\aleph_0 < \kappa$. Second, $\kappa \le \operatorname{cf}(\kappa)$, where $\operatorname{cf}(\kappa)$ is the cofinality of $\kappa$, that is, of the least ordinal of cardinality $\kappa$; since $\operatorname{cf}(\kappa) \le \kappa$ always holds, this says that $\kappa$ is regular, $\operatorname{cf}(\kappa) = \kappa$. Third, $\kappa$ is a strong limit: $2^{\lambda} < \kappa$ for every cardinal $\lambda < \kappa$.
