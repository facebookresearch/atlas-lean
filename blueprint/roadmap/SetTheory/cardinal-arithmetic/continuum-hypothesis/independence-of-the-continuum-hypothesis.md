# Independence of the continuum hypothesis from ZFC

If ZFC is consistent, then ZFC + CH and ZFC + ¬CH are both consistent; so neither CH nor its negation
is derivable in ZFC, assuming ZFC is consistent. Here CH is the statement $2^{\aleph_0} = \aleph_1$
formulated in the first-order language of set theory, and a theory is consistent when no
contradiction is derivable from it in first-order logic. The first half is Gödel's theorem (1938),
proved by showing that CH holds in the constructible universe $L$; the second is Cohen's theorem
(1963), proved by forcing. Both are relative consistency statements: each is an implication between
consistency assertions, not a proof of the consistency of ZFC.

## Expansion

A sound expansion fixes the statement prerequisites: the object-level
first-order language of set theory, the axioms of ZFC as a set of sentences, and the metatheory in
which the two implications are proved; derivability and consistency are the first-order notions
linked above. It then has separate pages for: CH holds in $L$ and the
relative consistency of ZFC + CH; forcing over a countable transitive model or by Boolean-valued
models, and the relative consistency of ZFC + ¬CH; and the corresponding statements for the
generalized continuum hypothesis. The existence of a countable transitive model of ZFC is a stronger
hypothesis than the consistency of ZFC, so a forcing argument that starts from such a model does not
by itself give the implication from the consistency of ZFC; each page states which hypothesis it
uses. The truth of CH inside one model of set theory is a different, semantic
statement and does not express independence.
