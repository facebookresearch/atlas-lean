# The axiom of determinacy contradicts choice

Not every `S ⊆ ℕ^ℕ` is determined: the
axiom of determinacy AD of Mycielski and Steinhaus contradicts the axiom of choice (Moschovakis, *Descriptive Set Theory*, 2nd ed., AMS 2009, Exercise
6A.6; Woodin, *The continuum hypothesis, Part I*, Notices AMS 48 (2001), section
"Determinacy"). The proof only uses a well-ordering of the strategies, that is of the reals.

In ZF the same argument shows that the axiom of determinacy refutes the existence of a well-ordering of the reals. AD is therefore studied in models of ZF without choice, such as `L(ℝ)` under large cardinal hypotheses. Woodin (*Supercompact cardinals, sets of reals, and weakly homogeneous trees*, PNAS 85 (1988)) showed, combined with work of Martin and Steel, that if there is a supercompact cardinal then every set of reals in `L(ℝ)` is determined. Consequences of AD are therefore stated pointclass by pointclass, for classes of determined sets.
