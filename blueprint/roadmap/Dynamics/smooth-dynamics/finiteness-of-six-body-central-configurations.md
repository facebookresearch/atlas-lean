# Finiteness of planar central configurations of six bodies

For six positive point masses in the plane under Newtonian gravity, are there only finitely many central configurations up to similarity, for every choice of the masses? This is the case n = 6 of the planar finiteness conjecture (also called the Chazy–Wintner conjecture), which is on Smale's list of problems for the twenty-first century.

## What would settle it

Normalize translation, rotation and scale, exclude collisions, and prove that the set of similarity classes of central configurations is finite for every vector of six positive masses, including all exceptional mass loci; or exhibit masses with infinitely many classes. A finiteness proof for generic masses does not settle the exceptional loci.

## Status

The cited sources pose this as an open question in the versions cited; this page does not certify its present status.

Chang and Chen recall that Moulton counted the collinear central configurations (n!/2 similarity classes), that Hampton and Moeckel settled finiteness for four bodies in 2006, and that Albouy and Kaloshin settled the planar five-body case in 2012 except for masses in a codimension-2 variety (as recalled in 2023); they develop symbolic computations toward the six-body case. A 2026 preprint by Chen and Tong, whose abstract separates finiteness for generic masses from finiteness on exceptional mass loci and defers the exceptional loci to a later paper, has not been checked beyond its abstract.

This page records the cited research question and asserts no theorem or present-status certification.
