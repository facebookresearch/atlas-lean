# Margulis's theorem on values of quadratic forms (Oppenheim conjecture)

Let $Q$ be a real quadratic form in $n \ge 3$ variables. Suppose that $Q$ is indefinite (it takes a positive and a
negative value), nondegenerate (no nonzero $x$ satisfies $Q(v + x) = Q(v - x)$ for all $v$) and not a scalar multiple
of a form with integer coefficients, that is, not of the form $x \mapsto c \sum_{i,j} a_{ij} x_i x_j$ with $c \in \mathbb{R}$
and $a_{ij} \in \mathbb{Z}$. Then $Q(\mathbb{Z}^n)$ is dense in $\mathbb{R}$.

Each hypothesis is needed: a definite form has discrete values on $\mathbb{Z}^n$, there are counterexamples in two
variables, and a multiple of an integral form has values in a discrete set (Morris, Remark 1.2.4). The proof, for
$n = 3$, applies Ratner's orbit closure theorem (or Margulis's direct argument) to the orbit of the subgroup
$\mathrm{SO}(2, 1)^\circ$, which is generated by unipotent elements, on $\mathrm{SL}(3, \mathbb{Z}) \backslash \mathrm{SL}(3, \mathbb{R})$.
For example, $x_0^2 + x_1^2 - \sqrt2\, x_2^2$ satisfies the three hypotheses.
