# Unipotent flows

A one-parameter subgroup $u_t$ of a Lie group $G$ is unipotent when each $u_t$ is a unipotent matrix, such as the
horocycle subgroup $u_t = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ t & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ of $\mathrm{SL}(2, \mathbb{R})$. For a lattice
$\Gamma$ in $G$, Ratner's orbit closure theorem says that the closure of every orbit of the flow
$\Gamma x \mapsto \Gamma x u_t$ on $\Gamma \backslash G$ is homogeneous: it is the image of $xS$ for a closed connected
subgroup $S$ containing the $u_t$, on which it has finite $S$-invariant volume. For $\Gamma = \mathrm{SL}(2, \mathbb{Z})$
this leaves only closed and dense horocycle orbits. Margulis's proof of the Oppenheim conjecture, through the orbits
of $\mathrm{SO}(2, 1)$ on $\mathrm{SL}(3, \mathbb{Z}) \backslash \mathrm{SL}(3, \mathbb{R})$, is the archetypal
application to number theory (D. W. Morris, *Ratner's theorems on unipotent flows*, arXiv:math/0310402, Sections 1.1
and 1.2).
