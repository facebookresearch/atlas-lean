# Cohomological triviality of mixing homshifts is undecidable

For every dimension $d \ge 2$, no algorithm decides, given a finite graph whose $d$-dimensional homshift is topologically mixing, whether that homshift carries a continuous cocycle that is not cohomologous to a group homomorphism.

Let $G$ be a finite connected undirected graph, possibly with self-loops, and $d \ge 2$. View $\mathbb{Z}^d$ as the grid graph, the Cayley graph of $\mathbb{Z}^d$ for the standard generators. The $d$-dimensional *homshift* $X^d_G$ is the subshift of finite type whose configurations are the graph homomorphisms from $\mathbb{Z}^d$ to $G$ (maps of vertices preserving adjacency), with the shift action $\sigma$ of $\mathbb{Z}^d$. It is topologically mixing if and only if $G$ is not bipartite.

A *cocycle* on a $\mathbb{Z}^d$-subshift $X$ is a map $c\colon \mathbb{Z}^d \times X \to \mathbb{G}$, with $\mathbb{G}$ a discrete group, such that

$$
c(\boldsymbol{m} + \boldsymbol{n}, z) = c(\boldsymbol{m}, \sigma^{\boldsymbol{n}} z)\, c(\boldsymbol{n}, z) \quad \text{for all } \boldsymbol{m}, \boldsymbol{n} \in \mathbb{Z}^d,\ z \in X ;
$$

it is *continuous* if each $c(\boldsymbol{n}, \cdot)$ is continuous. Two cocycles $c, c'$ with values in $\mathbb{G}$ are *cohomologous* if $c(\boldsymbol{n}, z) = b(\sigma^{\boldsymbol{n}} z)^{-1} c'(\boldsymbol{n}, z)\, b(z)$ for a continuous $b\colon X \to \mathbb{G}$. A cocycle is *trivial* if it is cohomologous to a group homomorphism, a cocycle with $c(\boldsymbol{n}, \cdot)$ constant for every $\boldsymbol{n}$, and $X$ is *cohomologically trivial* if every continuous cocycle on it is trivial.

**Theorem.** For every $d > 1$, it is not possible to decide, given a finite graph $G$ such that $X^d_G$ is topologically mixing, whether $X^d_G$ has a nontrivial continuous cocycle (Theorem 1.2 of the source).

## Expansion

The main theorem of the source (Theorem 1.1) says that, for $d > 1$ and $X^d_G$ mixing, $X^d_G$ is cohomologically trivial if and only if the *even square group* of $G$ is trivial. This group is the group of reduced closed walks of even length at a base vertex modulo square-equivalence, the relation generated by inserting conjugates of the 4-cycles of $G$, an index-two subgroup of the square group of $G$ when $G$ is not bipartite. Every finitely presented group is the square group of a bipartite graph (by earlier work the source cites), and adding self-loops turns it into the even square group of a graph whose homshift is mixing; since triviality of finitely presented groups is undecidable (Adian–Rabin), triviality of the even square group of such graphs is undecidable (Proposition 3.9), and Theorem 1.1 transfers this to cohomological triviality. The undecidability is not an instance of the Rice-type theorem for SFTs: homshifts have decidable non-emptiness and computable entropy, so that theorem does not apply to them.
