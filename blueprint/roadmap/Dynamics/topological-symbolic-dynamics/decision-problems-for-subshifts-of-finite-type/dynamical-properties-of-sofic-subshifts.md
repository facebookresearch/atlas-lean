# Nontrivial dynamical properties of sofic subshifts on a group with undecidable domino problem are undecidable

On a finitely generated group with undecidable domino problem, Rice's theorem holds for sofic subshifts: every dynamical property that some sofic subshift has and some sofic subshift lacks is undecidable from sofic presentations.

Let $G$ be a finitely generated group, with [sofic $G$-subshifts and their presentations](sofic-subshifts-on-groups.md) and the [domino problem](domino-problem-of-a-group.md); the empty set counts as a sofic subshift. A dynamical property of sofic $G$-subshifts is a property invariant under conjugacy, and it is *nontrivial* if some sofic $G$-subshift satisfies it and some sofic $G$-subshift does not.

**Theorem.** Let $G$ be a finitely generated group with undecidable domino problem. Then every nontrivial dynamical property of sofic $G$-subshifts is undecidable: no algorithm decides, given a sofic presentation, whether the sofic subshift it presents has the property (Theorem 5.1 of the source).

For subshifts of finite type the analogous statement fails: containing a fixed point is a nontrivial dynamical property of $G$-SFTs that is decidable for every finitely generated group $G$ (Proposition 3.1).

## Expansion

Replacing the property $\mathscr{P}$ by its negation, one may assume that the empty subshift does not satisfy it; fix a sofic subshift $Y_+$ that does, presented as the image of an SFT $X_+$ under a local rule $\mu_+$. Given an SFT presentation $(A, \mathcal{F})$, compute a presentation of an SFT conjugate to $X_+ \times X_{(A,\mathcal{F})}$ and compose the projection to the first factor with $\mu_+$. The resulting sofic subshift is $Y_+$ when $X_{(A,\mathcal{F})}$ is non-empty and is empty otherwise, so it satisfies $\mathscr{P}$ exactly when $X_{(A,\mathcal{F})} \ne \emptyset$. This is a computable many-one reduction from the complement of the domino problem of $G$ to $\mathscr{P}$, which is therefore undecidable.
