# Berger properties of SFTs on a group with undecidable domino problem are undecidable

If a finitely generated group has undecidable domino problem, then every dynamical property of its subshifts of finite type that is satisfied by some SFT $X_+$, fails on every SFT factoring onto some SFT $X_-$, and admits a morphism $X_+ \to X_-$, is undecidable from presentations.

Let $G$ be a finitely generated group, with SFTs, presentations, morphisms, factor maps and the domino problem as in [Subshifts of finite type on a finitely generated group and the domino problem](subshifts-of-finite-type-on-groups.md); the empty set counts as an SFT. A dynamical property $\mathscr{P}$ of $G$-SFTs (a property invariant under conjugacy) is a *Berger property* if there are two $G$-SFTs $X_+$ and $X_-$ such that

1. $X_+$ satisfies $\mathscr{P}$;
2. every $G$-SFT that factors onto $X_-$ fails to satisfy $\mathscr{P}$;
3. there is a morphism from $X_+$ to $X_-$.

The subshift $X_+$ may be empty.

**Theorem.** Let $G$ be a finitely generated group with undecidable domino problem, such as $\mathbb{Z}^d$ with $d \ge 2$. Then every Berger property of $G$-SFTs is undecidable: no algorithm decides, given an SFT presentation $(A, \mathcal{F})$, whether $X_{(A,\mathcal{F})}$ has the property (Theorem 3.6 of the source).

In particular (Corollary 3.7), every nontrivial dynamical property of $G$-SFTs that is preserved under factor maps and satisfied by the empty subshift, or preserved under extensions and not satisfied by the empty subshift, is undecidable.

## Expansion

Given a presentation $(A, \mathcal{F})$, let $Z$ be the disjoint union of $X_+$ and $X_{(A,\mathcal{F})} \times X_-$; a presentation of an SFT conjugate to $Z$ is computable from $(A, \mathcal{F})$, since direct products and disjoint unions of SFTs are computable at the level of presentations (Lemmas 3.2 and 3.3). If $X_{(A,\mathcal{F})} = \emptyset$ then $Z$ is conjugate to $X_+$ and satisfies $\mathscr{P}$. If $X_{(A,\mathcal{F})} \ne \emptyset$ then $Z$ factors onto $X_-$, using the projection of the product and the morphism $X_+ \to X_-$, so $Z$ fails $\mathscr{P}$. Hence a decision procedure for $\mathscr{P}$ would decide the domino problem of $G$. The name follows the analogy with Markov properties of finitely presented groups in the Adian–Rabin theorem. The source lists transitivity and minimality among the properties the theorem covers; on the other hand some properties of $G$-SFTs, such as containing a fixed point, are decidable for every finitely generated $G$ (Proposition 3.1), so not every nontrivial property is undecidable.
