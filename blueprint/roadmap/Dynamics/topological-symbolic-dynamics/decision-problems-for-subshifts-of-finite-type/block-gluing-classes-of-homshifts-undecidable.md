# The block gluing classes of two-dimensional homshifts are computably inseparable

No algorithm separates the finite graphs whose two-dimensional homshift is $O(\log n)$-block gluing from those whose homshift has block-gluing rate $\Theta(n)$; in particular it is undecidable whether a two-dimensional homshift is $o(n)$-block gluing.

Let $X^2_G$ be the two-dimensional [homshift](homshifts.md) of a finite graph $G$, and let $O(g)$-block gluing, $o(g)$-block gluing and block-gluing rate $\Theta(g)$ be as in [quantified block gluing](quantified-block-gluing.md), read by optimal order.

**Theorem (Chandgotia–Gangloff–Hellouin de Menibus–Oprocha).** The class of finite graphs $G$ for which $X^2_G$ is $O(\log n)$-block gluing and the class of those for which $X^2_G$ has block-gluing rate $\Theta(n)$ (it is $O(n)$-block gluing but not $o(n)$-block gluing) are computably inseparable: no algorithm, given $G$, answers correctly whenever $G$ lies in one of the two classes (Theorem 1.2 of the source, whose proof uses the optimal-rate reading). In particular it is undecidable, given $G$, whether $X^2_G$ is $o(n)$-block gluing (Section 6.2.2). The same holds for phased block gluing: the classes of graphs whose two-dimensional homshift is $O(\log n)$-phased block gluing and of those whose homshift has phased block-gluing rate $\Theta(n)$ are computably inseparable (Corollary 5.25).

This contrasts with general subshifts of finite type, where undecidability comes from simulating Turing machines, which homshifts cannot do because their constraints are invariant under rotations and reflections. Whether strong irreducibility of a homshift is decidable is left open (Question 1.1).

## Expansion

The *square group* of a graph $G$ is the group of closed non-backtracking walks at a base vertex modulo the relation generated by inserting $4$-cycles of $G$, and the *square cover* is the corresponding quotient of the universal cover. A Galois correspondence between graph covers and subgroups shows that the square group is finite if and only if the square cover is finite. Every homshift $X^d_G$ with $d > 1$ is $O(n)$-phased block gluing, and for non-bipartite $G$ it is $f$-block gluing exactly when it is $f$-phased block gluing (Lemmas 2.7 and 2.8 of the source). By Gangloff, Hellouin de Menibus and Oprocha, a two-dimensional homshift is $O(\log n)$-phased block gluing when the square cover is finite and has phased block-gluing rate $\Theta(n)$ when it is infinite. The source proves that from any finite presentation one can algorithmically construct a bipartite graph whose square group is the presented group (Theorem 5.1, a realization theorem sketched earlier by Gao, Jackson, Krohne and Seward). Since finiteness is a Markov property, by the Adian–Rabin theorem (Theorem 5.23 of the source) no algorithm decides from a presentation whether the group is finite; hence the two block gluing classes are computably inseparable. To pass from phased to unphased block gluing, the proof adds a self-loop to the bipartite graph, which makes it non-bipartite while keeping control of the square group. The Adian–Rabin theorem for finiteness, the realization theorem and the Gangloff–Hellouin de Menibus–Oprocha dichotomy are prerequisites still to be added; the Adian–Rabin theorem rests on the unsolvability of the word problem.
