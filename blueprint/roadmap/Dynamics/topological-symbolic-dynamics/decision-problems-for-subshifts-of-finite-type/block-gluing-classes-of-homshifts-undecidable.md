# The block gluing classes of two-dimensional homshifts are computably inseparable

No algorithm decides, given a finite graph, whether its two-dimensional homshift is $\Theta(n)$-block gluing or $O(\log n)$-block gluing: the two classes cannot be separated by any algorithm.

Let homshifts $X^2_G$ and quantified block gluing be as in [Homshifts and quantified block gluing](homshifts-and-quantified-block-gluing.md).

**Theorem (Chandgotia–Gangloff–Hellouin de Menibus–Oprocha).** The class of finite graphs $G$ whose two-dimensional homshift $X^2_G$ is $\Theta(n)$-block gluing and the class of those whose homshift is $O(\log n)$-block gluing are computably inseparable. That is, it is not possible to decide algorithmically whether a two-dimensional homshift is $\Theta(n)$-block gluing or $O(\log n)$-block gluing (Theorem 1.2 of the source). The classes of $\Theta(n)$-phased block gluing and $O(\log n)$-phased block gluing two-dimensional homshifts are also computably inseparable (Corollary 5.25).

This contrasts with general subshifts of finite type, where undecidability comes from simulating Turing machines, which homshifts cannot do because their constraints are invariant under rotations and reflections. Whether strong irreducibility of a homshift is decidable is left open (Question 1.1).

## Expansion

The *square group* of a graph $G$ is the group of closed non-backtracking walks at a base vertex modulo the relation generated by inserting $4$-cycles of $G$, and the *square cover* is the corresponding quotient of the universal cover. A Galois correspondence between graph covers and subgroups shows that the square group is finite if and only if the square cover is finite. By Gangloff, Hellouin de Menibus and Oprocha, a two-dimensional homshift is $\Theta(n)$-phased block gluing when the square cover is infinite and $O(\log n)$-phased block gluing when it is finite. The source proves that from any finite presentation one can algorithmically construct a bipartite graph whose square group is the presented group (Theorem 5.1, a realization theorem sketched earlier by Gao, Jackson, Krohne and Seward). Since finiteness is a Markov property, by the Adian–Rabin theorem (Theorem 5.23 of the source) no algorithm decides from a presentation whether the group is finite; hence the two block gluing classes are computably inseparable. To pass from phased to unphased block gluing, the proof adds a self-loop to the bipartite graph, which makes it non-bipartite while keeping control of the square group. The Adian–Rabin theorem for finiteness, the realization theorem and the Gangloff–Hellouin de Menibus–Oprocha dichotomy have no pages; the Adian–Rabin theorem rests on the unsolvability of the word problem.
