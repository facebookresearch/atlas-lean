# Whether two automorphisms of a full shift generate a free group of rank two is undecidable

For every finite alphabet whose torsion problem is undecidable (such alphabets exist), no algorithm decides, given two reversible cellular automata on the full shift (automorphisms given by local rules), whether they generate a free group of rank two.

Let $S$ be a finite alphabet and $\mathrm{Aut}(S^{\mathbb{Z}})$ the automorphism group of the full shift $S^{\mathbb{Z}}$: the homeomorphisms of $S^{\mathbb{Z}}$ commuting with the shift, that is, the reversible cellular automata. By the Curtis–Hedlund–Lyndon theorem every such automorphism $f$ has a radius $r$ and a local rule $F\colon S^{2r+1} \to S$ with $f(x)_i = F(x_{[i-r, i+r]})$, and an automorphism is given to an algorithm by such a local rule; whether two local rules define the same cellular automaton is decidable (Lemma 8 of the source and the remark after it). The *torsion problem* of $\mathrm{Aut}(S^{\mathbb{Z}})$ is to decide, given $f \in \mathrm{Aut}(S^{\mathbb{Z}})$, whether $f^m = \mathrm{id}$ for some $m > 0$.

**Theorem.** Let $S$ be a finite alphabet for which the torsion problem of $\mathrm{Aut}(S^{\mathbb{Z}})$ is undecidable; such an alphabet exists (Theorem 3 of the source). Given $f, g \in \mathrm{Aut}(S^{\mathbb{Z}})$, it is undecidable whether the subgroup $\langle f, g \rangle$ is isomorphic to the free group $F_2$ (Theorem 6 of the source).

## Expansion

The proof combines two results of the source. The first is its main theorem: for $|S| \ge 2$, the class of groups isomorphic to subgroups of $\mathrm{Aut}(S^{\mathbb{Z}})$ is closed under free products (Theorem 2). For disjoint alphabets $A$ and $B$ of the same size, its proof embeds $\mathrm{Aut}(A^{\mathbb{Z}}) * \mathrm{Aut}(B^{\mathbb{Z}})$ in $\mathrm{Aut}((C \sqcup A^2 \sqcup B^2)^{\mathbb{Z}})$, where $C$ is the set of bijections $A^2 \to B^2$: each factor acts on the conveyor belts formed by the maximal runs over its own subalphabet, and a single symbol of $C$ between two belts passes changes at the end of one belt to the end of its neighbour. The alphabet is then changed to any alphabet with at least two symbols by Lemma 4, which follows from Lemma 7 (the endomorphism monoid of every full shift embeds in that of every uncountable sofic shift, proved with unbordered marker words). The second is the undecidability of the torsion problem of $\mathrm{Aut}(S^{\mathbb{Z}})$ for some alphabet $S$ (Theorem 3), which rests on Kari and Ollinger's theorem that periodicity of reversible one-dimensional cellular automata is undecidable (J. Kari and N. Ollinger, "Periodicity and immortality in reversible computing", MFCS 2008, Lecture Notes in Computer Science 5162, 419–430, https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-540-85238-4_34); arXiv:1507.00820v1 attributes Theorem 3 to Kari's paper "The nilpotency problem of one-dimensional cellular automata" (SIAM J. Comput. 21 (1992), 571–586), a result about nilpotency, while its introduction derives its undecidability corollaries from the periodicity results of Kari and Ollinger, and the published article's reference list keeps Kari and Ollinger and no longer lists the 1992 paper. Applied to $f$, the embeddings give $f_1, f_2 \in \mathrm{Aut}(S^{\mathbb{Z}})$ with $\langle f_1, f_2 \rangle \cong \langle f \rangle * \langle f \rangle$, which is free of rank two exactly when $f$ has infinite order. arXiv:1507.00820v1 states this reduction without detailed proof. A good expansion links pages for the closure of subgroups of $\mathrm{Aut}(S^{\mathbb{Z}})$ under free products (Theorem 2, with Lemmas 4 and 7), for the undecidability of the torsion problem (Theorem 3, after Kari and Ollinger), and for the computability of the embeddings from local rules, which the reduction needs.
