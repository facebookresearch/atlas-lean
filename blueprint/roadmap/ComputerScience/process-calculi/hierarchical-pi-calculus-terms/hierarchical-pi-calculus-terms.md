# Hierarchical terms of the pi-calculus

A π-calculus term is hierarchical if its restrictions can be labeled by the nodes of a fixed finite forest so that every reachable term has a structurally congruent representative in which nested active restrictions follow strictly increasing paths of that forest; hierarchical terms are depth-bounded (Theorem 3 of the source).

**Terms and semantics.** Terms of the (monadic, replication-based) π-calculus, as in the source, which assumes channels carry one name and notes that the polyadic extension is straightforward, are built from $\mathbf 0$, guarded choices of prefixes $\bar a\langle b \rangle.P$, $a(x).P$ and $\tau.P$, parallel composition $P \parallel Q$, restriction $\nu x.P$ and replication $!M$ of a choice $M$. Structural congruence $\equiv$ is the least relation that respects α-conversion of bound names, makes $+$ and $\parallel$ associative and commutative with $\mathbf 0$ as neutral element, and satisfies $\nu a.\mathbf 0 \equiv \mathbf 0$, $\nu a.\nu b.P \equiv \nu b.\nu a.P$, $!P \equiv P \parallel !P$ and $P \parallel \nu a.Q \equiv \nu a.(P \parallel Q)$ if $a \notin \mathrm{fn}(P)$ (Definition 1). The reduction relation $P \to Q$ is generated by communication between matching input and output prefixes and by $\tau$-steps, up to $\equiv$; $\to^*$ is its reflexive and transitive closure and $\mathrm{reach}(P) = \{Q : P \to^* Q\}$. The *reachability problem* asks, given terms $P$ and $Q$, whether a term structurally congruent to $Q$ is in $\mathrm{reach}(P)$.

**Forests and depth.** Each congruence class of terms is represented by labeled forests $\mathrm{forest}(Q)$, $Q \equiv P$: $\mathrm{forest}(\mathbf 0)$ is empty, a sequential process (a choice or a replication) is a leaf, $\nu x.Q'$ is a node $x$ above $\mathrm{forest}(Q')$, and parallel composition is disjoint union. The *nesting of restrictions* is $\mathrm{nest}_\nu(\mathbf 0) = \mathrm{nest}_\nu(M) = \mathrm{nest}_\nu(!M) = 0$, $\mathrm{nest}_\nu(\nu x.P) = 1 + \mathrm{nest}_\nu(P)$ and $\mathrm{nest}_\nu(P \parallel Q) = \max(\mathrm{nest}_\nu(P), \mathrm{nest}_\nu(Q))$, and the *depth* of a term is the least nesting among the terms structurally congruent to it, $\mathrm{depth}(Q) = \min\{\mathrm{nest}_\nu(Q') : Q' \equiv Q\}$. A term $P$ is *depth-bounded* if some $k$ satisfies $\mathrm{depth}(Q) \le k$ for every $Q \in \mathrm{reach}(P)$ (Definition 4 of the source, after Meyer).

**Hierarchical terms.** Fix a finite forest $(\mathcal T, \prec)$ of *base types*, with $<$ the strict ancestor order. An *annotated* term writes restrictions as $\nu(x : t)$ with $t \in \mathcal T$. An annotated term is *$\mathcal T$-compatible* if one of its forest representations has every root-to-leaf trace $(x_1, t_1) \cdots (x_k, t_k)\, A$ with $t_1 < t_2 < \cdots < t_k$. A term $P$ is *hierarchical* if there are a finite forest $\mathcal T$ and an annotation of $P$, extended consistently along reductions (active restrictions keep their annotations, newly activated ones get some annotation), such that every annotated term reachable from it is $\mathcal T$-compatible.

## Expansion

The forest $\mathcal T$ is a shape invariant for the communication topology: in a client–server system, a hierarchy server > client > mailbox > data expresses that every mailbox is private to one client and every client to one server. Since a $\mathcal T$-compatible forest has traces of length at most the height of $\mathcal T$, hierarchical terms are depth-bounded. The source gives a type system whose typable terms ("typably hierarchical" terms) are hierarchical, with type inference, and shows that the fragment contains $\mathrm{CCS}^!$. For depth-bounded terms termination and coverability are decidable (Meyer; Wies, Zufferey and Henzinger).
