# Hierarchical terms of the pi-calculus

A π-calculus term is hierarchical if its restrictions can be labeled by the nodes of a fixed finite forest so that, in every reachable term, nested active restrictions always follow strictly increasing paths of that forest; hierarchical terms are depth-bounded.

**Terms and semantics.** Terms of the (polyadic, replication-based) π-calculus are built from $\mathbf 0$, guarded choices of prefixes $\bar a\langle \vec b \rangle.P$, $a(\vec x).P$ and $\tau.P$, parallel composition $P \parallel Q$, restriction $\nu x.P$ and replication $!M$ of a choice $M$. Structural congruence $\equiv$ is the usual one (commutative monoid for $\parallel$, scope extrusion, α-conversion, unfolding of replication). The reduction relation $P \to Q$ is generated by communication between matching input and output prefixes and by $\tau$-steps, up to $\equiv$; $\mathrm{reach}(P) = \{Q : P \to^* Q\}$. The *reachability problem* asks, given terms $P$ and $Q$, whether a term structurally congruent to $Q$ is in $\mathrm{reach}(P)$.

**Forests and depth.** Each congruence class of terms is represented by labeled forests $\mathrm{forest}(Q)$, $Q \equiv P$: $\mathrm{forest}(\mathbf 0)$ is empty, a sequential process (a choice or a replication) is a leaf, $\nu x.Q'$ is a node $x$ above $\mathrm{forest}(Q')$, and parallel composition is disjoint union. A term is *depth-bounded* if some $k$ bounds the nesting depth of active restrictions of every reachable term (Meyer).

**Hierarchical terms.** Fix a finite forest $(\mathcal T, \prec)$ of *base types*, with $<$ the strict ancestor order. An *annotated* term writes restrictions as $\nu(x : t)$ with $t \in \mathcal T$. An annotated term is *$\mathcal T$-compatible* if one of its forest representations has every root-to-leaf trace $(x_1, t_1) \cdots (x_k, t_k)\, A$ with $t_1 < t_2 < \cdots < t_k$. A term $P$ is *hierarchical* if there are a finite forest $\mathcal T$ and an annotation of $P$, extended consistently along reductions (active restrictions keep their annotations, newly activated ones get some annotation), such that every annotated term reachable from it is $\mathcal T$-compatible.

## Expansion

The forest $\mathcal T$ is a shape invariant for the communication topology: in a client–server system, a hierarchy server > client > mailbox > data expresses that every mailbox is private to one client and every client to one server. Since a $\mathcal T$-compatible forest has traces of length at most the height of $\mathcal T$, hierarchical terms are depth-bounded. The source gives a type system whose typable terms ("typably hierarchical" terms) are hierarchical, with type inference, and shows that the fragment contains $\mathrm{CCS}^!$. For depth-bounded terms termination and coverability are decidable (Meyer; Wies, Zufferey and Henzinger).
