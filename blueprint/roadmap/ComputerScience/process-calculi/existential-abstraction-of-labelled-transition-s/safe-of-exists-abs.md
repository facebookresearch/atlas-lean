# Safety transfers from the existential abstraction

Let $L$ be a labelled transition system with states $S$ and labels $\Lambda$, given by its transition relation $s \xrightarrow{\mu} t$; its existential abstraction along a function $\alpha : S \to T$ is the system $L_\alpha$ on states $T$ with the same labels in which $a \xrightarrow{\mu} b$ holds exactly when $s \xrightarrow{\mu} t$ in $L$ for some $s, t \in S$ with $\alpha(s) = a$ and $\alpha(t) = b$. A multistep transition $s \xrightarrow{\mu_1 \cdots \mu_n} t$ is a chain of transitions $s = s_0 \xrightarrow{\mu_1} s_1 \cdots \xrightarrow{\mu_n} s_n = t$ (for the empty list, $t = s$), and $t$ is reachable from $s$ when such a chain exists for some finite list of labels, possibly empty. Let $\mathrm{Init}$ and $\mathrm{Bad}$ be predicates on $S$. Suppose that every abstract state $a \in T$ that is reachable in $L_\alpha$ from $\alpha(s_0)$ for some $s_0$ with $\mathrm{Init}(s_0)$ is not the image $\alpha(s)$ of any $s$ with $\mathrm{Bad}(s)$. Then for all $s_0, t \in S$ with $\mathrm{Init}(s_0)$ and $t$ reachable from $s_0$ in $L$, $\mathrm{Bad}(t)$ fails.

The conclusion is a safety (reachability) property only.
