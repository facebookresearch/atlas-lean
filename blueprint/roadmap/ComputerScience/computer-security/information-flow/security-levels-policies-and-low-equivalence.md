# Security levels, policies and low equivalence

Security levels carry a relation $\sqsubseteq$, read "may flow to"; in the lattice model of information flow they form a lattice $(\mathcal{L}, \sqsubseteq)$, in the simplest case the two-point lattice $L \sqsubseteq H$ of public and secret data. A *security policy* $\Gamma : V \to \mathcal{L}$ assigns a level to each program variable, and a *store* $\sigma : V \to A$ assigns a value to each variable. For an observer at level $\ell$, two stores $\sigma_1$ and $\sigma_2$ are *low equivalent*, written $\sigma_1 =_\ell \sigma_2$, if $\sigma_1(x) = \sigma_2(x)$ for every variable $x$ with $\Gamma(x) \sqsubseteq \ell$.

The definition uses only the relation $\sqsubseteq$, not the lattice operations, and for every $\ell$ low equivalence is an equivalence relation on stores whatever that relation is ([equivalence](low-equiv-equivalence.md)). When $\sqsubseteq$ is a preorder, $\sigma_1 =_{\ell'} \sigma_2$ implies $\sigma_1 =_\ell \sigma_2$ for $\ell \sqsubseteq \ell'$: an observer at a higher level distinguishes at least the stores that one at a lower level distinguishes ([antitonicity](antitone-low-equiv.md)). In the two-point lattice, $=_L$ relates the stores that agree on the public variables, and $=_H$ only equal stores.

In the lattice model a flow of information from $x$ to $y$ is permitted when $\Gamma(x) \sqsubseteq \Gamma(y)$. For a preorder $\sqsubseteq$ and values with at least two elements, $\Gamma(x) \sqsubseteq \Gamma(y)$ holds exactly when copying $x$ into $y$ maps $\ell$-low-equivalent stores to $\ell$-low-equivalent stores for every level $\ell$ ([permitted flows](forall-low-equiv-update-iff-le.md)). Least upper bounds give the level of information combined from several variables: in a join-semilattice with a least element, and again for values with at least two elements, all stores with $\sigma_1 =_\ell \sigma_2$ agree on a finite set $S$ of variables exactly when $\bigsqcup_{x \in S} \Gamma(x) \sqsubseteq \ell$ ([joins](forall-low-equiv-eq-on-iff-sup-le.md)).
