# Shuffled protocols attain central accuracy for sums but not, with one message per user, for selection

In the shuffle model, for $\varepsilon \in (0,1)$ and $\delta$ not too small (at least about $2^{-\varepsilon n}$), the sum of $n$ bits can be computed with $(\varepsilon,\delta)$-differential privacy and expected additive error $O\bigl(\sqrt{\log(1/\delta)}/\varepsilon\bigr)$, independent of $n$, with one one-bit message per user: each user reports its bit with probability $1 - p$ and a uniformly random bit otherwise, and the analyzer debiases the shuffled count. The replacement probability is $p = \lambda/n$ with $\lambda \in (0, n)$ chosen piecewise (Cheu et al., Theorem 4.1, for $\varepsilon$ at least a constant times $\log(1/\delta)/n$): $\lambda$ of order $\log(1/\delta)/\varepsilon^2$, so $p$ of order $\log(1/\delta)/(\varepsilon^2 n)$, when $\varepsilon \ge \sqrt{\kappa\log(1/\delta)/n}$ for a suitable constant $\kappa$, and $\lambda = n - \Theta\big(\varepsilon n^{3/2}/\sqrt{\log(1/\delta)}\big)$ otherwise, so that $p$ stays below $1$ where $\log(1/\delta)/(\varepsilon^2 n)$ would exceed it. In the nonsaturated regime $1/\sqrt n \le \varepsilon < 1$, where $\sqrt n/\varepsilon \le n$, pure local protocols require error $\Omega(\sqrt{n}/\varepsilon)$ (Beimel et al. and Chan et al., as cited by Cheu et al., Section 1), while the central model achieves $O(1/\varepsilon)$. No lower bound above $n/2$ is possible for any $\varepsilon$, since the constant estimate $n/2$ always errs by at most $n/2$; so for $\varepsilon < 1/\sqrt n$ the local bound saturates. With $r = \lceil \varepsilon\sqrt n\rceil \ge 1$ one-bit messages per user, which is $\Theta(\varepsilon\sqrt n)$ when $\varepsilon \ge 1/\sqrt n$ and one message otherwise (Theorem 2.1; $r = \lceil \varepsilon\sqrt n\rceil$ in the proof of Corollary C.3), shuffled protocols also sum values in $[0,1]$ with error $O\bigl(\log(n/\delta)/\varepsilon\bigr)$, independent of $n$ up to logarithmic factors, for $\varepsilon \in (0,1)$ and $\delta$ at least about $\varepsilon n\, 2^{-\varepsilon n}$ (Theorem 2.1). In contrast, in the one-message shuffle model, where every user sends a single message, every $(1, n^{-10})$-differentially private protocol that solves the selection problem (output a coordinate whose column sum is within $n/10$ of the maximum among $d$ columns) with high probability needs $n = \Omega(d^{1/17})$, even for inputs drawn i.i.d. from a product distribution, whereas the central model needs only $n = O(\log d)$. This separation concerns one-message protocols; it does not separate unrestricted multi-message shuffling from the central model. Together with the summation result it places the one-message shuffle model strictly between the local and central models.

## Proof sketch

After shuffling, the analyzer sees only the number of ones, which is the true count plus a sum of independent random replacements; with replacement rate $\lambda/n$ the number of replaced bits is binomial with mean $\lambda$, and the debiased noise has variance $O(\log(1/\delta)/\varepsilon^2)$ in both regimes of $\lambda$, and binomial noise of this variance is $(\varepsilon,\delta)$-differentially private for neighbouring counts. For the lower bound, if a one-message shuffled protocol is $(\varepsilon, \delta)$-differentially private, its local randomizer is itself $(\varepsilon + \ln n, \delta)$-differentially private, so known lower bounds for local protocols for selection, after converting approximate local privacy to pure local privacy, apply.
