# Optimal excess empirical risk for private convex empirical risk minimization

Let $\mathcal{W} \subseteq \mathbb{R}^d$ be a nonempty, closed, convex set of finite diameter $D$, let $\ell(w, z)$ be convex and $L$-Lipschitz in $w$ for every data point $z$, let $\hat F_S(w) = \frac1n\sum_i \ell(w, z_i)$ be the empirical risk of a dataset $S$ and $F(w) = \mathbb{E}_{z \sim \mathcal{D}}[\ell(w,z)]$ the population risk, assumed finite (finiteness at one point implies it everywhere, by Lipschitzness). Since $\mathcal{W}$ is compact and both risks are continuous, their minima over $\mathcal{W}$ exist, and the Euclidean projection $\Pi_{\mathcal{W}}$ onto $\mathcal{W}$ is well defined. The minimax excess empirical risk of differentially private algorithms, the best worst case over datasets, satisfies the following bounds in the bounded-privacy regime $0 < \varepsilon \le 1$, with $0 < \delta = o(1/n)$ for approximate privacy (positive, so that $\log(n/\delta)$ in the upper bounds is finite). The lower bounds (Bassily, Smith and Thakurta, Theorems 5.2, 5.3 and 5.5) carry no factor logarithmic in $1/\delta$; the upper bounds for approximate privacy come from the privacy analysis of noisy SGD and do.

- For $(\varepsilon,\delta)$-DP and convex losses: at most $O\!\left(LD\,\min\Big\{1, \dfrac{\sqrt{d}\,\log^2(n/\delta)}{\varepsilon n}\Big\}\right)$ (Bassily, Smith and Thakurta's noisy stochastic gradient descent), and at least $\Omega\!\left(LD\,\min\Big\{1, \dfrac{\sqrt{d}}{\varepsilon n}\Big\}\right)$.
- For $\varepsilon$-DP and convex losses: $\Theta\!\left(LD\,\min\Big\{1, \dfrac{d}{\varepsilon n}\Big\}\right)$.
- For $(\varepsilon,\delta)$-DP and $\lambda$-strongly convex losses: at most $O\!\left(\min\Big\{LD, \dfrac{L^2 d\,\log^3(n/\delta)}{\lambda\varepsilon^2 n^2}\Big\}\right)$, and at least $\Omega\!\left(\min\Big\{LD, \dfrac{L^2 d}{\lambda\varepsilon^2 n^2}\Big\}\right)$, the lower bound being proved for $\lambda$ of order $L/D$, the largest strong convexity that $L$-Lipschitz losses on a set of diameter $D$ allow.

Here the lower bounds say that for every private algorithm there is a dataset (with linear losses over a ball in the first two cases and quadratic losses in the third) on which the excess empirical risk is at least the stated amount with probability at least an absolute positive constant, hence also in expectation; they say nothing about a given instance, on which a private algorithm may do better. The small-$\delta$ regime is necessary: for $\delta$ close to $1$, releasing an empirical minimizer with probability $\delta$ and a fixed point otherwise is $(0, \delta)$-differentially private and has excess risk at most $(1 - \delta)LD$, which can be below the square-root bound. The bounded-$\varepsilon$ regime is necessary for these constant-probability lower bounds: on a one-dimensional ball with linear losses, the mechanism that releases the endpoint minimizing the empirical risk with probability $e^{\varepsilon}/(1+e^{\varepsilon})$ and the other endpoint otherwise (either endpoint with probability $1/2$ when both minimize) is $\varepsilon$-differentially private, and on every such instance its excess risk is positive with probability at most $1/(1+e^{\varepsilon})$, which tends to $0$ as $\varepsilon$ grows. The upper bounds are achieved in polynomial time (noisy SGD for approximate privacy; the exponential mechanism over $\mathcal{W}$, sampled via log-concave sampling, for pure privacy), and assume $0 < \delta < 1/n$.

## Proof sketch

For $(\varepsilon,\delta)$-DP, Bassily, Smith and Thakurta run projected stochastic (sub)gradient descent for $n^2$ steps, each using the subgradient at one uniformly sampled data point plus Gaussian noise; privacy follows from amplification by subsampling and advanced composition, which cost the $\log^2(n/\delta)$ factor, and the standard convergence bound for noisy stochastic subgradient descent over $\mathcal{W}$ gives excess risk $O(LD\sqrt{d}\log^2(n/\delta)/(\varepsilon n))$; under $\lambda$-strong convexity a decreasing step size gives the strongly convex bound. The logarithmic factors come from the privacy analysis and are absent from the lower bounds. Lower bounds reduce from private estimation of one-way marginals: for linear losses $\ell(w, z) = -\langle w, z\rangle$ over the unit ball with $z \in \{\pm 1/\sqrt{d}\}^d$, minimizing empirical risk to small error recovers the signs of most marginals, which the fingerprinting lower bound forbids for $n \ll \sqrt{d}/\varepsilon$; the packing argument gives the pure-DP bound.
