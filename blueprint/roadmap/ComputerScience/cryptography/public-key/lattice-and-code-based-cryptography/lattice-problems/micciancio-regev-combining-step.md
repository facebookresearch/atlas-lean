# Micciancio–Regev combining step: uniform SIS queries from Gaussian lattice samples

Let $L = L(B) \subset \mathbb{R}^n$ be a full-rank lattice with basis $B$, let $q \ge 2$, and let $s \ge \eta_\varepsilon(L)$, the smoothing parameter of $L$. Sample $y_1, \dots, y_m$ independently from a discrete Gaussian of width $s$ on the fine grid $q^{-1}L$, and let $c_i \in q^{-1}L / L \cong \mathbb{Z}_q^n$ be the coset of $y_i$ modulo $L$, written in the basis $B$; collect the $c_i$ as the columns of $C \in \mathbb{Z}_q^{n \times m}$. Then (i) the matrix $C$ is within statistical distance $m\varepsilon/2$ of uniform on $\mathbb{Z}_q^{n\times m}$, and conditionally on $c_i$ the vector $y_i$ is a discrete Gaussian over the coset $q^{-1}Bc_i + L$; (ii) for every $z \in \mathbb{Z}^m$ with $C z \equiv 0 \pmod q$, the combination $v = \sum_i z_i y_i$ lies in $L$, and $\|v\|_2 \le \|z\|_1 \cdot \max_i \|y_i\|_2 \le \sqrt m\,\|z\|_2 \cdot \max_i \|y_i\|_2$. For $m$ polynomial in $n$, $\max_i \|y_i\|_2 \le s\sqrt n$ with overwhelming probability, so an $\ell_2$-SIS solution with $\|z\|_2 \le \beta$ for the (almost uniform) matrix $C$ then yields a lattice vector of length at most $\beta s \sqrt{mn}$ by Cauchy–Schwarz. This discrete-Gaussian form of the combining step is the one used in later reductions, for example Micciancio and Peikert (2013, Lemma 3.6, with the samples scaled by $q$); Micciancio and Regev's own sampling and combining procedures (Lemmas 5.7 and 5.8) sample continuous Gaussians modulo the basis parallelepiped and combine them through a full-rank sublattice $S$, with output within distance $\sqrt{mn}\,\|S\|\,\|z\|/q$ of the combination $\sum_i z_i c_i$ of their samples $c_i$. Iterating their procedure gives the worst-case to average-case reductions only for a large enough modulus: for polynomially bounded $m$ and $\beta$, negligible $\varepsilon$ and $q \ge 8n\sqrt m\,\beta$, a probabilistic polynomial-time reduction from $\mathrm{GIVP}^{\eta_\varepsilon}_\gamma$ (find $n$ linearly independent lattice vectors of length at most $\gamma\,\eta_\varepsilon(L)$; hence from $\mathrm{SIVP}$ with an $\tilde O(\beta\sqrt n)$ factor) in the worst case with $\gamma = 8\beta\sqrt n$ to $\ell_2$-SIS with parameters $q, m, \beta$ on the average (Corollary 5.13, derived from Theorem 5.9), and, for polynomially bounded $q \ge 4\sqrt m\, n^{1.5}\beta$, one from $\mathrm{GapCVP}'_\gamma$ with $\gamma = 14\pi\sqrt n\,\beta$ to the variant of $\ell_2$-SIS whose solutions must lie outside $2\mathbb{Z}^m$ (Theorem 5.23), through which they reach $\mathrm{GapSVP}$. A lower bound on $q$ is needed: for $q = 2$ and $m > n$, Gaussian elimination modulo $2$ solves $\ell_2$-SIS with $\beta = \sqrt m$ in polynomial time.

## Proof sketch

By Poisson summation, for $s \ge \eta_\varepsilon(L)$ the Gaussian mass of each coset of $L$ in $q^{-1}L$ lies within a factor $1 \pm \varepsilon$ of $s^n/\det L$, while the average mass over the $q^n$ cosets is at least $s^n/\det L$; hence each $c_i$ is within statistical distance $\varepsilon/2$ of uniform on $\mathbb{Z}_q^n$, and the $m$ independent columns within $m\varepsilon/2$. Linearity gives $\sum_i z_i y_i \in L$ whenever $Cz \equiv 0$, and the norm bound follows from the triangle inequality, Cauchy–Schwarz and the Gaussian norm tail.
