# Pohlig–Hellman reduction to prime-order subgroups

Let $G$ be cyclic of order $N = \prod_{i} p_i^{e_i}$ with known factorization. Discrete logarithms in $G$ can be computed from discrete logarithms in the subgroups of prime order $p_i$: for each $i$, compute $\alpha \bmod p_i^{e_i}$ by working in the subgroup generated by $g^{N/p_i^{e_i}}$ (digit by digit, or recursively), then combine by the Chinese remainder theorem. With baby-step giant-step on the prime-order subgroups the total cost is $O\bigl(\sum_i e_i(\log N + \sqrt{p_i})\bigr)$ group operations. Hence the hardness of discrete logarithms is governed by the largest prime factor of the group order.

## Proof sketch

Raising to the power $N/p_i^{e_i}$ projects onto the $p_i$-primary component and maps $\alpha$ to $\alpha \bmod p_i^{e_i}$; each component is handled by the prime-power algorithm, and CRT reassembles $\alpha$.
