# Real world of the key-exchange experiment

Let $G$ be a finite commutative group, $g \in G$ and $q \ge 1$ an integer, and let an eavesdropper
map a transcript, a candidate key in $G$ and coins in a finite nonempty set $S$ to a bit. The real
world draws $a, b \in \mathbb{Z}/q$ and coins $s \in S$ uniformly, and outputs the eavesdropper's bit
on the transcript $(g^a, g^b)$, the key Alice outputs, and $s$ (Katz and Lindell, Definition 10.1).
