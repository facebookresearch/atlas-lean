# Elliptic-curve discrete logarithm problem

Given points $P$ and $Q$ of an elliptic curve over a finite field, with $Q$ in the cyclic subgroup generated by $P$, the elliptic-curve discrete logarithm problem (ECDLP) asks for an integer $k$ with $Q=[k]P$.

Let $\mathbb{F}_q$ be a finite field and $E$ an elliptic curve over $\mathbb{F}_q$ given by a Weierstrass equation. Its group $E(\mathbb{F}_q)$ consists of the affine solutions over $\mathbb{F}_q$ together with the point at infinity $O$, under the chord-and-tangent law written additively; for $k\in\mathbb{Z}$ and $P\in E(\mathbb{F}_q)$, $[k]P$ is the $k$-fold sum of $P$, with $[0]P=O$ and $[-k]P=-[k]P$.

An instance of the ECDLP is a triple $(E,P,Q)$ with $P,Q\in E(\mathbb{F}_q)$ and $Q\in\langle P\rangle$. A solution is an integer $k$ with

$$
Q=[k]P .
$$

If $P$ has order $n$, the solutions form one residue class modulo $n$, and one usually asks for the representative $0\le k<n$; for $P=O$ the only instance is $Q=O$ with answer $0$. This is the discrete logarithm problem of the group $E(\mathbb{F}_q)$ (given $g,h$, find $a$ with $h=g^a$), written additively, restricted to instances that have a solution. In cryptographic use one typically takes $n$ prime and $Q=[k]P$ for uniform $k$, which is the discrete-logarithm experiment in the cyclic group $\langle P\rangle$ of order $n$.

## Expansion

The group law on the points of a Weierstrass curve is the standard chord-and-tangent law. Galbraith's Definition 13.0.1 leaves the distribution of the instance open; the experiment page fixes the uniform one used in security statements and is one example of it.
