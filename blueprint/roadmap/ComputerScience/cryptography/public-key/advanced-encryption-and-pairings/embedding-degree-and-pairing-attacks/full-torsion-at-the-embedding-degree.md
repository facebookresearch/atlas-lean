# All $r$-torsion is rational over the embedding-degree extension

If $r$ is a prime dividing $\#E(\mathbb{F}_q)$ with $r\nmid q$ and $r\nmid q-1$, then $E[r]\subseteq E(\mathbb{F}_{q^k})$ if and only if $r\mid q^k-1$; so the field generated by the $r$-torsion of $E$ is $\mathbb{F}_{q^{k(q,r)}}$ (Balasubramanian–Koblitz).

**Theorem.** Let $E$ be an elliptic curve over $\mathbb{F}_q$ and let $r$ be a prime with $r\mid\#E(\mathbb{F}_q)$, $\gcd(r,q)=1$ and $r\nmid q-1$. For every integer $k\ge1$,

$$
E[r]\subseteq E(\mathbb{F}_{q^k}) \iff r\mid q^k-1 .
$$

Here $E[r]$ is the group of points of $E$ over an algebraic closure of $\mathbb{F}_q$ killed by $r$. In particular the least $k$ with $E[r]\subseteq E(\mathbb{F}_{q^k})$ is the embedding degree $k(q,r)$, and then $E[r]$ is the direct sum of the Frobenius eigenspaces $E[r]\cap\ker(\pi-1)=E(\mathbb{F}_q)[r]$ and $E[r]\cap\ker(\pi-q)$, each cyclic of order $r$.

The hypothesis $r\nmid q-1$ cannot be dropped: when $r\mid q-1$ the embedding degree is $1$, while $E[r]\subseteq E(\mathbb{F}_q)$ requires $E(\mathbb{F}_q)$ to contain a subgroup isomorphic to $(\mathbb{Z}/r\mathbb{Z})^2$.

## Expansion

Write $\#E(\mathbb{F}_q)=q+1-t$. Since $r$ is prime to the characteristic, $E[r]$ has $r^2$ points and is a two-dimensional vector space over $\mathbb{F}_r$. The $q$-power Frobenius $\pi$ acts $\mathbb{F}_r$-linearly on $E[r]$ with characteristic polynomial $x^2-tx+q\bmod r$ (trace $t$ and determinant $q$; an annihilating relation alone would not show that $q$ occurs as an eigenvalue), and $r\mid q+1-t$ gives $x^2-tx+q\equiv(x-1)(x-q)\pmod r$. Since $q\not\equiv1\pmod r$ the eigenvalues $1$ and $q$ are distinct and both occur, so $\pi$ is diagonalizable on $E[r]$ with one-dimensional eigenspaces, and $\pi^k$ acts trivially on $E[r]$ exactly when $q^k\equiv1\pmod r$; this proves both directions. Prerequisite still to add: the characteristic polynomial of Frobenius acting on $E[r]$ is $x^2-tx+q\bmod r$, with $t$ the Frobenius trace (for instance, determinant $q$ from the Weil pairing and trace $t$ from the point count). Sutherland's Lemma 23.33 is cited only for the case $q\not\equiv1\pmod n$: for $q\equiv1\pmod n$ Frobenius need not act trivially on $E[n]$ (over $\mathbb{F}_5$ the curve $y^2=x^3+1$ has a single rational point of order $2$).
