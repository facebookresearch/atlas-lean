# Distortion map

A distortion map for a point $P$ of prime order $r$ and a non-degenerate bilinear pairing $e$ on $E[r]$ is an endomorphism $\psi$ of $E$ with $e(P,\psi(P))\ne1$; then $(x,y)\mapsto e(x,\psi(y))$ is a nontrivial symmetric bilinear pairing on $\langle P\rangle$.

Let $E$ be an elliptic curve over $\mathbb{F}_q$, let $r$ be a prime with $r\nmid q$ and $r\mid\#E(\mathbb{F}_q)$, let $E[r]$ be the $r$-torsion of $E$ over an algebraic closure $\overline{\mathbb{F}}_q$, and let $e:E[r]\times E[r]\to\mu_r$ be a non-degenerate bilinear pairing. Let $P\in E(\mathbb{F}_q)$ have order exactly $r$. A *distortion map* with respect to $E$, $r$, $e$ and $P$ is an endomorphism $\psi$ of $E$ defined over $\overline{\mathbb{F}}_q$ (a morphism $E\to E$ fixing $O$, hence a group homomorphism) such that

$$
e(P,\psi(P))\ne1 .
$$

For such $\psi$ the map $\hat e(x,y)=e(x,\psi(y))$ on $\langle P\rangle\times\langle P\rangle$ satisfies $\hat e([a]P,[b]P)=\zeta^{ab}$ with $\zeta=e(P,\psi(P))$ of order $r$; it is a symmetric bilinear pairing of the cyclic group $\langle P\rangle$ of order $r$ into $\mu_r$ that is nontrivial. If $e$ is alternating ($e(X,X)=1$ for all $X$), every distortion map satisfies $\psi(P)\notin\langle P\rangle$.

**Example.** Let $a\in\{0,1\}$, $E_a:y^2+y=x^3+x+a$ over $\mathbb{F}_2$, and $\psi(x,y)=(x+s^2,\,y+sx+t)$ with $s\in\mathbb{F}_{2^2}$, $t\in\mathbb{F}_{2^4}$, $s^2=s+1$, $t^2=t+s$. For $l$ odd and $P\in E_a(\mathbb{F}_{2^l})$ of odd prime order $r$, one has $\psi(P)\in E_a(\mathbb{F}_{2^{4l}})\setminus E_a(\mathbb{F}_{2^{2l}})$, so $\psi(P)\notin\langle P\rangle$, and $\psi$ is a distortion map for $P$ and the Weil pairing $e_r$.

## Expansion

$\hat e([a]P,[b]P)=e([a]P,[b]\psi(P))=\zeta^{ab}$ uses that $\psi$ is a homomorphism and that $e$ is bilinear; $\zeta\ne1$ in $\mu_r$ has order $r$. For alternating $e$, $\psi(P)=[c]P$ would give $\zeta=e(P,P)^c=1$. In the example, $\psi(P)$ and $P$ are independent elements of order $r$, so they generate $E[r]$, and the Weil pairing of two points of order $r$ is nontrivial exactly when they generate a non-cyclic group (Sutherland, Corollary 23.31). The bilinear-group page records the induced pairing on the cyclic group $\langle P\rangle$; the full pairing $e$ lives on the rank-two group $E[r]$. Prerequisites still to add: endomorphisms of $E$, the structure of $E[r]$ as a two-dimensional $\mathbb{F}_r$-vector space, and, for the example, the Weil pairing and its non-degeneracy on independent points. The bilinear-group page serves as algebraic vocabulary for the induced pairing on $\langle P\rangle$.
