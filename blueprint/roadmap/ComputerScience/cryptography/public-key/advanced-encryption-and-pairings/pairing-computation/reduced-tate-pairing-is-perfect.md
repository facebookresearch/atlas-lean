# The reduced Tate–Lichtenbaum pairing is perfect

If $n$ divides $\#F-1$ for a finite field $F$, the reduced Tate–Lichtenbaum pairing $E(F)[n]\times E(F)/nE(F)\to\mu_n$ is bilinear and perfect: each of the two groups is identified with the group of homomorphisms from the other into $\mu_n$ (Frey–Rück).

**Theorem.** Let $F$ be a finite field with $Q$ elements, $E$ an elliptic curve over $F$, and $n\ge1$ with $n\mid Q-1$. The reduced Tate–Lichtenbaum pairing

$$
\hat t_n:E(F)[n]\times E(F)/nE(F)\longrightarrow\mu_n
$$

is bilinear, and the induced homomorphisms

$$
E(F)[n]\to\operatorname{Hom}\bigl(E(F)/nE(F),\mu_n\bigr),\qquad E(F)/nE(F)\to\operatorname{Hom}\bigl(E(F)[n],\mu_n\bigr)
$$

are isomorphisms. In particular, if $P\in E(F)[n]$ and $\hat t_n(P,R)=1$ for all $R\in E(F)$ then $P=O$, and if $R\in E(F)$ and $\hat t_n(P,R)=1$ for all $P\in E(F)[n]$ then $R\in nE(F)$. Since $z\mapsto z^{(Q-1)/n}$ is an isomorphism $F^\times/(F^\times)^n\to\mu_n$, the same holds for the unreduced pairing $t_n$.

## Expansion

Bruin's proof, for the Jacobian $J$ of a complete smooth geometrically connected curve over a finite field $k$ with $n\mid\#k-1$ (here the curve is $E$ and $J=E$ with its canonical principal polarization): the absolute Galois group $G_k=\operatorname{Gal}(\bar k/k)$ is procyclic, topologically generated by the $\#k$-power Frobenius $\sigma$, and for a finite discrete $G_k$-module $M$ the continuous cohomology satisfies $H^1(G_k,M)\cong M/(\sigma-1)M$ (finiteness is needed: for $M=\mathbb{Z}$ with trivial action every continuous homomorphism $G_k\to\mathbb{Z}$ is zero, while $M/(\sigma-1)M=\mathbb{Z}$); only the finite module $J[n]\,(\bar k)$ is used. The Kummer sequence for multiplication by $n$ and Lang's theorem $H^1(G_k,J(\bar k))=0$ identify $J(k)/nJ(k)$ with $H^1(G_k,J[n]\,(\bar k))$; the finite module $M=J[n]\,(\bar k)$, killed by $\#k-1$, has a perfect pairing between the invariants of its Cartier dual and $H^1(G_k,M)$ (Lemma 2.1); Cartier duality for $[n]$, via the Weil pairing, turns this into a perfect pairing $J[n]\,(k)\times J(k)/nJ(k)\to k^\times$; and Theorem 4.1 shows, using Weil reciprocity over $\bar k$ for divisors with disjoint supports, that it equals the Frey–Rück pairing $f(D_R)^{(\#k-1)/n}$. Bilinearity also follows directly from Weil reciprocity (Galbraith, Theorem 26.3.3(1)). The hypothesis $n\mid Q-1$ cannot be weakened to "$F^\times$ contains a nontrivial $n$-th root of unity": for $y^2=x^3+1$ over $\mathbb{F}_5$, $E(\mathbb{F}_5)$ is cyclic of order $6$, while for $n=6$ the target $\mathbb{F}_5^\times/(\mathbb{F}_5^\times)^6$ has order $2$. Prerequisites still to add: Lang's theorem for elliptic curves over finite fields, continuous first cohomology of the procyclic absolute Galois group of a finite field with coefficients in a finite discrete module, Cartier duality for $E[n]\,(\bar k)$ through the Weil pairing, and Weil reciprocity over $\bar k$.
