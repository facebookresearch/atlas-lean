# Interactive arguments

Let $f$ take bitstrings to a finite set of values with a fixed bitstring encoding. On common input $x$ of length $n$, the prescribed deterministic prover $P$ first sends a claim $y$ for $f(x)$. This is separate from the next $k(n)$ messages. Either party may send the first of those messages; the senders then alternate. Each sees $x$, $y$ and all messages sent so far, in order. Only the verifier $V$ sees the private tape $r$ of independent fair bits. It uses the **same tape** to compute its messages and to decide whether to accept. With $r$ and the strategy fixed, the transcript and the decision are deterministic. Write $p(A,x)$ for the probability, over $r$, that $V$ accepts when interacting with strategy $A$.

Fix errors $\delta_c,\delta_s\in[0,1]$. In both an **interactive proof** and an **argument**, the honest prover's claim and replies must be computable, but need not be efficient. **Completeness** is pointwise: for every $x$, the prescribed $P$ claims $f(x)$ and $p(P,x)\geq 1-\delta_c$. The two systems differ in their soundness condition:

- **Statistical soundness (proof).** For every deterministic strategy $A$ and every $x$ on which its initial claim is wrong, $p(A,x)\leq\delta_s$.
- **Computational soundness (argument, fixed-error asymptotic convention).** For each fixed deterministic strategy $A$ with uniform polynomial-time Turing algorithms for both its initial claim and its replies, there is a length $N_A$ such that for **every** $x$ with $|x|\geq N_A$ on which its initial claim is wrong, $p(A,x)\leq\delta_s$. The threshold can depend on $A$ but not on $x$. There is no negligible additive term, separate security parameter or common concrete time budget.

Thaler's Definition 3.2 restricts soundness to polynomial-time strategies; it does not specify a length threshold. The second clause above makes an asymptotic reading precise for a uniform family indexed by input length. On a strictly pointwise reading, a strategy could have a finite table of replies specialised to any one fixed input. In the asymptotic clause, short exceptional inputs need not satisfy the bound, but a fixed efficient strategy cannot have exceptions at arbitrarily large lengths. Every statistically sound proof satisfies the argument condition with $N_A=0$. Thaler calls the error parameters valid when both are at most $1/3$.

**Resource convention.** The count $k(n)$, tape length and payload capacity per message are fixed natural-coefficient polynomials in $n$. Only the prefix fitting in a slot is transmitted. There are $\lceil k(n)/2\rceil$ rounds after the claim. The finite encoded range gives one upper bound on the bit length of the initial claim; adding $k(n)$ times the slot width therefore bounds all bits sent. On a tape of the scheduled length, every local view through message $k(n)$ has polynomial length. There are at most $k(n)+1$ calls to the verifier's polynomial-time send and decision routines, so their total machine time is polynomial in $n$. Separately, forming the views by tagging and copying bounded words takes polynomial time: each copy is linear in the view length and only polynomially many views are needed.
