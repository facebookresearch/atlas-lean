# Polynomial commitment schemes

A polynomial commitment scheme for polynomials of degree at most $t$ over $\mathbb{Z}_p$ consists of six algorithms: $\mathrm{Setup}(1^\kappa, t)$ producing a public key $PK$ (and a secret key not used afterwards); $\mathrm{Commit}(PK, \phi)$ producing a commitment $C$ and opening information $d$; $\mathrm{Open}(PK, C, \phi, d)$ revealing the polynomial together with $d$; $\mathrm{VerifyPoly}(PK, C, \phi, d)$ checking a full opening; $\mathrm{CreateWitness}(PK, \phi, i, d)$ producing $(i, \phi(i), w_i)$; and $\mathrm{VerifyEval}(PK, C, i, \phi(i), w_i)$ checking an evaluation. It must be correct (for $(C, d) = \mathrm{Commit}(PK, \phi)$, the opening $(\phi, d)$ and every witness $\mathrm{CreateWitness}(PK, \phi, i, d)$ verify); *polynomial binding* (no efficient adversary outputs $C$ and two pairs $(\phi, d) \ne (\phi', d')$ with $\phi \ne \phi'$ both accepted by $\mathrm{VerifyPoly}$); and *evaluation binding* (no efficient adversary outputs $C$ and two accepted evaluation witnesses for different values at the same index). It is *hiding* if, for a polynomial chosen at random among those of degree at most $t$, an adversary given $PK$, $C$ and at most $\deg \phi$ accepted evaluations cannot determine the value at any further index: with non-negligible probability for efficient adversaries (computational hiding), or at all for unbounded ones (unconditional hiding).
