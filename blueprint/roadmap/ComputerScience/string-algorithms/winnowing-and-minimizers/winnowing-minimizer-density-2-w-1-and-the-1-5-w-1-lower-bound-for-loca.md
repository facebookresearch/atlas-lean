# Winnowing: density 2/(w+1) and the lower bound for local fingerprinting

Let $h_1, h_2, \dots$ be the hashes of the consecutive $k$-grams of a document, where $k$ is the noise threshold, and let $w = t - k + 1$ for a guarantee threshold $t \ge k$. An algorithm is local if, for every window $h_i, \dots, h_{i+w-1}$ of $w$ consecutive hashes, it selects one of these hashes as a fingerprint and the choice depends only on the window's contents. Winnowing selects in each window the minimum hash, taking the rightmost occurrence in case of ties. Schleimer, Wilkerson and Aiken (2003) prove a deterministic guarantee: for every document, any local algorithm finds every matching pair of substrings of length at least $t$ (Lemma 1). Their density bounds concern random input, modeled here by independent hashes uniformly distributed on a continuum, so that ties occur with probability zero and the relative order of any $w + 1$ consecutive hashes is uniformly random. In this model the density of winnowing, the expected fraction of hashes selected as fingerprints, tends to $2/(w + 1)$ as the number of hashes in the document tends to infinity (Section 3.1, which ignores ties); and every local algorithm with noise threshold $k$ and guarantee $t = w + k - 1$ has density at least $1.5/(w + 1)$ (Theorem 1), so winnowing is within $33\%$ of the lower bound; they conjecture that $2/(w + 1)$ is a lower bound for every local algorithm in this model. For hashes from a finite range, ties have positive probability and $2/(w + 1)$ is only an approximation for large ranges: with $w = 2$ and independent fair binary hashes, rightmost tie-breaking selects a new position in $6$ of the $8$ equally likely triples, so the limiting density is $3/4$ rather than $2/3$.

## Expansion

1. The model: $k$-gram hashes, windows of size $w$, local algorithms, and the noise and guarantee thresholds.
2. Lemma 1: a match of length at least $t$ contains a full window of hashes, and a local algorithm selects the same hash in both copies.
3. The density of winnowing for tie-free random hashes: a charging argument in which a window is charged when it selects a hash not selected by the previous window, which happens when the minimum of the union of two consecutive windows lies at its leftmost or rightmost position, each with probability $1/(w + 1)$.
4. Theorem 1: the lower bound for random hashes, using only pairs of windows far enough apart to be disjoint.
5. Separate target: the exact density of winnowing with rightmost tie-breaking for i.i.d. hashes uniform on a finite range, and its convergence to $2/(w + 1)$ as the range grows.
6. Comparison with the 0 mod $p$ selection method, as two separate statements. Section 3.1.1: ordinary 0 mod $p$, which selects the hashes divisible by $p$, taken at the same density as winnowing; for $p > 1$ it has no match-length guarantee, since a window of any length may contain no hash divisible by $p$, and the source computes the probability that it misses a match. Section 3.1.2: Safe 0 mod $p$, which also selects hashes to cover the gaps, taken at the same guarantee $t$ as winnowing, where its density is larger.
