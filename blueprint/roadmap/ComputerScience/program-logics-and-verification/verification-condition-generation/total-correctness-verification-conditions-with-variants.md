# Total-correctness verification conditions with variants

Write $[P]\, C\, [Q]$ for a total-correctness specification. States assign standard integers to variables, with total expression evaluation in the standard integer interpretation. Annotate commands as for [syntactic verification conditions](syntactic-verification-conditions.md), and also give every while loop an integer-valued expression $E$ (the variant) after its invariant: $[P]\ \mathtt{while}\ S\ \mathtt{do}\ \{R\}[E]\, C\ [Q]$. The verification conditions for assignments, conditionals and sequences are generated by the same rules as for partial correctness, and for a loop they are

- $P \Rightarrow R$;
- $R \wedge \neg S \Rightarrow Q$;
- $R \wedge S \Rightarrow E \ge 0$;
- the verification conditions of $[R \wedge S \wedge (E = n)]\, C\, [R \wedge (E < n)]$,

where $n$ is an auxiliary variable not occurring in $P$, $C$, $S$, $R$, $E$ or $Q$. They follow the while rule for total correctness: from $[P \wedge S \wedge (E = n)]\, C\, [P \wedge (E < n)]$ and $P \wedge S \Rightarrow E \ge 0$ infer $[P]\ \mathtt{while}\ S\ \mathtt{do}\ C\ [P \wedge \neg S]$, with $n$ fresh for $P,C,S,E$. Nonnegative integer values at continuing loop heads admit no infinite strictly decreasing chain, which is the well-foundedness role played by the natural-valued measure in the [variant rule for while loops](../hoare-logic/while-variant.md).
