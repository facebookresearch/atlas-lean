# Named, de Bruijn and locally nameless lambda terms

There are three representations of untyped $\lambda$-terms:
- *named terms* $t ::= x \mid \lambda x.\, t \mid t\, t$, taken up to $\alpha$-equivalence;
- *locally nameless* terms $t ::= \mathrm{bvar}\ i \mid \mathrm{fvar}\ x \mid \mathrm{abs}\ t \mid \mathrm{app}\ t\ t$, where bound variables are de Bruijn indices and free variables keep their names;
- pure *de Bruijn* terms $t ::= i \mid \lambda.\, t \mid t\, t$.

A locally nameless term is *locally closed* if every index $i$ lies under at least $i + 1$ abstractions. For example, $\mathrm{abs}\ (\mathrm{bvar}\ 1)$ is not locally closed. Define $\ulcorner t \urcorner_\ell$ for a named term $t$ and a list $\ell$ of names, the enclosing binders from innermost outward:
- $\ulcorner x \urcorner_\ell = \mathrm{bvar}\ i$ if $x$ first occurs in $\ell$ at position $i$, and $\mathrm{fvar}\ x$ otherwise;
- $\ulcorner \lambda x.\, t \urcorner_\ell = \mathrm{abs}\ \ulcorner t \urcorner_{x :: \ell}$;
- $\ulcorner t_1\, t_2 \urcorner_\ell = \mathrm{app}\ \ulcorner t_1 \urcorner_\ell\ \ulcorner t_2 \urcorner_\ell$.

Write $\ulcorner t \urcorner = \ulcorner t \urcorner_{[\,]}$. For example, $\lambda x.\, x\, y$ maps to $\mathrm{abs}\ (\mathrm{app}\ (\mathrm{bvar}\ 0)\ (\mathrm{fvar}\ y))$.

**Theorem.**
1. $t =_\alpha t'$ if and only if $\ulcorner t \urcorner = \ulcorner t' \urcorner$. So $\ulcorner \cdot \urcorner$ is well defined and injective on $\alpha$-classes.
2. The image of $\ulcorner \cdot \urcorner$ is exactly the set of locally closed terms. Every locally closed term is $\ulcorner t \urcorner$ for some $t$, obtained by closing each abstraction with a fresh name.
3. Capture-avoiding substitution commutes with the translation: $\ulcorner t[u/x] \urcorner = \ulcorner t \urcorner [x \mapsto \ulcorner u \urcorner]$, where the right-hand side replaces $\mathrm{fvar}\ x$. Moreover, if $\ulcorner \lambda x.\, t \urcorner = \mathrm{abs}\ s$, then the *opening* $s^{\ulcorner u \urcorner}$, which replaces the index of the outermost binder by $\ulcorner u \urcorner$, equals $\ulcorner t[u/x] \urcorner$.
4. Hence $\beta$-reduction commutes with the translation: $t \to_\beta t'$ iff $\ulcorner t \urcorner \to_\beta \ulcorner t' \urcorner$, with $\beta$ on locally closed terms generated by $\mathrm{app}\ (\mathrm{abs}\ s)\ u \to s^u$, the opening of $s$ with $u$, and closed under application and under abstraction by opening: $\mathrm{abs}\ s \to \mathrm{abs}\ s'$ when $s^{\mathrm{fvar}\, y} \to s'^{\mathrm{fvar}\, y}$ for every name $y$ outside some finite set.
5. Pure de Bruijn terms correspond in the same way to closed named terms, and to named terms with a fixed ordering of their free variables by extending $\ell$. In this case the image consists of the terms whose indices are in scope, *well-scoped* with respect to the length of $\ell$.

**Proof sketch.**
1. Induct on terms, generalizing over $\ell$ and over a bijection between the names bound in $t$ and $t'$. A bound occurrence is translated to its binder distance, which renaming does not change.
2. Every index produced is in scope, since it is a position in $\ell$. Conversely, closing with fresh names inverts $\ulcorner \cdot \urcorner$.
3. Induct on $t$. Freshness of bound names makes the substitution capture-free, and opening replaces exactly the index of the outermost binder.
4. This follows from part 3, because redexes correspond one to one.
