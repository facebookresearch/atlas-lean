# Closed terms have a uniquely determined evaluation cost and value

Consider call-by-value reduction $t \to u$ on intrinsically typed terms of the simply typed lambda calculus (left-to-right and weak: an application first reduces its function position, then its argument once the function is a value, and $(\lambda b)\,a$ contracts to the instantiation $b[a]$ only when $a$ is a value, a value being a lambda abstraction; no step goes under a binder), and write $t \to^n v$ for reduction in exactly $n$ steps. For every term $t$ of type $A$ in the empty context there are a natural number $n$ and a term $v$ such that $t \to^n v$, $v$ is a value, and for every natural number $m$ and term $w$ with $t \to^m w$ and $w$ a value, $m = n$ and $w = v$. The cost counts one-step reductions.
