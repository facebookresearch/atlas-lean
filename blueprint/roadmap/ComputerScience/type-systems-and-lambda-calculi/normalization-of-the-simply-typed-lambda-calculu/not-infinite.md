# Strongly normalizing terms begin no infinite call-by-value reduction

Consider call-by-value reduction $t \to u$ on intrinsically typed terms of the simply typed lambda calculus (left-to-right and weak: an application first reduces its function position, then its argument once the function is a value, and $(\lambda b)\,a$ contracts to the instantiation $b[a]$ only when $a$ is a value, a value being a lambda abstraction; no step goes under a binder). If a term $t$ of type $A$ in context $\Gamma$ is strongly normalizing for call-by-value reduction (accessible for the converse relation: inductively, a term is strongly normalizing when every term it reduces to in one step is), then there is no sequence $(s_n)_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$ of terms of type $A$ in $\Gamma$ with $s_0 = t$ and $s_n \to s_{n+1}$ for every $n$.
