# System-wide weak normalization implies strong normalization for pure type systems

Take an arbitrary sort type $S$, an axiom relation on pairs of sorts, and a dependent product rule on triples of sorts, without functionality assumptions. Expressions are de Bruijn variables, sorts, applications, domain-annotated abstractions $\lambda(A).b$, and products $\Pi(A).B$. One-step $\beta$ contracts $(\lambda(A).b)\,a$ to capture-avoiding $b[a/0]$ and closes under both positions of application, abstraction and product, including annotated domains. Conversion is the equivalence generated by these steps.

Use the pure type system judgment $\Gamma\vdash M:A$: an axiom types a sort in the empty context; variable introduction uses a well-sorted declaration and raising; weakening raises both the expression and its type; products use the specified sort triple; abstractions require a typed body and a well-sorted product; applications instantiate the product codomain; conversion requires the new type to have a sort. Contexts are built by adjoining a declaration of a well-sorted type. An expression $M$ is legal in $\Gamma$ if some $A$ satisfies $\Gamma\vdash M:A$ **or** $\Gamma\vdash A:M$.

If for every valid context every legal expression has a finite $\beta$-reduction path to an expression with no outgoing $\beta$ step, then in every valid context every legal expression is accessible for the converse of one-step $\beta$ (every one-step reduct is recursively accessible). The assumption is system-wide, not weak normalization of the individual expression in the conclusion.

## Depends on

- The annotated de Bruijn syntax, compatible closure, and typed legal-expression predicate above.

## Proof depends on

- General substitution and subject-reduction theory for annotated, possibly nonfunctional pure type systems, and a system-wide argument excluding nonaccessible terms from weak normalization.

## Expansion

Identify a native de Bruijn PTS grammar and all typed/context rules, including legality in either position of the typing judgment. Isolate invariance under reduction within annotations and the reflection from system-wide weak to strong normalization. A normalization proof for a particular functional calculus does not discharge the general hypothesis.
