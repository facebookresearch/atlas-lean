# Translating a shifted raw term cancels the shift

Raw terms over a type $B$ of base types are de Bruijn variables $\mathrm{var}\,n$ ($n \in \mathbb{N}$), applications $\mathrm{app}\,f\,a$, and abstractions $\mathrm{lam}\,A\,t$ annotated with a simple type $A$. Locally nameless terms with natural-number names are bound variables $\mathrm{bvar}\,i$ ($i \in \mathbb{N}$, a de Bruijn index), free variables $\mathrm{fvar}\,x$ ($x \in \mathbb{N}$), unannotated abstractions $\mathrm{abs}\,t$ and applications $\mathrm{app}\,t\,u$. For $d \in \mathbb{N}$ and a list $\mathit{names}$ of natural numbers, the translation $\mathrm{tr}_{d,\mathit{names}}$ of raw terms to locally nameless terms sends $\mathrm{var}\,n$ to $\mathrm{bvar}\,n$ if $n < d$ and otherwise to $\mathrm{fvar}$ of the entry of $\mathit{names}$ at position $n - d$ (counting from $0$; the default $0$ when $n - d$ is at least the length of $\mathit{names}$), and is homomorphic on applications, with $\mathrm{lam}\,A\,t \mapsto \mathrm{abs}\,(\mathrm{tr}_{d+1,\mathit{names}}\,t)$. Shifting a raw term by $k$ at cutoff $c$ replaces each variable index $n \ge c$ by $n + k$, leaves smaller indices unchanged, and increases the cutoff by one under each lambda.

For every raw term $t$, all $k, c \in \mathbb{N}$ and every list $\mathit{names}$, the translation $\mathrm{tr}_{k + c,\mathit{names}}$ of $t$ shifted by $k$ at cutoff $c$ equals $\mathrm{tr}_{c,\mathit{names}}\,t$.
