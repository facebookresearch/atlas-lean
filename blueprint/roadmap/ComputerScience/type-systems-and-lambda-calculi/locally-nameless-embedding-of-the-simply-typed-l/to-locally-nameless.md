# Canonical locally nameless embedding of intrinsic terms

Locally nameless terms with natural-number names are bound variables $\mathrm{bvar}\,i$ ($i \in \mathbb{N}$, a de Bruijn index), free variables $\mathrm{fvar}\,x$ ($x \in \mathbb{N}$), unannotated abstractions $\mathrm{abs}\,t$ and applications $\mathrm{app}\,t\,u$. The erasure of an intrinsically typed term (a term of type $A$ in a context $\Gamma$, a list of simple types) is the raw term obtained by replacing each variable by its de Bruijn index (its position in $\Gamma$, counting from $0$ at the front), keeping applications, and keeping each lambda's parameter type as annotation. For $d \in \mathbb{N}$ and a list $\mathit{names}$ of natural numbers, the translation $\mathrm{tr}_{d,\mathit{names}}$ of raw terms to locally nameless terms sends $\mathrm{var}\,n$ to $\mathrm{bvar}\,n$ if $n < d$ and otherwise to $\mathrm{fvar}$ of the entry of $\mathit{names}$ at position $n - d$ (counting from $0$; the default $0$ when $n - d$ is at least the length of $\mathit{names}$), and is homomorphic on applications, with $\mathrm{lam}\,A\,t \mapsto \mathrm{abs}\,(\mathrm{tr}_{d+1,\mathit{names}}\,t)$.

For a term $t$ of type $A$ in context $\Gamma$, the canonical embedding $t^{\mathrm{LN}}$ is the embedding with names $[0, 1, \dots, |\Gamma| - 1]$, that is, $\mathrm{tr}_{0,[0,\dots,|\Gamma|-1]}$ applied to the erasure of $t$: the source variable at position $j$ becomes $\mathrm{fvar}\,j$, and variables bound inside $t$ become bound variables with the same de Bruijn index.
