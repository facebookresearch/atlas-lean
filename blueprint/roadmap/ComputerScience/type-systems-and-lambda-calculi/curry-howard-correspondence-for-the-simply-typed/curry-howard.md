# Curry–Howard correspondence for the simply typed lambda calculus, soundness direction

Propositions over a type $B$ of atoms are built from atoms, conjunction, disjunction and implication $\varphi \to \psi$. Simple types over $B$ are read as propositions by sending a base type $b$ to the atom $b$ and a function type $A \Rightarrow C$ to the implication $\lceil A \rceil \to \lceil C \rceil$, where $\lceil A \rceil$ denotes the proposition of $A$. For base types with decidable equality, the hypotheses $H(\Gamma)$ of a context $\Gamma$ (a list of simple types) are the finite set $\{\lceil A \rceil : A \in \Gamma\}$. Derivations are sequent-style natural deduction for minimal logic over a theory $T$ (a set of propositions), with finite sets of hypotheses: an axiom rule for members of $T$, an assumption rule deriving $\Delta \vdash \varphi$ when $\varphi \in \Delta$, introduction and elimination rules for conjunction and disjunction, implication introduction (from $\Delta \cup \{\varphi\} \vdash \psi$ derive $\Delta \vdash \varphi \to \psi$) and implication elimination (from $\Delta \vdash \varphi \to \psi$ and $\Delta \vdash \varphi$ derive $\Delta \vdash \psi$).

For every theory $T$, context $\Gamma$, simple type $A$ and term $t$ of type $A$ in $\Gamma$, the sequent $H(\Gamma) \vdash \lceil A \rceil$ is derivable over $T$. In particular (taking $T = \emptyset$), the type of every term is derivable in minimal implicational logic from the hypotheses of its context.
