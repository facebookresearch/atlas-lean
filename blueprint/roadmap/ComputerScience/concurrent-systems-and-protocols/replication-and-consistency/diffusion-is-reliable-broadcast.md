# Diffusion implements reliable broadcast

Consider processes, which may crash, and messages that carry their sender, a sequence number and a payload. A state gives the set of crashed processes; for each process, the sequence number of its next message and the set of messages it has received; and for each ordered pair of processes $q, p$, the channel from $q$ to $p$, the multiset of copies of messages that $q$ has sent to $p$ and $p$ has not yet received. In the initial state no process has crashed, every sequence number is $0$, and every set and channel is empty. A process that has not crashed takes the following steps of the diffusion algorithm.

- *R-broadcast*: it R-broadcasts a message $m$ whose sender it is, with its next sequence number, which it then increments, and sends a copy of $m$ to every process, itself included.
- *Receipt*: it takes a copy of a message $m$ from a channel to it. If it has not received $m$ before, it sends a copy of $m$ to every process unless it is the sender of $m$, and then adds $m$ to the messages it has received and R-delivers $m$. Otherwise it discards the copy.
- *Crash*: it crashes, and takes no step afterwards. It may also crash during an R-broadcast of $m$, which is still an R-broadcast of $m$, or during the receipt of a message $m$ it has not received before, which then does not R-deliver $m$. It has then sent copies of $m$ only to the processes of some finite set.

A step may also leave the state unchanged. Take an execution: states $s_0, s_1, \dots$, with $s_0$ initial, and steps $\mu_0, \mu_1, \dots$, where $\mu_t$ takes $s_t$ to $s_{t+1}$; its event at time $t$ is the R-broadcast or R-deliver of $\mu_t$, if any. A process is correct if it never crashes. Suppose that channels to correct processes are reliable: whenever a copy of $m$ is in the channel from $q$ to a correct process $p$ in $s_t$, some step $\mu_{t'}$ with $t' \ge t$ is a receipt by $p$ of a copy of $m$ from that channel. Then the execution, with its correct processes and its events, satisfies [reliable broadcast](reliable-broadcast.md): validity, agreement and uniform integrity. Neither the number of processes nor the number of crashes is bounded.

This is the algorithm of Chandra and Toueg's Figure 4, in an abstraction of their model. In their model a step of a process sends a message to at most one process, so sending a message to all processes takes several steps, and a crash can interrupt them. Here one step sends a message to every process, and a crash during that step leaves copies only at the processes of some finite set. Chandra and Toueg also consider $n$ processes, of which up to $n - 1$ may crash, and their runs require every correct process to take infinitely many steps; the statement above holds without these restrictions.

**Proof.** A process numbers its messages consecutively, so no message is R-broadcast twice, and a copy of a message is in a channel only after its sender has R-broadcast it. A process R-delivers $m$ only when it receives $m$ for the first time, so at most once, and only after the sender of $m$ has R-broadcast $m$: this is uniform integrity. If a correct process $p$ R-broadcasts $m$, it sends $m$ to itself and, as its channel to itself is reliable, later receives that copy. By then it has R-delivered $m$, at that receipt or an earlier one, and after the R-broadcast, since no copy of $m$ exists before it: this is validity. If a correct process $p$ R-delivers $m$, then $p$ has sent $m$ to every process: it relayed $m$, or it is the sender of $m$ and R-broadcast $m$ without crashing. Every correct process then receives a copy of $m$, and R-delivers $m$ then or has done so before, which gives agreement.
