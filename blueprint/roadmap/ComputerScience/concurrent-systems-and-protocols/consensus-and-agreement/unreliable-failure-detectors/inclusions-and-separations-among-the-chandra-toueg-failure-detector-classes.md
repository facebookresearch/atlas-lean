# Inclusions and separations among the Chandra–Toueg failure detector classes

Fix $n$ processes and consider failure patterns (monotone crash sets over discrete time) in which at least one process is correct, together with suspect-set histories. The eight classes of Chandra and Toueg (perfect $\mathcal P$, quasi-perfect $\mathcal Q$, strong $\mathcal S$, weak $\mathcal W$ and their eventual versions $\Diamond\mathcal P, \Diamond\mathcal Q, \Diamond\mathcal S, \Diamond\mathcal W$) are defined by strong or weak completeness combined with strong, weak, eventual strong or eventual weak accuracy. Class $X$ is contained in class $Y$ whenever the completeness and accuracy properties of $X$ imply those of $Y$: strong completeness implies weak completeness, strong accuracy implies weak accuracy and eventual strong accuracy, and each of these two implies eventual weak accuracy. This gives the twelve inclusions $\mathcal P \subseteq \mathcal Q$, $\mathcal S \subseteq \mathcal W$, $\Diamond\mathcal P \subseteq \Diamond\mathcal Q$ and $\Diamond\mathcal S \subseteq \Diamond\mathcal W$ (weakening completeness), and $\mathcal P \subseteq \mathcal S$, $\mathcal P \subseteq \Diamond\mathcal P$, $\mathcal S \subseteq \Diamond\mathcal S$, $\Diamond\mathcal P \subseteq \Diamond\mathcal S$, $\mathcal Q \subseteq \mathcal W$, $\mathcal Q \subseteq \Diamond\mathcal Q$, $\mathcal W \subseteq \Diamond\mathcal W$ and $\Diamond\mathcal Q \subseteq \Diamond\mathcal W$ (weakening accuracy), and their composites. Chandra and Toueg compare the classes by reducibility instead: $\mathcal C \succeq \mathcal C'$ if for every detector in $\mathcal C$ some algorithm transforms it into a detector in $\mathcal C'$, and $\mathcal C \cong \mathcal C'$ if each is reducible to the other. Under reducibility the four completeness-weakening inclusions are not separations, since $\mathcal P \cong \mathcal Q$, $\mathcal S \cong \mathcal W$, $\Diamond\mathcal P \cong \Diamond\mathcal Q$ and $\Diamond\mathcal S \cong \Diamond\mathcal W$ (Corollary 3.5). The accuracy-weakening relations $\mathcal P \succeq \mathcal S$, $\Diamond\mathcal P \succeq \Diamond\mathcal S$, $\mathcal P \succeq \Diamond\mathcal P$, $\mathcal S \succeq \Diamond\mathcal S$ and $\mathcal P \succeq \Diamond\mathcal S$ are all strict, and $\mathcal S$ and $\Diamond\mathcal P$ are incomparable (Theorem 8.1, stated there without proof); together with Corollary 3.5 this characterizes all relations among the eight classes under reducibility.

## Proof sketch

Inclusions follow by unfolding the completeness and accuracy properties; weak completeness and weak accuracy use the existence of a correct process. The equivalences of Corollary 3.5 come from the trivial reduction (Observation 2.6.1) and the suspect-forwarding reduction from weak to strong completeness (Theorem 3.4). As set inclusions, derived from the definitions and not stated by Chandra and Toueg, all twelve inclusions are strict for $n \ge 3$; for $n = 2$ weak and strong completeness coincide, so the four completeness-weakening inclusions become equalities while the eight accuracy inclusions stay strict; and for $n = 1$ weak and strong accuracy coincide as well, and the classes collapse to $\mathcal P \subsetneq \Diamond\mathcal P$. The set separations are explicit histories in which no property of the larger class fails: suspecting a correct process only at time $0$ breaks perpetual accuracy but not eventual accuracy (any $n$); with two correct processes, one permanently suspecting the other and itself never suspected, weak accuracy holds but eventual strong accuracy fails ($n \ge 2$); with one crashed and two correct processes, permanent suspicion of the crashed process by only one of them gives weak but not strong completeness ($n \ge 3$). For $n \le 2$ a crash leaves exactly one correct process, so the two completeness properties coincide, and for $n = 1$ the only process is correct, so strong and weak accuracy coincide, perpetual and eventual alike.
