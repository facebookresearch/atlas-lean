# Locality of the output of a $t$-round algorithm

Let $A$ be an [algorithm](local-algorithm.md) in the [LOCAL model](the-local-model.md) and $t : \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{N}$, let $G$ and $G'$ be simple graphs on the same finite set $V$ of $n$ nodes, $\mathrm{id}, \mathrm{id}' : V \to \{1, \dots, n^c\}$ injective identifier maps with the same exponent $c$, $x, x' : V \to I$ inputs and $v \in V$. Suppose that $\mathrm{id}(u) = \mathrm{id}'(u)$ and $x(u) = x'(u)$ for every node $u$ at distance at most $t(n)$ from $v$ in $G$, and that every node at distance less than $t(n)$ from $v$ in $G$ has the same neighbours in $G$ as in $G'$. Then $A$, run as a $t$-round algorithm, outputs the same at $v$ on $G$ with $\mathrm{id}$ and $x$ as on $G'$ with $\mathrm{id}'$ and $x'$. The algorithm $A$ and the number $t(n)$ of rounds are the same in both runs.

Distances are taken in $G$; a node in another connected component is at infinite distance. Nothing is assumed about the edges between two nodes at distance at least $t(n)$ from $v$.

**Proof.** The output at $v$ is a function of the state of $v$ after $t(n)$ rounds. Show more generally that for every $k$, if the identifiers and inputs agree at distance at most $k$ from $v$ and the nodes at distance less than $k$ from $v$ have the same neighbours in both graphs, then $v$ is in the same state after $k$ rounds in both executions. This goes by induction on $k$, for all nodes $v$ at once. The case $k = 0$ is the hypothesis at $v$. For $k + 1$, the node $v$, at distance $0 < k + 1$, has the same neighbours in $G$ and $G'$ and the same identifier, which addresses their messages to it; its own state after $k$ rounds agrees by induction; and so do the states after $k$ rounds of its neighbours $u$, since a node at distance at most $k$ (or less than $k$) from $u$ is at distance at most $k + 1$ (or less than $k + 1$) from $v$.
