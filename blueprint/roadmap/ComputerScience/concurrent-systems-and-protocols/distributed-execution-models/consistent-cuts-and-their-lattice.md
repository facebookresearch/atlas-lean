# Consistent cuts and their lattice

Fix a well-formed execution of a finite set $P$ of processes: a finite sequence of events, each of which belongs to a process and may receive the message sent by an earlier event. Its events, identified with their positions, form a finite set $E$, partially ordered by the causality (happened-before) relation $<$, the transitive closure of process order (an earlier event of the same process) and message order (the sending of a message before its receipt). The *initial event* of a process is its first event. Each event $e$ carries its vector timestamp $C(e) \in \mathbb{N}^P$, computed by the vector clock algorithm.

- **Cuts.** A *cut* is a set $C \subseteq E$ that contains the initial event of every process and, with any event of a process, all earlier events of the same process. A cut $C_2$ is *later than* $C_1$ if $C_1 \subseteq C_2$. The cuts are closed under union and intersection, so with these operations they form a lattice.
- **Consistent cuts.** A cut $C$ is *consistent* if it is left-closed under causality: $e' \in C$ and $e < e'$ imply $e \in C$. Equivalently, every message received in $C$ was sent in $C$: if $e' \in C$ receives the message sent by $e$, then $e \in C$. The consistent cuts are closed under union and intersection, so they form a sublattice of the lattice of cuts. Any finitely many consistent cuts, at least one, therefore have a consistent cut later than all of them, namely their union, and one earlier than all of them, namely their intersection.
- **Vector-time criterion.** The *global time* $t_X \in \mathbb{N}^P$ of a set $X \subseteq E$ is the componentwise maximum of the timestamps of its events, and the zero vector if $X$ is empty. For a cut $X$ whose last event on each process $i$ is $c_i$, $t_X = \sup_{i \in P} C(c_i)$, and $X$ is consistent if and only if $t_X = (C(c_i)[i])_{i \in P}$, that is, if and only if $C(c_j)[i] \le C(c_i)[i]$ for all processes $i$ and $j$.
- **Isomorphism.** The *possible time vectors* of $E$ are the global times of its consistent cuts. They form a sublattice of $\mathbb{N}^P$, ordered componentwise, and $X \mapsto t_X$ is an isomorphism from the lattice of consistent cuts, ordered by inclusion, onto the lattice of possible time vectors: it is an order isomorphism, and it sends the union and the intersection of two consistent cuts to the componentwise maximum and minimum of their global times.

A process need not have any events: the statements do not assume an initial event for each process, and the vector-time criterion concerns the cuts that have a last event on every process.
