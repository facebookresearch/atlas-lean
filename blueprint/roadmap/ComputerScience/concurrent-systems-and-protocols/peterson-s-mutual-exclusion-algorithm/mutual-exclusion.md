# Mutual exclusion in Peterson's algorithm

In Peterson's algorithm for two processes, process $i$ repeatedly raises its flag, sets $\mathrm{turn}$ to the other process, waits while the other process's flag is raised and $\mathrm{turn}$ favors the other process, enters its critical section, leaves it, and then lowers its flag and returns to the start. Each of these steps is one atomic step of the process: raising the flag, setting $\mathrm{turn}$, one test of the waiting condition (which changes nothing while the condition holds and otherwise enters the critical section), leaving the critical section, and lowering the flag. In no state reachable from an initial state (both processes idle, both flags down, arbitrary $\mathrm{turn}$) are both processes in their critical sections.
