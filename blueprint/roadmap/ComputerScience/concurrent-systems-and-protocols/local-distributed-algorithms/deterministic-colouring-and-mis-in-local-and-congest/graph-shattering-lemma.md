# Graph shattering after a randomized pre-shattering phase

Let $G = (V, E)$ be a simple connected graph with $n$ vertices and maximum degree $\Delta \ge 2$, whose vertices carry distinct identifiers from $\{1, \dots, n^{O(1)}\}$. Fix a constant $k \ge 1$ and let $c$ be a sufficiently large constant depending on $k$. Suppose a randomized process leaves a random set $B \subseteq V$ of surviving (undecided) vertices such that, for every set $S$ of vertices that are pairwise at distance greater than $k$ in $G$, $\Pr[S \subseteq B] \le \Delta^{-c\lvert S \rvert}$. This joint condition holds, for instance, when every vertex survives with probability at most $\Delta^{-c}$ and its survival is determined by random bits within distance $\lfloor k/2 \rfloor$ of it, independent across vertices. Then with probability at least $1 - n^{-\Omega(c)}$, $B$ contains no set of at least $\log_\Delta n$ vertices that are pairwise at distance greater than $k$ and connected in the power graph $G^{2k+1}$. Consequently every connected component of $G[B]$ has $O(\Delta^{k} \log_\Delta n)$ vertices, so the remaining problem can be solved on components of size $\mathrm{poly}(\Delta) \cdot \log n$.

## Proof sketch

A connected component $C$ of $G[B]$ contains a set $S$ that is pairwise more than $k$ apart and connected in $G^{2k+1}$ with $\lvert S \rvert \ge \lvert C \rvert / O(\Delta^k)$: add vertices of $C$ greedily in breadth-first order, each time discarding the $O(\Delta^k)$ vertices within distance $k$ of the new one; every vertex of $C$ is then within distance $k$ of $S$, and consecutive choices are joined by paths of length at most $2k+1$. The pairwise lower bound $k+1$ is on all pairs of $S$, while the upper bound $2k+1$ is only on the edges of a spanning tree of $S$ in $G^{2k+1}$; so such an $S$ of size $t$ contains a $t$-vertex subtree of $G^{2k+1}$, and there are at most $n \cdot (4\Delta^{2k+1})^t$ such trees. By the joint condition each tree lies in $B$ with probability at most $\Delta^{-ct}$, and a union bound over trees with $t = \lceil \log_\Delta n \rceil$ gives failure probability $n^{-\Omega(c)}$ for large $c$.
