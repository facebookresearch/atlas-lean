# Approximating the optimal cost of almost-sure reachability in POMDPs is undecidable

In a partially observable Markov decision process with integer costs, the least expected total cost of reaching a target almost surely cannot be determined algorithmically: deciding whether this optimal cost is $-\infty$ or $1$, given that one of the two holds, is undecidable, and the source concludes that it cannot be approximated additively or multiplicatively for any precision $\epsilon > 0$.

**Setting (Chatterjee, Chmelík, Gupta and Kanodia).** A POMDP is a tuple $G = (S, A, \delta, Z, \mathcal{O}, \lambda_0)$ with a finite set $S$ of states, a finite set $A$ of actions, a probabilistic transition function $\delta : S \times A \to \mathcal{D}(S)$, a finite set $Z$ of observations, an observation function $\mathcal{O} : S \to Z$ and an initial distribution $\lambda_0$ whose support lies in a single observation class. A *play* is an infinite sequence $s_0 a_0 s_1 a_1 \dots$ with $s_0$ in the support of $\lambda_0$ and $\delta(s_i, a_i)(s_{i+1}) > 0$ for all $i$. A *strategy* maps each finite prefix of a play to a distribution on $A$ and is *observation-based*: two prefixes with the same sequence of observations and actions receive the same distribution. A strategy $\sigma$ and $\lambda_0$ determine a probability measure $\mathbb{P}^\sigma_{\lambda_0}$ on plays with expectation $\mathbb{E}^\sigma_{\lambda_0}$.

The objective has a set $T \subseteq S$ of absorbing target states and a cost function $c : S \times A \to \mathbb{Z}$ with $c(t, a) = 0$ for $t \in T$. The total cost of a play is $\mathrm{Total}(s_0 a_0 s_1 a_1 \dots) = \sum_{i \ge 0} c(s_i, a_i)$. A strategy is *almost-sure winning* if it reaches $T$ with probability $1$; then $\mathrm{Total}$ is finite almost surely, but its expectation need not exist, since $\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{Total}^+]$ and $\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{Total}^-]$ can both be infinite. A strategy's value $\mathrm{val}(\sigma) = \mathbb{E}^\sigma_{\lambda_0}[\mathrm{Total}] \in [-\infty, \infty]$ is defined when $\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{Total}^+] < \infty$ or $\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{Total}^-] < \infty$, and the *optimal cost* is

$$
\mathrm{optCost} = \inf \{\, \mathrm{val}(\sigma) : \sigma \text{ observation-based, almost-sure winning, with } \mathrm{val}(\sigma) \text{ defined} \,\}.
$$

**Theorem (Chatterjee, Chmelík, Gupta and Kanodia).** The following promise problem is undecidable: given a POMDP with rational transition probabilities and a rational initial distribution, a set $T$ of absorbing target states and an integer cost function $c$, such that either $\mathrm{optCost} = -\infty$ or $\mathrm{optCost} = 1$, decide which of the two holds. It remains undecidable for POMDPs with a single observation and costs that depend only on the state and take values in $\{-1, 0, 1\}$ (Lemmas 1–3 of the copy read). The reduction starts from the strict emptiness problem for probabilistic finite automata with threshold $\tfrac12$. Section 2 of the source takes the expectation for every almost-sure winning strategy without addressing strategies whose expected cost is undefined; restricting the infimum to strategies with defined value keeps its argument: the witnesses of Lemma 1 are pure finite-memory strategies whose plays have bounded length before the target, and the round-by-round lower bound behind Lemma 3 holds whenever the expectation exists.

The source derives from this, in one sentence, that approximating $\mathrm{optCost}$ is undecidable for every $\epsilon > 0$, additively and multiplicatively (Theorem 1 of the copy read). There the *additive* and *multiplicative approximation problems* ask for an almost-sure winning strategy $\sigma$ with $\mathrm{val}(\sigma) \le \mathrm{optCost} + \epsilon$, respectively $\mathrm{val}(\sigma) \le \mathrm{optCost} \cdot (1 + \epsilon)$; the source does not say how such a strategy is output or what the bound means when $\mathrm{optCost} = -\infty$, so the promise problem above is the precise statement. In contrast, for positive costs $c : S \times A \to \mathbb{N}$ both approximation problems are decidable (Theorem 2 of the copy read).

## Expansion

The reduction is from the strict emptiness problem for probabilistic finite automata: does some finite word $w$ have acceptance probability greater than $\frac{1}{2}$? A probabilistic automaton is a POMDP with one observation. From an automaton with states $Q$, final states $F$ and initial state $q_0$ one builds a POMDP with states $Q \times \{-1, 1\} \cup \{\mathit{good}, \mathit{bad}, t\}$, initial state $(q_0, 1)$, a single observation, and three new actions. One new action moves $(q, 1)$ to $(q, -1)$, and the letters of the automaton move $(q, -1)$ to $(q', 1)$ with the automaton's probabilities, so each simulated letter costs $1 - 1 = 0$. A second new action leads from $(q, -1)$ to $\mathit{good}$ (cost $-1$) if $q \in F$ and to $\mathit{bad}$ (cost $1$) otherwise, and from either back to the initial state; a third action moves the initial state to the absorbing target $t$, and unavailable actions lead to a losing absorbing state of cost $1$. If some word is accepted with probability greater than $\frac{1}{2}$, repeating it makes each round have negative expected cost, so repeating it many times before stopping drives the expected total cost to $-\infty$ while still reaching $t$ almost surely; otherwise every almost-sure winning strategy has expected cost at least $1$, and the optimal cost is $1$. An approximation algorithm would separate the two cases.
