# Points of reachable cells lie in the reachability union

Let $F \subseteq \mathbb{R}^2$ have a trapezoidal decomposition: open vertical trapezoids $T_1, \dots, T_m$ (each $T$ given by reals $x_1 < x_2$ and affine lower and upper side lines $b, u$ with $b(x_1) \le u(x_1)$, $b(x_2) \le u(x_2)$, and $b(x_1) < u(x_1)$ or $b(x_2) < u(x_2)$; its carrier is $\{ (x,y) : x_1 < x < x_2,\ b(x) < y < u(x) \}$) and walls $S_1, \dots, S_k$ (open vertical segments $\{ x_S \} \times (y_1, y_2)$), such that the carriers of distinct cells are disjoint and their union is $F$. A wall $S$ is incident to a trapezoid $T$ when it lies on the right side of $T$ ($x_2 = x_S$, $b(x_2) \le y_1$, $y_2 \le u(x_2)$) or on its left side ($x_1 = x_S$, $b(x_1) \le y_1$, $y_2 \le u(x_1)$). The incidence graph has the cells (trapezoids and walls) as vertices, and joins a trapezoid to a wall exactly when they are incident; two trapezoids or two walls are never adjacent. For a cell $c_I$, the reachability union $R(c_I)$ is the set of points lying in the carrier of some cell reachable from $c_I$ in the incidence graph. If a cell $c$ is reachable from $c_I$ and $p$ lies in the carrier of $c$, then $p \in R(c_I)$.
