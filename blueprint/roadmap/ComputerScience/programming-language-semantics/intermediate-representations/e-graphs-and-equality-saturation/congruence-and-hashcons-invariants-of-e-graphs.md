# Congruence and hashcons invariants of e-graphs

Let $(U, M, H)$ be an [e-graph](e-graphs.md). It defines three relations: on e-class ids, $a \equiv_{\mathrm{id}} b$ iff $\mathrm{find}(a) = \mathrm{find}(b)$; on e-nodes, $n_1 \equiv_{\mathrm{node}} n_2$ iff some $M[a]$ contains both; on represented terms, $t_1 \equiv_{\mathrm{term}} t_2$ iff some e-class represents both. The relation on ids is an equivalence relation. Throughout, the e-classes named by distinct canonical ids are assumed pairwise disjoint, so that they partition the stored e-nodes and each stored e-node lies in exactly one e-class. Under this premise the relation on stored e-nodes is an equivalence relation, and without it it need not be: with $M[A] = \{x, y\}$ and $M[B] = \{y, z\}$ for distinct canonical ids $A$ and $B$, $x \equiv_{\mathrm{node}} y$ and $y \equiv_{\mathrm{node}} z$ but not $x \equiv_{\mathrm{node}} z$. The relation on terms is one when the congruence invariant below holds, but not in general: after merging $A = \{a\}$ and $B = \{b\}$ without repairing the parents $P = \{x, f(A)\}$ and $Q = \{y, f(B)\}$, both $P$ and $Q$ represent $f(a)$, yet no e-class represents both $x$ and $y$. One step of *congruence* relates $f(a_1, a_2, \dots) \cong f(b_1, b_2, \dots)$ iff $a_i \equiv_{\mathrm{id}} b_i$ for all $i$. The *congruence closure* $\cong^*$ of $\equiv_{\mathrm{node}}$ is the least equivalence relation $R$ on e-nodes that contains $\equiv_{\mathrm{node}}$ and relates $f(a_1, \dots, a_m)$ and $f(b_1, \dots, b_m)$ whenever, for each $i$, $a_i \equiv_{\mathrm{id}} b_i$ or some e-node of $M[a_i]$ is related by $R$ to some e-node of $M[b_i]$; it is obtained by iterating congruence steps to a fixed point. One step does not suffice: with e-nodes $a$, $b$, $f(a)$, $f(b)$, $g(f(a))$ and $g(f(b))$ after merging the classes of $a$ and $b$, one step relates the two $f$-nodes but not the two $g$-nodes, whose children are still different classes.

- **Congruence invariant.** $\equiv_{\mathrm{node}}$ equals $\cong^*$ on the stored e-nodes: congruent stored e-nodes lie in the same e-class. Unique membership of each stored e-node is the partition premise above, not a consequence of this equation.
- **Hashcons invariant.** For all e-nodes $n$ and ids $a$, $n \in M[a]$ iff $H[\mathrm{canonicalize}(n)] = \mathrm{find}(a)$. Then $\mathrm{lookup}(n) = H[\mathrm{canonicalize}(n)]$ finds the e-class, if any, containing an e-node congruent to $n$.

The operation $\mathrm{add}(n)$ returns $\mathrm{lookup}(n)$ if it exists and otherwise creates a new e-class $\{n\}$; $\mathrm{merge}(a, b)$ unions $a$ and $b$ in $U$ and sets $M[a]$ and $M[b]$ to $M[a] \cup M[b]$. *E-matching* a pattern $p$ with variables returns the pairs $(\sigma, c)$ of a substitution of e-class ids for the variables and an e-class $c$ representing $p[\sigma]$. A rewrite $\ell \to r$ is applied by computing $\mathrm{merge}(c, \mathrm{add}(r[\sigma]))$ for every match $(\sigma, c)$ of $\ell$.
