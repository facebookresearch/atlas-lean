# Profit lost by optimizing scaled profits

For $n$ items with natural weights $w_i$ and profits $p_i$, a natural capacity $W$ and a natural $K \ge 1$, write $P(S) = \sum_{i \in S} p_i$ and $R(S) = \sum_{i \in S} \lfloor p_i / K \rfloor$ for a set $S$ of items, rounding each item separately (two items of profit $1$ with $K = 2$ have scaled profit $0 + 0$, not $\lfloor 2/2 \rfloor$); a set $T$ is feasible when $\sum_{i \in T} w_i \le W$. If $R(T) \le R(S)$ for every feasible $T$, then the optimum satisfies $\mathrm{OPT} \le P(S) + nK$; equivalently, every feasible $T$ has $P(T) \le P(S) + nK$, since $\mathrm{OPT}$ is the maximum of $P$ over feasible sets. The theorem does not require $S$ to be feasible, nor $\mathrm{OPT}$ to exceed $P(S)$. The scaling factor is a natural number with natural-number division; the real factor $K = \varepsilon P / n$ of the approximation scheme is not covered.
