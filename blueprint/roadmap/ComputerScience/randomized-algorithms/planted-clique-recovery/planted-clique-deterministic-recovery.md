# Exact recovery of a planted clique by degree cleanup

Let $G$ be a graph on $n$ vertices containing a clique $Q$ of size $k$, with $k < n/6$, and let $z \in \mathbb{R}^n$ have $z_v = n - k$ for $v \in Q$ and $z_v = -k$ for $v \notin Q$. If $x = c\,(z - d)$ for some $c \ne 0$ and some $d \in \mathbb{R}^n$ with $\|d\|_2^2 \le kn^2/60$, then the set $W$ of the $k$ coordinates of $x$ largest in absolute value (ties broken arbitrarily) contains at least $5k/6$ vertices of $Q$. If moreover $G$ is the planted-clique graph $G(n, 1/2, Q)$ for a uniformly random $k$-set $Q$ (Alon, Krivelevich and Sudakov's $G(n, 1/2, k)$), $k \ge 10\sqrt n$ and $k = O(\sqrt{n \log n})$, then almost surely, that is with probability tending to $1$ as $n \to \infty$, for every such $x$, every vertex outside $Q$ has at most $(1 + o(1))k/2 + k/6 < 3k/4$ neighbours in $W$, every vertex of $Q$ has at least $3k/4$, and so the **degree cleanup** $\{v : v \text{ has at least } 3k/4 \text{ neighbours in } W\}$ equals $Q$ exactly.

## Proof sketch

This is the analysis of Algorithm A of Alon, Krivelevich and Sudakov (Section 2.2, penultimate paragraph for the first part, last paragraph for the cleanup). Scaling by $c$ does not change $W$, so take $c = 1$. At most $9k/60 < k/6$ coordinates of $d$ exceed $n/3$ in absolute value, since each contributes more than $n^2/9$ to $\|d\|_2^2$. Let $k_1$ of them lie in $Q$ and $k_2$ outside, so $k_1 + k_2 < k/6$. Every other coordinate in $Q$ has $|x_v| \ge n - k - n/3 > n/2$ and every other coordinate outside $Q$ has $|x_v| \le k + n/3 < n/2$, using $k < n/6$. So at least $k - k_1$ vertices of $Q$ and at most $k_2$ vertices outside $Q$ have $|x_v| > n/2$, and $W$ contains at least $k - \max(k_1, k_2) \ge 5k/6$ vertices of $Q$, so $|W \setminus Q| \le k/6$. In $G(n, 1/2, k)$ each vertex outside $Q$ has a $\mathrm{Binomial}(k, 1/2)$ number of neighbours in $Q$, so by Hoeffding's inequality and a union bound (deviation $\sqrt{\ln n / k} \to 0$ per vertex gives failure probability at most $n \cdot n^{-2} = 1/n$) almost surely all of them have at most $(1 + o(1))k/2$, hence at most $(1 + o(1))k/2 + k/6 < 3k/4$ neighbours in $W$; a vertex of $Q$ is adjacent to every other vertex of $W \cap Q$, so it has at least $5k/6 - 1 \ge 3k/4$ neighbours in $W$ for large $n$.

Deterministic cleanup: if $12 \le k < n/6$ and every vertex outside $Q$ has fewer than $7k/12$ neighbours in $Q$, then the degree cleanup equals $Q$ for every $x$ as above, with no randomness. A vertex of $Q$ has at least $5k/6 - 1 \ge 3k/4$ neighbours in $W$ (as $k \ge 12$); a vertex outside $Q$ has fewer than $7k/12 + k/6 = 3k/4$ neighbours in $W$, because $|W \setminus Q| \le k/6$.

Variant for the signed adjacency matrix $M$ ($M_{uv} = 1$ for an edge, $-1$ for a non-edge, $0$ on the diagonal): if $\|M - \mathbf{1}_Q\mathbf{1}_Q^\top\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le k/16$, then for a unit eigenvector $x$ for the largest eigenvalue of $M$, and more generally for every unit vector $x$ with $\|x - \sigma\,\mathbf{1}_Q/\sqrt{k}\|_2 \le 1/8$ for some sign $\sigma \in \{\pm 1\}$, the set $T$ of the $k$ largest coordinates of $x$ in absolute value misses at most $k/16$ vertices of $Q$; if moreover every vertex outside $Q$ has at most $5k/8$ neighbours in $Q$, the cleanup with threshold $3k/4$ and $T$ in place of $W$ equals $Q$. Proof: write $M = \mathbf{1}_Q\mathbf{1}_Q^\top + E$ and $u = \mathbf{1}_Q/\sqrt{k}$. The largest eigenvalue is $\lambda \ge u^\top M u \ge 15k/16$; projecting $Mx = \lambda x$ onto $u^\perp$ gives $\lambda \sin\theta \le \|E\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le k/16$ for the angle $\theta$ between $x$ and $\pm u$, so $\|x - \sigma u\|_2^2 \le 2\sin^2\theta \le 2/225 < 1/64$. Each pair of a missed vertex of $Q$ and a vertex of $T \setminus Q$ contributes at least $1/(4k)$ to $\|x - \sigma u\|_2^2$, so at most $k/16$ vertices are missed. The diagonal of $M$ is $0$, so $\|E\|_{\mathrm{op}} \ge 1$ and the hypothesis forces $k \ge 16$; vertices of $Q$ then have at least $15k/16 - 1 \ge 3k/4$ neighbours in $T$, and outsiders at most $5k/8 + k/16 < 3k/4$.
