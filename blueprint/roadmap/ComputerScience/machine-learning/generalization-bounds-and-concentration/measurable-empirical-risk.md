# Measurability of the empirical risk

For a sample of size $n \ge 1$, if the sample maps $\omega \mapsto z_i(\omega)$ and the loss $z \mapsto L(f, z)$ are measurable, then for each predictor $f$ the empirical risk $\omega \mapsto \hat R(f)$ on the random sample is measurable. It makes the deviation events of the uniform deviation bounds measurable.

## Sources

- F. Bach, *Learning Theory from First Principles*, MIT Press (2024); author PDF at <https://www.di.ens.fr/~fbach/ltfp_book.pdf>, Section 2.2, Definition 2.2 (empirical risk); the measurability statement is a routine fact not stated in the source.
