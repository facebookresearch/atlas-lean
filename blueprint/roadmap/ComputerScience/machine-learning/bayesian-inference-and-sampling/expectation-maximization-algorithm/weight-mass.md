# Latent masses as sums of posterior ratios

Let $Z$ be a finite set, let $f(x, z)$ be real numbers indexed by observations $x$ and latent values $z \in Z$, and let $x_1, \dots, x_N$ be data. For strictly positive weights $w$ on $Z$ (with $w(z) > 0$ for every $z$ and $\sum_{z \in Z} w(z) = 1$) write $p_w(x, z) = w(z)\,f(x, z)$ for the weight-times-likelihood joint model and $p_w(z \mid x) = p_w(x, z) / \sum_{z' \in Z} p_w(x, z')$ for its posterior, read as $0$ when the denominator is $0$. The mass of the latent value $z$ at the weights $w$ is the sum of ratios $m_w(z) = \sum_{n=1}^N p_w(z \mid x_n)$, defined for arbitrary real $f$. When each observed row defines a probability distribution, for example when the likelihoods $f(x, z)$ are nonnegative and every observed marginal $\sum_{z'} p_w(x_n, z')$ is positive, $p_w(\cdot \mid x_n)$ is the posterior distribution of the latent value of $x_n$, and $m_w(z)$ is the expected number of observations assigned to $z$. Without such hypotheses it need not be: with one observation, two latent values, weights $(1/2, 1/2)$ and $f(x, \cdot) = (-2, 4)$, the joint entries are $(-1, 2)$ and the first mass is $-1$.
