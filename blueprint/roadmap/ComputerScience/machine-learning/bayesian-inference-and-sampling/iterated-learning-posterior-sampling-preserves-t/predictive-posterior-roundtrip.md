# Averaging the posterior over the predictive recovers the prior

Let $H$ be a finite set of hypotheses with prior $\pi$, $D$ a finite set of data, and $L(h)$ a probability distribution on $D$ for each hypothesis $h$. Averaging the posterior over the predictive law recovers the prior: $\sum_d P(d) P(h \mid d) = \pi(h)$ for every hypothesis $h$. This is the finite form of the general fact that composing the posterior kernel with the predictive law gives back the prior.
