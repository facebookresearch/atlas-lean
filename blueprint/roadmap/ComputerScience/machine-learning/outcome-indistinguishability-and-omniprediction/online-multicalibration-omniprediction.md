# Oracle-efficient online multicalibration and omniprediction

In the online adversarial setting, where contexts and outcomes are chosen by an adversary and the forecaster predicts $p_t \in [0,1]$ each round, there is an algorithm that achieves online ($\ell_2$-swap) multicalibration with respect to infinite benchmark classes $F$ by an efficient reduction to an online squared-loss regression oracle for $F$ (a no-external-regret learner for the squared loss against $F$) rather than by enumerating $F$, against adaptive adversaries; consequently it is an online omnipredictor (sublinear excess loss against the best $f \in F$ for every Lipschitz convex loss, the loss being chosen after the predictions) and in fact a swap omnipredictor. Write $F_B = \{f \in F : f(x)^2 \le B \text{ for all } x\}$ and $B' = (1+\sqrt{B})^2$. The general guarantee (Corollaries 3.1 and 4.1) assumes that $F$ contains the constant function $1$ (Assumption 2.1), that the regression oracle has a regret bound against $F_{B'}$, and that $F_{B'}$ has finite sequential fat-shattering dimension at every scale; it then holds with probability $1 - \rho$ over the forecaster's randomness for multicalibration with respect to $F_B$. For $d$-dimensional linear functions $x \mapsto \langle\theta, x\rangle$ with parameter norm bounded in terms of $B$, against adversaries whose contexts satisfy $\|x_t\|_2 \le 1$ (a bound on $\|\theta\|_2$ alone does not bound the predictions on unbounded contexts), the online linear regression algorithm of Azoury and Warmuth is an efficient oracle with normalized regret $\|\theta\|_2^2/T + 2d\ln(T+1)/T$ (Theorem 3.3), and with $T^{1/4}$ discretization levels the algorithm attains, with probability $1 - \rho$, $\ell_2$-swap-multicalibration error $\tilde O(T^{-1/4})$ (Corollary 3.2) and swap-omniprediction error $\tilde O\big((C_{\mathcal{L}} + 4D_{\mathcal{L}})\sqrt{d}\,B^3\ln^{1/4}(1/\rho)\,T^{-1/8}\big)$ for a family $\mathcal{L}$ of convex losses whose scale and derivative in the action are bounded by $C_{\mathcal{L}}$ and $D_{\mathcal{L}}$ (Corollary 4.2), the $\tilde O$ hiding factors logarithmic in $T$ and $1/\rho$. Previous online multicalibration algorithms required enumerating every $f \in F$ each round and therefore only applied to small finite classes. In the other direction, some adaptive adversary forces expected swap-omniprediction error $\Omega(T^{-0.472})$ against convex Lipschitz losses for every forecaster (a lower bound on the expectation over the transcript; Corollary 5.3), while a non-oracle-efficient algorithm attains, with probability $1-\rho$, omniprediction at rate $\tilde O(T^{-1/2})$ (Corollary 5.2, total excess loss $\tilde O(\sqrt{T})$) for finite Boolean classes against all bi-monotone losses bounded in $[-1,1]$ (losses $\ell(y, t)$ monotone in $t$ in opposite directions for $y = 0$ and $y = 1$), which the authors present as an online separation between the rates obtainable for omniprediction and swap omniprediction.

## Proof sketch

Multicalibration with respect to a class closed under affine transformations is characterized by a swap-regret condition: on each level set of the forecast, no $f \in F$ has smaller squared error than the forecast. The algorithm obtains this contextual swap regret by keeping a copy of the regression oracle for each (discretized) forecast value and advancing only the copy whose value is played, adapting the external-to-swap reductions of Blum–Mansour and Ito; the characterization then gives $\ell_2$-swap multicalibration, and omniprediction follows as in the batch argument applied to empirical averages. The lower bound transfers the Qiao–Valiant calibration lower bound.
