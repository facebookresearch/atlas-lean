# Unbiased high-dimensional predictions give downstream decision makers no swap regret

Consider sequential prediction of an adversarially chosen state $s_t$ in a convex compact set $C \subseteq \mathbb{R}^d$, normalized so that $2\max_{y \in C}\|y\|_\infty \le 1$ (for a general $C$ every bias bound below is multiplied by $2\max_{y \in C}\|y\|_\infty$, since already a single forecast can be off by that much in a coordinate), by a randomized forecast $\hat s_t \sim \psi_t$, required to be unbiased conditional on every event $E$ in a family $\mathcal{E}$ of conditioning events (functions of the context, the history and the forecast, with values in $[0,1]$): $\big|\mathbb{E}\sum_{t \le T} E(x_t, \hat s_t)\,(\hat s_{t,i} - s_{t,i})\big|$ small for every $E \in \mathcal{E}$ and coordinate $i$. There is an algorithm achieving expected bias $O\big(\ln(2d|\mathcal{E}|T) + \sqrt{\ln(2d|\mathcal{E}|T)\,\sum_t \mathbb{E}[E(x_t, \hat s_t)^2]}\big)$ for every $E$ and $i$, the expectation being over the randomized forecasts (Theorem 3.1), with per-round time at round $t$ polynomial in $d$, $|\mathcal{E}|$, the cost of evaluating the events and $t$ (polylogarithmic in $t$ when the events are binary and disjoint). This bounds the expectation of a signed sum, not the sum on each realized transcript, where positive and negative errors in different runs can cancel in the expectation. The downstream guarantee is a statement about transcripts (Theorem 4.1): call a transcript $\alpha$-unbiased with respect to $\mathcal{E}$, for a function $\alpha$, if $\big|\sum_{t \le T} E(x_t, \hat s_t)(\hat s_{t,i} - s_{t,i})\big| \le \alpha(n_T(E))$ for every $E \in \mathcal{E}$ and coordinate $i$, where $n_T(E) = \sum_{t \le T} E(x_t, \hat s_t)$ (Definitions 2.2 and 2.3). Choosing $\mathcal{E}$ to consist of the binary best-response events $E_{u,a}(\hat s) = \mathbf{1}[\delta_u(\hat s) = a]$, where $\delta_u(\hat s) \in \arg\max_a u(a, \hat s)$ with ties broken lexicographically (so that the events $E_{u,a}$, $a \in A$, are disjoint), of polynomially many downstream decision makers with $K$ actions and utilities $u : A \times C \to [0,1]$ that are linear and $L$-Lipschitz in the $\ell_\infty$ norm in the state, on every transcript that is $\alpha$-unbiased with respect to these events, each decision maker who best responds to the forecasts as if they were correct has swap regret at most $2L\sum_{a}\alpha(n_T(E_{u,a}))/T$, and at most $2LK\,\alpha(T/K)/T$ when $\alpha$ is concave, simultaneously. A transcript on which the bias bound of Theorem 3.1 holds pathwise, with $\alpha(n) = O\big(\ln(2d|\mathcal{E}|T) + \sqrt{\ln(2d|\mathcal{E}|T)\,n}\big)$, therefore gives swap regret $O\big(L\sqrt{K\ln(2d|\mathcal{E}|T)/T} + LK\ln(2d|\mathcal{E}|T)/T\big)$, the optimal $\tilde O(\sqrt{K/T})$ rate; the expectation bound of Theorem 3.1 alone does not imply this without a separate concentration argument; the construction extends to large combinatorial action spaces, to extensive-form games, and to online multiclass prediction sets with multigroup coverage.

## Proof sketch

Unbiasedness conditioned on the event "decision maker $i$ best-responds with action $a$" says that, on the rounds where $i$ plays $a$, the average realized state equals the average forecast, so any swap $a \mapsto a'$ gains at most the conditional bias times the utility's Lipschitz constant. The unbiased predictions themselves are obtained by a reduction to a minimax problem in each round: an experts algorithm (the multi-scale multiplicative weights with correction of Chen, Luo and Wei) maintains weights over (coordinate, sign, event) triples measuring the cumulative biases, and the forecaster solves for a distribution over forecasts that is a near-equilibrium of the resulting zero-sum game, which exists by the minimax theorem.
