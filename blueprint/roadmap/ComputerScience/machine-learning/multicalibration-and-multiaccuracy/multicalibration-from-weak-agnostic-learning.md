# Multicalibration is efficiently reducible to weak agnostic learning of the group class

Let $D$ be a distribution over $X \times \{0,1\}$ with Bayes predictor $p^*(x) = \mathbb{E}[y \mid x]$, let $p : X \to [0,1]$ be a predictor, and let $\mathcal{C}$ be a collection of functions $c : X \to [-1,1]$ (indicators of subpopulations being the main example). Suppose $\mathcal{C}$ admits a weak agnostic learner: given samples from any distribution over $X \times [-1,1]$ for which some $c \in \mathcal{C}$ has correlation at least $\alpha$ with the label, it outputs $h$ (possibly outside $\mathcal{C}$, with bounded range) with correlation at least $\mathrm{poly}(\alpha)$. Then an $\alpha$-multicalibrated (with respect to $\mathcal{C}$) predictor can be computed with $\mathrm{poly}(1/\alpha)$ calls to the weak learner, used as the auditor on residuals restricted to level sets. Since the weak learner is promised only a positive correlation, the auditor must test both residual signs: for each level $v$ it is run on the labels $y - v$ and on the labels $v - y$ (restricted to the level set), and a returned hypothesis is used as a correction, with the corresponding sign, only after its correlation with that signed residual is confirmed on fresh samples, as in Hébert-Johnson, Kim, Reingold and Rothblum, Section 4.2 and Claim 4.3. Testing $y - v$ alone misses violations of the other sign: with $v = 1/2$, two equally likely groups $S$ and $X \setminus S$, $y = 0$ on $S$ and $y = 1$ off $S$, and $\mathcal{C} = \{\mathbf{1}_S\}$, the predictor is calibrated overall, yet $\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{1}_S(x)(y - v)] = -1/4$, so no member of $\mathcal{C}$ has positive correlation with $y - v$ and the promise gives nothing. The oracle-call count and the sample count are different quantities: each call is fed its own fresh labelled sample of the size the weak learner needs and must succeed with a per-call confidence $1 - \xi'$, with $\xi'$ small enough that a union bound over all calls and statistical estimates keeps the overall failure probability below a target $\xi$, so the total number of samples is the number of calls times the weak learner's sample cost at confidence $1-\xi'$, plus the samples used to estimate the level-set statistics. In Hébert-Johnson, Kim, Reingold and Rothblum's formulation, with sets $S \in \mathcal{C}$ and relative errors on the level sets of the sets with $|S| \ge \gamma|X|$, a $(\rho,\tau)$-weak agnostic learner with $\rho \le \alpha^2\lambda\gamma/2$ and $\tau = \mathrm{poly}(\alpha,\lambda,\gamma)$ running in time $T$ (which includes drawing its own sample on each call) gives an $(\alpha,\lambda)$-multicalibrated predictor in time $T \cdot \mathrm{poly}(1/\alpha, 1/\lambda, 1/\gamma)$, where $\lambda$ is the width of the discretization of $[0,1]$. Conversely, an algorithm that learns $\alpha$-multicalibrated predictors on the large sets of $\mathcal{C}$ in time $T$ yields a $(\rho,\tau)$-weak agnostic learner for $\mathcal{C}$ in time $T\cdot\mathrm{poly}(1/\tau)$ whenever $\tau \le \min\{\rho - 2\gamma,\ \rho/4 - 4\alpha\}$, so for worst-case targets the computational complexity of multicalibration is governed by weak agnostic learning of $\mathcal{C}$.

## Proof sketch

The signed residual $\pm(y - v)$ on the level set $\{p = v\}$, with the sign of the violation, is a labelled distribution on which a violating $c$ has correlation at least $\alpha$, so the weak learner returns a correction direction $h$ with correlation $\mathrm{poly}(\alpha)$, and the potential argument still applies with $h$ in place of $c$. For the converse, given labels $y \in [-1,1]$ for which some set of $\mathcal{C}$ has correlation $\rho$, either a constant hypothesis already has correlation $\tau$, or one learns a predictor $x$ that is multicalibrated for the labels with respect to $\mathcal{C} \cup \{X\}$ and returns $h = \mathrm{sgn}(x)$, whose correlation with the labels is large because $x$ is calibrated on the whole domain and multiaccurate on the correlated set.
