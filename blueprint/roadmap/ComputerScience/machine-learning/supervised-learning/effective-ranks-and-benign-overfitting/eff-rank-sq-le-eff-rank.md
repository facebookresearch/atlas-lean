# $r_k(\Sigma^2) \le r_k(\Sigma)$

Let $\lambda_1 \ge \lambda_2 \ge \dots \ge 0$ be the eigenvalues of a covariance operator $\Sigma$, with $\sum_i \lambda_i < \infty$, and let $k \in \mathbb{N}$ with $\lambda_{k+1} > 0$. Then $r_k(\Sigma^2) \le r_k(\Sigma)$, where $r_k(\Sigma^2)$ is the effective rank of the squared sequence $\lambda_i^2$, because $\sum_{i>k} \lambda_i^2 \le \lambda_{k+1} \sum_{i>k} \lambda_i$.

## Sources

- P. L. Bartlett, P. M. Long, G. Lugosi and A. Tsigler, *Benign overfitting in linear regression*, Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences 117 (2020) 30063–30070; arXiv:1906.11300 (v3), Lemma 5.
