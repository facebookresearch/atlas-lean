# Effective rank $r_k$

For a real sequence $\lambda_1, \lambda_2, \dots$ (the eigenvalues $\lambda_1 \ge \lambda_2 \ge \dots \ge 0$ of a covariance operator $\Sigma$, with $\sum_i \lambda_i < \infty$) and $k \in \mathbb{N}$,

$$r_k(\Sigma) = \frac{\sum_{i > k} \lambda_i}{\lambda_{k+1}},$$

where a series that does not converge (absolutely) is taken to be $0$ and a quotient by $0$ is $0$. The effective rank $r_k(\Sigma^2)$ is that of the squared sequence. For the identity on $\mathbb{R}^p$, $r_0 = p$.

## Sources

- P. L. Bartlett, P. M. Long, G. Lugosi and A. Tsigler, *Benign overfitting in linear regression*, Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences 117 (2020) 30063–30070; arXiv:1906.11300 (v3), Definition 3.
