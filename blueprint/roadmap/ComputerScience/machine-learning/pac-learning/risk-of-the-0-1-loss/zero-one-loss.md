# The 0-1 loss as an abstract loss

The 0-1 loss of a hypothesis $h$ at a labelled example $z = (x, y)$ is $1$ if $h(x) \ne y$ and $0$
otherwise, written in the form $L(h, z)$ used by the expected and empirical risk of the
generalization-bound pages.

## Sources

- F. Bach, *Learning Theory from First Principles*, MIT Press (2024), Section 2.1.
