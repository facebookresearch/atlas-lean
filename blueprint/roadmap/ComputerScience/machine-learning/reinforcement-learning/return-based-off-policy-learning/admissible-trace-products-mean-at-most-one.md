# Admissible trace products have mean at most one

For a causal trace admissible for $(\pi, \mu)$, the expected trace product $\mathbb{E}^\mu_{x,a}\bigl[\prod_{s=1}^t c_s\bigr]$ is at most $1$ for every $t$, so the expected discounted trace mass lies in $[1, 1/(1-\gamma)]$ and the return-based operator is defined.

Let $M$ be a Markov decision process with a finite nonempty set of states $X$, a finite nonempty set of actions $A$ and transition probabilities $P(x' \mid x,a)$ that are nonnegative and sum to $1$ over $x'$, and let $0 \le \gamma < 1$. Let $\pi, \mu$ be stochastic policies and $c$ a causal trace admissible for $(\pi, \mu)$. Then for every pair $(x,a)$, every $t \ge 1$ and every history $h_t$ of length $t$,

$$\sum_{b \in A} \mu(b \mid x_t)\, c_t(b, h_t) \le 1, \qquad \mathbb{E}^\mu_{x,a}\Bigl[\prod_{s=1}^t c_s\Bigr] \le 1 ;$$

the finite action sum on the left is a version of the conditional expectation $\mathbb{E}^\mu_{x,a}[c_t \mid h_t]$ given the σ-field generated by $h_t$, and it is the version used on histories of probability zero. Consequently the expected discounted trace mass satisfies $1 \le C(x,a) \le 1/(1-\gamma)$.

## Expansion

Admissibility bounds the action sum by $\sum_b \pi(b \mid x_t) = 1$. By the product form of the law, $\mathbb{P}(h_t = h, a_t = b) = \mathbb{P}(h_t = h)\, \mu(b \mid x_t)$ for $t \ge 1$, so on every event $\{h_t = h\}$ the trace $c_t$ and the action sum have the same integral; these finitely many events generate the σ-field of $h_t$, which makes the action sum a version of $\mathbb{E}[c_t \mid h_t]$. The product $\prod_{s<t} c_s$ is a function of $h_t$, so summing over the finitely many histories gives $\mathbb{E}[\prod_{s \le t} c_s] = \sum_h \mathbb{P}(h_t = h) \prod_{s<t} c_s(h) \sum_b \mu(b \mid x_t)\, c_t(b, h) \le \mathbb{E}[\prod_{s<t} c_s]$, and induction from the empty product gives the bound. Summing $\gamma^t$ times these bounds gives $C \le \sum_t \gamma^t$; $C \ge 1$ comes from the $t = 0$ term.
