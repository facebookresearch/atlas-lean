# Variance-reduced Q-value iteration

With a generative model whose samples cost constant time, variance-reduced Q-value iteration computes an $\varepsilon$-optimal policy of a discounted MDP with probability $1-\delta$ using time and samples $\tilde O(|S||A|/((1-\gamma)^3\varepsilon^2))$, for $\varepsilon \in (0, 1/2]$ and $\delta \in (0, 1)$, and space $O(|S||A|)$.

**Setting.** Let $M = (S, A, P, r, \gamma)$ be a discounted MDP with finite state and action sets, known rewards $r \in [0, 1]^{S \times A}$ and $\gamma \in (0, 1)$, accessed only through a [generative model](generative-model-of-an-mdp.md) that returns a sample of $P(\cdot \mid s, a)$ in $O(1)$ time. A policy $\pi$ is $\varepsilon$-optimal if $\|V^* - V^\pi\|_\infty \le \varepsilon$. For $v : S \to \mathbb R$ write $(Pv)(s, a) = \sum_{s'}P(s' \mid s, a)v(s')$ and $\mathrm{Var}_P(v) = P(v^2) - (Pv)^2$ (squares entrywise), and let $\mathcal T_\pi$ be the Bellman operator of a deterministic stationary policy $\pi$.

**Algorithm (variance-reduced QVI).** Write $\beta = (1-\gamma)^{-1}$ and fix absolute constants $c_1, c_2, c_3 > 0$ and an absolute factor $\kappa \ge 1$. A halving routine takes $u \in (0, \beta]$, $\delta' \in (0, 1)$, values $v^{(0)}$ and a policy $\pi^{(0)}$ with $v^{(0)} \le \mathcal T_{\pi^{(0)}}v^{(0)}$ and $V^* - v^{(0)} \le u\mathbf 1$. It sets

$$R = \lceil c_1\beta\ln(4\beta/u)\rceil + 1, \qquad m_1 = \Big\lceil c_2\beta^3u^{-2}\log\frac{8|S||A|}{\delta'}\Big\rceil, \qquad m_2 = \Big\lceil c_3\beta^2\log\frac{2R|S||A|}{\delta'}\Big\rceil, \qquad \alpha_1 = \frac{1}{m_1}\log\frac{8|S||A|}{\delta'}.$$

From $m_1$ samples $s^{(1)}, \dots, s^{(m_1)}$ at each pair $(s, a)$ it forms the empirical mean $\tilde w(s, a)$ of $v^{(0)}(s^{(j)})$ and the empirical variance $\hat\sigma(s, a) = \frac{1}{m_1}\sum_j v^{(0)}(s^{(j)})^2 - \tilde w(s, a)^2$. It shifts the mean down by the first confidence width $\kappa\big(\sqrt{2\alpha_1\hat\sigma} + 4\alpha_1^{3/4}\|v^{(0)}\|_\infty + \frac23\alpha_1\|v^{(0)}\|_\infty\big)$, giving $w$. For $R$ rounds it then estimates $P(v^{(i)} - v^{(0)})$ from $m_2$ fresh samples per pair, shifts this estimate down by $(1-\gamma)u/8$ to obtain $g^{(i)}$, forms $Q^{(i)} = r + \gamma(w + g^{(i)})$, and takes the greedy values and policy, keeping the previous ones at states where the value would decrease. When $c_1, c_2, c_3$ are sufficiently large absolute constants depending only on $\kappa$ and $v^{(0)} \ge 0$, as in every call of the full algorithm, it outputs $v, \pi$ with $v \le \mathcal T_\pi v$ and $V^* - v \le (u/2)\mathbf 1$ with probability at least $1-2\delta'$.

The full algorithm, for $\varepsilon \in (0, 1/2]$ and $\delta \in (0, 1)$, starts from $v = 0$ and calls the routine $K = \lceil\log_2(1/((1-\gamma)\varepsilon))\rceil$ times. The $i$-th call uses $u = 2^{-i+1}\beta$ and $\delta' = (1-\gamma)\varepsilon\delta/2$, and each call draws fresh samples.

**Theorem.** For every absolute factor $\kappa \ge 1$ there are absolute constants $c_1, c_2, c_3$ and $C$ such that for every such MDP $M$ and all $\varepsilon \in (0, 1/2]$ and $\delta \in (0, 1)$, the full algorithm halts in time at most

$$T = C\,\frac{|S||A|}{(1-\gamma)^3}\Big(\frac{1}{\varepsilon^2} + \log\frac{1}{(1-\gamma)\varepsilon}\Big)\log\frac{|S||A|}{(1-\gamma)\delta\varepsilon}\log\frac{1}{(1-\gamma)\varepsilon},$$

uses space at most $C|S||A|$, queries the generative model for at most $T$ fresh samples, and with probability at least $1-\delta$ outputs a policy $\pi$ with $V^* - \varepsilon\mathbf 1 \le V^\pi \le V^*$.

The source states the result as "there exists an algorithm" after fixing $M$; its proof analyses the one algorithm described above, uniformly over MDPs accessed through the generative model, which is the form stated here. The statement differs from the printed one in four places, each following the source's own proof.

- *Rounds.* Algorithm 1 prints $R = \lceil c_1\beta\ln(\beta/u)\rceil$, which is $0$ at $u = \beta$, where the outer loop starts. Its proof of Proposition 5.4.1 uses $R = \lceil\beta\ln(4\beta/u)\rceil + 1$, so that $\gamma^{R-1}\beta \le u/4$, and that form is used here. With $R = 0$ the halving step fails: one state, one action, reward $1$ and $\gamma = 1/2$ give $V^* = \beta = 2$, and $v^{(0)} = 0$ meets the input conditions for $u = \beta$, but the output $v = 0$ misses $V^* - v \le u/2 = 1$.
- *Accuracy range.* The source takes $\varepsilon \in (0, 1)$. Its factor $\log(1/((1-\gamma)\varepsilon))$ tends to $0$ as $(1-\gamma)\varepsilon \to 1$, while every run takes time at least $|S||A|$. For $\varepsilon \le 1/2$ both logarithms are at least $\log 2$.
- *Time.* The printed bound lacks the term $\log(1/((1-\gamma)\varepsilon))$ added to $1/\varepsilon^2$. Summing the per-call time of Proposition 5.4.1 over the $K$ calls gives rounds costing of order $|S||A|\beta^3\sum_i\log(4\beta/u_i)$, which is of order $K^2$ times $|S||A|\beta^3$ times a logarithm. For fixed $\varepsilon$ and $\gamma \to 1$ this exceeds the printed bound by a factor of order $\varepsilon^2\log(1/((1-\gamma)\varepsilon))$. When $1/\varepsilon^2 \ge \log(1/((1-\gamma)\varepsilon))$ the extra term at most doubles $T$, so there the bound is the printed one. The new bound is the source's Proposition E.2.1 (total time $\tau\log(\varepsilon^{-1}(1-\gamma)^{-1})$ for $\log(\varepsilon^{-1}(1-\gamma)^{-1})$ calls of per-call time $\tau$) composed with the per-call time of its Proposition 5.4.1, summed over the calls instead of simplified by that proof's last step, "Since $\log[(1-\gamma)^{-1}u^{-1}] = O(\log[(1-\gamma)^{-1}]u^{-2})$", which fails at $u = \beta/2$ for large $\beta$. This concerns the round schedule $R \propto \beta\log(\beta/u)$ of the described algorithm, printed or as in the proof, not the existence of an algorithm with the printed rate. The bound $\|Q^* - Q^{(0)}\|_\infty \le \beta$ used to choose $R$ is loose after the first stage, and a schedule with a number of rounds of order $\beta$, independent of $u$, may attain the printed rate. That variant is not the described algorithm and is not analysed here.
- *Explicit parameters.* $\kappa = 1$ is the source's width. The ceilings make the sample sizes integers. The number of calls is that of Algorithm 2. The per-call guarantee is the one the proof of Proposition 5.4.1 gives through Lemma 5.4, probability at least $1-2\delta'$ (its statement prints $1-\delta'$), so $\delta'$ is half the per-call confidence $(1-\gamma)\varepsilon\delta$ of Proposition E.2.1; the source writes $\Theta(\cdot)$ for the count.

The paper fixes no convention for logarithms that would change these endpoints: its preliminaries (Section 3) define notation for sets, vectors and MDPs only, and it writes $\ln$ in $R$ and $\log$ elsewhere. For $\varepsilon \ge 1$ the authors do not claim optimality. Up to logarithmic factors the sample count matches the lower bound, attributed by the authors to the generative-model lower-bound construction, $\Omega(|S||A|\log(|S||A|/\delta)/((1-\gamma)^3\varepsilon^2))$ for finding $\varepsilon$-optimal policies, and the running time is linear in the number of samples.

## Expansion

Three ideas combine. *Monotonicity:* keeping $v^{(i)} \le \mathcal T_{\pi^{(i)}}v^{(i)}$ gives $v^{(i)} \le V^{\pi^{(i)}}$, so a $u$-accurate value yields a $u$-optimal policy without the usual loss of a factor $1/(1-\gamma)$; the one-sided (downward shifted) estimates preserve it with high probability. *Variance reduction:* $Pv^{(0)}$ is estimated once to high accuracy, while each round only needs $P(v^{(i)} - v^{(0)})$ with $\|v^{(i)} - v^{(0)}\|_\infty = O(u)$, which costs $\tilde O((1-\gamma)^{-2})$ samples per pair independently of $u$. *Total variance:* the per-round errors are of order $\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}_P(V^*)/m_1}$, and their accumulation $(I - \gamma P^{\pi^*})^{-1}\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}_P(V^*)}$ is $O((1-\gamma)^{-3/2})$ rather than $O((1-\gamma)^{-2})$, which removes the last factor $1/(1-\gamma)$. Bernstein-type bounds for averages of independent bounded samples and Hoeffding's inequality control the empirical means and variances. *First width.* Fix a pair $(s, a)$. Conditionally on the earlier calls, $v^{(0)}$ is fixed with values in $[0, B]$, $B = \|v^{(0)}\|_\infty$, and the $m_1$ samples are independent draws from the finite row $P(\cdot \mid s, a)$. So the partial sums of $v^{(0)}(s^{(j)}) - (Pv^{(0)})(s, a)$ form a martingale along a step kernel on the finite set $S$, with increments at most $B$ in absolute value and deterministic conditional variance $\sigma = \mathrm{Var}_P(v^{(0)})(s, a)$. Write $L = \log(8|S||A|/\delta')$, so $\alpha_1 = L/m_1$.

- *Bernstein's inequality for sums.* The deterministic-variance two-sided case of Freedman's maximal inequality, with $v = m_1\sigma$ and $b = B$, inverted at $e^{-L}$ per tail, gives $|\tilde w - Pv^{(0)}| \le \sqrt{2\alpha_1\sigma} + \frac23\alpha_1 B$ at the pair. Each tail fails with probability at most $\delta'/(8|S||A|)$. This is the source's Lemma 5.1. If $\sigma = 0$, every draw equals $(Pv^{(0)})(s, a)$ almost surely, so the sample mean is exact and the bound holds with no tail inequality; this includes $B = 0$, as in the first call, where $v^{(0)} = 0$.
- *Variance estimate.* Two-sided Hoeffding bounds for the means of $v^{(0)}(s^{(j)})^2 \in [0, B^2]$ and $v^{(0)}(s^{(j)}) \in [0, B]$, at level $2L$, give $|\hat\sigma - \sigma| \le 3B^2\sqrt{\alpha_1} \le 4B^2\sqrt{2\alpha_1}$. Hence $\sqrt{2\alpha_1\sigma} \le \sqrt{2\alpha_1\hat\sigma} + 2^{7/4}\alpha_1^{3/4}B$, so $w \le Pv^{(0)}$ for every $\kappa \ge 1$, the source's width included.
- *Union.* The two Bernstein tails cost $\delta'/(4|S||A|)$ per pair, and the four Hoeffding tails cost at most $4(\delta'/(8|S||A|))^2 \le \delta'/(16|S||A|)$. So the first stage fails with probability at most $\frac{5}{16}\delta' \le \delta'$, within the source's Lemma 5.1. The round estimates fail with probability at most $\delta'$ by the source's Lemma 5.3, used for each of the $R$ rounds at confidence $\delta'/R$. So a call fails with probability at most $2\delta'$, as in the source's Lemma 5.4.
- *Sub-exponential route.* The listed sub-exponential bounds alone give, at the same $e^{-L}$, centered samples bounded by $b$ with variance at most $\sigma$ sub-exponential with parameters $(\sqrt{2\sigma}, 2b)$ (for $2b|t| < 1$ the factor $1/(1 - b|t|)$ is at most $2$), their sum sub-exponential with parameters $(\sqrt{2m_1\sigma}, 2b)$, and the two tail regimes on the sum and its negation give $|\tilde w - Pv^{(0)}| \le 2\sqrt{\alpha_1\sigma} + 4\alpha_1 B$. With the variance estimate this is at most $2\sqrt{\alpha_1\hat\sigma} + 4 \cdot 2^{1/4}\alpha_1^{3/4}B + 4\alpha_1 B$. Since $m_1 \ge c_2 L$ gives $\alpha_1 \le 1$ for $c_2 \ge 1$, the first width covers it for every $\kappa \ge 3$. So the sub-exponential bounds suffice for $\kappa \ge 3$, while $\kappa \in [1, 3)$, the source's width included, uses the sharp Bernstein bound obtained from Freedman's inequality.

*Larger $\kappa$.* A wider first shift still makes $w$ a lower bound for $Pv^{(0)}$ with high probability, so the invariant $v \le \mathcal T_\pi v$ survives. The three terms of the width scale like $\sqrt{\alpha_1}$, $\alpha_1^{3/4}$ and $\alpha_1$, so multiplying them by $\kappa$ is undone by multiplying $c_2$, and hence $m_1$, by $\kappa^2$. The second shift $(1-\gamma)u/8$ is deterministic and stays fixed: it accumulates through $(I - \gamma P^{\pi^*})^{-1}$ to $u/8$ within the halving budget, and no sample size would offset a larger one. The $m_2$ samples control only the noise of the round estimates.

*Cost.* A call takes time $O(|S||A|(m_1 + Rm_2))$, that is

$$O\Big(\frac{|S||A|}{(1-\gamma)^3}\Big(\frac{1}{u^2}\log\frac{8|S||A|}{\delta'} + \log\frac{4}{(1-\gamma)u}\log\frac{4|S||A|}{(1-\gamma)^2u\delta'}\Big)\Big),$$

using $2R \le C\beta^2/u$. The call succeeds with probability at least $1-2\delta'$ by the union above. In the full algorithm, $K \le \log_2(1/((1-\gamma)\varepsilon)) + 1 \le 1/((1-\gamma)\varepsilon)$, so the failure probabilities $2\delta' = (1-\gamma)\varepsilon\delta$ sum to at most $2K\delta' \le \delta$. Moreover $u_i = 2^{-i+1}\beta > \varepsilon$, so $\sum_i u_i^{-2} < \frac43\varepsilon^{-2}$, and $\sum_i\log(4\beta/u_i) \le K\log(4\beta/\varepsilon)$, of order $\log^2(1/((1-\gamma)\varepsilon))$. Every logarithm is at most a constant times $\log(|S||A|/((1-\gamma)\delta\varepsilon))$, which gives $T$.
