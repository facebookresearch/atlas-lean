# Sample complexity of exploration of R-max

With an optimistic initial value $Q^* \le U \le 1/(1-\gamma)$, R-max with suitable inputs in a discounted MDP is, with probability $1-\delta$, $\varepsilon$-optimal on all but $\tilde O(|\{(s,a) : U(s,a) \ge V^*(s) - \varepsilon\}|\, S/(\varepsilon^3(1-\gamma)^6))$ steps, which is $\tilde O(S^2 A/(\varepsilon^3(1-\gamma)^6))$ when $U \equiv 1/(1-\gamma)$.

**Setting.** Let $M = \langle S, A, T, R, \gamma\rangle$ be a finite MDP with reward distributions in $[0, 1]$ and $0 \le \gamma < 1$, and let $U$ be an admissible heuristic: $Q^*(s, a) \le U(s, a) \le V_{\max}$ for all pairs, where $V_{\max} = 1/(1-\gamma)$. R-max with inputs $m$ (a visit threshold) and $\varepsilon_1$ (the optimality, in its model, of the policies it plans) counts the experiences $n(s, a)$ of each pair, capped at $m$, and keeps the empirical mean reward $\hat R(s, a)$ and empirical next-state distribution $\hat T(\cdot \mid s, a)$ of the first $m$ experiences. It starts from $Q = U$ and always acts greedily with respect to $Q$, breaking ties in a fixed order. Whenever some $n(s, a)$ reaches $m$, it runs $N = \lceil \ln(2/(\varepsilon_1(1-\gamma)^2))/(1-\gamma)\rceil$ sweeps of value iteration for the equations

$$Q(s, a) = \hat R(s, a) + \gamma\sum_{s'}\hat T(s' \mid s, a)\max_{a'}Q(s', a') \ \text{ if } n(s, a) \ge m, \qquad Q(s, a) = U(s, a) \ \text{ otherwise},$$

updating the entries of the pairs with $n(s, a) \ge m$ in place, starting from the current $Q$, and keeping $Q = U$ at the other pairs.

**Theorem.** Suppose $0 < \varepsilon < 1/(1-\gamma)$ and $0 < \delta < 1$, and let $M$ be any such MDP. There are inputs $m = m(1/\varepsilon, 1/\delta)$ and $\varepsilon_1$ with

$$m = O\Big(\frac{(S + \ln(SA/\delta))V_{\max}^2}{\varepsilon^2(1-\gamma)^2}\Big), \qquad \frac{1}{\varepsilon_1} = O\Big(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Big),$$

such that, if R-max runs on $M$ with these inputs and $\mathcal A_t$, $s_t$ denote its policy and state at time $t$, then with probability at least $1-\delta$ the inequality $V^{\mathcal A_t}_M(s_t) \ge V^*_M(s_t) - \varepsilon$ holds for all but

$$O\Big(\frac{|\{(s, a) \in S \times A : U(s, a) \ge V^*(s) - \varepsilon\}|}{\varepsilon^3(1-\gamma)^3}\Big(S + \ln\frac{SA}{\delta}\Big)V_{\max}^3\ln\frac{2}{\delta}\ln\frac{4}{\varepsilon(1-\gamma)}\Big)$$

time steps $t$; the constants in the $O(\cdot)$ terms are absolute. The source prints $0 \le \varepsilon$ and $0 \le \delta$, but $m$ is defined only for positive values. Three further departures follow the source's own propositions and proof.

- *Logarithms.* The source prints $\ln(1/\delta)\ln(1/(\varepsilon(1-\gamma)))$ inside $O(\cdot)$. With absolute constants these factors tend to $0$ as $\delta \to 1$ or $\varepsilon(1-\gamma) \to 1$, while R-max can be forced to take a bad first step. Take two states $s_1, z$ and actions $a, b$. At $s_1$, $b$ pays $1-\theta$ and stays, while $a$ pays $0$ and moves to $z$, which pays $0$ and returns to $s_1$ with probability $q$. Let $U \equiv 1/(1-\gamma)$ except $U(s_1, b) = Q^*(s_1, b)$. Then R-max takes $a$ at its first step, and for $\gamma = 1/2$, $\varepsilon(1-\gamma) = 1-\eta$, $\theta = \eta/2$ and $q = \eta/4$ that step's policy has value at most $q/(1+q) < \eta = V^*(s_1) - \varepsilon$. The source's proof applies its Theorem 10, which gives $4\varepsilon$-optimality with probability $1-2\delta$. Applying it at $\varepsilon/4$ and $\delta/2$ gives the logarithms stated here. The counted set at $\varepsilon/4$ is contained in the one at $\varepsilon$, and $S + \ln(2SA/\delta) \le 2(S + \ln(SA/\delta))$ keeps the bound on $m$.
- *Scale.* The source allows any $V_{\max} \in [0, 1/(1-\gamma)]$ above $U$ and states its bounds with that $V_{\max}$. Two steps of its proof fail for smaller $V_{\max}$. First, its Lemma 12 sets the reward accuracy to $C\varepsilon(1-\gamma)/V_{\max}$, and a reward error of that size contributes up to $C\varepsilon/V_{\max}$ to the values, more than $\varepsilon$ when $V_{\max}$ is small. Take one state, $\gamma = 0$, an action $a$ with reward $\eta/8$ and an action $b$ with a Bernoulli($\eta$) reward, $U \equiv V_{\max} = \eta$, $\varepsilon = \eta/4$ and $\delta = 1/10$. The bound on $m$ stays bounded as $\eta \to 0$, with probability $(1-\eta)^m \to 1$ all $m$ rewards of $b$ are $0$, and R-max then plays $a$ at every later step, with value $\eta/8 < V^* - \varepsilon = 3\eta/4$. Second, its Theorem 10 assumes $Q_t \le V_{\max}$ at all times, but Algorithm 1 does not clip its values. Take two states $x \to y \to x$ with one action, $\gamma = 1/2$, rewards $3/4$ at $x$ and $0$ at $y$, and $U \equiv V_{\max} = 1$. Once $x$ is known and $y$ is not, every sweep gives $Q(x) = 3/4 + U(y)/2 = 5/4$. The theorem is therefore stated for $V_{\max} = 1/(1-\gamma)$, the source's choice without prior information; $U$ may still be any admissible heuristic below it. Then $Q_t \le V_{\max}$ at all times, since empirical rewards lie in $[0, 1]$, Lemma 12 holds since $V_{\max} \ge 1$, and the bound on $m$ is at least $1$, so it admits a positive integer $m$.
- *Planning.* Algorithm 1 (line 18) runs $\lceil \ln(1/(\varepsilon_1(1-\gamma)))/(1-\gamma)\rceil$ sweeps, which by the source's Proposition 4 brings $Q$ within $\varepsilon_1$ of the model's optimal action values. The source takes $\varepsilon_1$ to be the closeness to optimality of the planned policies. From sup error $\varepsilon_1$, its Proposition 3 bounds a greedy policy's loss in the model only by $2\varepsilon_1/(1-\gamma)$, so that count alone does not establish the accuracy condition the proof uses. The count here applies Proposition 4 at $\varepsilon_1(1-\gamma)/2$, so that by Proposition 3 the greedy policy is $\varepsilon_1$-optimal in the model. This changes the planning computation; the bounds on $m$ and on the number of non-$\varepsilon$-optimal steps are unchanged.

With $U(s, a) = V_{\max} = 1/(1-\gamma)$ the counted set has at most $SA$ pairs and the bound is $\tilde O(S^2 A/(\varepsilon^3(1-\gamma)^6))$, where $\tilde O$ hides logarithmic factors.

## Expansion

Write $\varepsilon_0 = \varepsilon/4$ and $\delta_0 = \delta/2$, give the model accuracy $\varepsilon_{\mathrm m} = \varepsilon_0/2 = \varepsilon/8$ and the planning input $\varepsilon_1 = \varepsilon_0/4 = \varepsilon/16$, write $\beta = \varepsilon_1(1-\gamma)/2$, and take $K_t = \{(s, a) : n_t(s, a) \ge m\}$. In the known state-action MDP $M_{K_t}$ and its empirical version $\hat M_{K_t}$, each unknown pair becomes a self-loop paying $(1-\gamma)U \le 1$, so both have rewards in $[0, 1]$ and values at most $V_{\max}$. Hoeffding's inequality for rewards and the $\ell_1$ deviation bound of Weissman et al. for next-state distributions, with a union bound over pairs at total confidence $\delta_0$, show that for $m \ge C V_{\max}^2 (S + \ln(SA/\delta_0))/(\varepsilon_{\mathrm m}^2(1-\gamma)^2)$, with probability at least $1-\delta_0$, every stationary policy's value in $\hat M_{K_t}$ is within $\varepsilon_{\mathrm m}$ of its value in $M_{K_t}$ at every time. This uses a simulation bound for models with rewards and transitions within $C\varepsilon_{\mathrm m}(1-\gamma)/V_{\max}$, valid because $V_{\max} \ge 1$. Since $\ln(SA/\delta_0) \le \ln(SA/\delta) + 1$ and $\varepsilon_{\mathrm m} = \varepsilon/8$, such an $m$ is within the stated bound.

*Planning.* The values stay in $[0, V_{\max}]$: they start at $U$, and an update $\hat R + \gamma\hat T\max Q$ of values in $[0, V_{\max}]$ stays there because $\hat R \in [0, 1]$. The model changes only when a pair joins $K$, and planning runs exactly then, so $Q_t$ is the output of the last run for the current model, or $U$, the model's exact solution, before any pair is known. The model's optimal action values $\hat Q^*$ also lie in $[0, V_{\max}]$, so a run starts within $V_{\max}$ of them. Each in-place sweep multiplies this sup-distance by at most $\gamma$, because every assignment in a sweep is a Bellman update of entries that are each no farther from $\hat Q^*$ than before the sweep. So after $N$ sweeps $\|Q_t - \hat Q^*\|_\infty \le \gamma^N V_{\max} \le e^{-(1-\gamma)N}/(1-\gamma) \le \beta$, as in the source's Proposition 4. For the greedy policy $\pi_t$ of $Q_t$, every state $s$ and every action $a$, $\hat Q^*(s, \pi_t(s)) \ge Q_t(s, \pi_t(s)) - \beta \ge Q_t(s, a) - \beta \ge \hat Q^*(s, a) - 2\beta$. With the Bellman equations for $\hat V^*$ and $\hat V^{\pi_t}$ this gives $\hat V^*(s) - \hat V^{\pi_t}(s) \le 2\beta + \gamma\max_{s'}(\hat V^*(s') - \hat V^{\pi_t}(s'))$, hence $\hat V^* - \hat V^{\pi_t} \le 2\beta/(1-\gamma) = \varepsilon_1$, the source's Proposition 3.

*Conditions.* On the model event, with $V_t = \max_a Q_t(\cdot, a)$:

- optimism: $V_t \ge \hat V^* - \beta \ge V^*_{M_{K_t}} - \varepsilon_{\mathrm m} - \beta \ge V^* - \varepsilon_0$, because replacing unknown pairs by self-loops paying $(1-\gamma)U$ can only raise values, and $\varepsilon_{\mathrm m} + \beta \le 5\varepsilon_0/8$;
- accuracy: $V_t - V^{\pi_t}_{M_{K_t}} \le (V_t - \hat V^*) + (\hat V^* - \hat V^{\pi_t}) + (\hat V^{\pi_t} - V^{\pi_t}_{M_{K_t}}) \le \beta + \varepsilon_1 + \varepsilon_{\mathrm m} \le 7\varepsilon_0/8$;
- learning complexity: a pair with $U(s, a) < V^*(s) - \varepsilon_0$ is never tried, since while unknown it has $Q_t(s, a) = U(s, a) < V^*(s) - \varepsilon_0 \le V_t(s)$. Every other pair escapes at most $m$ times before joining $K$, and the action values change only at the steps when a pair joins $K$ and planning runs, so update events (counted once per step, not per entry or sweep) and escape events number at most $2|\{(s, a) : U(s, a) \ge V^*(s) - \varepsilon_0\}|\, m \le 2|\{(s, a) : U(s, a) \ge V^*(s) - \varepsilon\}|\, m$.

Since also $Q_t \le V_{\max}$ at all times, the PAC-MDP theorem at $(\varepsilon_0, \delta_0)$ gives $4\varepsilon_0 = \varepsilon$-optimality with probability at least $1 - 2\delta_0 = 1-\delta$ on all but the stated number of steps, with $\ln(1/\delta_0) = \ln(2/\delta)$, $\ln(1/(\varepsilon_0(1-\gamma))) = \ln(4/(\varepsilon(1-\gamma)))$ and $1/\varepsilon_0 = 4/\varepsilon$. The online samples are not independent, but the tail bounds still hold by a martingale argument. The source pairs this with a lower bound of order $(SA/\varepsilon^2)\ln(S/\delta)$ for algorithms with deterministic policies that change only at the current state (its Theorem 26).
