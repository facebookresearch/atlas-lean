# Query reporting with public raw-bit layouts

Fix a query on encoded relations, its valid inputs, a common probability law, and one family of protocols. The finite relation vocabulary is fixed; the worker count $p>0$, vector of relation lengths, and wiring schedule are public parameters, not payload values. In the distributed convention a schedule is a bijection from local slots to relation/offset pairs. Slot counts differ by at most one. A worker reads the *values at those positions*, without any preliminary re-encoding. Every such schedule is tested on every valid payload of its shape. In the separate convention the workers start empty, and each relation server starts with its own word.

Each worker computes from its initial word, the common random seed and its private history. A round's proposed sends depend on the old views; accepted deliveries are available for subsequent computation, identified by sender. Relation servers only propose sends in the first round. A worker-to-itself channel is empty. The round load is the maximum over recipients of the sum of all incoming word lengths, including relation-server messages.

The family reports the query in $R$ rounds at $0\leq\varepsilon\leq1$ and $0\leq\eta<1$ if a single $C>0$ works for every positive $p$, every shape, every schedule and every valid payload. With $M$ the total number of input bits, the cap is the natural-number floor of $CM/p^{1-\varepsilon}$. An over-cap proposal aborts before that barrier delivers anything; **delivered** traffic respects the cap even on such draws. There is no assertion that all *proposals* fit. On each input the measurable event of completion with the union of the workers' finite reports exactly equal to the query answer must have probability at least $1-\eta$. Aborting is not reporting the empty set. The seed law is fixed independently of all inputs and can, for example, be that of a common random-bit tape.
