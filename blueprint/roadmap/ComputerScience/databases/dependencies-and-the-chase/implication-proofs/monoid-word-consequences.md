# Word consequences over all monoids and over finite monoids

An input consists of a finite alphabet $\Gamma$, a finite list $E$ of equations between words in $\Gamma^*$, and words $u,v\in\Gamma^*$. Say that $E$ implies $u=v$ over a class of monoids if, for every monoid $M$ in the class and every homomorphism $h:\Gamma^*\to M$ satisfying $h(s)=h(t)$ for every $s=t$ in $E$, one has $h(u)=h(v)$. Neither the problem for the class of all monoids nor the problem for the class of all finite monoids is decidable. The latter quantifies over all finite monoids; its input is not the multiplication table of one given finite monoid.

## Expansion

Separate the unrestricted proof from the finite-model proof. For the unrestricted case, the quotient of the free monoid by the congruence generated by $E$ witnesses failure exactly for pairs of words not convertible by the Thue rules. That quotient need not be finite. The finite case needs its own undecidability result for consequence over finite semigroups and the passage to monoids (adjoin an identity for equations of nonempty words); it does not follow by restricting the unrestricted theorem to finite models.
