# LOCAL lower bounds transfer to component-stable MPC algorithms

MPC inputs are *legal* graphs: each vertex carries an ID and a name from $[\mathrm{poly}(n)]$, the names are unique in the whole graph, but the IDs need only be unique within each connected component, so an input may contain several copies of a component with the same IDs. Call a randomized MPC algorithm *component-stable* if its output at each vertex $v$ is a deterministic function of the topology and IDs, but not the names, of the connected component of $v$, of $v$ itself, of the exact number $n$ of vertices and the maximum degree $\Delta$ of the whole input graph, and of the shared random seed. Call a class of graphs *normal* if it is closed under removing vertices and under disjoint unions; the class of all graphs and the class of forests are normal, the class of trees is not. A problem is *$R$-replicable* if, for every $G \in \mathcal{G}$ with at least two vertices, a labelling of $G$ is valid whenever the labelling that copies it onto a disjoint union of at least $|V(G)|^R$ copies of $G$ (with the same IDs as $G$) and fills in fewer than $|V(G)|$ isolated vertices (sharing one ID) with a common label is valid. Let $\Pi$ be an $O(1)$-replicable graph problem (a class that contains every locally checkable labelling problem and the constant approximations of maximum matching and of minimum vertex cover) that has a $T(N, \Delta)$-round lower bound in the randomized LOCAL model with shared randomness on a normal class $\mathcal{G}$, in the following sense: no LOCAL algorithm that receives an $n$-vertex graph of $\mathcal{G}$ with component-unique IDs, a shared random seed, the exact maximum degree $\Delta$ and an input size estimate $N$ with $n \le N \le \mathrm{poly}(n)$, runs in $T(N, \Delta)$ rounds and outputs a valid labelling with probability at least $1 - 1/N$ on every such input. (The IDs here are only unique within components, unlike the globally unique identifiers of the standard LOCAL model; Czumaj, Davies and Parter adopt this convention so that replicated components are legal inputs.) The function $T$ is *constrained*: $T(N, \Delta) = O(\log^{\gamma} N)$ for all $\Delta \le N$, for a constant $\gamma \in (0, 1)$, and $T(N^c, \Delta) \le c\, T(N, \Delta)$ for every $c \ge 1$. If some component-stable MPC algorithm with strongly sublinear local memory $S = n^{\alpha}$, for a constant $\alpha \in (0, 1)$, and $\mathrm{poly}(n)$ global memory solves $\Pi$ on legal $n$-vertex graphs of $\mathcal{G}$ with maximum degree $\Delta$ in $o(\log T(n, \Delta))$ rounds with success probability at least $1 - 1/n$, then a low-space MPC algorithm distinguishes one $n$-vertex cycle from two $n/2$-vertex cycles in $o(\log n)$ rounds with success probability at least $1 - 1/n$. Hence, assuming the one-versus-two-cycle conjecture, every such component-stable algorithm needs $\Omega(\log T(n, \Delta))$ rounds; in particular, any constant approximation of maximum matching (so also maximal matching), even on forests, any constant approximation of minimum vertex cover, and maximal independent set require $\Omega(\log\log n)$ rounds. A LOCAL lower bound on trees lifts only to forests, since the lifting builds disjoint unions of induced subgraphs of the hard instances.

## Proof sketch

The LOCAL lower bound gives two graphs whose radius-$t$ neighbourhoods of a node agree, for some $t$ with $\log t = \Theta(\log T)$, while the node's output distributions under any correct algorithm differ; so a component-stable MPC algorithm running in $o(\log T)$ rounds, too few to gather radius-$t$ balls by graph exponentiation, must be sensitive to changes at distance more than $t$. This sensitivity, applied to inputs built from the two graphs and an $s$-$t$ path, yields an $o(\log n)$-round strongly sublinear MPC algorithm for $D$-diameter $s$-$t$ connectivity with $D = T(n, \Delta)$, and hence for one cycle versus two cycles; replicability and normality guarantee that the disjoint unions built in this step are again valid inputs of $\Pi$ in $\mathcal{G}$. The matching, vertex-cover and MIS bounds lift the randomized $\Omega(\sqrt{\log n/\log\log n})$ LOCAL lower bounds of Kuhn, Moscibroda and Wattenhofer, which also hold with shared randomness.
