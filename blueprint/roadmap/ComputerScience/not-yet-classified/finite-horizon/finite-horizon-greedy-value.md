# Backward recursion attains the finite-horizon value

Let $M$ be a Markov decision process with finitely many states, a finite nonempty action set, real rewards $r(s,a)$ and real transition weights $P(s,a,s')$ (not required to be nonnegative or to sum to $1$), and let $\gamma$ be any real discount. Let $V_0 = 0$ and let $V_{k+1}$ be one Bellman optimality update of $V_k$. For a policy, the weighted $k$-step return is $0$ for $k = 0$ and, for $k + 1$ steps remaining, $r(s,a) + \gamma \sum_{s'} P(s,a,s')\, W_k(s')$ at the policy's action $a$, where $W_k$ is its $k$-step return. The policy that, with $k + 1$ steps remaining, acts greedily with respect to $V_k$ has weighted $k$-step return equal to $V_k(s)$, for every horizon $k$ and state $s$. When the transition weights are nonnegative and sum to $1$ over $s'$, this return is the expected discounted return.
