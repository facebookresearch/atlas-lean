# Minkowski sums of disjoint convex sets are pseudodisks

A finite collection of closed regions in the plane, each bounded by a closed Jordan curve, is a collection of *pseudodisks* if the boundaries of any two of them meet in at most two points. Let $T_1, \dots, T_n$ be convex polygons with pairwise disjoint interiors and let $R$ be a convex polygon. Then $\{T_i \oplus R : 1 \le i \le n\}$ is a collection of pseudodisks (after a perturbation into general position): no two of them cross, because for each direction the extreme points of $T_i \oplus R$ are sums of extreme points of $T_i$ and of $R$, and the outer common tangents of $T_i$ and $T_j$ separate the directions in which each is the more extreme.
