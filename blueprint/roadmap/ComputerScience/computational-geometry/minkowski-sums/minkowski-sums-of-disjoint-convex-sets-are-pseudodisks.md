# Minkowski sums of disjoint convex sets are pseudodisks

A finite collection of convex sets $o_1, \dots, o_n$ in the plane is a collection of *pseudodisks* if for any two distinct $o_i, o_j$ both set differences $o_i \setminus o_j$ and $o_j \setminus o_i$ are connected; otherwise $o_i$ and $o_j$ have a *crossing intersection*. Let $T_1, \dots, T_n$ be convex polygons with pairwise disjoint interiors and let $R$ be a convex polygon. Then $\{T_i \oplus R : 1 \le i \le n\}$ is a collection of pseudodisks: no two of them have a crossing intersection, because for each direction the extreme points of $T_i \oplus R$ are sums of extreme points of $T_i$ and of $R$, and the outer common tangents of $T_i$ and $T_j$ separate the directions in which each is the more extreme.
