# Doubly-connected edge list

A *doubly-connected edge list* represents a subdivision of the plane by a planar straight-line graph, not necessarily connected, by three sets of records. Each undirected edge is stored as two oppositely directed *half-edges*; a half-edge $e$ stores its *twin* (the opposite half-edge), its origin vertex $e.\mathrm{org}$ (its destination is $e.\mathrm{twin}.\mathrm{org}$), the face $e.\mathrm{left}$ to its left, and the next and previous half-edges $e.\mathrm{next}$ and $e.\mathrm{prev}$ along the boundary cycle of that face that contains $e$, which runs counterclockwise for an outer cycle and clockwise for an inner cycle. Each vertex stores its coordinates and one half-edge with origin at it. The boundary of a face consists of an outer boundary cycle (none for the unbounded face) and zero or more inner boundary cycles, one for each hole; each face stores one half-edge of its outer cycle and a list with one half-edge of each inner cycle. Following $\mathrm{next}$ pointers from these half-edges enumerates the boundary of a face in time proportional to its number of edges.
