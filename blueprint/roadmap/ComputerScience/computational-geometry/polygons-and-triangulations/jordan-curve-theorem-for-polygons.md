# Jordan curve theorem for polygons

Let $P \subset \mathbb{R}^2$ be the boundary curve of a simple polygon. Then $\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus P$ has exactly two connected components. Exactly one of them is bounded, the *interior* of $P$; the other, the *exterior*, contains the complement of every disk that contains $P$. With the parity $\pi$ of the upward vertical ray, the interior consists of the points of odd parity and the exterior of the points of even parity.

Since $P$ is compact, the complement of a large disk containing $P$ is connected and lies in one component, which is therefore unbounded, while the other component lies inside the disk.
