# Banach–Tarski paradox

In $\mathbb{R}^3$ there are points $c_1$ and $c_2$ such that the closed balls $\bar B(c_1, 1)$ and
$\bar B(c_2, 1)$ are disjoint and the closed unit ball $\bar B(0, 1)$ is finitely equidecomposable
by isometries with $\bar B(c_1, 1) \cup \bar B(c_2, 1)$: the unit ball can be partitioned into
finitely many pieces that, moved by isometries of $\mathbb{R}^3$, form a partition of two disjoint
unit balls.

## Idea

The pieces cannot all be Lebesgue measurable, since isometries preserve volume. So the paradox
shows that no finitely additive, isometry-invariant function on all subsets of $\mathbb{R}^3$
gives the unit ball a finite positive value, in contrast with the plane, where Banach constructed
such a finitely additive measure. The proof finds a free group on two generators inside the
rotation group; that group splits paradoxically into pieces that, after translation by the group,
make two copies of it. With the axiom of choice this transfers to the sphere, up to countably
many points, and then to the ball. The other statements set up equidecomposability and its
properties.
