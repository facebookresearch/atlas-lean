# Szpilrajn's Problem 170

Let $S$ be a subset of the plane such that every subset of $\mathbb{R}^2$ homeomorphic to $S$ is
Lebesgue measurable. Then $S$ is absolutely measurable: it is measurable for the outer measure
induced by every measure on the Borel sets of $\mathbb{R}^2$. The hypothesis concerns every subset
of the plane homeomorphic to $S$, not only the images of $S$ under homeomorphisms of the whole
plane.

## Idea

Lebesgue measurability depends on the measure, while absolutely, or universally, measurable sets
are those that every Borel measure measures; Borel and analytic sets are examples. Szpilrajn asked
whether a set all of whose homeomorphic copies are Lebesgue measurable must be absolutely
measurable, so that a topological hypothesis about one measure controls all measures. The
affirmative answer, recorded in the Scottish Book, follows from an unpublished result of von
Neumann.
