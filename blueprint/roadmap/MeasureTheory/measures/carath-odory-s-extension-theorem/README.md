# Carathéodory extension theorem

Let $\mathcal{A}$ be an algebra of sets on $\alpha$ and $\mu_0 : \mathcal{A} \to [0, \infty]$ an
additive content that is $\sigma$-subadditive on $\mathcal{A}$: whenever
$s_0, s_1, \ldots \in \mathcal{A}$ and $\bigcup_i s_i \in \mathcal{A}$,
$\mu_0(\bigcup_i s_i) \le \sum_i \mu_0(s_i)$. If $\alpha$ is a countable union of sets of
$\mathcal{A}$ of finite content, then there is exactly one measure on the $\sigma$-algebra
$\sigma(\mathcal{A})$ generated by $\mathcal{A}$ that agrees with $\mu_0$ on $\mathcal{A}$.

## Idea

Measures are easy to define on simple sets, such as finite unions of intervals, boxes or
cylinder sets, and hard to define directly on a whole $\sigma$-algebra. The theorem bridges the
gap. The outer measure $\mu^*(E) = \inf \sum_i \mu_0(A_i)$, over countable covers of $E$ by sets of
$\mathcal{A}$, restricts to a measure on the sets that split every set additively (Carathéodory's
criterion); these include $\mathcal{A}$, and $\sigma$-subadditivity makes $\mu^*$ agree with
$\mu_0$ there. Uniqueness follows from the $\pi$-$\lambda$ theorem, using the finiteness
hypothesis. Lebesgue measure arises this way from the lengths of finite unions of intervals.
