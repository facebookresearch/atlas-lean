# σ-algebras

A σ-algebra on a set $X$ is a family $\mathcal{M}$ of subsets of $X$ that contains $\emptyset$, contains
$X \setminus E$ whenever it contains $E$, and contains $\bigcup_n E_n$ whenever it contains $E_1, E_2, \ldots$. The
pair $(X, \mathcal{M})$ is a measurable space, and the members of $\mathcal{M}$ are its measurable sets.
Equivalently, $\mathcal{M}$ is nonempty and closed under complements and countable unions.

## Idea

A σ-algebra is the kind of domain on which a measure can be countably additive: by Vitali's theorem no
translation-invariant measure defined on all subsets of $\mathbb{R}$ gives each interval its length, so measures are
defined on σ-algebras instead. A σ-algebra is also closed under countable intersections and differences, and any
intersection of σ-algebras is one, so every family of sets generates a smallest σ-algebra containing it. The main
example is the Borel σ-algebra of a topological space, generated by the open sets, the card's second statement; on
$\mathbb{R}$ it is also generated by the half-open intervals $(a, b]$.
