# Suprema of sequences of measurable functions are measurable

Let $\alpha$ be a conditionally complete linear order with its order topology, which is second countable, and its
Borel σ-algebra, such as $[0, \infty]$, $[-\infty, \infty]$ or $\mathbb{R}$, and let $\delta$ be a set with a σ-algebra.
For a countable family $(f_i)$ of measurable functions $\delta \to \alpha$, the pointwise supremum
$x \mapsto \sup_i f_i(x)$ is measurable. The supremum is taken in $\alpha$, with the value that $\alpha$ assigns by
convention when the values $f_i(x)$ form an empty set or one not bounded above, as can happen in $\mathbb{R}$.
