# Borel σ-algebras of countable products

Let $X_i$, for $i$ in a countable set, be second countable topological spaces with their Borel σ-algebras. Then the
product σ-algebra on $\prod_i X_i$, generated by the coordinate projections, is the Borel σ-algebra of the product
topology. This is the general form of the identification of the Borel sets of $\mathbb{R}^n$: taking $X_i = \mathbb{R}$
for $i = 1, \ldots, n$, the Borel σ-algebra of $\mathbb{R}^n$ is the product of $n$ copies of the Borel σ-algebra of
$\mathbb{R}$.

## Idea

The coordinate projections are continuous, so the product σ-algebra is contained in the Borel σ-algebra. Conversely,
second countability makes every open set of the product a countable union of basic open boxes, each of which depends on
finitely many coordinates and lies in the product σ-algebra, so every Borel set does too. Without second countability
the two σ-algebras can differ, even for a product of two spaces. The identification is what makes a function with
values in $\mathbb{R}^n$ measurable exactly when its coordinates are, and a random vector a family of random variables.
