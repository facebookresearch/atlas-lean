# Dynkin's π–λ theorem

Let $\alpha$ be a set whose σ-algebra is generated by a family $\mathcal{P}$ of subsets of $\alpha$ that is a π-system:
$A \cap B \in \mathcal{P}$ whenever $A, B \in \mathcal{P}$ and $A \cap B$ is nonempty. Let $C$ be a property of the
measurable subsets of $\alpha$ such that

- $C(\emptyset)$ holds, and $C(A)$ holds for every $A \in \mathcal{P}$;
- if $C(A)$ holds, then $C(\alpha \setminus A)$ holds;
- if $A_1, A_2, \ldots$ are pairwise disjoint measurable sets with $C(A_i)$ for every $i$, then $C(\bigcup_i A_i)$ holds.

Then $C(A)$ holds for every measurable $A \subseteq \alpha$.
