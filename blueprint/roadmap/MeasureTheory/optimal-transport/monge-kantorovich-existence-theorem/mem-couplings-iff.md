# Couplings as sets and as predicates

Let $\mu$ and $\nu$ be probability measures on measurable spaces $X$ and $Y$, and let $\pi$ be a
probability measure on $X \times Y$. Then $\pi$ belongs to the set of [couplings](couplings.md)
$\Pi(\mu, \nu)$ if and only if $\pi$ is a
[coupling of $\mu$ and $\nu$](../kantorovich-duality/is-coupling.md) in the sense used for
Kantorovich duality and the Wasserstein distances: both say that the marginals of $\pi$ are $\mu$
and $\nu$. The two definitions differ only in encoding (a set of measures with a probability
condition, versus a predicate on probability measures).
