# Integral of a product of coordinate functions over Euclidean space

For a natural number `d`, a field `𝕜` equal to `ℝ` or `ℂ` and functions `f_0, …, f_{d-1} : ℝ → 𝕜`,
the integral over the `d`-dimensional Euclidean space, with its volume (Lebesgue) measure, of the
product of the coordinate functions is the product of the one-dimensional integrals:
`∫ ∏_i f_i(x_i) dx = ∏_i ∫ f_i(t) dt`, where `x_i` is the `i`-th coordinate of `x`. No integrability
is assumed: the identity holds for all functions `f_i`, an integral of a non-integrable function
being `0` by convention. It computes the integral of a product of one-dimensional functions, such as
a product wave function in `d` dimensions, coordinate by coordinate.
