# Integral of a nonnegative integrable function

Let $\mu$ be a measure on a measurable space $\Omega$ and let $f \colon \Omega \to \mathbb{R}$ be $\mu$-integrable with
$f \ge 0$ $\mu$-almost everywhere. For a real number $a$ write $a^+ = \max(a, 0) \in [0, \infty)$. Then, in
$[0, \infty]$,

$$
\Big(\int_\Omega f \, d\mu\Big)^+ = \int_\Omega f^+ \, d\mu,
$$

where on the left $\int f \, d\mu$ is the integral of the integrable real function $f$, a real number, and on the
right the integral is that of the function $x \mapsto f(x)^+$ with values in $[0, \infty)$, taken in $[0, \infty]$.
