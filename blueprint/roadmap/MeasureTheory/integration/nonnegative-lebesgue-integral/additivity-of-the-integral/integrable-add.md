# The sum of two integrable functions is integrable

Let $\mu$ be a measure on a measurable space $\Omega$. Let $E$ be a topological space that is an additive monoid with
continuous addition, equipped with a continuous function $\|\cdot\|_e \colon E \to [0, \infty]$ such that
$\|0\|_e = 0$ and $\|x + y\|_e \le \|x\|_e + \|y\|_e$ for all $x, y \in E$ (no homogeneity is required); for example
$\mathbb{R}$ or any normed group, with $\|x\|_e = \|x\|$. A function $f \colon \Omega \to E$ is $\mu$-integrable when it is $\mu$-almost everywhere
strongly measurable (equal $\mu$-almost everywhere to a pointwise limit of simple functions) and
$\int_\Omega \|f\|_e \, d\mu < \infty$, an integral in $[0, \infty]$. If $f, g \colon \Omega \to E$ are $\mu$-integrable,
then $f + g$ is $\mu$-integrable.
