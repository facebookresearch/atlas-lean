# Fubini's theorem for iterated integrals

Let $\mu$ and $\nu$ be s-finite measures on sets $\alpha$ and $\beta$ with σ-algebras, for instance σ-finite measures,
and let $G$ be a real normed vector space. If $f \colon \alpha \times \beta \to G$ is integrable for the product measure
$\mu \times \nu$, then

$$\int \Big( \int f(x, y) \, d\nu(y) \Big) \, d\mu(x) = \int \Big( \int f(x, y) \, d\mu(x) \Big) \, d\nu(y).$$

The integrals are Bochner integrals, which for real and complex functions are Lebesgue integrals: the integral of a
function that is not integrable, such as a section of $f$ on a null set, is $0$, and every integral is $0$ when $G$ is
not complete.
