# Nonzero pairwise orthogonal vectors in a real or complex inner product space are linearly independent

Let $V$ be an inner product space over $\mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$, and let $(v_i)_{i \in I}$ be a family of nonzero vectors of $V$ with $\langle v_i, v_j \rangle = 0$ whenever $i \neq j$. Then $(v_i)_{i \in I}$ is linearly independent. Such a family is injective, since $v_i = v_j$ with $i \neq j$ would give $\langle v_i, v_i \rangle = 0$; in particular, an orthogonal set of vectors that does not contain $0$ is linearly independent.

- Stephen H. Friedberg, Arnold J. Insel and Lawrence E. Spence, Linear Algebra, 4th ed., Prentice Hall, 2003, §6.2, Corollary 2 to Theorem 6.3, p. 342.
