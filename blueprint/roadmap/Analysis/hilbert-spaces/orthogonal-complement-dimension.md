# Dimension of the orthogonal complement in a finite-dimensional real or complex inner product space

Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional inner product space over $\mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$, let $W$ be a subspace of $V$, and let $W^\perp = \{v \in V : \langle w, v \rangle = 0 \text{ for all } w \in W\}$ be its orthogonal complement. Then $\dim W + \dim W^\perp = \dim V$. Since $\langle w, v \rangle = 0$ exactly when $\langle v, w \rangle = 0$, the order of the arguments in the definition of $W^\perp$ does not matter.

- Stephen H. Friedberg, Arnold J. Insel and Lawrence E. Spence, Linear Algebra, 4th ed., Prentice Hall, 2003, §6.2, Theorem 6.7(c), p. 352.
