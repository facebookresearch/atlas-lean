# Pitt's theorem

Let $\mathbb{K}$ be $\mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$ and let
$1 \le q < p < \infty$. Then every bounded linear operator
$T : \ell^p(\mathbb{N}, \mathbb{K}) \to \ell^q(\mathbb{N}, \mathbb{K})$ is compact.

## Sources

- H. R. Pitt, A Note on Bilinear Forms, J. London Math. Soc. s1-11 (1936), 174–180, DOI 10.1112/JLMS/S1-11.3.174, compactness of operators $\ell^p \to \ell^q$ for $q < p$; textbook F. Albiac and N. J. Kalton, Topics in Banach Space Theory, Springer, 2006. The statement here is the case of scalars $\mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$: for $q < p < \infty$ every bounded operator $\ell^p \to \ell^q$ is compact.
