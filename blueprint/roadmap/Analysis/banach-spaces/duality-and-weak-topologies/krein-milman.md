# Krein–Milman theorem

A compact convex set in a Hausdorff locally convex space is the closed convex hull of its extreme points; for a closed disc in the plane, the extreme points are the points of the boundary circle. Mathlib states the theorem for real spaces, which also covers complex ones, and without assuming the set nonempty. Let $E$ be a real topological vector space whose topology is Hausdorff and locally convex: addition and multiplication by real scalars are continuous, and every point has a basis of convex neighbourhoods. Let $K \subseteq E$ be compact and convex. A point $a \in K$ is an *extreme point* of $K$ if, whenever $a$ lies in the open segment between two points $x_1, x_2 \in K$, $x_1 = a$ (and then, by symmetry, $x_2 = a$). Then $K$ is the closure of the convex hull of its set of extreme points, where the convex hull consists of the finite combinations $\sum_i t_i x_i$ of extreme points $x_i$ with real weights $t_i \ge 0$ summing to $1$. If $K$ is empty, both sides are empty. A complex locally convex Hausdorff space is also a real one, by restricting the scalars to $\mathbb{R}$; its convex sets, convex hulls and extreme points are the same in both readings, since convex combinations always have real weights. This gives the familiar statement for real and complex spaces.
