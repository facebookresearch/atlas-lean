# Classical subsolutions are viscosity subsolutions

Let $\Omega$ be open. If $u$ is differentiable at every point of $\Omega$ and
$F(x, u(x), Du(x)) \le 0$ for all $x \in \Omega$, then $u$ is a viscosity subsolution of $F = 0$ on
$\Omega$.

At an interior local maximum of $u - \varphi$ the derivative of $u - \varphi$ vanishes, so
$D\varphi(x) = Du(x)$ and the viscosity inequality is the classical one. In the second-order theory
degenerate ellipticity enters here; at first order nothing else is needed.
