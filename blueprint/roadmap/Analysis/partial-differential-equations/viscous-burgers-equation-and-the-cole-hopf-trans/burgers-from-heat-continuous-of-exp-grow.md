# Joint continuity of the Cole–Hopf field of an exponentially growing profile

Notation: for an initial profile $f$, $H f(t,x) = \int G_t(x-y) f(y)\,dy$ for $t > 0$ ($G_t$ the one-dimensional heat kernel) and $H f(t,x) = f(x)$ for $t \le 0$; the Cole–Hopf field of a positive profile $f$ with viscosity $\nu$ is $u(t,x) = -2\nu\, \partial_x \varphi(t,x) / \varphi(t,x)$ with $\varphi(t,x) = H f(\nu t, x)$. For a continuous initial velocity $u_0$ and an anchor $x_0$, the anchored profile is $f(x) = \exp\bigl(-\tfrac{1}{2\nu}\int_{x_0}^x u_0\bigr)$, and the constructed Burgers solution is the Cole–Hopf field of this profile. The viscous Burgers equation is $u_t + u\,u_x = \nu\,u_{xx}$. Positive `C¹` profiles with exponentially growing derivative give joint continuity
of the Cole–Hopf field. Continuity allows any scaling `ν`; the heat profile is
stationary where `ν * t ≤ 0`. No Burgers PDE is asserted for nonpositive viscosity.
