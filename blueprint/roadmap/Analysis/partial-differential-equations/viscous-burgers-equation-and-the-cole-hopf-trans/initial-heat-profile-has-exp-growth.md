# Exponential growth of the anchored heat profile of a bounded velocity

Notation: for an initial profile $f$, $H f(t,x) = \int G_t(x-y) f(y)\,dy$ for $t > 0$ ($G_t$ the one-dimensional heat kernel) and $H f(t,x) = f(x)$ for $t \le 0$; the Cole–Hopf field of a positive profile $f$ with viscosity $\nu$ is $u(t,x) = -2\nu\, \partial_x \varphi(t,x) / \varphi(t,x)$ with $\varphi(t,x) = H f(\nu t, x)$. For a continuous initial velocity $u_0$ and an anchor $x_0$, the anchored profile is $f(x) = \exp\bigl(-\tfrac{1}{2\nu}\int_{x_0}^x u_0\bigr)$, and the constructed Burgers solution is the Cole–Hopf field of this profile. A function $g$ has exponential growth when $|g(x)| \le M e^{c|x|}$ for some $M, c \ge 0$. The viscous Burgers equation is $u_t + u\,u_x = \nu\,u_{xx}$. Bounded velocity yields at most exponential growth of its anchored heat profile.
