# Leading real eigenvalue (tr M + √(tr² M − 4 det M))/2

For a real $2 \times 2$ matrix $M$, the leading real eigenvalue is

$$\lambda_+(M) = \frac{\operatorname{tr} M + \sqrt{(\operatorname{tr} M)^2 - 4 \det M}}{2},$$

with $\sqrt{x}$ read as $0$ for $x < 0$. When the discriminant $(\operatorname{tr} M)^2 - 4 \det M$
is nonnegative it is the larger eigenvalue of $M$. When the discriminant is negative it is the
common real part $\operatorname{tr} M / 2$ of the two complex eigenvalues, not an eigenvalue. For a
mode matrix near a neutral Turing mode with negative trace it is the critical eigenvalue that
crosses zero.
