# Time derivative of the time-scaled heat solution for exponentially growing data

Let $\Phi(t, x) = (4\pi t)^{-1/2} e^{-x^2/(4t)}$ be the heat kernel on $\mathbb{R}$, and for $f : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ let $u(t, x) = \int \Phi(t, x - y) f(y)\, dy$ for $t > 0$ (and $u(t, x) = f(x)$ for $t \le 0$). Let $f$ be almost everywhere strongly measurable with at most exponential growth, $\nu > 0$ and $t > 0$. For every $x$, $s \mapsto u(\nu s, x)$ is differentiable at $t$ with derivative $\nu\, \partial_x^2 u(\nu t, x)$.
