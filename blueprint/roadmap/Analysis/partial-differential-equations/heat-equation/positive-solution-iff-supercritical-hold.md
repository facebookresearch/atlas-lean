# Liouville theorem for the semilinear heat equation

This settles [Quittner's conjecture on positive entire solutions of u_t − Δu = uᵖ](positive-solution-iff-supercritical.md) in the affirmative: its statement holds.

Let $N \ge 1$ be an integer and $p > 1$ a real number. A positive classical solution of $u_t - \Delta u = u^p$ on $\mathbb{R}^N \times \mathbb{R}$ is a twice continuously differentiable function $u(x, t) > 0$ satisfying the equation at every point, where $u_t$ is the derivative in $t$ and $\Delta u$ the Laplacian in $x$. Such a solution exists if and only if $p(N - 2) \ge N + 2$.

- If $p(N - 2) \ge N + 2$, the equation has positive bounded radial steady states, which are positive classical solutions.
- If $p(N - 2) < N + 2$, that is, $1 < p < p_S$ with $p_S$ the Sobolev exponent, there is no positive classical solution on $\mathbb{R}^N \times \mathbb{R}$, with no assumption of boundedness, symmetry or decay (Quittner, Theorem 1). In particular there is none for $N \le 2$.
