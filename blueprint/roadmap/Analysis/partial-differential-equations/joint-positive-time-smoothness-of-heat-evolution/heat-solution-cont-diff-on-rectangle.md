# The heat solution is smooth on positive-time rectangles

Let $\Phi(t, x) = (4\pi t)^{-1/2} e^{-x^2/(4t)}$ be the heat kernel on $\mathbb{R}$, and for $f : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ let $u(t, x) = \int \Phi(t, x - y) f(y)\, dy$ for $t > 0$ (and $u(t, x) = f(x)$ for $t \le 0$). Let $f$ be almost everywhere strongly measurable and bounded, and let $I \subseteq (0, \infty)$ and $J \subseteq \mathbb{R}$. Then $u$ is jointly $C^\infty$ on $I \times J$; neither set needs to be bounded.
