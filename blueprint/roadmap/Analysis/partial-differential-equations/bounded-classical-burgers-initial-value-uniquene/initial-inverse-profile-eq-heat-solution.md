# Initial-time inverse normalization gives the heat evolution

Let $\nu>0$ and $T\ge0$. Assume $u:[0,\infty)\times\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$ is jointly continuous, jointly $C^2$ at positive times, satisfies $u_t+uu_x=\nu u_{xx}$ for every positive time and spatial point, and is uniformly bounded on $[0,T]\times\mathbb R$. For every anchor $a$, its initial-time normalized inverse profile equals $P_{\nu t}\phi_0(x)$ for $0\le t\le T$, where $\phi_0(x)=\exp(-(2\nu)^{-1}\int_a^x u(0,y)\,dy)$ and $P$ is the Gaussian heat semigroup. The inverse profile is $\exp(N_a(t)-(2\nu)^{-1}\int_a^x u(t,y)\,dy)$ with the initial-time normalization $N_a$ described in the topic index. Whole-half-line continuity, regularity and the PDE are retained; only the velocity bound is restricted to the finite strip.
