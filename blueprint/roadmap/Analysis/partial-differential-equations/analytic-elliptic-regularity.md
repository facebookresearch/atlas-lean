# Analytic elliptic regularity

This node is still to be broken down. It covers the analyticity of solutions of elliptic equations with analytic
coefficients, which the regularity of automorphic forms and Harish-Chandra's analyticity theorem use. Let $X$ be a real
analytic manifold and $D$ a linear differential operator on $X$ with real analytic coefficients. $D$ is elliptic at $x$
if its principal symbol $\sigma(D)_x$, a homogeneous polynomial on $T^*_x X$, vanishes only at $0$, and elliptic if it
is elliptic at every point.

- **Theorem.** If $D$ is elliptic, every distribution $u$ on $X$ with $Du$ real analytic, in particular every
  distributional solution of $Du = 0$, is a real analytic function.
- **Invariant elliptic operators on Lie groups.** Let $G$ be a real [Lie group](../../Geometry/differential-topology/lie-group.md)
  and $K$ a compact subgroup. The Laplace operator of a left-invariant Riemannian metric on $G$ that is also invariant
  under right translations by $K$ is elliptic, has real analytic coefficients and is given by an element of
  $U(\mathfrak{g})^K$. For a Cartan decomposition $\mathfrak{g} = \mathfrak{k} \oplus \mathfrak{p}$ of a semisimple Lie
  algebra, the operator $\Omega = 2 C_{\mathfrak{k}} + C_{\mathfrak{g}}$ built from the Casimir operator $C_{\mathfrak{g}}$ of $\mathfrak{g}$ for the
  Killing form $B$ and the Casimir operator $C_{\mathfrak{k}}$ of $\mathfrak{k}$ for the positive definite form $-B$ on $\mathfrak{k}$ is the sum of
  the squares of a basis of $\mathfrak{g}$ orthonormal for the positive definite form $-B(X, \theta Y)$, with $\theta$ the Cartan
  involution, hence elliptic, and it lies in the subalgebra generated by $Z(\mathfrak{g})$ and $U(\mathfrak{k})$. For a reductive Lie
  algebra $\mathfrak{g} = \mathfrak{z} \oplus [\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g}]$, beyond Borel's semisimple case, this operator, formed for $[\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g}]$, misses the central
  directions; $\Omega + \sum_j Z_j^2$, for a basis $(Z_j)$ of the real centre $\mathfrak{z}$, is again a sum of squares of a basis of $\mathfrak{g}$,
  hence elliptic, and again lies in that subalgebra. The elements of $U(\mathfrak{g})$ act here as left-invariant differential
  operators, through the distributions supported at the identity.

## Expansion

A good expansion has a definition page for elliptic operators, a statement page for the theorem, first for smooth
solutions of homogeneous equations and then for distributions and analytic right-hand sides, and a statement page for
the invariant elliptic operators on Lie groups, each citing the sources below with locators. Casselman follows the proof
of F. John through plane wave decompositions; the other classical proof, through estimates of derivatives, is due to
Morrey and Nirenberg.

An expansion along Casselman's route also writes out this step of its own proof:

- the Cauchy–Kovalevskaya theorem for a noncharacteristic analytic Cauchy problem of arbitrary order, reduced to a first-order
  system (Casselman, Proposition 8.3), from which the plane-wave solutions are built. The vault's page on the
  Cauchy–Kovalevskaya theorem treats a scalar first-order equation and does not cover it.

  Proposition 8.3 (the noncharacteristic analytic Cauchy problem of arbitrary order).
- A. Borel, "Automorphic forms on reductive groups", lecture notes (Park City),
