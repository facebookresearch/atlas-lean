# Bounded partial derivatives of x³/(x² + y²)

Let $f : \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}$ satisfy $f(0,0) = 0$ and $f(x, y) = x^3/(x^2 + y^2)$ for $(x, y) \ne (0, 0)$. Rudin, Exercise 9.14(a), with existence: both partial derivatives of `f` exist at every
point of the plane, the first with values in `[0, 9 / 8]` and the second with values in
`[-1, 1]`.

## Sources

- W. Rudin, *Principles of Mathematical Analysis*, 3rd ed., McGraw-Hill, 1976, Chapter 9, Exercise 14(a).
