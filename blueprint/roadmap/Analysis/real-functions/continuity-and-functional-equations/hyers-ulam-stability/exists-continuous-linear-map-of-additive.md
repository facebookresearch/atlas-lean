# Continuous Hyers–Ulam stability

Let $E$ and $F$ be real normed spaces with $F$ complete, let $\delta \ge 0$, and let $f \colon E \to F$ satisfy
$\|f(x + y) - f(x) - f(y)\| \le \delta$ for all $x, y$. If $f$ is continuous at $0$ (in particular if $f$ itself is
continuous), then there is a continuous real-linear map $A \colon E \to F$ with $\|f(x) - A(x)\| \le \delta$ for all
$x$: the additive approximant from Hyers' theorem is continuous. The proof: continuity of $f$ at $0$ bounds $f$ on a
small ball, so the approximant is bounded on that ball, and an additive map bounded on a ball is continuous at $0$ by
the dyadic scaling argument.
