# Existence of the left-endpoint Riemann–Stieltjes integral

For $f, h : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ and $a, b \in \mathbb{R}$, the left-endpoint integral of $f$ against $h$ on $[a, b]$ exists when the left-endpoint sums $\sum f(u)\,(h(x) - h(u))$ over the cells $(u, x]$ of the partitions of $[a, b]$ converge along refinement (see [the left-endpoint Riemann–Stieltjes integral](left-rsintegral.md), whose partitions insert both endpoints). For $a > b$ every partition has the single cell $(b, a]$, so the integral always exists, with value $f(b)\,(h(a) - h(b))$; it is not an oriented integral.
