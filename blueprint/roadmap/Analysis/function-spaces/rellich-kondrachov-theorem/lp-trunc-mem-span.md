# Truncations lie in the span of the first basis vectors

Let $\mathbb{K}$ be $\mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$. For every $N \in \mathbb{N}$ and $x \in \ell^2(\mathbb{N}, \mathbb{K})$, the truncation $x^{(N)} = (x_0, \dots, x_{N-1}, 0, \dots)$ lies in the span of the first $N$ standard basis vectors $e_0, \dots, e_{N-1}$.
