# Existence of minimizers for quasiconvex elastic energies

Extend elasticity existence theory from stronger convexity assumptions to quasiconvex energies that become infinite as a deformation collapses or reverses orientation.

## What would settle it

Under Ball's full admissibility, coercivity, boundary, and determinant-barrier hypotheses, prove that the infimum of $\int_\Omega W(Dy)$ is attained when W is quasiconvex. Standard p-growth theorems cannot simply be invoked when their upper-growth assumptions conflict with the determinant barrier.

## Status

The cited sources pose this as an open question in the versions cited; this page does not certify its present status. The supporting open-problem statement is older or its status evidence is incomplete; check later literature before relying on it.

Ball states this as Problem 1 and explains that existence theorems for general quasiconvex integrands assume growth conditions incompatible with the requirement that the energy tend to infinity as the determinant tends to zero.

This page records the cited research question and asserts no theorem or present-status certification.
