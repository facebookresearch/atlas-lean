# Periodic approximant is a periodic point

Let $\mathbb{K}$ be $\mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$, let $B$ be the backward shift on $\ell^2(\mathbb{N}, \mathbb{K})$, let $\mu \in \mathbb{K}$ with $|\mu| > 1$ and $N \ge 1$. For every $y \in \ell^2(\mathbb{N}, \mathbb{K})$, the [periodic approximant](periodic-approx.md) $P_{\mu, N}(y)$ is a periodic point of $\mu B$ of period $N$: $(\mu B)^N P_{\mu, N}(y) = P_{\mu, N}(y)$.
