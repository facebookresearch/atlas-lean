# Spectral theorem for unbounded self-adjoint operators, and trace-class operators

This node is still to be broken down. It covers the Hilbert-space operator theory that the pages on the
coefficients of square-integrable representations and on distribution characters use. Inner products are linear in the
second variable, as in Reed and Simon.

- **Spectral theorem.** Let $A$ be a self-adjoint operator, possibly unbounded, with domain $D(A)$ on a
  separable Hilbert space $\mathcal{H}$. There are a finite measure space $(M, \mu)$, a unitary operator
  $U \colon \mathcal{H} \to L^2(M, \mu)$ and a real-valued measurable function $f$ on $M$, finite almost everywhere,
  such that $\psi \in D(A)$ if and only if $f \cdot U\psi \in L^2(M, \mu)$, and $UAU^{-1}$ is multiplication by $f$
  on $U(D(A))$. Equivalently, $A = \int \lambda \, dP_\lambda$ for a unique projection-valued measure $P$ on
  $\mathbb{R}$, and bounded Borel functions $g$ define bounded operators $g(A)$ through a $*$-homomorphism. The
  projection-valued measure form and the functional calculus hold on an arbitrary Hilbert space, which is the
  orthogonal direct sum of separable subspaces invariant under $A$. Unbounded Borel functions define self-adjoint
  operators $g(A)$ for real $g$; for $A \ge 0$ this gives the positive self-adjoint square root $A^{1/2}$.
- **Polar decomposition.** Let $T \colon \mathcal{H} \to \mathcal{H}'$ be a closed densely defined operator between Hilbert
  spaces. Then $T^* T$ is a positive self-adjoint operator, $\lvert T \rvert = (T^* T)^{1/2}$ has domain
  $D(\lvert T \rvert) = D(T)$ with $\lVert \lvert T \rvert \psi \rVert = \lVert T \psi \rVert$, and $T = U \lvert T \rvert$ for a unique partial isometry
  $U$ with initial space $(\ker T)^\perp$ and final space the closure of the range of $T$.
- **Trace-class operators.** A bounded operator $A$ on a Hilbert space is trace class if the sum of nonnegative terms
  $\operatorname{tr} \lvert A \rvert = \sum_{i \in I} \langle \lvert A \rvert \varphi_i, \varphi_i \rangle$ is finite for an orthonormal basis
  $(\varphi_i)_{i \in I}$, where $\lvert A \rvert = (A^* A)^{1/2}$; this does not depend on the basis. The trace-class operators
  form a two-sided $*$-ideal of the bounded operators; for $A$ trace class, $\operatorname{tr} A = \sum_{i \in I} \langle \varphi_i, A \varphi_i \rangle$
  converges absolutely and does not depend on the orthonormal basis, $\operatorname{tr}$ is linear, and
  $\operatorname{tr}(AB) = \operatorname{tr}(BA)$ for every bounded $B$.

## Expansion

A good expansion has statement pages for the multiplication-operator, functional-calculus and
projection-valued-measure forms of the spectral theorem, for the polar decomposition, and definition and statement
pages for the trace class and the trace, each citing the sources below with locators.

- Reed and Simon deduce the unbounded spectral theorem from the spectral theorem for the bounded normal operator
  $(A + i)^{-1}$, an extension of the bounded self-adjoint case through the commuting self-adjoint real and imaginary
  parts of the resolvent.
- On a nonseparable space, let $R = (A + i)^{-1}$. The closed subspaces generated by one vector under the $*$-algebra
  $C^*(R)$ generated by $R$, the closures of the spans of the $R^j (R^*)^k v$, are separable and reduce $R$, hence $A$: they and
  their orthogonal complements are invariant. Zorn's lemma gives an orthogonal decomposition into such subspaces, on
  which the separable statements apply and glue. Subspaces merely invariant under $R$ do not suffice: for multiplication
  $U$ by $z$ on $L^2$ of the circle and $A = i(I + U)(I - U)^{-1}$, the subspace generated by $1$ under $R = (I - U)/(2i)$ is the
  Hardy space, whose orthogonal complement is not $R$-invariant.
- Reed and Simon state the polar decomposition for an operator on one Hilbert space, through the closed quadratic form
  $\psi \mapsto \lVert T\psi \rVert^2$. For two spaces, apply it to the closed operator $(\psi, \psi') \mapsto (0, T\psi)$ on
  $\mathcal{H} \oplus \mathcal{H}'$, with domain $D(T) \oplus \mathcal{H}'$.
- Reed and Simon treat the trace class on a separable space. A trace-class operator is compact, so it vanishes on the
  orthogonal complement of a separable subspace, and the statements reduce to that case.
