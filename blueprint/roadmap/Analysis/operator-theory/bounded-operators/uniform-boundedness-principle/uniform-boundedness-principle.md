# Uniform boundedness principle (Banach–Steinhaus theorem)

If a family of bounded linear operators from a Banach space to a normed space, over $\mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$, is bounded at each point, then the operator norms of the family are bounded. Mathlib proves this for semilinear maps between seminormed spaces, with only the domain complete. Let $\mathbb{K}$ and $\mathbb{K}'$ be nontrivially normed fields, such as $\mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$, and let $\sigma \colon \mathbb{K} \to \mathbb{K}'$ be a ring homomorphism with $|\sigma(c)| = |c|$ for all $c \in \mathbb{K}$. Let $X$ be a complete seminormed space over $\mathbb{K}$, let $Y$ be a seminormed space over $\mathbb{K}'$, and let $(T_i)_{i \in I}$ be any family of continuous $\sigma$-semilinear maps $T_i \colon X \to Y$, that is, additive maps with $T_i(cx) = \sigma(c)\,T_i(x)$. Suppose that for every $x \in X$ there is a real number $C_x$ with $\|T_i x\| \le C_x$ for all $i \in I$. Then there is a real number $C$ with $\|T_i\| \le C$ for all $i \in I$, where $\|T_i\|$ is the operator norm. The codomain need not be complete and the family need not be countable. For the familiar statement take $\mathbb{K} = \mathbb{K}'$, equal to $\mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$, and $\sigma$ the identity, so that the $T_i$ are bounded linear operators; replacing $C$ by $\max(C, 0)$ gives a nonnegative bound, also for an empty family.
