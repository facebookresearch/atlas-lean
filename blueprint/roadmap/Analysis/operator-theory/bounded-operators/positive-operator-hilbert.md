# Positive operator on a real or complex inner product space

A bounded linear operator $A$ on a complex Hilbert space $H$ is positive when $\langle Ah, h\rangle$ is a nonnegative real number for every $h \in H$. Mathlib defines, for $\mathbb{K} = \mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$ and an inner product space $E$ over $\mathbb{K}$, not necessarily complete, a continuous linear operator $T\colon E \to E$ to be positive when it is symmetric, $\langle Tx, y\rangle = \langle x, Ty\rangle$ for all $x, y \in E$, and the real part of $\langle Tx, x\rangle$ is nonnegative for every $x \in E$; Mathlib’s inner product is conjugate-linear in its first argument, and whether a number is real and nonnegative does not depend on that convention. Over the complex numbers Mathlib proves that $T$ is positive exactly when, for every $x \in E$, the number $\langle Tx, x\rangle$ is real, that is, equal to its real part, and nonnegative, with no completeness assumption. Over the real numbers symmetry is a separate requirement: the rotation of the plane by a right angle has $\langle Tx, x\rangle = 0$ for every $x$ but is not symmetric.
