# Adjoint of a bounded operator between Hilbert spaces

Every bounded linear operator $A$ on a Hilbert space $H$ over $\mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$ has a unique bounded linear operator $A^\dagger$ on $H$, its *adjoint*, with $\langle Ax, y\rangle = \langle x, A^\dagger y\rangle$ for all $x, y \in H$; for a complex matrix acting on $\mathbb{C}^n$ it is the conjugate transpose. Mathlib defines the adjoint more generally between two spaces: for complete inner product spaces $E$ and $F$ over $\mathbb{K} = \mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$, the adjoint is a map $A \mapsto A^\dagger$ from the bounded linear operators $E \to F$ to the bounded linear operators $F \to E$, with $\langle x, A^\dagger y\rangle = \langle Ax, y\rangle$ and $\langle A^\dagger y, x\rangle = \langle y, Ax\rangle$ for all $x \in E$ and $y \in F$. This map is bundled as a conjugate-linear isometric equivalence: $(A + B)^\dagger = A^\dagger + B^\dagger$, $(cA)^\dagger = \bar{c}\,A^\dagger$ for $c \in \mathbb{K}$, $\|A^\dagger\| = \|A\|$, and $A \mapsto A^\dagger$ is a bijection, with $(A^\dagger)^\dagger = A$; each $A^\dagger$ is itself linear and bounded, and no invertibility of $A$ is assumed. The adjoint is unique: for bounded linear operators $B\colon F \to E$ and $C\colon E \to F$, $C = B^\dagger$ exactly when $\langle Cx, y\rangle = \langle x, By\rangle$ for all $x \in E$ and $y \in F$. Inner products are conjugate-linear in the first argument, following Mathlib; over $\mathbb{R}$ the map $A \mapsto A^\dagger$ is linear. Taking $E = F = H$ gives the operator $A^\dagger$ on $H$ above.
