# Bounded inverse theorem

A bijective bounded linear operator between real or complex Banach spaces has a bounded inverse. This is the bijective case of the open mapping theorem for Banach spaces: if $T$ is a continuous bijection that maps open sets to open sets, then for every open set $U$ the preimage of $U$ under $T^{-1}$ is $T(U)$, which is open, so $T^{-1}$ is continuous. Mathlib states the result for semilinear equivalences between normed spaces over two fields. Let $\mathbb{K}$ and $\mathbb{K}'$ be nontrivially normed fields and let $\sigma \colon \mathbb{K} \to \mathbb{K}'$ and $\sigma' \colon \mathbb{K}' \to \mathbb{K}$ be ring homomorphisms that are inverse to each other and preserve the norm. Let $E$ be a complete normed space over $\mathbb{K}$ and $F$ a complete normed space over $\mathbb{K}'$, and let $e \colon E \to F$ be a $\sigma$-semilinear bijection, packaged with its inverse as a semilinear equivalence. If $e$ is continuous, then its inverse is continuous. The inverse is continuous but need not be isometric. For linear maps between normed spaces, continuity is the same as boundedness; taking $\mathbb{K} = \mathbb{K}'$ equal to $\mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$ and $\sigma$, $\sigma'$ the identity gives the familiar statement.
