# Dyadic maximal function

The dyadic maximal function of $f \colon \mathbb{R} \to E$ is

$$M_d f(x) = \sup_{k \in \mathbb{Z}} A_k f(x) = \sup_{I \ni x \text{ dyadic}} \frac{1}{|I|} \int_I \|f\|,$$

the supremum of the [dyadic averages](dyadic-average.md) over all dyadic intervals containing $x$; it takes values in $[0, \infty]$ and is measurable. This is definition (1.4) of T. Hytönen, *Dyadic analysis and weights*, lecture notes, University of Helsinki, version of 2 May 2014 in dimension $d = 1$.

The dyadic maximal function is the simplest maximal operator: dyadic intervals are nested, so the maximal intervals of any family of dyadic intervals with bounded lengths are disjoint, and no covering lemma is needed for its weak type (1,1) bound. On the line, every finite interval $I$ lies in an interval $J$ with $3|I| < |J| \le 6|I|$ from the standard dyadic system or from one shifted copy of it (T. Hytönen, *Dyadic analysis and weights*, lecture notes, University of Helsinki, version of 2 May 2014, Proposition 2.9), so the Hardy–Littlewood maximal function over intervals is at most $6$ times the larger of the two dyadic maximal functions, and the dyadic bounds carry over.

## Background

- [Doob's maximal inequalities](../../../../Probability/martingales/doob-s-maximal-inequalities/README.md): on $[0,1)$ with Lebesgue measure and for $\|f\|$ integrable on $[0,1)$, $A_k f$ for $k \ge 0$ is the conditional expectation of $\|f\|$ given the $\sigma$-algebra generated by the level-$k$ intervals, so $\sup_{k \ge 0} A_k f$ is Doob's maximal function of a nonnegative martingale and the weak type (1,1) bound is Doob's maximal inequality.
