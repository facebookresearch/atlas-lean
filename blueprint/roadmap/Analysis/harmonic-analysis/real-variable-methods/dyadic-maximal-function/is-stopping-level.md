# Stopping level

A level $k \in \mathbb{Z}$ is the stopping level of $x$ at height $c$ for $f$ when

$$A_k f(x) > c \quad\text{and}\quad A_j f(x) \le c \text{ for all } j < k,$$

so that the level-$k$ [dyadic interval](dyadic-interval.md) $Q$ containing $x$ is a maximal dyadic interval whose [average](dyadic-average.md) exceeds $c$: these are the maximal cubes $\mathcal{Q}_\lambda^*$ of the proof of T. Hytönen, *Dyadic analysis and weights*, lecture notes, University of Helsinki, version of 2 May 2014, Proposition 1.5, in dimension one.

The stopping level is unique; every point of $Q$ has the same stopping level, so two stopping intervals are equal or disjoint (the dyadic case of T. Hytönen, *Dyadic analysis and weights*, lecture notes, University of Helsinki, version of 2 May 2014, Lemma 1.3(2)); and $A_k f(x) \le 2c$, since the parent interval has twice the length and average at most $c$.
