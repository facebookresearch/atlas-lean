# Low total influence implies low-degree spectral concentration

For every $f : \{-1, 1\}^n \to \mathbb{R}$ and every $\varepsilon > 0$, the Fourier spectrum of $f$
is $\varepsilon$-concentrated on degree up to $\mathbf{I}[f]/\varepsilon$:

$$\sum_{|S| > \mathbf{I}[f]/\varepsilon} \hat f(S)^2 \le \varepsilon .$$

Together with the Low-Degree Algorithm this makes functions of small total influence (for
example monotone functions) learnable from random examples.

## Sources

- R. O'Donnell, *Analysis of Boolean Functions*, Cambridge University Press, 2014 (arXiv:2105.10386), Proposition 3.2 (Definition 3.1 for $\varepsilon$-concentration).
