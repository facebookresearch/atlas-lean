# Proposition 4

If $T \in \mathcal{S}'(\mathbb{R})$, and define $S$ by $S(\varphi) = T(\check{\varphi})$, then $S \in \mathcal{S}'(\mathbb{R})$ and $\hat{S} = T$. In other words, the mapping $T \mapsto \check{T}$ defined by $\check{T}(\varphi) = T(\check{\varphi})$ inverts the Fourier transform on $\mathcal{S}'(\mathbb{R})$.
