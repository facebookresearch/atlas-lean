# Argument principle for a simple closed curve

Let $f : \mathbb{C} \to \mathbb{C}$ be meromorphic on an open set $\Omega$, and let $\gamma$ be a simple closed piecewise $C^1$ curve in $\Omega$ whose inside lies in $\Omega$, which winds once around every point of its inside (positive orientation), and at every point of which $f$ has meromorphic order $0$. Then only finitely many points of the inside of $\gamma$ have nonzero order, and

$$\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_\gamma \frac{f'(z)}{f(z)}\,dz = N - P,$$

where $N$ and $P$ are the sums over the inside of $\gamma$ of the positive and of the negative parts of the meromorphic order of $f$: the zeros and poles of the meromorphic function, counted with multiplicity, after removable values are corrected. Point values do not matter: $f(z) = z$ with $f(0)$ changed to $1$ has no pointwise zero, but its order at $0$ is $1$ and the unit-circle integral counts it.
