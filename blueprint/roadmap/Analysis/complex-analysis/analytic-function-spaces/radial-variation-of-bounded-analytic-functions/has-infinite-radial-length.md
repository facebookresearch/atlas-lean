# Radial images of infinite length

For $f : \mathbb{C} \to \mathbb{C}$ and $\theta \in \mathbb{R}$, the criterion is that the speed $r \mapsto \bigl|\frac{d}{dr} f(r e^{i\theta})\bigr|$ of the [radial curve](radial.md) at angle $\theta$ is not Lebesgue integrable on $(0, 1)$. The derivative is total: it is taken to be $0$ at any $r$ where the radial curve is not differentiable. For an arbitrary $f$ this is therefore a condition on that speed function, not the length of a possibly nonsmooth path.

When $f$ is holomorphic on the open unit disc, the radial curve is smooth on $(0, 1)$ and the integral of its speed is its length, so the criterion says that the image of the radius at angle $\theta$, traced as a curve, has infinite length.
