# A transcendental entire function preserving rationality on the real line

This settles [Erdős Problem 226 on entire functions preserving the rationals](nonlinear-entire-rational-iff.md) in the affirmative: its statement holds.

Barth and Schneider proved that for countable dense sets $A, B \subseteq \mathbb{R}$ there is a transcendental entire function $f$ whose restriction to $\mathbb{R}$ is a monotone homeomorphism of $\mathbb{R}$ onto itself taking $A$ onto $B$; thus for real $x$, $f(x) \in B$ exactly when $x \in A$, that is $f^{-1}(B) \cap \mathbb{R} = A$. With $A = B = \mathbb{Q}$ this answers the question, and $f$ is not of the form $z \mapsto az + b$ because it is transcendental. The condition concerns real points only: by the great Picard theorem a transcendental entire function takes every complex value, with at most one exception, infinitely often in $\mathbb{C}$, so the complex preimages of $B$ are not confined to $A$. erdosproblems.com/226 lists the problem as proved.
