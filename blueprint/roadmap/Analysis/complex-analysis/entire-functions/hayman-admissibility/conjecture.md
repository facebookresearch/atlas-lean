# Hayman's conjectured lower bound 1/4 for the limsup of b(r)

Hayman showed that $\limsup_{r\to\infty} b(r) \ge A$ for every transcendental entire function, for an absolute constant $0.18 < A \le 1/4$ ([Hayman's lower bound](hayman-bound.md)), and the source conjectures that the correct constant is $1/4$.

The proposition linked here is the lower bound with that constant: for every transcendental entire function $f$,

$$
\limsup_{r\to\infty} b(r) \ge \tfrac14,
$$

where $b(r) = (r\,d/dr)^2 \log M(r, f)$ is [Hayman's function b(r)](hayman-b.md), with its convention at radii where a derivative does not exist, and the limsup is taken in the extended reals. It does not assert that $1/4$ cannot be replaced by a larger constant; that historical sharpness claim is separate. Its negation is a transcendental entire function with $\limsup_{r\to\infty} b(r) < 1/4$, a counterexample to the bound itself.

Its resolution: [Kjellberg's refutation of the lower bound 1/4 for b(r)](conjecture-refuted.md).
