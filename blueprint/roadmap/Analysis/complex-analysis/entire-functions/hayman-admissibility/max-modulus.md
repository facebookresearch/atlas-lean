# Maximum modulus of an entire function

For $f : \mathbb{C} \to \mathbb{C}$ and real $r$, the maximum modulus is the real supremum

$$
M(r, f) = \sup\{|f(z)| : |z| \le r\}.
$$

For continuous $f$ and $r \ge 0$ the set is nonempty and compact, so the supremum is attained and is the classical maximum of $|f|$ on the closed disc (by the maximum principle, on the circle $|z| = r$ when $f$ is entire). For $r < 0$ the set is empty and the real-supremum convention gives $M(r, f) = 0$; only large $r$ matter below.
