# Maximum term of a power series

For coefficients $a_n \in \mathbb{C}$ ($n \ge 0$) and real $r$, the maximum term is the real supremum

$$
\mu(r) = \sup_{n \ge 0} |a_n| r^n,
$$

which by the convention for real suprema is $0$ when the family is not bounded above. For an arbitrary sequence the supremum need not be attained: with $r = 1$ and $a_n = 1 - 1/(n+2)$ it equals $1$, but no term equals $1$.

Classically, for the power series of an entire function $f(z) = \sum a_n z^n$ and $r \ge 0$, the terms $|a_n| r^n$ are nonnegative and tend to $0$, so the supremum is attained and $\mu(r) = \max_n |a_n| r^n$ is the largest term.
