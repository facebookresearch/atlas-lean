# Multipliers do not depend on the normalizing map

Let $g$ be a Möbius transformation and $k \in \mathbb{C}$. Then $k$ is a multiplier of $g$ in the normal form (12) if and only if $g$ has two distinct fixed points $\alpha, \beta$ on the Riemann sphere such that every Möbius map $T$ with $T\alpha = 0$ and $T\beta = \infty$ satisfies $T(g x) = k \cdot T x$ for all $x$. So the normal form does not depend on the choice of $T$; for finite $\alpha, \beta$ one such map is $T z = (z - \alpha)/(z - \beta)$.
