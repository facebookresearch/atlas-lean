# Mittag-Leffler's theorem

Let $(b_\nu)_{\nu \in \mathbb{N}}$ be pairwise distinct complex numbers with $|b_\nu| \to \infty$, and let $P_\nu$ be complex polynomials with zero constant term. Then:

1. there is a meromorphic function $f$ on $\mathbb{C}$, analytic at every point outside $\{b_\nu\}$, such that for each $\nu$ the difference $f(z) - P_\nu\big((z - b_\nu)^{-1}\big)$ agrees on a punctured neighborhood of $b_\nu$ with a function analytic at $b_\nu$; and
2. there are polynomials $p_\nu$ such that a function $f$ has the properties in (1) if and only if there is an entire function $g$ with
$$f(z) - g(z) = \sum_{\nu} \Big(P_\nu\big((z - b_\nu)^{-1}\big) - p_\nu(z)\Big)$$
for every $z \notin \{b_\nu\}$, the series converging there.

The zero constant term is the source's normalization making $P_\nu\big((z - b_\nu)^{-1}\big)$ a genuine singular part; a constant term could be absorbed into $g$.
