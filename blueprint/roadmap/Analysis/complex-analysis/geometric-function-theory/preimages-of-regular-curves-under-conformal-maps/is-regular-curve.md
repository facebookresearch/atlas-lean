# Ahlfors-regular curves

A set $\Gamma \subseteq \mathbb{C}$ is a regular curve when it is [regular](is-regular.md), that is, there is $C \ge 0$ with $\mathcal{H}^1(\Gamma \cap B(c, r)) \le C r$ for every disc $B(c, r)$ of radius $r > 0$, and it is the image $\gamma([a, b])$ of a compact interval $[a, b] \subset \mathbb{R}$ under a map $\gamma$ continuous on $[a, b]$.

The compact interval matters: the whole real line satisfies the length bound and is a continuous image of $\mathbb{R}$, but it is not a regular curve in this sense, since a continuous image of a compact interval is compact.
