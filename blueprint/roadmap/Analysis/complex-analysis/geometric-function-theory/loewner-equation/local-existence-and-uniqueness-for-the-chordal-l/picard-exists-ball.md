# Picard–Lindelöf theorem with solutions confined to a ball

Let $E$ be a real Banach space, $f : \mathbb{R} \times E \to E$, $t_{\min} \le t_0 \le t_{\max}$, $x_0 \in E$ and $a, r, L, K \ge 0$. Assume the Picard–Lindelöf conditions on $[t_{\min}, t_{\max}] \times \bar B(x_0, a)$: $f(t, \cdot)$ is $K$-Lipschitz on the closed ball $\bar B(x_0, a)$ for every $t \in [t_{\min}, t_{\max}]$, $f(\cdot, x)$ is continuous on $[t_{\min}, t_{\max}]$ for every $x \in \bar B(x_0, a)$, $\|f\| \le L$ on $[t_{\min}, t_{\max}] \times \bar B(x_0, a)$, and $L \max(t_{\max} - t_0, t_0 - t_{\min}) \le a$. Then there is $\alpha : \mathbb{R} \to E$ with $\alpha(t_0) = x_0$, $\alpha'(t) = f(t, \alpha(t))$ for $t \in [t_{\min}, t_{\max}]$ (derivatives within $[t_{\min}, t_{\max}]$), and $\alpha(t) \in \bar B(x_0, a)$ for every $t \in \mathbb{R}$.
