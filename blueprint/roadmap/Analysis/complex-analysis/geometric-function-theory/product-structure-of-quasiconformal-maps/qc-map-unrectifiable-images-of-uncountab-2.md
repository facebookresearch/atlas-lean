# Bishop–Hakobyan–Williams theorem on unrectifiable images of lines

This settles [Quasiconformal images of uncountably many lines without rectifiable curves](qc-map-unrectifiable-images-of-uncountab.md) in the affirmative: its statement holds.

Bishop, Hakobyan and Williams (§1.4, Corollary 1.8) construct, for Hausdorff dimensions arbitrarily close to $1$, a quasiconformal map $f$ of the plane and a set $S \subseteq \mathbb{R}$ of that dimension such that the image of every whole line $\mathbb{R} \times \{y\}$, $y \in S$, has no rectifiable subarc, and they state that this answers AimPL Problem 5.3. This result suffices for the linked statement. $S$ has positive dimension, so it is uncountable. $S$ contains no interval, because a quasiconformal map is absolutely continuous on almost every line and so maps segments of almost every horizontal line to rectifiable curves; hence $S$ is totally disconnected. A curve in the image of the union of these lines therefore pulls back under $f$ to a curve whose second coordinate ranges over a connected subset of $S$, a single point, so it lies in one line image; the image of the union of the lines $\mathbb{R} \times \{y\}$, $y \in S$, contains no nonconstant rectifiable curve. Composing with the rotation $z \mapsto iz$, which turns these horizontal lines into the vertical lines through the points of $-S$ and keeps the map quasiconformal, gives the linked statement with $E = -S$.

Separately, Theorem 1.9(3) reaches Hausdorff dimension equal to $1$, with a compact $S \subset [0, 1]$, but for the segments $[0, 1] \times \{y\}$, $y \in S$: a segment whose image has no rectifiable subarc says nothing about the rest of its line, so Theorem 1.9 does not by itself give the linked statement about whole lines.
