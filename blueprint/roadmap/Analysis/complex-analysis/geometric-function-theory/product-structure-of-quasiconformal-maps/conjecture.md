# Must quasiconformal maps preserving coordinate directions split?

AimPL Problem 5.5 (Mapping theory in metric spaces) asks whether quasiconformal maps of the plane whose differential preserves or switches the coordinate directions must be product maps. The proposition linked here is the affirmative answer, a proposed statement and not a theorem: every [quasiconformal](is-quasiconformal.md) homeomorphism $f$ of $\mathbb{R}^2$ whose differential at almost every point [maps each coordinate direction to a multiple of a coordinate direction](has-diagonal-or-swap-differential.md), with the same pattern, diagonal or anti-diagonal, for both directions, splits: there are functions $g, h : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ with $f(x_1, x_2) = (g(x_1), h(x_2))$ for all $(x_1, x_2)$, or $f(x_1, x_2) = (g(x_2), h(x_1))$ for all $(x_1, x_2)$.
