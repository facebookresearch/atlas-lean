# Positive point charges with more equilibrium points than Maxwell’s bound allows

Resolved false (unrefereed, AI-assisted; recorded by the problem list): Arathoon, Ball and Kvalheim
exhibit five positive point charges whose potential has a finite equilibrium set with at least 24
points, while Maxwell's bound allows `(5 - 1)² = 16`.

## Sources

- A. Eremenko, My favorite unsolved problems: Equilibrium points of Newtonian potentials (Maxwell), Question 2, https://www.math.purdue.edu/~eremenko/dvi/equil2.pdf; resolution: P. Arathoon, G. Ball and M. D. Kvalheim, The Maxwell Conjecture is False, arXiv:2607.27197 (2026), unrefereed preprint whose construction was suggested by an LLM; Eremenko's unsolved-problems page records it: 'A counterexample to Maxwell's conjecture is found, though it holds for 3 points. All AI assisted.', Theorem 1: for charges 1, 1, 1 at the vertices of an equilateral triangle and q_ε, q_ε at (0, 0, ±ε) the potential has at least 24 non-degenerate critical points for small ε > 0, and 'a small perturbation to the charge strengths gives a potential with a finite number k ≥ 24 of critical points all of which are non-degenerate' (§2: all charges stay positive, so the critical points lie in the convex hull of the charges); 24 > (5 - 1)² = 16, https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27197.
