# Strong maximum principle for planar harmonic functions

Let $U \subseteq \mathbb{C}$ be open and connected and let $u : \mathbb{C} \to \mathbb{R}$ be harmonic
on $U$. If $u$ attains its maximum over $U$ at a point $x_0 \in U$, then $u = u(x_0)$ on all of
$U$. No boundedness of $U$ is needed.

This is the strong maximum principle in dimension two, deduced from the maximum modulus principle
for holomorphic functions; the statement in every dimension is the
[strong maximum principle for harmonic functions](eq-on-closure-of-is-max-on-harmonic-on-n.md).

## Informally uses

- [A planar harmonic function is constant near an interior maximum](exists-ball-eq-on-of-is-max-on-complex.md):
  the set where $u$ attains its maximum is open; it is relatively closed by continuity, so it is
  all of the connected set $U$.

## Background

- [Strong maximum principle for harmonic functions](eq-on-closure-of-is-max-on-harmonic-on-n.md):
  the statement in every finite dimension, for bounded connected $U$ and the maximum over
  $\overline U$.
