# Types of a planar linearization

The seven types of a planar linearization with trace $\tau$ and determinant $\Delta$, assigned by
the [trace–determinant class](linear-type-2.md):

- saddle: $\Delta < 0$, real eigenvalues of opposite signs;
- degenerate: $\Delta = 0$, a zero eigenvalue;
- center: $\tau = 0 < \Delta$, purely imaginary eigenvalues (a linear center; the equilibrium is
  not hyperbolic);
- stable node: $\tau < 0 < \Delta$ and $4\Delta \le \tau^2$, real negative eigenvalues;
- stable focus: $\tau < 0$ and $\tau^2 < 4\Delta$, non-real eigenvalues with negative real part;
- unstable node: $\tau > 0$ and $0 < \Delta \le \tau^2 / 4$, real positive eigenvalues;
- unstable focus: $\tau > 0$ and $\tau^2 < 4\Delta$, non-real eigenvalues with positive real
  part.
