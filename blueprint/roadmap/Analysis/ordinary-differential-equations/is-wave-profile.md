# Traveling-wave profile of the Fisher–KPP equation

For real $D$, $r$ and $c$, a pair of functions $U, U' \colon \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ is a
traveling-wave profile of speed $c$ when, for every $z$, $U$ has derivative $U'(z)$ at $z$ and $U'$ has
derivative

$$\frac{-c\,U'(z) - r\,U(z)(1 - U(z))}{D}$$

at $z$, the quotient being read as $0$ when $D = 0$. For $D \ne 0$ this says that $U$ is twice
differentiable and satisfies the profile equation $D U'' + c U' + r U(1 - U) = 0$ of
$\partial_t u = D \partial_x^2 u + r u(1 - u)$ for waves $u(x, t) = U(x - ct)$.
