# Forward-invariant subsets lie in the largest one

For a vector field $f$ on a real normed space, a set $A$ is invariant when through every point of $A$ there is a solution of $x' = f(x)$ on all of $\mathbb{R}$ staying in $A$, and forward invariant when through every point of $A$ there is a solution on $[0, \infty)$ staying in $A$. The largest forward-invariant subset of $Z$ is the union of all forward-invariant subsets of $Z$. Every forward-invariant $A \subseteq Z$ is contained in it.
