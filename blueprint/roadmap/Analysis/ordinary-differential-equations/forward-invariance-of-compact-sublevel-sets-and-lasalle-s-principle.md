# Forward invariance of compact sublevel sets and LaSalle's principle for differential equations

Let $E$ be a real Banach space, let $D \subseteq E$ be open, and let $f : \mathbb{R} \times E \to E$ be continuous on $\mathbb{R} \times D$ and locally Lipschitz in the state, uniformly in time: every point of $\mathbb{R} \times D$ has a neighbourhood $N$ and a constant $L \ge 0$ with $\|f(t, u) - f(t, w)\| \le L \|u - w\|$ whenever $(t, u), (t, w) \in N \cap (\mathbb{R} \times D)$. Let $V : D \to \mathbb{R}$ be continuously differentiable, let $c \in \mathbb{R}$, and suppose that the sublevel set $K = \{x \in D : V(x) \le c\}$ is compact. Fix $t_0 \in \mathbb{R}$ and suppose that $DV(x)[f(t, x)] \le 0$ for every $t \ge t_0$ and every $x \in D$.

1. **Continuation and invariance.** For every $x_0 \in K$, the maximal solution $x$ of $x' = f(t, x)$, $x(t_0) = x_0$, in $\mathbb{R} \times D$ is defined on all of $[t_0, \infty)$, the function $t \mapsto V(x(t))$ is nonincreasing there, and $x(t) \in K$ for every $t \ge t_0$.
2. **Autonomous case and LaSalle's principle.** Suppose moreover that $f$ does not depend on $t$, and that $f$ is Lipschitz on $K$ with some constant $L$. For $x_0 \in K$ and $t \ge 0$ let $\varphi_t(x_0)$ be the value at time $t$ of the solution with $x(0) = x_0$. Then $(\varphi_t)_{t \ge 0}$ is a continuous semiflow on $K$, every orbit stays in $K$, and $t \mapsto V(\varphi_t(x_0))$ is nonincreasing. Consequently, let $Z = \{y \in K : V(\varphi_t(y)) = V(y) \text{ for all } t \ge 0\}$ and let $M = \{y \in K : \varphi_t(y) \in Z \text{ for all } t \ge 0\}$, the largest forward-invariant subset of $Z$. Then for every $x_0 \in K$ the ω-limit set of $x_0$ is nonempty and contained in $M$, and $\varphi_t(x_0)$ eventually enters every open set containing $M$.

Part 1 is a continuation and invariance statement only; it does not assert convergence for time-dependent $f$.

**Proof.** For part 1, let $x$ be the maximal solution, defined on an open interval $I \ni t_0$ with $x(t) \in D$ for $t \in I$. The function $g(t) = V(x(t))$ is differentiable on $I$ with $g'(t) = DV(x(t))[f(t, x(t))] \le 0$ for $t \ge t_0$, so $g$ is nonincreasing on $I \cap [t_0, \infty)$ and $V(x(t)) \le V(x_0) \le c$, that is, $x(t) \in K$ there. Suppose $b = \sup I < \infty$. Then $[t_0, b] \times K$ is a compact subset of $\mathbb{R} \times D$ containing $(t, x(t))$ for every $t \in [t_0, b)$, which contradicts the fact that the maximal solution eventually leaves every compact subset of $\mathbb{R} \times D$ as $t$ increases. So $b = \infty$.

For part 2, part 1 gives each orbit on $[0, \infty)$ inside $K$, and uniqueness of solutions gives $\varphi_{s + t} = \varphi_s \circ \varphi_t$ and $\varphi_0 = \mathrm{id}$. For $x, y \in K$ the difference $e(t) = \varphi_t(x) - \varphi_t(y)$ satisfies $\|e(t)\| \le \|x - y\| + L \int_0^t \|e(s)\|\, ds$, because both orbits stay in $K$, so Grönwall's inequality gives $\|\varphi_t(x) - \varphi_t(y)\| \le e^{Lt} \|x - y\|$. Together with the continuity of each orbit in $t$, this gives $\|\varphi_t(x) - \varphi_s(y)\| \le e^{Lt}\|x - y\| + \|\varphi_t(y) - \varphi_s(y)\|$, so $(t, x) \mapsto \varphi_t(x)$ is continuous on $[0, \infty) \times K$. The space $K$ is Hausdorff and compact, $V$ is continuous on it and nonincreasing along orbits by part 1, so LaSalle's invariance principle applies to every $x_0 \in K$.
