# The large and small arcs of the Nyquist contour

Let $L = n/d$ be a proper rational function with real coefficients and coprime $n$, $d$, with $d + n$ not identically zero (equivalently, $1 + L \not\equiv 0$), and let $L(\infty) = \lim_{|s| \to \infty} L(s)$, which exists because $L$ is proper.

1. **Large arc.** $L(Re^{i\theta}) \to L(\infty)$ as $R \to \infty$, uniformly in $\theta$. In particular, if $1 + L(\infty) \ne 0$, then for all large $R$ the image of the half-circle $\{Re^{i\theta} : |\theta| \le \pi/2\}$ under $1 + L$ lies in a disc about $1 + L(\infty)$ that does not contain $0$, so it changes the argument of $1 + L$ by less than $\pi$ and contributes no net turn about $0$ in the limit; and every zero and pole of $1 + L$ lies in $\{|s| < R\}$.
2. **Small indentations.** Let $i\omega_0$ be a pole of $L$ of order $k \ge 1$ on the imaginary axis, and let $\gamma_\varepsilon(\varphi) = i\omega_0 + \varepsilon e^{i\varphi}$ for $\varphi$ from $-\pi/2$ to $\pi/2$. There is $\varepsilon_0 > 0$ such that for $0 < \varepsilon < \varepsilon_0$ the closed half-disc $\{i\omega_0 + \rho e^{i\varphi} : 0 < \rho \le \varepsilon,\ |\varphi| \le \pi/2\}$ contains no zero or pole of $L$ or of $1 + L$, and $L(\gamma_\varepsilon(\varphi)) = c\,\varepsilon^{-k}e^{-ik\varphi}(1 + r_\varepsilon(\varphi))$ with $c \ne 0$ and $\sup_\varphi |r_\varepsilon(\varphi)| \to 0$ as $\varepsilon \to 0$. So $|L| \to \infty$ uniformly on the indentation, and the net change of a continuous argument of $L$ along it tends to $-k\pi$ as $\varepsilon \to 0$; for a fixed small $\varepsilon$ it need not equal $-k\pi$ exactly (for $L(s) = 1 + 1/s$ at $\omega_0 = 0$ it is $-2\arctan(1/\varepsilon)$).
3. **Counting.** Consequently, for every large $R$ and every small enough indentation radius, the poles of $L$ and the zeros of $1 + L$ inside the indented contour are exactly those in the open right half-plane.

**Proof.** For part 1, write $L(s) = L(\infty) + \tilde n(s)/d(s)$ with $\deg\tilde n < \deg d$, so that $|L(s) - L(\infty)| \le C/|s|$ for $|s|$ large; choose $R$ larger than the moduli of all roots of $d$ and $d + n$ and with $C/R < |1 + L(\infty)|/2$. For part 2, the zeros and poles of $L$ and $1 + L$ are finite in number, so a small enough half-disc around $i\omega_0$ contains none of them besides the excluded centre. Near $i\omega_0$, $L(s) = (s - i\omega_0)^{-k}g(s)$ with $g$ holomorphic and $g(i\omega_0) = c \ne 0$, and $(\varepsilon e^{i\varphi})^{-k} = \varepsilon^{-k}e^{-ik\varphi}$; continuity of $g$ gives the uniform remainder. The argument of $L$ along the indentation is $\arg c - k\varphi + \arg(1 + r_\varepsilon(\varphi))$, whose last term is uniformly small, so its net change over $\varphi \in [-\pi/2, \pi/2]$ is $-k\pi$ up to an error tending to $0$. Part 3 combines parts 1 and 2: the indented contour encloses the part of $\{|s| < R,\ \operatorname{Re} s > 0\}$ outside the half-discs, which by parts 1 and 2 contains the same zeros and poles as the open right half-plane, since imaginary-axis poles of $L$ are poles, not zeros, of $1 + L$, and coprimality prevents cancellation.
