# Convergence of Ramanujan's product series in a sector

Let $a, b, x \in \mathbb{C}$ with
$b \neq 0$ and let $0 < \varepsilon < \pi$. Then there is a real number $R$ such that for every
complex $z$ with $|(z + a)/b| \ge R$ and $-\pi + \varepsilon \le \arg((z + a)/b) \le
\pi - \varepsilon$, the series

$$\sum_{j=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-b)^j x^{j+1}}{\prod_{r=0}^{j} (z + a + b(r + 1))}$$

converges absolutely.

## Sources

- Berndt, Ramanujan's Notebooks Part I, Chapter 3, Entry 9(i), definitions printed p. 53 / PDF p. 63 and result printed p. 54 / PDF p. 64. The upper sector bound keeps $(z + a)/b$ off the negative real axis, so no factor of the denominator vanishes; the source's other hypotheses (the upper bound on $\varepsilon$, the radius and the lower sector bound) are not needed for the conclusion.
