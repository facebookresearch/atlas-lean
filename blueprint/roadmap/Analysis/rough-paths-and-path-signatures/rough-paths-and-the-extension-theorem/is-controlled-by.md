# $p$-variation controlled by $\omega$ (Lyons)

Let $\iota$ be a finite alphabet. Tensor series are given by their coefficients $F(w)$ on words $w$ over $\iota$, and the degree-$i$ level of $F$ carries the $\ell^1$ norm $\|F^i\| = \sum_{|w| = i} |F(w)|$. Let $p, \beta \in \mathbb{R}$, $n \in \mathbb{N}$, $J \subseteq \mathbb{R}$, let $\omega$ be a real function of two times and let $(s, t) \mapsto X_{s,t}$ be a family of tensor series. The levels $1, \dots, n$ of $X$ are controlled by $\omega$ with weight $\beta$ if

$$
\|X^i_{s,t}\| \le \frac{\omega(s,t)^{i/p}}{\beta\, \Gamma(i/p + 1)} \qquad (1 \le i \le n,\ s \le t \text{ in } J),
$$

where $\Gamma(i/p + 1) = (i/p)!$ (T. Lyons, *Differential equations driven by rough signals*, Rev. Mat. Iberoam. 14 (1998), Lemma 2.1.1 and inequality (2.20)).

The definition allows every real $p$, $\beta$ and every real-valued $\omega$, with the following conventions for the operations in the bound: division by $0$ gives $0$; for $x \ge 0$, $x^a$ is the usual power, with $0^0 = 1$ and $0^a = 0$ for $a \ne 0$; for $x < 0$, $x^a = e^{a \log |x|} \cos(\pi a)$; and $\Gamma$ takes the value $0$ at the nonpositive integers. In Lyons's setting, $p \ge 1$, $\beta > 0$ and $\omega \ge 0$, and none of these conventions is needed.
