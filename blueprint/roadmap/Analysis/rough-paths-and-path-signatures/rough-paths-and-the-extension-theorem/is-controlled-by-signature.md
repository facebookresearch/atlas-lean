# The signature of a continuously differentiable path is controlled by its length, with $p = 1$

Let $\iota$ be a finite alphabet and let $X$ be a path in $\mathbb{R}^\iota$ with continuous velocity $v = (v^j)_{j \in \iota}$; write $\omega(s, t) = \int_s^t \sum_j |v^j(u)|\, du$ for its $\ell^1$ length between $s$ and $t$, and $S(X)_{s,t}$ for its [signature](../signature-of-a-path/signature.md). Then for every $n$ and every $J \subseteq \mathbb{R}$, the levels $1 \le i \le n$ of the signature satisfy Lyons's estimate $\|S(X)^i_{s,t}\| \le \omega(s,t)^{i/p}/(\beta\, (i/p)!)$ with $p = 1$ and $\beta = 1$, that is, $\|S(X)^i_{s,t}\| \le \omega(s, t)^i / i!$ for $s \le t$ in $J$, with the $\ell^1$ norm on each level (T. Lyons, *Differential equations driven by rough signals*, Rev. Mat. Iberoam. 14 (1998), inequalities (2.20)–(2.21)).
