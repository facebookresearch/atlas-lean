# Chain rule for derivatives of functions of one variable over normed fields

If $g$ is holomorphic on an open set $U \subseteq \mathbb{C}$ with $g(U) \subseteq V$ and $f$ is holomorphic on an open set $V \subseteq \mathbb{C}$, then $f \circ g$ is holomorphic on $U$ with $(f \circ g)^\prime(z) = f^\prime(g(z))\,g^\prime(z)$ for every $z \in U$. Mathlib states the chain rule more generally, at a single point and for two fields: let $\mathbb{K}$ and $\mathbb{K}^\prime$ be nontrivially normed fields, with $\mathbb{K}^\prime$ a normed algebra over $\mathbb{K}$ (for example $\mathbb{K} = \mathbb{K}^\prime = \mathbb{C}$, or $\mathbb{K} = \mathbb{R}$ and $\mathbb{K}^\prime = \mathbb{C}$), let $h\colon \mathbb{K} \to \mathbb{K}^\prime$ and $h_2\colon \mathbb{K}^\prime \to \mathbb{K}^\prime$ be functions and let $x \in \mathbb{K}$. If $h_2$ has derivative $h_2^\prime$ at the point $h(x)$ and $h$ has derivative $h^\prime$ at $x$, then $h_2 \circ h$ has derivative $h_2^\prime \cdot h^\prime$ at $x$. Here a function $\varphi$ has derivative $\varphi^\prime$ at a point $y$ when $\varphi(y^\prime) = \varphi(y) + (y^\prime - y)\,\varphi^\prime + o(y^\prime - y)$ as $y^\prime \to y$; no differentiability away from the two points and no completeness is assumed. For $\mathbb{K} = \mathbb{K}^\prime = \mathbb{C}$ this gives the holomorphic statement: extend $f$ and $g$ arbitrarily outside $V$ and $U$; since $U$ and $V$ are open, complex differentiability of $g$ on $U$ and of $f$ on $V$ gives derivatives at every $z \in U$ and at $g(z) \in V$, and the rule applies at each $z \in U$.
