# Logarithm of a product

Let $z_1, z_2$ and $z_1 z_2$ lie in the slit plane $\mathbb{C} \setminus (-\infty, 0]$, and let $\operatorname{Log}$ and $\operatorname{Arg}$ be the principal logarithm and argument, with $\operatorname{Arg} w \in (-\pi, \pi)$ there. Then

$$\operatorname{Log}(z_1 z_2) = \operatorname{Log} z_1 + \operatorname{Log} z_2 + 2\pi i\, n$$

for some integer $n \in \{-1, 0, 1\}$; moreover $n = 0$ when $-\pi < \operatorname{Arg} z_1 + \operatorname{Arg} z_2 < \pi$, and in particular when $z_1 > 0$ (Theorem 3 of Lecture 2 of the MIT 18.112 notes).

The source defines $\operatorname{Log}$ only on the slit plane, so it requires $z_1 z_2$ to lie there too; the conclusion holds for all nonzero $z_1, z_2$ with the principal logarithm extended to $(-\infty, 0)$ by $\operatorname{Arg} = \pi$.
