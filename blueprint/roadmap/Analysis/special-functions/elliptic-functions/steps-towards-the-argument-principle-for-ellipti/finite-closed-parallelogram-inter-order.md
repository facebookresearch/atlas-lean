# Finitely many zeros and poles in a closed parallelogram

Let $f : \mathbb{C} \to \mathbb{C}$
be meromorphic at every point of $\mathbb{C}$, with finite meromorphic order at every point, and let
$z_0, \omega_1, \omega_2 \in \mathbb{C}$. Then the set of points
$P = z_0 + t_1\omega_1 + t_2\omega_2$ with $t_1, t_2 \in [0, 1]$ at which the order of $f$ is
nonzero, that is, the set of zeros and poles of $f$ in the closed parallelogram with vertex $z_0$
and sides $\omega_1$, $\omega_2$, is finite.

## Sources

- Milne, *Elliptic Curves*, 2nd ed., Chapter III, Proposition 2.1, pp. 86–87: the sums in (b) and (c) run over the points of $D$ where $f$ has a zero or a pole, and "Each sum is finite." The source takes $f$ elliptic for $\omega_1, \omega_2$; periodicity and linear independence of $\omega_1, \omega_2$ are not used and are dropped. The finite-order hypothesis says that $f$ vanishes on no punctured neighbourhood of any point, that is, $f$ is not the zero meromorphic function near any point; it is needed and is not implied by $f$ being nonzero at some point. A function that vanishes on a punctured neighbourhood of every point, such as the zero function or the indicator function of a lattice, has infinite, hence nonzero, order at every point.
