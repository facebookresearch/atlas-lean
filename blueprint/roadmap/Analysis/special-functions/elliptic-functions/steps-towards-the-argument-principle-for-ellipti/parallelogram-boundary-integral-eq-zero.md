# Opposite sides of a period parallelogram cancel

Let $g : \mathbb{C} \to \mathbb{C}$ and
$z_0, \omega_1, \omega_2 \in \mathbb{C}$. If $g(z + \omega_1) = g(z)$ and $g(z + \omega_2) = g(z)$
for all $z$, then the boundary integral of $g$ over the parallelogram with vertex $z_0$ and sides
$\omega_1$, $\omega_2$ is $0$.

## Sources

- Milne, *Elliptic Curves*, 2nd ed., Chapter III, proof of Proposition 2.1, p. 87: "Because $f$ is periodic, the integrals over opposite sides of $D$ cancel, and so the integral is zero." No integrability, meromorphy or linear independence of $\omega_1, \omega_2$ is needed: the integrands over opposite sides agree pointwise.
