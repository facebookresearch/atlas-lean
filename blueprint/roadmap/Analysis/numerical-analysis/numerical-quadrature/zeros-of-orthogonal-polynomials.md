# Zeros of orthogonal polynomials

Let $w$ be a weight function on $[a, b]$ and let $q$ be a nonzero real polynomial of degree $n + 1$ that is [$w$-orthogonal](orthogonal-polynomial-for-a-weight.md) to the polynomials of degree at most $n$. Then the $n + 1$ zeros of $q$ are real, simple, and lie in the open interval $(a, b)$.

If $q$ changed sign in $(a, b)$ only at $r \le n$ points $t_1, \dots, t_r$, then $q(x) \prod_i (x - t_i)$ would have one sign on $(a, b)$, and its integral against $w$ could not vanish, although $\prod_i (x - t_i)$ has degree at most $n$.
