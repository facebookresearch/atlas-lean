# Time derivative of the traveling sine wave

For all real $c_0, a, v, L, t, x$, with $\theta = 2\pi(x - vt)/L$, the function $\tau \mapsto c_0 + a \sin(2\pi(x - v\tau)/L)$ has derivative

$$\partial_t c(t,x) = -v\, a\,\frac{2\pi}{L}\cos\theta$$

at $t$.

Real division by zero is $0$: when $L = 0$ the quotients $2\pi(x - vt)/L$ and $2\pi/L$ are $0$, so the [traveling wave](traveling-wave.md) is the constant $c_0$ and the statement holds with $2\pi/L = 0$.
