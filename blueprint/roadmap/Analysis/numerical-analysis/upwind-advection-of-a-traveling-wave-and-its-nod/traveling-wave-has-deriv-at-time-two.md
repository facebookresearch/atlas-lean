# Second time derivative of the traveling sine wave

For all real $c_0, a, v, L, t, x$, with $\theta = 2\pi(x - vt)/L$, the time derivative $\tau \mapsto \partial_t c(\tau, x)$ of $c(t,x) = c_0 + a \sin\!\left(\frac{2\pi (x - v t)}{L}\right)$ has derivative

$$-v^{2}\, a\left(\frac{2\pi}{L}\right)^{2}\sin\theta$$

at $t$.

Real division by zero is $0$: when $L = 0$ the quotients $2\pi(x - vt)/L$ and $2\pi/L$ are $0$, so the [traveling wave](traveling-wave.md) is the constant $c_0$ and the statement holds with $2\pi/L = 0$.
