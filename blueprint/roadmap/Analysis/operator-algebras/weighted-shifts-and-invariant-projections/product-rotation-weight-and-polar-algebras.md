# Product rotation realization with atomic finite polar algebras and a diffuse full polar algebra

Let $X=(\mathbb R/\mathbb Z)^{\mathbb N}$ with product Haar probability, write $x_i$ for representatives in $[0,1)$, and use coordinatewise order on these representatives. There exist sequences $(\theta_i),(c_i)$ with $0<\theta_i<1$, $c_i>0$, $\sum_i\theta_i<\infty$, and $1,\theta_0,\theta_1,\ldots$ linearly independent over $\mathbb Q$. Put $w(x)=\exp(-\sum_i c_i\lfloor x_i+\theta_i\rfloor)$ with the extended nonnegative sum and $\exp(-\infty)=0$. Then $w>0$ almost everywhere and $\int-\log w=+\infty$.

For every finite coordinate set $I$, there is a sequence of finite measures on $X\times X$ converging against all continuous real tests to this triangular law: from two independent points $(x,z)$, restrict to $x_i<z_i$ for all $i\in I$, and replace the second point by $y$ equal to $z$ on $I$ and $x$ elsewhere. In each approximating measure, almost every pair is ordered and lies on a positive orbit step $y=x+n\theta$ from a finite set of positive integers depending on that measure. Both marginals have densities that are nonnegative almost everywhere with respect to product Haar measure, and one nonnegative constant bounds both $L^2$ norms for every approximating measure. The sequence and its bound may depend on $I$. The triangular law is the restriction and pushforward just described, without renormalization.

On $H=L^2(X;\ell_2(\mathbb Z;\mathbb C))$, let $U$ shift fibers by $(U\xi)(x,k)=\xi(x,k-1)$ and $\pi(g)$ multiply coordinate $(x,k)$ by $g(x+k\theta)$. Let $M$ be generated by $U$ and all bounded measurable multipliers $\pi(g)$, with constant trace vector $e=\delta_0$ in the fibers. It is a nonzero diffuse scalar-center factor with faithful tracial unit vector $e$ (vanishing at a positive operator implies that operator is zero) and with a separable complex Banach predual in the complex-linear isometric-dual convention. The member $T=U\pi(w)$ is nonzero, norm-quasinilpotent, has spectrum $\{0\}$, has only trivial invariant projections within $M$ (if $p\in M$ is a star projection with $(1-p)Tp=0$, then $p=0$ or $p=1$) and has determinant zero and Brown measure $\delta_0$ for the vector trace. The determinant is $\inf_{\epsilon>0}\exp(\operatorname{Re}\tau\log(T^*T+\epsilon I)/2)$; the Brown condition identifies the determinant of $T-zI$ for all $z$ with the infimum of the corresponding potentials $\exp(\int\log(|\lambda-z|^2+\epsilon)\,d\delta_0(\lambda)/2)$.

Writing $B=(T^*T)^{1/2}$, the shift $U$ is unitary and $T=UB$. The map $B$ is injective and the closed span of its eigenspaces over some countable set of eigenvalues is all of $H$. For every finite $F\subset\mathbb Z$, the von Neumann algebra generated by $\{U^nBU^{-n}:n\in F\}$ is abelian and atomic: every nonzero projection dominates a minimal nonzero one. The algebra generated by all integer conjugates is abelian and has no such atoms.

## Depends on

- Product Haar measures, representativewise order, orbit multipliers and the [generated-algebra model](../../not-yet-classified/generated/generated-by.md).
- Polar modulus, vector-trace determinant and translated logarithmic potential as defined above.

## Proof depends on

- Construction of costs and independent angles with all finite-coordinate sampling, and the associated tracial factor, spectral, projection and polar-algebra results.

## Expansion

Separate ordered-orbit sampling and its uniform marginal densities from logarithmic divergence and operator estimates. Identify the finite and full generated multiplier sigma algebras to prove atomicity and diffuseness separately. The weak sampling assertion by itself does not identify either polar algebra.
