# Free hypergraph C*-algebras

The free hypergraph C*-algebra of a finite hypergraph is the universal unital C*-algebra generated by one projection for each vertex, subject to the relation that the projections of the vertices of each edge sum to the identity.

A *hypergraph* $H$ consists of a finite set $V(H)$ of vertices and a set $E(H) \subseteq 2^{V(H)}$ of edges such that every vertex lies in at least one edge.

**Definition.** The *free hypergraph C\*-algebra* of $H$ is the finitely presented unital [C\*-algebra](../../cstar-algebra.md)

$$
C^*(H) = \Big\langle (p_v)_{v \in V(H)} \;\Big|\; p_v = p_v^* = p_v^2, \ \ \sum_{v \in e} p_v = 1 \ \text{for all } e \in E(H) \Big\rangle ,
$$

the presentation being taken in the category of unital C\*-algebras and unital $*$-homomorphisms. It exists because every generator is a projection and so has norm at most $1$. Unital representations of $C^*(H)$ on a Hilbert space $\mathcal H \ne 0$ correspond to families of [closed subspaces](../../../../Topology/topological-modules/closed-linear-subspace.md) of $\mathcal H$ indexed by $V(H)$ such that the subspaces of the vertices of each edge are pairwise orthogonal and span $\mathcal H$; such a family is a *quantum representation* of $H$.

The algebra $C^*(H)$ may be zero. For instance, if $H$ has three vertices and its edges are the three pairs, then $p_1 + p_2 = p_1 + p_3 = p_2 + p_3 = 1$ forces $p_1 = p_2 = p_3 = 1/2$, which is not a projection unless $1 = 0$.

## Expansion

Stating this definition needs the universal unital C\*-algebra of a presentation whose relations bound the norms of the generators: the source constructs $C^*(H)$ as the completion of the free $*$-algebra $\mathbb C[H]$ on the projections $p_v$, modulo the relations, in the supremum of the seminorms given by all its $*$-representations, which is finite because the relations bound the norm of every generator by $1$ (the paragraph after Definition 2.1 of the source, which refers to Ozawa for the construction). A good expansion adds and links a page for that construction.
