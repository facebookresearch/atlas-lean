# Positive square roots are unique

Let $A$ be a ring, possibly without unit, with an involution $a \mapsto a^*$, that is also a real vector space whose
scalars commute and associate with its multiplication. Suppose that $A$ carries a Hausdorff topology in which addition
and negation are continuous and multiplication is continuous in each variable separately; that it is ordered so that
$a \le b$ exactly when $b - a$ is a finite sum of elements of the form $c^* c$; that it has a continuous functional
calculus for its self-adjoint elements by continuous real functions vanishing at $0$; and that every nonnegative element
of $A$ has its real quasispectrum in $[0, \infty)$. C*-algebras satisfy these hypotheses, and so do the $n \times n$
real or complex matrices, ordered by $M \le N$ when $N - M$ is positive semidefinite. If $a$ and $b$ are elements of $A$
with $0 \le a$ and $0 \le b$, then $a\,a = b\,b$ if and only if $a = b$. So a nonnegative element of $A$ has at most one
nonnegative square root.
