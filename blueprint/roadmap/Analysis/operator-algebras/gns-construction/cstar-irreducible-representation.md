# Irreducible representation of a C*-algebra

Let $A$ be a C\*-algebra and $H$ a complex Hilbert space. A representation of $A$ on $H$ is a $*$-homomorphism $\pi\colon A \to B(H)$ into the bounded linear operators on $H$. It is *irreducible* if $\pi$ is not the zero map and the only closed linear subspaces $M \subseteq H$ that are invariant under $\pi(A)$, meaning that $\pi(a)M \subseteq M$ for every $a \in A$, are $M = \{0\}$ and $M = H$. We exclude the zero representation: the zero map passes the invariant-subspace test whenever $H$ has dimension at most one. If $A$ is unital, an irreducible representation satisfies $\pi(1) = I$, since $\pi(1)$ is a projection whose range is a closed invariant subspace, nonzero because $\pi$ is not the zero map; so it is also a representation in the unital sense.
