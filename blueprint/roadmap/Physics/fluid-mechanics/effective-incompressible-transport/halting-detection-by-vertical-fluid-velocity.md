# Finite-machine halting detected by two vertical-velocity observables

Let $P(s)=\theta(3s+3)\theta(3-3s)$ with $\theta$ the smooth transition that is zero on $(-\infty,0]$, one on $[1,\infty)$, and $e^{-1/x}/(e^{-1/x}+e^{-1/(1-x)})$ on $(0,1)$. The target asserts a natural number $B>0$ bounding $|P''(s)|$ for all real $s$, chosen once for the uniform construction. For every positive computable $\nu$ (rational name error $2^{-k}$), every pair of positive alphabet and state sizes, and every Mathlib `TM0` machine $M$ on those finite alphabets and states with word $w$, there are force families $F_{\rm tor},F_{\rm cyl}$ with viscosity as their first real variable. In this model the initial state and blank symbol are the distinguished defaults; each optional table instruction either writes a symbol or moves one cell and changes state, and the computation halts exactly when `TM0.eval M w` is defined. This is not an unnamed simulation of an arbitrary machine model.

For both families the fixed source program compiled from $B,M,w$ represents the force on the corresponding domain: for each component, iterated coordinate derivative in **viscosity, time and three spatial variables**, dyadic coordinate names of a point with positive viscosity, and positive rational tolerance, its finite rational trial search terminates with error at most the tolerance. The associated rational-box search terminates with a nonnegative bound for the same derivative everywhere on any closed rational box whose lower viscosity endpoint is positive. These are properties of the specified compiler, not merely the existence of an unrelated approximating program.

- **Unit three-torus.** At viscosity $\nu$, the force is smooth on the cover, factors through the unit torus at each time, and is zero outside the projection of a compact $K\subset(0,1)^2$ in the horizontal coordinates, with every spacetime derivative bounded on each finite time slab. There is a smooth incompressible velocity $u$ from rest solving the forced equation with zero pressure, and

    $$
    (\exists t\ge0,\ x\in\mathbb R^3:\ 1/32<x_2<1/8,\ u_3(t,x)>1/2)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad M\text{ halts on }w.
    $$

    The comparison class uses periodic velocity and pressure, $C^2$ spatial velocity slices, $C^1$ spatial pressure slices, and continuous velocity, right-at-zero time derivative, spatial derivatives through order two and pressure and gradient on every finite slab. Pressure has zero mean on the unit cube at every nonnegative time. Every competitor with these properties, the same force and zero data has velocity $u$ and pressure zero there.
- **Cylinder $\mathbb R^2\times(\mathbb R/\mathbb Z)$.** At viscosity $\nu$, the force is smooth, independent of the vertical variable, has globally bounded mixed derivatives of each order in time and space, and has compact horizontal support on every finite time slab. There is a smooth zero-data incompressible solution $u$ with pressure zero. For each nonnegative time $u_3$ is nonnegative and integrable over the cylinder. Halting is equivalent to the existence of $t\ge0$ such that the integral of $u_3(t,\cdot)$ over the open horizontal half-plane $x_2>0$ times the circle exceeds $1/2$. Circle integration uses the representative interval $(0,1]$. The comparison class uses spatially periodic-in-height $C^2$ velocity and $C^1$ pressure slices, $C_tH_x^2\cap C_t^1L_x^2$ velocity and $C_tH_x^1$ pressure on each finite slab with the same cylinder measure, continuous classical derivatives as for the torus, and uniformly bounded velocity and first spatial derivatives on each slab. Every competitor with this force and zero data in that class has velocity $u$ and pressure zero for $t\ge0$.

In both domains the equation is $\partial_tu+D_xu\,u=-\nabla p+\nu\Delta u+f$ on the cover, using the right time derivative at zero and Cartesian spatial coordinates. The two detectors are observables of the velocity field, not material trajectories.

## Expansion

Separate the torus pointwise estimate from the cylinder mass estimate, sharing the fixed compiler and bump bound. Prove the finite-stack representation respects the quotient by trailing blanks, then verify that the coefficient interval searches terminate and approximate the exact force derivatives. Prove positive vertical mass and integrability before using the totalized integral. Establish the two uniqueness results with their respective zero-mean and square-integrability pressure hypotheses.
