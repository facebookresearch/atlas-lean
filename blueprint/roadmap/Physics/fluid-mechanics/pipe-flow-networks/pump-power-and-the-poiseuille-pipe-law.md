# Pump power and the Poiseuille pipe law

The hydraulic power delivered to a fluid flowing at volumetric rate $Q$ through a pressure rise $\Delta p$ is $\dot W = Q\,\Delta p$.
Take Poiseuille's law $\Delta p = 8\mu L Q/(\pi R^4)$ as the constitutive law of a horizontal tube of length $L > 0$ and radius
$R > 0$ carrying an incompressible fluid of viscosity $\mu > 0$ in laminar flow, with $Q$ signed. Then:

- the dissipated power $Q\,\Delta p = 8\mu L Q^2/(\pi R^4)$ is nonnegative and vanishes only at $Q = 0$; reversing $Q$ reverses
  $\Delta p$;
- tubes in series carrying the same flow have pressure drops that add, with total resistance $\sum_i 8\mu L_i/(\pi R_i^4)$;
- the pump power needed to drive the flow $Q$ through such a series is $Q^2 \sum_i 8\mu L_i/(\pi R_i^4)$.

The law is stipulated, not derived from the Navier–Stokes equations, and turbulent friction factors are outside this result.
