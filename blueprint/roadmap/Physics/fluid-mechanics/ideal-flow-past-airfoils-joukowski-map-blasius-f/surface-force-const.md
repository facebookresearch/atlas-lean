# A constant pressure exerts no net force on a circle

For all real `c` and `a`, the constant pressure `p ≡ c` exerts zero [horizontal](surface-force-x.md)
and [vertical](surface-force-y.md) pressure force on the circle of radius `a`: `F_x = F_y = 0`,
because `∫₀^{2π} cos θ dθ = ∫₀^{2π} sin θ dθ = 0`. So adding a constant to the pressure, such as the
far-field pressure `p∞`, does not change the force. Physically `a > 0`; the identity holds for every
real `a`.
