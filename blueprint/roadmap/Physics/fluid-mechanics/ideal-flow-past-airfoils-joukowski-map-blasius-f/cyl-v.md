# Vertical velocity v of the flow past a cylinder with circulation

For real `U`, `a`, `Γ`, `x`, `y`, with `r² = x² + y²`, the vertical velocity is
`v(x, y) = −2Ua²xy/r⁴ + Γx/(2πr²)`. It is meant for `r ≠ 0`; with the convention that division by
zero gives `0`, its value at the origin is `v(0, 0) = 0`.

At `z = x + iy ≠ 0`, `−v` is the imaginary part of the complex velocity `U(1 − a²/z²) + Γ/(2πi z)`,
read as `u − iv` ([complex form](circle-velocity-zero-eq-cyl.md)); with the
[horizontal velocity](cyl-u.md) `u` it gives the velocity field `(u, v)`. In the model, `a > 0` is
the radius of a cylinder centred at the origin, the flow region is `r ≥ a`, the free stream has
speed `U` along the positive `x`-axis and `Γ` is the circulation about the cylinder (`Γ > 0`
counterclockwise).
