# Fréchet derivatives in coordinates are inner products with the gradient

For a scalar field $f$ on three-dimensional space, an origin $o$, a point $p$ and a coordinate vector $w$, the Fréchet derivative of $v \mapsto f(v + o)$ at $p - o$, applied to $w$, equals $\langle \nabla f(p), w\rangle$. Where $f$ is not differentiable both sides are $0$ by convention (the Fréchet derivative and the gradient are then taken to be zero), so this need not agree with a classical one-sided or directional derivative there.
