# Darcy's law with intrinsic permeability and hydraulic head

Let a fluid of constant density $\rho > 0$ and viscosity $\mu > 0$ flow through a porous medium with constant symmetric positive
semidefinite intrinsic permeability matrix $K$, under gravity $-g e_z$ with $g > 0$, with Darcy flux
$q = -\frac{1}{\mu} K(\nabla p + \rho g\,e_z)$. Then $q \cdot (\nabla p + \rho g\,e_z) \le 0$: the flow dissipates. For an isotropic
medium, $K = kI$ with $k > 0$, the hydraulic head $h = p/(\rho g) + z$, in the same length unit, gives $q = -K_h \nabla h$ with
hydraulic conductivity $K_h = k\rho g/\mu$, and a homogeneous confined layer of thickness $b > 0$ has transmissivity $T = K_h b$. The
pore velocity is $q/\phi$, defined only for porosity $\phi > 0$. Deriving Darcy's law from pore-scale flow is outside this result.
