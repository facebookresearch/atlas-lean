# Mean and derivative do not commute without domination

For every real $\omega$ and $s$ (with the convention $x/0 = 0$, so everything vanishes at $\omega = 0$), the realization $s \mapsto f(s, \omega) = \frac{s^3}{\omega^2}e^{-s^2/\omega}$ is differentiable at $s$ with derivative $\big(\frac{3s^2}{\omega^2} - \frac{2s^4}{\omega^3}\big)e^{-s^2/\omega}$, which vanishes at $s = 0$. On the probability space $(0, 1]$ with Lebesgue measure, the ensemble mean is $s\,e^{-s^2}$, whose derivative at $0$ is $1$; and no integrable function bounds the derivatives almost everywhere on any neighbourhood of $s = 0$.
