# Equal mixed partials of q give the head wave equation h_tt = a² h_xx

Let `a`, `g` and `A` be positive (wave speed, gravitational acceleration, pipe area), and let `h`,
`q` (head and discharge) and `h_t`, `h_x`, `q_t`, `q_x` be real functions of `(t, x)`. Suppose that
for all real `t` and `x`: `h(·, x)` and `q(·, x)` have the derivatives `h_t(t, x)` and `q_t(t, x)`
at `t`; `h(t, ·)` and `q(t, ·)` have the derivatives `h_x(t, x)` and `q_x(t, x)` at `x`; and the
water-hammer equations `h_t + (a² / (g A)) q_x = 0` and `q_t + g A h_x = 0` hold. Let moreover
`h_xx`, `q_xt` and `q_tx` be real functions of `(t, x)` such that, for all `t` and `x`, `h_x(t, ·)`
has the derivative `h_xx(t, x)` at `x`, `q_x(·, x)` has the derivative `q_xt(t, x)` at `t`, and
`q_t(t, ·)` has the derivative `q_tx(t, x)` at `x`. If `q_xt(t, x) = q_tx(t, x)` for all `t` and
`x`, then at every point `(t, x)` the function `h_t(·, x)` has the derivative `a² h_xx(t, x)` at
`t`: the head satisfies the wave equation `h_tt = a² h_xx`, with `h_tt` the time derivative of the
field `h_t`.
