# Normal shock: stagnation temperature and pressure ratio

Let $\gamma > 1$, $R > 0$, $c_p = \gamma R/(\gamma - 1)$, and let $(\rho_1, u_1, p_1, T_1)$, $(\rho_2, u_2, p_2, T_2)$ with $\rho_1, T_1, \rho_2, T_2 > 0$ satisfy the normal-shock jump conditions. For $k = 1, 2$ let the stagnation state $(\rho_{0k}, p_{0k}, T_{0k})$ satisfy $\rho_{0k} > 0$, $c_p T_k + u_k^2/2 = c_p T_{0k}$, $p_{0k} = \rho_{0k} R T_{0k}$ and $p_k/\rho_k^\gamma = p_{0k}/\rho_{0k}^\gamma$. Then $T_{02} = T_{01}$ and

$$\frac{p_{02}}{p_{01}} = \exp(-\Delta s / R), \qquad \Delta s = \frac{R}{\gamma - 1} \log\frac{T_2}{T_1} + R \log\frac{\rho_1}{\rho_2}.$$

This holds on either branch of the jump conditions.
