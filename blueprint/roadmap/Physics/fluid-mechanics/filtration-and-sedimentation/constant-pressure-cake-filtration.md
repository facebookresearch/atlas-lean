# Constant-pressure cake filtration

For constant filter area $A > 0$, pressure drop $\Delta P > 0$, filtrate viscosity $\mu > 0$, medium resistance $R_m > 0$, specific
cake resistance $\alpha \ge 0$ and solids concentration $c \ge 0$, the filtrate volume $V(t)$ with $V(0) = 0$ and

$$\frac{dV}{dt} = \frac{A\,\Delta P}{\mu\,(R_m + \alpha c V/A)}$$

satisfies $t = \dfrac{\mu \alpha c}{2 A^2 \Delta P} V^2 + \dfrac{\mu R_m}{A\,\Delta P} V$. This map from volume to time is strictly
increasing on $V \ge 0$; inverting it gives the unique nonnegative filtrate volume, linearly when $\alpha c = 0$ and by the positive
root of a quadratic otherwise. With $R_m = 0$ the initial value problem is singular at $V = 0$ and is not covered. Compressible cakes
are outside this result.
