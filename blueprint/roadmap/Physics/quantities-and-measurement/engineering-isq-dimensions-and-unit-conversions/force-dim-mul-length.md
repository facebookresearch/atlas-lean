# Force times length has the dimension of energy

Writing $[X]$ for the ISQ dimension of a quantity $X$: $[F]\,\mathsf{L} = \mathsf{M}\,\mathsf{L}^{2}\,\mathsf{T}^{-2}$, the energy dimension; this covers both a torque and work.

The same identity gives the dimension of the compliance $J = F u$ (the work of the end force on its displacement), and of torsional stiffness, a torque per radian, since plane angle is dimensionless; the dimension alone does not distinguish $\mathrm{N\,m/rad}$ from $\mathrm{J}$.
