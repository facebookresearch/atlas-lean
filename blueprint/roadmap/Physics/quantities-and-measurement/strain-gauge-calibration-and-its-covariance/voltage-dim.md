# Electric-potential dimension over the seven ISQ base quantities

The voltage dimension is the electric-potential dimension

$$
\mathsf{L}^2\,\mathsf{M}\,\mathsf{T}^{-3}\,\mathsf{I}^{-1}
$$

over the seven base quantities of the International System of Quantities (length, mass, time,
electric current, thermodynamic temperature, amount of substance and luminous intensity); its SI
unit is the volt, $\mathrm{kg\,m^2\,s^{-3}\,A^{-1}}$. It is defined as the image of the voltage
dimension $\mathsf{L}^2\,\mathsf{T}^{-2}\,\mathsf{M}\,\mathsf{Q}^{-1}$ of the five-base system
(length, time, mass, charge and temperature) under its inclusion into the ISQ base quantities, which
keeps length, mass, time and temperature and sends charge $\mathsf{Q}$ to $\mathsf{I}\,\mathsf{T}$;
its [exponents](voltage-dim-exponent.md) are $2$, $1$, $-3$ and $-1$ at length, mass, time and
electric current and $0$ at the other three base quantities. In the strain-gauge calibration it is
the dimension of the gauge voltage, of the offset and of the gain, which is in volts per unit of the
dimensionless strain.
