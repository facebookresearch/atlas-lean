# Real masses of a probability mass function form a finite distribution

For a probability mass function $q$ on a finite set $\Omega$, whose masses $q(\omega)$ are extended
nonnegative reals summing to one (so each is finite), the masses read as real numbers are
nonnegative and sum to one:

$$
q(\omega) \ge 0 \text{ for every } \omega \in \Omega, \qquad \sum_{\omega \in \Omega} q(\omega) = 1.
$$

They are thus the weights of a finite distribution, the setting of the weighted means, variances and
covariances of the strain-gauge calibration.
