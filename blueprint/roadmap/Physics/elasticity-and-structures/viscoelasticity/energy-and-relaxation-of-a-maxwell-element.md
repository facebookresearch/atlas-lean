# Energy and relaxation of a Maxwell element

A Maxwell element, a spring of modulus $E > 0$ in series with a dashpot of viscosity $\eta > 0$, obeys
$\varepsilon' = \sigma'/E + \sigma/\eta$. Its stored elastic energy density $V = \sigma^2/(2E)$ satisfies

$$V' = \sigma\,\varepsilon' - \frac{\sigma^2}{\eta},$$

power supplied per unit volume minus power dissipated per unit volume in the dashpot. For a uniform element of fixed volume,
multiplying the identity by that volume gives total energy and power. At held strain, $\varepsilon' = 0$, the stress relaxes as
$\sigma(t) = \sigma_0 e^{-E t/\eta}$ for $t \ge 0$, with relaxation time $\eta/E$. These are statements about one linear element with
constant parameters, not a calibrated model of a pavement layer or a structure; temperature and rate calibration, fatigue, healing and
nonlinear contact are outside this result.
