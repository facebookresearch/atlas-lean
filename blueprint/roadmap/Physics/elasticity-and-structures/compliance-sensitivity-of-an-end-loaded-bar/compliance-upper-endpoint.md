# For F ≠ 0 the upper endpoint meets a one-sided first-order condition

For an end force $F \ne 0$ and $0 < k_{\min} < k_{\max}$, at the upper endpoint $k_{\max}$ of the
feasible interval:

- the [compliance](compliance.md) $J(F, \cdot)$ has the derivative $-F^2 / k_{\max}^2$ within
  $[k_{\min}, k_{\max}]$;
- this derivative is negative, so $k_{\max}$ is not a stationary point;
- for every [feasible direction](feasible-directions.md) $d$ at $k_{\max}$ (these are the
  $d \le 0$), the first-order change $-F^2 / k_{\max}^2 \cdot d$ is nonnegative, and it is zero only
  for $d = 0$.

So $k_{\max}$ satisfies the one-sided first-order condition for a minimum of the compliance on the
interval without being a stationary point.
