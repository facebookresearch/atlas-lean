# A force per length has the translational stiffness dimension M T⁻²

Dimensions are those of the International System of Quantities: products of powers of the seven base
dimensions, such as $\mathsf{M}\,\mathsf{L}\,\mathsf{T}^{-2}$ for a force and
$\mathsf{M}\,\mathsf{L}^2\,\mathsf{T}^{-2}$ for an energy, with $\mathsf{L}$ the length dimension.
The translational stiffness dimension is defined as the force dimension divided by $\mathsf{L}$,
that is $\mathsf{M}\,\mathsf{T}^{-2}$, so the identity

$$
\dim(\text{force}) / \mathsf{L} = \dim(\text{translational stiffness})
= \mathsf{M}\,\mathsf{T}^{-2}
$$

holds by that definition. Read for the [axial stiffness](axial-stiffness.md) $k = EA / L$: an axial
rigidity, a force, over a length has the dimension of a translational stiffness. The statement is an
identity between dimensions and does not involve the stiffness itself.
