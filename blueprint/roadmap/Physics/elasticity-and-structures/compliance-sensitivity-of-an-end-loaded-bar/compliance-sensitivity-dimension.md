# An energy per translational stiffness has dimension length squared

Dimensions are those of the International System of Quantities: products of powers of the seven base
dimensions, such as $\mathsf{M}\,\mathsf{L}\,\mathsf{T}^{-2}$ for a force and
$\mathsf{M}\,\mathsf{L}^2\,\mathsf{T}^{-2}$ for an energy, with $\mathsf{L}$ the length dimension.
An energy per translational stiffness has the dimension of a squared length:

$$
\dim(\text{energy}) / \dim(\text{translational stiffness})
= \mathsf{M}\,\mathsf{L}^2\,\mathsf{T}^{-2} / (\mathsf{M}\,\mathsf{T}^{-2}) = \mathsf{L}^2.
$$

Read for the [sensitivity](has-deriv-at-compliance.md) $dJ / dk = -F^2 / k^2$ of the compliance to
the stiffness. The statement is an identity between dimensions only.
