# A force over a translational stiffness has the length dimension

Dimensions are those of the International System of Quantities: products of powers of the seven base
dimensions, such as $\mathsf{M}\,\mathsf{L}\,\mathsf{T}^{-2}$ for a force and
$\mathsf{M}\,\mathsf{L}^2\,\mathsf{T}^{-2}$ for an energy, with $\mathsf{L}$ the length dimension. A
force over a translational stiffness has the length dimension:

$$
\dim(\text{force}) / \dim(\text{translational stiffness})
= \mathsf{M}\,\mathsf{L}\,\mathsf{T}^{-2} / (\mathsf{M}\,\mathsf{T}^{-2}) = \mathsf{L}.
$$

Read for the [tip displacement](tip-displacement.md) $u_{\mathrm{tip}} = F / k$ and the
[scalar adjoint](adjoint-displacement.md) $p = F / k$: both are lengths. The statement is an
identity between dimensions only.
