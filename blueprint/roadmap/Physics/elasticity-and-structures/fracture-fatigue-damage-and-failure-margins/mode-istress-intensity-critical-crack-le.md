# For positive $K_{IC}, Y, \sigma$: $K_I(a_c) = K_{IC}$

Let the fracture toughness $K_{IC}$, the geometry factor $Y$ and the peak tensile stress $\sigma$ be
positive. At the [critical crack length](critical-crack-length.md) $a_c = (K_{IC}/(Y\sigma))^2/\pi$,
the [mode-I stress intensity](mode-istress-intensity.md) equals the fracture toughness:

$$K_I(a_c) = Y \sigma \sqrt{\pi a_c} = K_{IC}.$$

This is the critical-crack criterion of linear-elastic fracture mechanics (Dowling, *Mechanical
Behavior of Materials*, 4th ed.), for the adopted intensity model. The positivity is used: with
$K_{IC} = -1$ and $Y = \sigma = 1$ the stress intensity at $a_c$ is $1$.
