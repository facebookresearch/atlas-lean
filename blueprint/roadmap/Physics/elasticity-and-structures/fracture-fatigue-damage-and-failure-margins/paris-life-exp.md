# Paris life in the logarithmic branch, $N_f = \log(a_c/a_0)/K$

For real numbers $K$, $a_0$ and $a_c$, the logarithmic-branch Paris life is

$$N_f = \frac{\log(a_c / a_0)}{K}.$$

For $K > 0$ and positive crack lengths $a_0$ and $a_c$, the
[logarithmic-branch crack](paris-crack-exp.md) $a_0 e^{KN}$, started at $a_0$,
[reaches the target length $a_c$ at $N = N_f$](paris-crack-exp-at-life.md), for example the critical
crack length, and $N_f$ is [positive when $a_0 < a_c$](paris-life-exp-pos.md). With the conventions
$x / 0 = 0$, $\log 0 = 0$ and $\log x = \log |x|$, $N_f = 0$ when $K = 0$, $a_0 = 0$ or $a_c = 0$,
and $N_f = \log|a_c/a_0| / K$ when $a_c / a_0 < 0$. Here $K$ is the aggregate growth coefficient of
the reduced Paris law, not a stress intensity.
