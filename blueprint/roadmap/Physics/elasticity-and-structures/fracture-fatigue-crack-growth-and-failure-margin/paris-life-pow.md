# Paris life in the power branch, $N_f = (a_c^r - a_0^r)/(rK)$

For real numbers $r$, $K$, $a_0$ and $a_c$, the power-branch Paris life is

$$N_f = \frac{a_c^r - a_0^r}{r K},$$

with real powers. For $K > 0$, $r \ne 0$ and positive crack lengths $a_0$ and $a_c$, it is the
number of cycles at which the [power-branch crack](paris-crack-pow.md)
$a(N) = \bigl(a_0^r + r K N\bigr)^{1/r}$, started at $a_0$,
[reaches the target length $a_c$](paris-crack-pow-at-life.md), for example the critical crack
length; it is [positive when $a_0 < a_c$](paris-life-pow-pos.md). With the convention $x / 0 = 0$,
$N_f = 0$ when $r = 0$ or $K = 0$; for nonpositive lengths the real-power conventions apply
($0^r = 0$ for $r \ne 0$, $s^r = |s|^r \cos(\pi r)$ for $s < 0$). Here $K$ is the aggregate growth
coefficient of the reduced Paris law, not a stress intensity.
