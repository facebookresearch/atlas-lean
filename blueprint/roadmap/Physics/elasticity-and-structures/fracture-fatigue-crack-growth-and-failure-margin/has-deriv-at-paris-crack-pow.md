# For $r \ne 0$ and positive base, the power crack solves the Paris law

Let $r \ne 0$ and let $K$ and $a_0$ be real. At every cycle count $N$ where the base
$B(N) = a_0^r + r K N$ is positive, the [power-branch crack](paris-crack-pow.md) $a = B^{1/r}$ is
differentiable at $N$ with

$$a'(N) = K\, a(N)^{1 - r}.$$

With $1 - r = m/2$ this is the reduced Paris law $da/dN = K a^{m/2}$; since $a(N) > 0$ there, the
power is the classical one. No sign condition on $K$ or $a_0$ is needed, but $r \ne 0$ is: for
$r = 0$ the formula is the constant $1$, whose derivative $0$ differs from $K$ when $K \ne 0$. Here
$K$ is the aggregate growth coefficient of the reduced Paris law, not a stress intensity.
