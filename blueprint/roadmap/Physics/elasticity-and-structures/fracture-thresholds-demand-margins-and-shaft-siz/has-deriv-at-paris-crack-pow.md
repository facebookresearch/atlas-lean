# Power-branch crack solves the Paris law

Let $r \ne 0$ and let $K, a_0, N$ be real with $a_0^{\,r} + r K N > 0$. Then the power-branch crack length $a(M) = (a_0^{\,r} + r K M)^{r^{-1}}$ is differentiable at $N$ with

$$a'(N) = K\, a(N)^{1 - r}.$$

With $r = 1 - m/2$ this is the Paris law $da/dN = K a^{m/2}$.

Here $x^y$ is the real power: $e^{y \log x}$ for $x > 0$, $\lvert x\rvert^y \cos(\pi y)$ for $x < 0$, and for $x = 0$ it is $1$ when $y = 0$ and $0$ otherwise. The hypothesis makes the base positive, but $a_0$ itself may be any real number.
