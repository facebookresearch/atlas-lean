# Critical crack length

For a fracture toughness $K_{IC}$, geometry factor $Y$ and peak tensile stress $\sigma$, the critical crack length is

$$a_c = \frac{1}{\pi} \left(\frac{K_{IC}}{Y \sigma}\right)^2,$$

with the convention $x / 0 = 0$. For positive toughness $K_{IC}$, geometry factor $Y$ and tensile stress $\sigma$, it is the crack length at which the mode-I stress intensity $Y \sigma \sqrt{\pi a}$ reaches $K_{IC}$. Outside that range the formula need not be a crossing point: with $K_{IC} = Y = 1$ and $\sigma = 0$ it gives $0$, while the intensity is $0$ for every crack length and never reaches $1$.
