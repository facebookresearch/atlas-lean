# The derivative of the load-path strain is −f/k

Let $L > 0$, let $f \colon \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ be continuous on $[0, L]$, and let $k$ and $P$
be any real numbers. For every $x \in [0, L]$, the derivative within $[0, L]$ (one-sided at the
ends) of the [load-path strain](fixed-traction-strain.md) $\varepsilon(x) = (P + \int_x^L f)/k$ is

$$\varepsilon'(x) = -\frac{f(x)}{k}.$$

So for $k > 0$ the strain decreases where the load is positive. No condition on $k$ is needed: for
$k = 0$ both sides are $0$, by the convention $a/0 = 0$.
