# Compatibility law of a thin closed rectangular cell

For real numbers $a$, $b$, $t$, $G$, $q$ and $\theta'$, the compatibility law holds if

$$\sum_{i=1}^{4} \frac{q}{G t}\, \ell_i = 2\, a b\, \theta',$$

where $(\ell_1, \ell_2, \ell_3, \ell_4) = (a, b, a, b)$ are the [wall lengths](cell-wall-length.md);
equivalently $\frac{q}{Gt} \cdot 2(a + b) = 2ab\, \theta'$. It is the declared reduced model of the
twist of a single thin closed cell (Bredt's second formula): the circulation of the wall shear
strain $q/(Gt)$ around the median line equals twice the enclosed area $ab$ times the twist rate
$\theta'$, for walls of thickness $t$ and shear modulus $G$ carrying the shear flow $q$. It is a
stated law, not derived from three-dimensional elasticity. In the model $a, b, t, G > 0$; with the
convention $x/0 = 0$, when $G t = 0$ the left side is $0$ and the law reads $ab\, \theta' = 0$.
