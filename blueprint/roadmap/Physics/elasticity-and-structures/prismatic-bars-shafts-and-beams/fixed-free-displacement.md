# Displacement of a fixed-free bar under a uniform axial load

For real numbers $EA$, $L$, $q$ and $x$, the fixed-free displacement is

$$u(x) = \frac{q\, x\, (2L - x)}{2\, EA}.$$

It is the displacement of a bar of length $L$ and axial rigidity $EA$, fixed at $x = 0$ and
traction-free at $x = L$, under the uniform distributed axial load $q$: for $L > 0$, $EA > 0$ and
$q \ne 0$ it satisfies $-EA\, u'' = q$, $u(0) = 0$ and $EA\, u'(L) = 0$
([fixed-free example](fixed-free-displacement-not-is-fixed-fix.md)). With $EA$ replaced by $k$ it is
the [fixed-traction displacement](fixed-traction-displacement.md) under the uniform load $q$ with
zero end force ([uniform-load formula](fixed-traction-displacement-const.md)). In the physical
reading $L > 0$, $EA > 0$ and $x \in [0, L]$; with the convention $a/0 = 0$ the formula is $0$ when
$EA = 0$.
