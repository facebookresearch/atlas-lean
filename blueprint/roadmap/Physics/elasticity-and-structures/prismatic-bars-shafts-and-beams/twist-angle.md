# Twist of a fixed-fixed shaft under a uniform distributed torque

For real numbers $GJ$, $L$, $t$ and $x$, the twist angle is

$$\varphi(x) = \frac{t\, x\, (L - x)}{2\, GJ}.$$

It is the twist, in radians, of a circular shaft of length $L$ and torsional rigidity $GJ$, fixed
against rotation at both ends, under the uniform distributed torque $t$: for $L > 0$ and $GJ > 0$ it
[solves the fixed-fixed problem](is-fixed-fixed-solution-twist-angle.md) $-GJ\, \varphi'' = t$,
$\varphi(0) = \varphi(L) = 0$, and [every solution equals it](eq-on-twist-angle.md) on $[0, L]$. In
the physical reading $L > 0$, $GJ > 0$ and $x \in [0, L]$; the formula is defined for all real
inputs, and with the convention $a/0 = 0$ it is $0$ when $GJ = 0$.
