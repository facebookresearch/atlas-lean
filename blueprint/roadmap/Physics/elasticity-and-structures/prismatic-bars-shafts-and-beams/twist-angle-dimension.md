# Dimension check: the shaft twist formula is dimensionless

In the group of physical dimensions generated by the seven ISQ base dimensions, with $\mathsf{L}$
the dimension of length and $\mathsf{F} = \mathsf{M}\, \mathsf{L}\, \mathsf{T}^{-2}$ that of force,

$$\mathsf{F}\, \mathsf{L} \,/\, \mathsf{L} \cdot \mathsf{L} \cdot \mathsf{L}
\,/\, (\mathsf{F}\, \mathsf{L}\, \mathsf{L}) = 1.$$

This is the dimension of the [twist angle](twist-angle.md) $t\, x\, (L - x)/(2\, GJ)$: a torque per
length times two lengths, divided by a torsional rigidity (a force times a length squared), is
dimensionless, as a plane angle in radians is. Only dimensions are compared, not numerical values.
