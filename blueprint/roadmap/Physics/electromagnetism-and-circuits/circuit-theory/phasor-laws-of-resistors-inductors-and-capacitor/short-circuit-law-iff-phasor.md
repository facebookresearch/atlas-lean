# A short circuit for sinusoids has zero voltage phasor

Let `ω > 0`, let `V` and `I` be complex phasors, and let `v` and `i` be the real voltage and current
whose values at every time `t` are the
[RMS waveforms](../average-power-of-sinusoidal-signals/rms-waveform.md) `v(t) = √2 Re(V e^{iωt})`
and `i(t) = √2 Re(I e^{iωt})`. Then the short-circuit law `v(t) = 0 · i(t)` holds at every time `t`
exactly when `V = 0`.
