# At R = 0 the admittance form of Ohm's law fails

The equivalence of `V = R I` and `I = V / R` fails at `R = 0`: it is not true that for all
complex `V` and `I`, `V = 0 · I` exactly when `I = V / 0`. With the convention `x / 0 = 0`,
`V = 0` and `I = 1` satisfy the first equation but not the second. So the
[admittance form](resistor-phasor-iff-admittance.md) needs `R ≠ 0`.
