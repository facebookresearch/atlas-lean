# Complex power absorbed by one phase

For complex phasors `V` (voltage) and `I` (current), the complex power is

`S = V Ī`,

the product of `V` with the complex conjugate of `I`, defined for all complex `V` and `I`. For RMS
phasors under the passive sign convention (the current `I` enters the element at the terminal whose
voltage `V` is measured as positive), `S` is the complex power absorbed, with real power `Re S` and
reactive power `Im S`. For the RMS waveforms of `V` and `I` at a frequency `ω > 0`, `Re S` is the
average of `v(t) i(t)` over a period
([average power](../../circuit-theory/average-power-of-sinusoidal-signals/average-rms-waveform-mul-rms-waveform.md)).
There is no factor `1/2`: for phasors given by peak amplitudes the absorbed complex power is written
`V Ī / 2`, and for `ω > 0` the average of the product of the peak waveforms over a period is
`Re(V Ī) / 2`
([peak phasors](../../circuit-theory/average-power-of-sinusoidal-signals/average-peak-waveform-mul-peak-waveform.md)).
