# Bolted fault current behind a nonzero pure reactance

For a complex source voltage $E$ and a real $X \neq 0$, a bolted fault, $Z_f = 0$, behind the purely
reactive Thévenin impedance $Z_{\mathrm{th}} = i X$ has [fault current](fault-current.md)

$$I_f = \frac{E}{i X} = \frac{-i\, E}{X}.$$

For $E \neq 0$ the current lags the source voltage by a quarter period when $X > 0$, an inductive
reactance and the usual physical case, and leads it when $X < 0$: with phasors rotating as
$e^{i\omega t}$, $\omega > 0$, multiplying a phasor by $e^{-i\theta}$ is a phase lag $\theta$, a
time delay $\theta / \omega$.
