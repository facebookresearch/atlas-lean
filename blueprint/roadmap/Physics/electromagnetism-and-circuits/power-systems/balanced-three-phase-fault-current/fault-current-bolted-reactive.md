# Bolted fault current behind a nonzero pure reactance

For a complex source voltage $E$ and a real $X \neq 0$, a bolted fault, $Z_f = 0$, behind the purely
reactive Thévenin impedance $Z_{\mathrm{th}} = i X$ has [fault current](fault-current.md)

$$I_f = \frac{E}{i X} = \frac{-i\, E}{X}.$$

With the phasor convention in which multiplying by $e^{-i\theta}$ is a lag by $\theta$, as for the
[balanced phases](../balanced-three-phase-power/balanced-phases.md), the current lags the source
voltage by a quarter period when $X > 0$, an inductive reactance and the usual physical case, and
leads it when $X < 0$.

## Sources

- J. J. Grainger and W. D. Stevenson Jr., *Power System Analysis*, McGraw-Hill (1994).
