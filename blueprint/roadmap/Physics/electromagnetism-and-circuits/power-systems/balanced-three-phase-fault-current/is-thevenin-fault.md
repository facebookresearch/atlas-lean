# Thévenin source and balanced fault laws for one phase

For complex numbers $E$, $Z_{\mathrm{th}}$, $Z_f$, $V$ and $I$, the Thévenin fault laws hold when

$$V = E - Z_{\mathrm{th}}\, I \quad \text{and} \quad V = Z_f\, I.$$

In the physical reading they describe one phase of a balanced positive-sequence system at a fixed
frequency: $E$ is the internal voltage phasor of the Thévenin equivalent at the fault point,
$Z_{\mathrm{th}}$ its Thévenin impedance, $Z_f$ the impedance of the balanced fault, and $V$ and $I$
the voltage and current phasors at the faulted terminals. In a power system the impedances have
nonnegative real parts (resistances) and the Thévenin reactance is usually positive; the definition
allows all complex values.
