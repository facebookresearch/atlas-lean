# Swapping the terminals reverses the terminal current

Let `S ⊆ ℝ²` be a measurable cross-section of Lebesgue measure (area) `A > 0`, let `ℓ > 0` and `V`
be real, and let `E, J : ℝ² → ℝ` satisfy `E = V / ℓ` (the
[uniform axial field](uniform-axial-field.md)) and the local Ohm law `J = σ E` at every point of
`S`, with `σ ≥ 0`. Then the [terminal current](terminal-current.md) of `−J` is `−I`, where
`I = ∫_S J` is the terminal current of `J`, and it equals `(σ A / ℓ) (−V)`: with the terminals
swapped the voltage drop is `−V` and the law still holds.
