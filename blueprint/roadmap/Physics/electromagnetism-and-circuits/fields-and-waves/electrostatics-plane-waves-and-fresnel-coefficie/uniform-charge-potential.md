# Potential of a uniformly charged interval

For reals `ρ` (uniform charge density), `ε` (absolute permittivity), `L` and `x`, the potential
formula of a uniformly charged interval `[0, L]` is `φ(x) = ρ / (2ε) · x (L − x)`; it vanishes at
both ends, [`φ(0) = 0`](uniform-charge-potential-zero.md) and
[`φ(L) = 0`](uniform-charge-potential-l.md). For `ε ≠ 0` it satisfies the
[discrete Poisson equation](poisson-second-difference.md)
`φ(x + h) − 2 φ(x) + φ(x − h) = −(ρ / ε) h²`. With the convention `x / 0 = 0` it is `0` everywhere
when `ε = 0`. In the physical reading `ε > 0` and `0 ≤ x ≤ L`; the formula is defined for every
real `x`.
