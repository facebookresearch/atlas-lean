# Without the time conversion, E_y = −c a(x) ∂f/∂s(ct, z)

Let `c > 0` be the speed of light, `a : ℝ → ℝ`, and `f : ℝ × ℝ → ℝ` infinitely differentiable. Let
`A` be the four-potential whose `y` component at the spacetime point `(x⁰, x¹, x², x³)` is
`a(x¹) f(x⁰, x³)`, with `x⁰` fed into `f` without dividing by `c`, and whose other components are
`0`. At physical time `t`, where `x⁰ = ct`, and position `(x, y, z)`, its electric field is

`E(t, x, y, z) = (0, -c a(x) ∂f/∂s(ct, z), 0)`,

with `∂f/∂s` the derivative of `f` in its first argument: a factor `c` and the time `ct` instead of
`t`.
