# Thermal voltage from the carrier-charge magnitude

For real numbers $k_B$, $T$ and $q$, the thermal voltage is

$$V_T = \frac{k_B T}{|q|},$$

with the magnitude $|q|$ of the carrier charge in the denominator, never the signed charge $q$.
With the convention $x / 0 = 0$, $V_T = 0$ when $q = 0$. In the physical reading $k_B > 0$ is the
Boltzmann constant in the chosen coherent unit system, $T > 0$ the temperature and $q \ne 0$ the
carrier charge; the definition takes any real inputs.
