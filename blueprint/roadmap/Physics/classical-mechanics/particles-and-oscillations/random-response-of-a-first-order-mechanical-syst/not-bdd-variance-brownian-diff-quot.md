# The variances of Brownian difference quotients are unbounded

Let `B` be a process indexed by times `t ≥ 0` whose finite-dimensional laws are those of a
standard Brownian motion, and let `t ≥ 0`. Then no real constant bounds the variances of the
difference quotients `(B(t + h) - B(t)) / h` over all steps `h > 0`: they equal `1 / h`
([variance](variance-brownian-diff-quot.md)), which grows without bound as `h → 0`. This is why
the damper equation is read in integral form.
