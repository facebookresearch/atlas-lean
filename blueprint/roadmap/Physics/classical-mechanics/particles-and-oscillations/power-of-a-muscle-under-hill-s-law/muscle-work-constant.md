# Work at a constant force under Hill's law

Let `a > 0`, `b > 0`, `F₀ > 0`, a constant force `c ∈ [0, F₀]` and a time `T ≥ 0`. If the
displacement `x` has derivative `v(c) = b (F₀ - c) / (c + a)` at every `t ∈ [0, T]` (the
[Hill shortening velocity](hill-velocity.md) at the constant force `c`), then the mechanical work
over `[0, T]`, the time integral of the constant power `c v(c)`, is

`∫₀ᵀ c v(c) dt = c (x(T) - x(0))`.
