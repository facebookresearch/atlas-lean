# Away from the centre, the inverse-square field is a gradient

Let `E` be a complete real inner product space, `μ` a real number and `x ∈ E` with `x ≠ 0`. Then
the potential `y ↦ -μ / ‖y‖` has gradient `(μ / ‖x‖³) x` at `x`, the value of the
[inverse-square field](../../gravitation-and-orbits/lipschitz-bounds-for-the-inverse-square-field/inverse-square-field.md).
It is the case `G = μ`, `M = 1` of the
[point-mass potential gradient](../../gravitation-and-orbits/newtonian-potential-of-a-point-mass/has-gradient-at-point-mass-potential.md).
