# The trim is a relative equilibrium

Let `B` be a mass distribution of mass `1`, with centre of mass `0` and inertia tensor
`diag(I_xx, I_yy, 1)` about it for some real `I_xx` and `I_yy`, and let `p₁` and `p₂` be real.
Consider the [planar motion](planar-motion.md) of `B` with position
`(X₁(t), X₂(t)) = (p₁ + t, p₂)` and zero pitch, `θ(t) = 0`. At every time `t`:

- its [longitudinal state](planar-state.md) is `0`;
- the [six-degree-of-freedom plant](six-dof-plant.md) holds, with the
  [longitudinal maps](longitudinal-maps.md) and gravity `(0, −1, 0)`;
- its centre of mass moves with the velocity `(1, 0, 0)`.

So the trim is a relative equilibrium, not a stationary world position.
