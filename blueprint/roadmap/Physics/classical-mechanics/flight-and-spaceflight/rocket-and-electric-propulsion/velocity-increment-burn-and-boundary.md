# For nonincreasing mass the velocity increment is nonnegative

Let `tb ≥ 0`, functions `m` and `v` with derivatives `m'` and `v'` at every `t ∈ [0, tb]`, `m > 0`
on `[0, tb]`, and the reduced variable-mass law `m(t) v'(t) = −ve m'(t)` on `[0, tb]`, with
`ve > 0`, and let `m` be nonincreasing on `[0, tb]`. Then `v(tb) − v(0) ≥ 0`; it is `0` if and only
if `m(tb) = m(0)`, and positive if and only if `m(tb) < m(0)`. No continuity of the derivatives is
needed.
