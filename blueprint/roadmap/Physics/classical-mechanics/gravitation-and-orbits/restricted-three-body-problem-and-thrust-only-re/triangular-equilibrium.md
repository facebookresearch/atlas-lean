# At the triangular points both partial derivatives of Ω vanish

For every real `μ` and for `s = 1` or `s = -1`, the triangular point `(1/2 - μ, s √3/2)` of the
orbital plane, in rotating-frame coordinates, is at distance `r₁ = r₂ = 1` from the
[first](primary-dist-one.md) and [second](primary-dist-two.md) primaries, so it is off both of
them and forms an equilateral triangle with them, and both partial derivatives of the
[effective potential](effective-potential.md) `Ω` vanish there: `Ω` has derivative `0` in `x` at
`1/2 - μ` with `y = s √3/2` fixed, and derivative `0` in `y` at `s √3/2` with `x = 1/2 - μ`
fixed. A particle at rest at either point therefore satisfies the unforced
[rotating equations](rotating-equations.md): the triangular points are equilibria in the rotating
frame. Nothing is stated about their stability. In the physical reading `0 < μ < 1`.
