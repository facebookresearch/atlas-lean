# Co-rotation at a triangular point solves Newton's equations

For every real `μ`, for `s = 1` or `s = -1` and at every time `t`, the inertial path
`X(t) = R(t) (1/2 - μ, s √3/2, 0)` of a particle at rest at a
[triangular point](triangular-equilibrium.md), with `R(t)` the
[orientation of the rotating frame](rotating-frame.md), satisfies Newton's law
`X''(t) = g(X(t), t)` under the [field of the two primaries](primary-gravity.md) `g`. The path is
the circle of radius `√(1 - μ + μ²)` about the barycenter, traversed counterclockwise at unit
angular speed. In the physical reading `0 < μ < 1`.
