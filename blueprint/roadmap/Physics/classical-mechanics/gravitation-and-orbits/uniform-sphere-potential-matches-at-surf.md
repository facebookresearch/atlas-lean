# Interior and exterior uniform-sphere potentials match at the surface

Let $G$ and $M$ be real and $R > 0$, and let

$$\Phi_{in}(r) = -\frac{GM}{2R}\Bigl(3 - \Bigl(\frac{r}{R}\Bigr)^2\Bigr), \qquad
\Phi_{out}(r) = -\frac{GM}{r}.$$

At $r = R$ the two agree, $\Phi_{in}(R) = -GM/R = \Phi_{out}(R)$, and both are differentiable at $R$
with the same derivative $GM/R^2$. This is a statement at the single point $r = R$; no piecewise
potential joining the two formulas is defined. In the physical reading, $\Phi_{in}$ and $\Phi_{out}$
are the potentials inside ($r \le R$) and outside ($r \ge R$) a uniform sphere of mass $M > 0$ and
radius $R$, with $G > 0$; both formulas are taken as given, and the identities hold for all real $G$
and $M$.
