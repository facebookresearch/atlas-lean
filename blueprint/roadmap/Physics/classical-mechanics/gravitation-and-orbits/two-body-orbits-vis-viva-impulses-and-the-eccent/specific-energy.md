# Specific energy of a relative two-body state

For a real `μ` and a state given by a position `x ∈ ℝ³` and a velocity `v ∈ ℝ³`, the specific energy
is `E = |v|² / 2 − μ / r`, with the [squared speed](speed-sq.md) `|v|² = v · v` and the
[distance](radius.md) `r = √(x · x)` from the attracting center at the origin. With the convention
`c / 0 = 0` it is `E = |v|² / 2` at the collision point `x = 0`. In the physical reading, `x` and
`v` are the position and velocity relative to the attracting center, `x ≠ 0`, `μ = G (m₁ + m₂) > 0`
is the gravitational parameter of the two masses, and `E` is the energy per unit reduced mass.
