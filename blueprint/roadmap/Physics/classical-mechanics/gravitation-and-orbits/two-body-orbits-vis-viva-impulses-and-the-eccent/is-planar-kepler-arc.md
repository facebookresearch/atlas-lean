# Premises of a planar Kepler arc on a time interval

For reals `μ`, `t₀`, `t₁` and real functions `x, y, vx, vy` of time, the planar position and
velocity coordinates relative to the attracting center, the planar Kepler-arc premises are:

- the gravitational parameter is positive, `μ > 0`: the central force attracts;
- the arc takes positive time, `t₀ < t₁`;
- the arc stays away from the center: `ρ(t) = √(x(t)² + y(t)²) > 0` for every `t` in `[t₀, t₁]`;
- at every `t` in `[t₀, t₁]`, `x` and `y` have derivatives `vx(t)` and `vy(t)`;
- at every `t` in `[t₀, t₁]`, `vx` and `vy` have derivatives `−μ x(t) / ρ(t)³` and
  `−μ y(t) / ρ(t)³`, the Newtonian central acceleration.

The derivatives are two-sided derivatives of functions defined on all of ℝ, also at the endpoints
`t₀` and `t₁`. No conservation law is a premise.
