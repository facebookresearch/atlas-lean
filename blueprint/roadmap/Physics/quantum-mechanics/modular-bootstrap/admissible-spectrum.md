# Admissible spectrum for spinless modular crossing

Let $c>1$ be the common left and right central charge, $c'=c-1$, $a=c'/12$, and $t=\Delta-a$. The physical setting is a unitary compact two-dimensional conformal field theory with discrete spectrum, a unique normalizable vacuum, and no conserved currents beyond Virasoro. Nonvacuum primary weights satisfy $h,\bar h>0$, with $\Delta=h+\bar h$ and no nonvacuum null descendants. The torus partition function is invariant under $S$; at zero angular potential only the inverse temperature $\beta>0$ and its reciprocal enter. Counting primaries by dimension does not require that they have spin zero.

In the spectral relaxation, take a countable family $(t_n,d_n)$, indexed by $n\ge1$, with $t_n>-a$, $d_n>0$, and only finitely many levels in every bounded interval. For each real $\beta>0$ require finiteness and crossing of the reduced partition function:

$$
\widehat Z(\beta)=e^{2\pi\beta a}(1-e^{-2\pi\beta})^2+\sum_{n\ge1}d_n e^{-2\pi\beta t_n},
\qquad \widehat Z(\beta)=\beta^{-1}\widehat Z(1/\beta).
$$

For a theory $\widehat Z(\beta)=|\eta(i\beta)|^2 Z(\beta)$, where $\eta$ is the Dedekind eta function. The vacuum is at $t=-a$ and its null descendants give the square factor; $t=0$ is the shifted threshold, not the vacuum. A primary is light if $t_n<0$. The gap is $\Delta_1=a+\min_n t_n$. No integrality of the positive weights or existence of a conformal field theory realizing an arbitrary admissible spectrum is assumed in the relaxation. The main results use $c\ge40$.

## Scope of the spectral relaxation

The defining data are the countable family and its convergent Laplace sums above. The crossing equation rules out an empty primary spectrum: as $\beta\to\infty$, the vacuum term at $\beta$ tends to infinity, while the vacuum term at $1/\beta$ multiplied by $\beta^{-1}$ tends to zero. The lower bound and local finiteness then give a smallest $t_n$ and only finitely many light levels. The character formulas explain why a theory in the stated physical setting gives these data; a theory realizing the family is not part of the definition.
