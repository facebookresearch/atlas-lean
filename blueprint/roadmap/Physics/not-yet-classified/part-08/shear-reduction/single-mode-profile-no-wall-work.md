# Dropping the moving-wall term predicts false decay

For $\rho > 0$, $\mu > 0$, $L > 0$, $A \ne 0$ and $t \ge 0$, let $\nu = \mu/\rho$ and

$$U(t, y) = \frac{A y}{L} - \frac{A}{\pi} \sin\Big(\frac{\pi y}{L}\Big) e^{-\nu (\pi/L)^2 t}.$$

Then the kinetic energy $K(s) = \int_0^L \rho\, U(s, y)^2/2 \, dy$ does not have derivative $-\mu \int_0^L U_y(t, y)^2 \, dy$ at $t$.
