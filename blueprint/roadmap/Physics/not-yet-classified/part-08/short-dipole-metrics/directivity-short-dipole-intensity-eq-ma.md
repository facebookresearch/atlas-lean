# Short-dipole directivity attains its maximum only at the equator

For $K \in \mathbb{R}$, let $U(\theta) = K \sin^2\theta$ be the short-dipole radiation intensity, $P_{\mathrm{rad}} = \int_0^{2\pi} \int_0^{\pi} U(\theta) \sin\theta \, d\theta\, d\phi$ its radiated power, and $D(\theta) = 4\pi U(\theta) / P_{\mathrm{rad}}$ its directivity. If $K > 0$ and $\theta \in [0, \pi]$, then

$$D(\theta) = \tfrac{3}{2} \iff \theta = \tfrac{\pi}{2}.$$
