# Lossless plane wave as the real part of a conjugate phasor

Let $\varepsilon > 0$, $\mu > 0$, $\omega > 0$, and let $\beta = \omega\sqrt{\mu\varepsilon/2}\,\sqrt{2}$ be the phase constant at zero conductivity. Then for all real $E_0$, $x$, $t$,

$$E_0\cos(\beta x - \omega t) = \operatorname{Re}\left(e^{i(\beta x - \omega t)}\,E_0\right).$$
