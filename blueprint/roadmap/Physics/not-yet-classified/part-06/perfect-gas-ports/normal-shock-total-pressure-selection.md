# Total pressure across the two branches

Let $\gamma > 1$ and $R > 0$, and let an upstream state $(\rho_1, u_1, p_1, T_1)$ and a downstream state $(\rho_2, u_2, p_2, T_2)$ with $\rho_1, T_1, \rho_2, T_2 > 0$ satisfy the normal-shock jump conditions (ideal-gas law, conservation of mass, momentum and total enthalpy) and differ. Let $a_1^2 = \gamma R T_1$ and $M_1 = |u_1|/a_1 > 0$. With $c_p = \gamma R/(\gamma - 1)$, let the stagnation states satisfy $c_p T_i + u_i^2/2 = c_p T_{0i}$, $\rho_{0i} > 0$, $p_{0i} = \rho_{0i} R T_{0i}$ and $p_i/\rho_i^\gamma = p_{0i}/\rho_{0i}^\gamma$ for $i = 1, 2$. Then:

- if $M_1 > 1$, the total pressure falls: $p_{02} < p_{01}$;
- if $M_1 < 1$, then $(\gamma - 1)/(2\gamma) < M_1^2 < 1$, the total pressure rises, $p_{01} < p_{02}$, and the specific entropy change $\frac{R}{\gamma - 1} \ln(T_2/T_1) + R \ln(\rho_1/\rho_2)$ is negative.
