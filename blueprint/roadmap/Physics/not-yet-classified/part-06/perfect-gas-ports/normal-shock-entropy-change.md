# Entropy change across the nontrivial branch

Let $\gamma > 1$ and $R > 0$, and let an upstream state $(\rho_1, u_1, p_1, T_1)$ and a downstream state $(\rho_2, u_2, p_2, T_2)$ with $\rho_1, T_1, \rho_2, T_2 > 0$ satisfy the normal-shock jump conditions of a calorically perfect gas: $p = \rho R T$ at both states and conservation of $\rho u$, $p + \rho u^2$ and $\tfrac{\gamma R}{\gamma - 1} T + u^2/2$. Let $a_1^2 = \gamma R T_1$, $M_1 = |u_1|/a_1$, $m = M_1^2$, $r(m) = \dfrac{(\gamma + 1) m}{(\gamma - 1) m + 2}$ and $P(m) = \dfrac{2\gamma m - (\gamma - 1)}{\gamma + 1}$. If the downstream state differs from the upstream state, then $m > (\gamma - 1)/(2\gamma)$, $m \ne 1$, and the specific entropy change $\tfrac{R}{\gamma - 1}\log(T_2/T_1) + R \log(\rho_1/\rho_2)$ equals

$$R\left(\frac{\log P(m)}{\gamma - 1} - \frac{\gamma \log r(m)}{\gamma - 1}\right).$$
