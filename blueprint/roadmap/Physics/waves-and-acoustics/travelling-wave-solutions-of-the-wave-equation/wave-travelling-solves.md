# The travelling wave A cos(kx − ckt) solves the wave equation

For all real `A`, `k`, `c`, `x` and `t`, the travelling wave `u(x, t) = A cos(k x - c k t)`
satisfies the one-dimensional wave equation `∂²u/∂t² = c² ∂²u/∂x²` at `(x, t)`, as its
[second time derivative](wave-second-deriv-time.md) is `-(c k)² u` and its
[second space derivative](wave-second-deriv-space.md) is `-k² u`. The wave is a function of
`x - c t`: for `c > 0` and `k ≠ 0` it travels towards increasing `x` at speed `c`. In the physical
reading `c > 0` is the wave speed; the identity holds for all real values, including zero
amplitude and zero wavenumber.
