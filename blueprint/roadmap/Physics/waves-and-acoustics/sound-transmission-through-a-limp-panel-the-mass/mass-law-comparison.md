# Limp-panel loss and its mass-law asymptote for x > 0

For every real `x > 0`, three facts hold, with `log₁₀` the base-10 logarithm and
`x⁻² = 1 / x²`: the split `10 log₁₀ (1 + x²) = 20 log₁₀ x + 10 log₁₀ (1 + x⁻²)`; the strict
inequality `20 log₁₀ x < 10 log₁₀ (1 + x²)`; and the doubling law
`20 log₁₀ (2x) = 20 log₁₀ x + 20 log₁₀ 2`. In the normal-incidence limp-panel model,
`x = ω m / (2 ρ c)` is a dimensionless ratio, with `ω` the angular frequency, `m` the mass of the
panel per unit area, `ρ` the density of the air and `c` the speed of sound in it, and the
transmission coefficient is `τ = 1 / (1 + x²)`, so `10 log₁₀ (1 + x²) = 10 log₁₀ (1 / τ)` is the
transmission loss in decibels; positive `ω`, `m`, `ρ` and `c` give `x > 0`. The loss is then the
mass-law asymptote `20 log₁₀ x` plus the positive correction `10 log₁₀ (1 + x⁻²)`, so it strictly
exceeds the asymptote, and doubling `x` (the frequency or the surface mass) adds `20 log₁₀ 2`,
about `6 dB`, to the asymptote.
