# Spatial crystallographic restriction: orders 1, 2, 3, 4 or 6

Let `R` be a real `3 × 3` matrix with `Rᵀ R = 1`, `R Rᵀ = 1` and `det R = 1`, with a unit vector
`u ∈ ℝ³` (`u₁² + u₂² + u₃² = 1`) such that `R u = u`, and suppose `R` is a
[lattice endomorphism](is-lattice-endomorphism.md). Let `n ≥ 1` be its order: `Rⁿ = 1` and
`R^m ≠ 1` for `0 < m < n`. Then `n ∈ {1, 2, 3, 4, 6}`. For a matrix of finite order, being a lattice
endomorphism is equivalent to being a [lattice symmetry](is-lattice-symmetry.md), so this bounds the
orders of the rotations in the point group of a full-rank lattice in space.
