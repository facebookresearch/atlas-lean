# Cubic invariance makes a symmetric rank-two tensor scalar

Let `S` be a real `3 × 3` matrix with `Sᵀ = S` that is invariant under conjugation by the three
quarter-turns about the coordinate axes: `R_xᵀ S R_x = S`, `R_yᵀ S R_y = S` and `R_zᵀ S R_z = S`,
with [`R_x`](rx90.md), [`R_y`](ry90.md) and [`R_z`](rz90.md). Then `S = S₁₁ · 1`, where `S₁₁` is the
first diagonal entry. Read with Neumann's principle: a symmetric second-rank property, such as the
thermal conductivity, of a crystal whose point group contains the quarter-turns about the cube axes
(classes `432` and `m3̄m`) or the corresponding rotoinversions (class `4̄3m`; inversion acts
trivially on second-rank tensors) is isotropic.
